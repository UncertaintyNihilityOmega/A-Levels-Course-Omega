## What is accounting?

Accounting has two main branches.

- **Financial accounting** records what has happened. Every transaction is entered by **double-entry bookkeeping** (each one affects two accounts), the records are checked, and at the end of the year they become **financial statements**: the statement of profit or loss (did the business make a profit?) and the statement of financial position (what does it own and owe?).
- **Cost and management accounting** looks forward. It works out what products really cost, sets budgets, compares results with plans, and helps managers decide: should we make this product or buy it in, accept this order, invest in this machine?

Accounting also uses **ratios** to analyse performance (profitability, liquidity, efficiency) and has rules and principles so that accounts are reliable and comparable.

## What do accountants do?

- **Auditors** check that a company's accounts are true and fair.
- **Management accountants** help businesses control costs and plan.
- **Tax advisers** help people and companies follow tax law.
- **Financial analysts** study accounts to value companies and advise investors.
- **Forensic accountants** investigate fraud.
- Many business leaders started in accounting, because it shows how every part of an organisation fits together.

## Why it matters

Investors, banks, governments and employees all rely on accounts being accurate. Good accounting helps businesses survive (most failures are really cash-flow failures), and bad or dishonest accounting has caused some of the largest business collapses in history. It is also a useful life skill: budgeting and understanding money.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level Accounting (9706)**, the syllabus for exams in 2026–2028. The course in this guide is planned in these parts:

- **AS financial accounting:** the accounting equation, books of prime entry, double entry, the trial balance and errors, bank reconciliation and control accounts, depreciation, accruals and prepayments, inventory, financial statements of sole traders, partnerships, limited companies, clubs, manufacturing accounts, incomplete records and ratio analysis.
- **AS cost and management accounting:** costing materials and labour, absorption costing, and marginal costing with break-even.
- **A Level financial accounting:** partnership changes, business purchase and mergers, company accounts in depth, statements of cash flows, international accounting standards, auditing and ethics, and interpreting accounts.
- **A Level cost and management accounting:** activity-based costing, budgeting, standard costing and variances, and investment appraisal.
- **Exam readiness:** paper guides and mock papers.

## Is it for you?

Accounting suits you if you like order, accuracy and numbers, and get satisfaction from making things balance. Most of the work is calculation and layout, done carefully and in the right format, but there are also written parts where you explain and recommend. You do not need advanced maths: arithmetic, percentages and care are what count.

It combines well with **Business**, **Economics** and Maths. It is a strong choice if you are considering accounting, finance or business degrees, or a professional accounting apprenticeship.
