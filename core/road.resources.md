@summary Where to find trustworthy study material: the official documents every candidate needs, then well-known free and paid websites, videos and books for each subject, and how to judge whether a resource is any good.

:::why
There is more A Level material online than anyone could use, and much of it is for the wrong board, an old syllabus, or simply wrong. The best students do not collect resources; they use a few good ones deeply. Start with the official documents, which define exactly what you will be asked, then add one or two trusted resources per subject.
:::

This chapter supports [Phase 2: Learn the content](#road.learn) and [Phase 4: Past papers and mark schemes](#road.pastpapers). Every resource named here was well established at the time of writing, but websites change, so check that a link still works and still covers your syllabus.

## Official resources come first

These documents come from the exam board itself. Nothing else is as reliable, and most of them are free.

| Resource | What it is | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Syllabus** (Cambridge) or **specification** (UK boards) | The official list of everything that can be examined, plus the paper structure | Print the content list and use it as a checklist; tick topics as you master them |
| **Specimen papers** | Sample papers written for a new syllabus | Your first look at the format; good early practice |
| **Past papers** | Real papers from earlier exam series | The core of your practice from Phase 4 onwards |
| **Mark schemes** | Exactly what earned marks in each past paper | Mark your own work strictly; learn the wording examiners accept |
| **Examiner reports** | Comments by the senior examiners after each series | Lists the common mistakes and what the best answers did |
| **Grade thresholds** | The raw marks needed for each grade in each series | Turn your past-paper scores into grades and set targets |
| **Formula lists and data booklets** | The formulas and data you are given in the exam, such as MF19 for Cambridge 9709 | Practise with the same booklet so you know what is given and what you must learn |

**Where to find them.** Cambridge publishes syllabuses, specimen papers and some past papers on each syllabus page of its website; the full archive is on its School Support Hub for registered schools. Pearson (for Edexcel IAL), AQA and OCR publish past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports on their websites, although the newest series is sometimes released to teachers only for a period. If you study privately, ask your exam centre what it can provide.

:::warn Check the syllabus code and the years
Each syllabus has a code (for example 9709 for Cambridge Mathematics or 9702 for Physics) and is valid for particular exam years. Old past papers may test topics that have since been removed, and may not include topics that are new. Use papers from the current syllabus first, and check older papers against the content list.
:::

## Mathematics

| Resource | Best for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Physics & Maths Tutor** | Past papers sorted by board, topic questions and revision notes | Free |
| **ExamSolutions** | Video lessons by topic and worked solutions to past-paper questions | Mostly free |
| **TLMaths** | Short videos covering the whole A Level Maths and Further Maths course, in order | Free |
| **Dr Frost Maths** | Online exam-style questions with instant marking, plus teaching slides | Free |
| **MadAsMaths** | Large practice booklets, including hard questions for A* and beyond | Free |
| **Underground Mathematics** | Rich problems from the University of Cambridge that build deep understanding | Free |
| **Khan Academy** | Filling gaps in GCSE-level algebra, trigonometry and early calculus | Free |

Most of these follow UK specifications, but the Pure Mathematics, Statistics and Mechanics content overlaps heavily with Cambridge 9709. Check the topic against your syllabus before you spend time on it.

For the practical papers, use your subject's practical chapter here and the examiner reports, which describe exactly how marks were lost in tables, graphs and evaluations.

## Business and Economics

| Resource | Best for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **tutor2u** | Topic notes, videos and up-to-date examples for Business and Economics | Free and paid |
| **Save My Exams** | Revision notes organised by board and topic | Free and paid |

In these subjects, examples from real businesses and economies lift answers into the higher levels. Keep a notebook of examples, including local ones: a Turkish exporter affected by exchange rates is just as good an example as a British one.

## Textbooks

One good textbook per subject is enough. On each syllabus page, Cambridge lists **endorsed** textbooks, which have been reviewed against that syllabus. Publishers of endorsed books include Cambridge University Press & Assessment, Hodder Education, Collins and Oxford University Press. For Edexcel IAL, Pearson publishes its own student books for each unit, and the UK boards list approved books for their specifications.

Before you buy, check that the book names your syllabus code and exam years. A cheap second-hand copy of an older edition may miss content that was added later, so compare its contents with the current syllabus.

## How to judge a resource

Before you trust a website, video or book, ask:

- **Is it for my board, my syllabus code and the current years?**
- **Who made it?** An exam board, an experienced teacher or a university is more reliable than an anonymous upload.
- **Does it explain why, not just what?** Good resources show full working and reasoning.
- **Does it match the mark scheme?** Compare one of its answers with an official mark scheme.
- **Does it make me active?** Questions and quizzes beat passive watching.

:::tip A small, strong toolkit
For each subject, aim for: the syllabus, one textbook, past papers with mark schemes, one question website, and your own flashcards. That is enough for an A*. More resources usually means less depth.
:::

## Avoid leaked and pirated material

Sometimes social media accounts claim to sell "leaked" papers before an exam. These are almost always scams. If a real paper were involved, using or sharing it would be **malpractice**, and boards can disqualify candidates from a subject or from all their exams. Never buy, download or share them. Equally, do not post or discuss questions online after you sit a paper: candidates in other time zones may not have taken it yet.

Pirated copies of textbooks and paid courses break copyright law, often carry malware, and are frequently old editions. Use the many free and legal resources above, your school or public library, and the official board websites instead.

:::recap
- Start with official resources: syllabus, specimen papers, past papers, mark schemes, examiner reports and grade thresholds.
- Always check the syllabus code and exam years; old papers may not match the current content.
- Mathematics: Physics & Maths Tutor, ExamSolutions, TLMaths, Dr Frost Maths, MadAsMaths, Underground Mathematics and Khan Academy.
- Sciences: Isaac Science, Chemguide, BioNinja, Save My Exams, Seneca, Cognito and Physics & Maths Tutor.
- Business and Economics: tutor2u, Save My Exams and real-world news examples.
- Use one endorsed textbook per subject and check it matches your syllabus.
- Judge every resource by board, author, depth, agreement with mark schemes and whether it makes you active.
- Never use leaked or pirated material: it is malpractice or illegal, and usually a scam.
:::
