## What is history at A Level?

Historians build explanations of the past from **evidence**: letters, speeches, newspapers, government records, photographs, statistics and the work of other historians. Every piece of evidence was made by someone, at a certain time, for a certain reason, so a large part of the subject is asking:

- **Who** made this source, **when**, and **why**? What could they know, and what did they want the reader to think?
- **What caused** an event, and which cause mattered most?
- **What changed** and what stayed the same?
- **Why do historians disagree**, and whose argument is better supported?

You show all of this in writing: source answers, analytical essays and judgements that you can defend.

## What do people who study history do?

- **Law, journalism and politics:** building a case from evidence and writing it clearly are the core skills of all three.
- **Civil service, policy and international organisations:** understanding how countries and societies got where they are.
- **Research, teaching, archives, museums and heritage.**
- **Business, consulting and finance:** employers value graduates who can read a lot, find what matters and argue a position.

## Why it matters

Every news story has a past. Knowing how revolutions start, how democracies fail, how wars break out and how they end makes the present easier to understand and harder to manipulate. History also trains you to check a claim against its source, which matters more than ever online.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level History (9489)**, the syllabus for exams in 2027–2029. There are four papers:

- **AS Level, Papers 1 and 2:** one option out of three: **Modern Europe, 1774–1924** (France, Germany, Russia), **the USA, 1820–1941** (Civil War, Gilded Age, Great Depression) or **international history, 1870–1939** (imperialism, the 1920s, the road to the Second World War). Paper 1 is a **sources** paper on one topic; Paper 2 has **essays** on the other two. Which topic goes on Paper 1 changes every year.
- **A Level, Paper 3:** **historical interpretations** on one topic: the origins of the First World War, the Holocaust, or the origins and development of the Cold War.
- **A Level, Paper 4:** a **depth study** with essays on one option: Europe 1919–41 (Mussolini, Stalin, Hitler), the USA 1945–93, or international history 1909–94 (Eastern Europe, South Africa and Zimbabwe, Malaysia and Indonesia).
- **Exam readiness:** a guide to each paper, mixed practice and a mock for every paper.

## Is it for you?

History suits you if you like stories about people and power, enjoy reading, and like arguing a case. You will read a lot and write a lot: clear paragraphs that make a point, back it with evidence and reach a judgement. There is no maths, but there is precision: dates, names and facts must be right.

It combines well with **English Literature**, **Economics**, a language, Politics, Geography or Law. History degrees usually ask for A Level History or another essay subject; it is also respected for law, politics, international relations and journalism.
