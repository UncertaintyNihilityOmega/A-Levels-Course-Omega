:::why
By exam day the learning is done, but marks can still be lost to things that have nothing to do with knowledge: a forgotten calculator, a misread question, fifteen minutes stuck on one part. This chapter removes those risks. It then walks you through what happens after the last paper, including what to do if results day does not go to plan.
:::

## The night before

- **Check the details** on your **statement of entry**, the document from your centre that lists your candidate number and each paper's date and start time. Confirm the time and the room, and set two alarms.
- **Pack your bag:**
  - photo ID (a passport or national ID card): private candidates will not be allowed in without it;
  - your statement of entry;
  - a permitted calculator with fresh batteries;
  - at least three black pens, two pencils, an eraser, a sharpener and a ruler, plus a protractor and compasses for Mathematics;
  - a clear pencil case and a clear water bottle, if your centre allows them.
- **Check your calculator is allowed.** Rules differ by board and subject. For example, Cambridge Mathematics does not allow graphic calculators or calculators that can do algebra.
- **Read your one-page sheet** for the paper, then stop. Do not start anything new.
- **Plan your route**, with time to spare for traffic, and go to bed at your normal time.

:::warn Pens, pencils and correction fluid
Cambridge papers ask for black or dark blue pen, and do not allow erasable pens or correction fluid. Use pencil only for diagrams and graphs, and for multiple-choice answer sheets, which are marked by machine and need a soft pencil. If you make a mistake in pen, cross it out with a single neat line and write the correction next to it.
:::

## The morning

- Eat breakfast and drink some water, even if you are nervous.
- Arrive at least 30 minutes early. Private candidates should check the arrival time their centre gives.
- Take a last look at your one-page sheet, then put it away before you enter the room.
- Leave your phone, smartwatch and any other device at home or hand them in as instructed. Having one with you in the exam room can count as malpractice, even if it is switched off.
- Go to the toilet before the exam starts.

## In the exam

### Reading the paper

1. Write your name, centre number and candidate number exactly as they appear on your statement of entry.
2. Read the front cover: the number of questions, the total marks, and whether you must answer every question or choose some.
3. If you have time, look through the whole paper for a minute so that nothing surprises you later.
4. Read each question twice. Underline the command word and the key data.
5. Look at the marks for each part and the space given for the answer. Both tell you how much the examiner expects.

### Timing: minutes per mark

Divide the time by the total marks to get your pace. For example, Cambridge Mathematics Paper 1 has 75 marks in 1 hour 50 minutes, which is about 1.5 minutes per mark, so a 6-mark question deserves about 9 minutes.

| Cambridge paper | Marks | Time | Minutes per mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics Paper 1 or 3 | 75 | 1 h 50 min | about 1.5 |
| Mathematics Paper 2, 4, 5 or 6 | 50 | 1 h 15 min | 1.5 |
| Physics, Chemistry or Biology Paper 1 (multiple choice) | 40 | 1 h 15 min | just under 2 per question |
| Physics, Chemistry or Biology Paper 2 (AS structured) | 60 | 1 h 15 min | 1.25 |
| Physics, Chemistry or Biology Paper 4 (A Level structured) | 100 | 2 h | 1.2 |
| Business Paper 2 | 60 | 1 h 30 min | 1.5 |
| Economics Paper 2 | 60 | 2 h | 2 |

To keep 10 minutes for checking in Mathematics Paper 1, work at about 1.3 minutes per mark. Edexcel IAL, AQA and OCR papers have different lengths, so work out your own pace from the front cover of a past paper.

:::exam Let the marks guide you
The marks for each part tell you what the examiner wants. A 2-mark "explain" question usually needs two separate points, or one point and its reason. A 1-mark "state" question needs a short answer, not a paragraph. For calculations, a part worth 4 or 5 marks needs several clear steps of working, because most of those marks are method marks.
:::

### When you are stuck

- **Move on** once you have spent about twice the time the marks deserve. Circle the question number and come back at the end.
- **Write what you know.** A formula, a labelled diagram or the first line of working can earn method marks.
- **Use answers given in the question.** If you cannot show that \(x = 4\) in part (a), use \(x = 4\) in part (b) anyway: the later marks are still available.
- **Never leave a multiple-choice question blank.** No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so eliminate what you can and choose.
- **If panic starts,** put your pen down, breathe slowly for thirty seconds, and read the next question. One hard question is only a few marks.
- **Need more space?** Use the extra page at the back of the booklet, and write the question number clearly.

### The last ten minutes

Check that you answered what was asked ("exact", "in terms of \(\pi\)", "to 3 significant figures"), that every answer has a unit where one is needed, that no part is left blank, and that your multiple-choice answer sheet is complete.

## After each paper

Leave the room and do not go through the answers with friends: it only creates worry about marks you can no longer change. Eat, rest, then spend thirty minutes planning for your next paper. If something affected your performance, such as illness, a family emergency or a disturbance in the room, tell your exams officer the same day. Your centre may be able to apply for **special consideration**, but only within a short time after the exam.

## Results day

- **When:** Cambridge results for the May/June series come out in August (the June 2026 results were released on 11 August 2026), and results for the October/November series in January. Each Edexcel IAL series has its own results day; check Pearson's key dates.
- **How:** your centre gives you your results, and some centres also give you a login to see them online.
- **University places:** if you applied through UCAS, check UCAS Hub on results day to see whether your place is confirmed. Before the exams, ask your centre whether it will send your results to UCAS.

Read your results calmly, and decide what to do before you phone anyone.

## Enquiries about results

If a grade is lower than you expected, your centre can ask the board to check it. Cambridge calls these **enquiries about results**; Pearson, AQA and OCR call them post-results services or reviews of results. The usual options are:

- a **clerical re-check** that all the marks were counted and added correctly;
- a **review of marking**, with a faster priority service when a university place depends on the result;
- a **copy of your marked script**, which shows exactly where you lost marks. This is very useful if you plan to resit.

Every service has a fee and a strict deadline, and it must be requested through your centre, including by private candidates. A review looks for marking errors; it is not a second marking, and **marks can go down as well as up**. It is usually worth it only if you believe you are close to a grade boundary.

## Resits

| Board | When you can resit | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge International | October/November (results in January), or the next May/June | You normally sit all the papers of the AS or A Level again; ask your centre whether anything can be carried forward, such as an AS result within 13 months |
| Pearson Edexcel IAL | January, May/June or October | Modular: you can resit only the units you choose, and your best result for each unit counts |
| AQA and OCR (UK A Levels) | May/June only | Linear: you sit all the papers again, a year later |

Resit when a university offer or a career depends on the grade, and when you know exactly what went wrong. If your university has already accepted you, the time is usually better spent preparing for the course. If you decide to resit, read the [study planner](#road.planner) example for a resit student, and start straight away.

:::tip Write your plan B before results day
In July, while you are calm, write down what you will do if you miss your offer by one grade, and by two. On results day you will then follow a plan instead of making big decisions in a panic.
:::

## UCAS Clearing and plan B

If you miss the conditions of your offer, do not assume the worst. Universities sometimes accept students who just miss their grades, so check UCAS Hub first, and phone the admissions office if your status is unclear (have your UCAS Personal ID ready). If you have no place, **Clearing** lets you apply for university courses that still have places. You phone universities, and if one makes you an offer, you add it as your Clearing choice in UCAS Hub. Clearing is busiest on A Level results day and in the days after.

Other good plan B routes include:

- a course with a **foundation year**, which has lower entry requirements;
- a **gap year** with resits, then a new application with real grades instead of predicted ones;
- universities in Turkey or other countries that accept A Level results;
- apprenticeships and work-based routes.

There is more on applications in [University applications and admissions tests](#road.university).

:::warn Do not grab the first Clearing offer
An offer on the phone can feel like relief, but you will spend three or four years on that course. Ask about the course content, the city and the costs, compare two or three options, and talk it through with someone you trust before you accept.
:::

:::recap
- Night before: statement of entry, photo ID, permitted calculator, black pens, pencils, eraser and ruler; normal bedtime.
- Morning: breakfast, arrive at least 30 minutes early, no phone or smartwatch in the room.
- Pace yourself by minutes per mark: about 1.5 in Cambridge Maths Paper 1 (75 marks in 1 hour 50 minutes).
- Stuck? Move on, write what you know, use given answers, never leave a multiple-choice question blank.
- Report illness or disruption to your exams officer the same day.
- Cambridge June results come out in August, November results in January. Enquiries about results have fees and short deadlines, and marks can go down.
- Resits: Cambridge in November or June, Edexcel IAL in January, June or October (best unit result counts), AQA and OCR in June only.
- Write a plan B before results day; Clearing, foundation years and gap years are all good routes.
:::
