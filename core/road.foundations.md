:::why
Most students who struggle at A Level do not struggle with the new ideas. They struggle because an old skill is shaky: rearranging a formula, handling a negative number, writing a clear paragraph. Every new topic then takes twice as long, and the student concludes "I'm not good at this subject" when the real problem is a two-hour gap from three years ago. A few weeks spent on foundations now saves months later.
:::

## What each A Level assumes

Cambridge writes its AS and A Level syllabuses for students who have studied the subject at IGCSE or GCSE (or an equivalent course). You do not need a top grade, but the core skills must be quick and reliable, because at A Level they are tools inside bigger problems, not the problem itself.

### Mathematics, and the maths inside the sciences

| Skill | You should be able to… | Repair it here |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Work with fractions, negative numbers, percentages and ratios without a calculator | [Numbers, fractions and percentages](#math.f.numbers) |
| Algebra | Expand and factorise; solve linear and simultaneous equations; rearrange formulas | [Algebra from scratch](#math.f.algebra) |
| Powers | Use the laws of indices, simplify surds, work in standard form | [Powers, roots, surds and standard form](#math.f.powers) |
| Graphs | Find a gradient, use \(y = mx + c\), plot points and read values from a graph | [Coordinates and straight lines](#math.f.graphs) |
| Geometry | Use Pythagoras' theorem, sine, cosine and tangent, angle facts, areas and volumes | [Geometry and basic trigonometry](#math.f.geometry) |

### Physics, Chemistry and Biology

The sciences use five maths skills constantly, so check these even if Mathematics is not one of your subjects.

- **Units and prefixes:** converting mm to m, kJ to J, \(\mathrm{cm^3}\) to \(\mathrm{dm^3}\), and knowing that \(1\,\mathrm{dm^3} = 1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}\).
- **Rearranging:** making any letter the subject of a formula such as \(pV = nRT\) or \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\).
- **Standard form:** writing \(0.000\,045\,6\) as \(4.56 \times 10^{-5}\) and entering it correctly on a calculator.
- **Significant figures:** giving an answer to the same number of significant figures as the data, usually 2 or 3.
- **Graphs:** choosing scales, drawing a line of best fit, and finding a gradient from a large triangle.

Each science also has its own starting knowledge: atoms, ions and equations in Chemistry; forces, energy and circuits in Physics; cells, organs and basic genetics in Biology. Check and repair these with [Physics from zero](#phys.f.basics), [Chemistry from zero](#chem.f.basics) and [Biology from zero](#bio.f.basics).

### Business, Economics and other essay subjects

Essay subjects assume you can read a long case study quickly, pick out the useful facts and numbers, calculate a percentage change, and write a clear paragraph that makes one point and explains it. No prior Business or Economics course is needed, but if these skills feel slow, start with [Business from zero](#bus.f.basics) and [Writing top-band business answers](#bus.writing).

## The ten-minute self-check

Do these without a calculator or notes. Give yourself one mark for each fully correct answer. Score 8 or more and you can start phase 2; otherwise, repair the skills you missed first, using the links above.

:::try Foundation self-check (Mathematics and sciences)
Q: Work out \(\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{3}{4}\).
A: Use a common denominator of 12: \(\tfrac{8}{12} + \tfrac{9}{12} = \tfrac{17}{12}\), which is \(1\tfrac{5}{12}\).
Q: Expand and simplify \((2x - 3)(x + 4)\).
A: \(2x^2 + 8x - 3x - 12 = 2x^2 + 5x - 12\).
Q: Factorise \(x^2 - 7x + 12\).
A: Two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to \(-7\) are \(-3\) and \(-4\), so \((x - 3)(x - 4)\).
Q: Make \(u\) the subject of \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\).
A: \(u^2 = v^2 - 2as\), so \(u = \pm\sqrt{v^2 - 2as}\).
Q: Solve the simultaneous equations \(2x + y = 7\) and \(x - y = 2\).
A: Adding them gives \(3x = 9\), so \(x = 3\). Then \(y = 7 - 2(3) = 1\).
Q: Simplify \(\dfrac{x^5 \times x^{-2}}{x^2}\).
A: Add the powers when multiplying and subtract when dividing: \(x^{5 - 2 - 2} = x^1 = x\).
Q: Write \(0.000\,045\,6\) in standard form, and give \(0.004\,562\) to 2 significant figures.
A: \(4.56 \times 10^{-5}\) and \(0.0046\).
Q: Convert 250 mm to metres, 3.5 kW to watts, and \(250\,\mathrm{cm^3}\) to \(\mathrm{dm^3}\).
A: \(0.25\,\mathrm{m}\), \(3500\,\mathrm{W}\) and \(0.25\,\mathrm{dm^3}\) (because \(1\,\mathrm{dm^3} = 1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}\)).
Q: Find the gradient and the \(y\)-intercept of the line \(2y - 6x = 8\).
A: Rearrange to \(y = 3x + 4\): the gradient is 3 and the line crosses the \(y\)-axis at \((0, 4)\).
Q: A price rises from $40 to $46. Find the percentage increase.
A: The increase is $6, and \(\tfrac{6}{40} \times 100 = 15\%\).
:::

For essay subjects, the check is a list of "I can" statements. Be honest: tick one only if you could do it today, under time pressure.

- I can read a one-page article in English in under five minutes and summarise it in three sentences.
- I can write a paragraph that makes one point, explains why, and gives an example.
- I can calculate a percentage change and say what it means in words.
- I can read a simple table or chart and describe the trend with numbers.
- I can define ten basic terms in my subject (for Business: profit, revenue, costs, market, stakeholder and so on) in one sentence each.

## Academic English for second-language learners

Every paper is set and marked in English, and many students who know the science well lose marks to language. The good news: exam English is a small, learnable language of its own.

- **Learn the command words.** "Describe" (say what happens) and "explain" (say why it happens) earn different marks. See [Command words decoded](#road.commands).
- **Keep a vocabulary notebook.** Four columns: the English term, the word in your own language, the definition in English, and an example sentence. Learn definitions as fixed phrases, exactly as the syllabus words them.
- **Write short, complete sentences.** Examiners reward correct science and clear reasoning, not elegant style. One idea per sentence is safer than one long sentence with three ideas.
- **Read in English every day.** Fifteen minutes of your textbook or a good subject website builds reading speed, which matters in long case-study and data questions.
- **Use the [glossary](#tools.glossary)** in this guide: every key term from every chapter is collected there.

| English term | Turkish | Definition in English |
|---|---|---|
| gradient | eğim | the change in \(y\) divided by the change in \(x\) |
| dynamic equilibrium | dinamik denge | forward and reverse reactions happen at equal rates, so concentrations stay constant |
| demand | talep | the quantity buyers are willing and able to buy at each price |
| evaluate | değerlendirmek | judge the strengths and weaknesses, then reach a conclusion |

:::warn "I got an A at IGCSE, so I can skip this"
Skills fade, especially over a long summer, and A Level needs them to be fast as well as correct. Do the self-check anyway: it takes ten minutes, and it either confirms you are ready or finds a gap while it is still cheap to fix.
:::

:::exam Foundation skills carry marks in every paper
A correct method with a slip in rearranging, a wrong power of ten or a missing unit can still lose the final accuracy mark. In Cambridge Mathematics, a non-exact answer not given to 3 significant figures (unless the question asks otherwise) can also cost marks. Examiners usually accept small spelling mistakes, but not when the misspelt word could be another technical term, such as ethane and ethene, or mitosis and meiosis.
:::

## How long should phase 1 take?

| Your self-check result | Plan |
|---|---|
| 8–10, and quick | About 2 weeks: repair anything you missed and read the "from zero" chapter for each science, then start phase 2 |
| 6–7 | 3–4 weeks: work through the foundation chapters for every skill you missed, then redo the self-check |
| 5 or fewer, or no IGCSE in the subject | 5–6 weeks: work through all the foundation chapters for that subject in order |
| Any score, if English is your second language | Add 15 minutes of subject reading in English every day, all the way to the exam |

:::tip Fix the core now, the rest just in time
You do not need to repair everything before you start learning. Fix the core skills now (algebra, rearranging, units, paragraph writing), then repair smaller gaps as topics need them. Every chapter in this guide begins with a "Before you start" line that links to exactly what it assumes.
:::

:::recap
- Most A Level difficulty comes from shaky GCSE/IGCSE skills, not from the new ideas.
- Maths skills to check: number, algebra, powers and standard form, graphs, geometry and trigonometry.
- Science skills to check: units and prefixes, rearranging, standard form, significant figures, graphs.
- Essay subjects need fast reading, clear paragraphs and confident use of data.
- Score 8 out of 10 on the self-check before moving on; repair what you missed with the foundation chapters.
- Second-language learners: learn the command words, keep a four-column vocabulary notebook and read in English daily.
- Phase 1 takes 2–6 weeks, depending on your score.
:::
