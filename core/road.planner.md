A good plan answers two questions: **is my goal realistic?** and **what should I do this week?** Enter your exam date and your subjects below. The planner counts the weeks you have and spreads them across the [six phases](#road.overview), so you can see when each phase should start and finish.

:::tip Which date should I enter?
Use the date of your **first** paper, not the last, because final revision has to be finished by then. If the timetable is not published yet, take the earliest date in your board's window for that exam series. Planning for a week too early is harmless; planning for a week too late is not.
:::

## Build or check a plan by hand

The planner does the arithmetic, but you should understand it so that you can check the result, or redo it with a pen and a calendar.

:::steps
1. Count the whole weeks from next Monday to your first paper.
2. Keep about one week in ten free as a **buffer** for illness, holidays and family events.
3. Working back from the exam, mark the last 6–8 weeks as final revision (phase 5) and the 4–6 weeks before that as full past papers (phase 4).
4. Mark the first 2–4 weeks as foundations (phase 1).
5. The weeks left in the middle are for learning (phase 2), with practice (phase 3) built into every week. Share them between your subjects' topics.
6. Add the fixed dates: entry deadline, mocks, holidays and UCAS deadlines.
:::

Then do a **reality check**. Each chapter of this guide shows a study time at the top (for example, 6–8 hours for [Quadratics](#math.p1.quadratics)). Add about half as much again for practice, multiply by the number of topics, and compare the total with the hours your learning weeks give you. If it does not fit, you have three honest choices: study more hours a week, take fewer subjects in this series, or move to a later series. Never solve the problem by cutting phases 4 and 5, because that is where grades are made.

## How to read your plan

- **Phase dates are targets, not walls.** Phases overlap. Start past-paper questions on each topic as soon as you finish it, long before phase 4 officially begins.
- **The learning weeks are the tightest.** If your plan gives you fewer than about 10 weeks per subject to learn the content, you are on an intensive track, and your weekly hours matter more than anything else.
- **Phase 5 has a minimum.** Even a short plan needs at least 3–4 weeks of final revision.

## Adapting the plan

Every plan goes wrong at some point. What matters is how you respond.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1–2 weeks behind | Use a buffer week, or add 3–5 hours a week for a month. Do not push every later phase back |
| 3 or more weeks behind | Decide now: more hours, one subject moved to the next series, or an AS Level only this time (a Cambridge AS Level is a qualification in its own right) |
| Ahead of schedule | Do more mixed practice and past-paper questions; do not race ahead to new content |
| A topic takes twice as long as planned | Normal for hard topics. Borrow time from a buffer week, never from practice |
| Ill or away for a week | Restart with a weekly review; do not try to "catch up" by working through the night |

Run the planner again once a month: it shows whether you are on track while there is still time to act.

:::warn A plan with no spare time breaks in week three
If every hour of every week is filled, the first cold or family wedding pushes everything back, and the plan starts to feel like a list of failures. Leave buffer weeks, keep one day a week free, and plan only the next week in detail.
:::

## Three example plans

### A. School student: Year 12 from September 2026, exams in June 2028

| Term | Main phases | Targets and milestones |
|---|---|---|
| Autumn 2026 (Year 12) | 1 in September, then 2 and 3 | Foundation self-checks in the first 3 weeks; weekly reviews from October; first unit tests in November |
| Spring 2027 | 2 and 3, plus past-paper questions by topic | Most AS content finished by Easter |
| Summer 2027 | AS exams in May/June if your school uses staged assessment; otherwise school exams | Start the A Level content in June; in the holidays, research universities and draft your personal statement |
| Autumn 2027 (Year 13) | 2 and 3 for the A Level content | UCAS: mid-October deadline for Oxford, Cambridge and medicine, January for most courses; admissions tests |
| Spring 2028 | Content finished by February; mocks; then 4 | From February, 1–2 timed papers per subject per week; at least 5 per component by Easter |
| Summer 2028 | 5 (the 8 weeks before your first paper), then 6 | Exams in May/June; results in August 2028 |

### B. Private candidate: October 2026 to June 2027, Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry

A full-time candidate studying about 36 hours a week for Cambridge 9709, 9702 and 9701 in the May/June 2027 series. Week 1 starts on Monday 5 October 2026, and the plan assumes a first paper in early May.

| Weeks | Dates | Phase | Mathematics 9709 | Physics 9702 | Chemistry 9701 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 5–18 Oct | 1 | Algebra, powers, graphs | Units, standard form, rearranging | Formulas, equations, basic moles |
| 3–8 | 19 Oct – 29 Nov | 2 + 3 | Pure 1 | AS topics 1–6 | AS topics 1–8 |
| 9–12 | 30 Nov – 27 Dec | 2 + 3 | Pure 3, first half | AS topics 7–11 | AS topics 9–16 |
| 13 | 28 Dec – 3 Jan | Buffer | Rest and catch up | Rest and catch up | Rest and catch up |
| 14–17 | 4–31 Jan 2027 | 2 + 3 | Pure 3, second half | A Level topics 12–18 | AS 17–22, A Level 23–26 |
| 18–21 | 1–28 Feb | 2 + 3 | Probability & Statistics 1, Mechanics | A Level topics 19–25 | A Level topics 27–37 |
| 22–25 | 1–28 Mar | 4 | Two timed papers a week | Timed papers; Paper 5 planning practice | Timed papers; Paper 5 planning practice |
| 26–30 | 29 Mar – 2 May | 5 | Final revision; full mocks in weeks 26 and 29 | As Maths | As Maths |
| 31 on | From 3 May | 6 | Exams | Exams | Exams |

:::warn This plan is intensive
Thirty weeks of 36 hours gives about 360 hours per subject: the same as Cambridge's guided learning hours, but without a teacher. It suits students with strong IGCSE grades in all three subjects who can study full-time. If you are more than two weeks behind in week 8, move your weakest subject to the October/November 2027 series. Cambridge science candidates also sit a practical exam (Paper 3), so check early that your centre can run it, and book some laboratory practice before March.
:::

### C. Resit student: sitting again in November

Emre sat A Level Chemistry in June 2027 and knows that Paper 4 went badly. He needs an A for a university application next year, so he starts preparing for the October/November 2027 series the week after his last June paper. Waiting for results day in August would cost him eight weeks. He plans 10–12 hours a week.

| Weeks | When | Phase | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Late June | 1: diagnosis | One paper of each component done cold and marked; every topic sorted into red, amber or green; ask the centre for the November entry deadline |
| 2–7 | July to mid-August | 2 + 3 | Relearn two red topics a week; daily flashcards; topic questions after each one |
| Results day | August | – | Confirm the grade; ask the centre about a copy of the marked script; make sure the November entry is in |
| 8–13 | Mid-August to late September | 4 | One or two timed papers a week, marked the same day; every lost mark redone a week later |
| 14–17 | October | 5 | Amber topics, condensed notes, two full mocks |
| 18 on | October/November | 6 | The exams; results in January |

## A weekly timetable template

Copy this pattern into a notebook or calendar and adapt it to your subjects. Each day mixes new learning with practice on earlier topics, and every week ends with a review.

| Day | Private candidate, full-time (about 36 hours) | School student (about 9 hours, plus homework) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 09:00–12:00 Maths, new topic · 13:30–16:30 Physics, new topic · 20 min flashcards | 1 hour: redo today's lesson examples without looking · 20 min flashcards |
| Tuesday | Chemistry, new topic · Maths, questions on Monday's topic · flashcards | 1 hour Chemistry questions · 20 min flashcards |
| Wednesday | Physics, questions · Chemistry, questions · flashcards | 1 hour Physics questions · 20 min flashcards |
| Thursday | Maths, new topic · Physics, new topic · flashcards | 1 hour Maths questions · 20 min error log |
| Friday | Chemistry, new topic · mixed questions from all three · flashcards | Free evening |
| Saturday | 09:00–12:00 one timed test or past-paper section · 30 min marking it and updating the error log | 2–3 hours: one timed section, marked; catch up |
| Sunday | 45 min weekly review and plan; the rest of the day free | 45 min weekly review and plan; homework |

:::exam Plan around the exam timetable
When the timetable is published, mark every paper on your plan. Note which paper comes first in each subject and any days with two papers. Then arrange your last two weeks in the same order, so the paper you sit first gets the first final mock.
:::

:::recap
- Enter the date of your **first** paper; if the timetable is not out yet, use the earliest date of the exam series window.
- Work back from the exam: final revision, then past papers, then learning. Keep about one week in ten as a buffer.
- Reality-check the learning weeks: each chapter's study time plus half again for practice, times the number of topics.
- If you fall 3 or more weeks behind, decide early: more hours, a later series for one subject, or AS only.
- Never shorten phases 4 and 5 to rescue phase 2.
- Review every Sunday and re-run the planner once a month.
:::
