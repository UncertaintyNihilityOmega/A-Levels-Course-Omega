:::why
Computer science is the study of how information is represented and processed, and how to make machines solve problems for us. Every app, website, game, bank, hospital system and AI model rests on it. This page explains what the subject really is (it is much more than "using computers") and whether it fits you.
:::

## What is computer science?

Computer science has two halves that support each other.

- **Theory:** how computers store everything as binary, how processors and memory work, how data travels across networks, how databases organise information, and which problems can be solved efficiently at all.
- **Programming and problem solving:** turning a problem into an **algorithm** (a precise list of steps), then into working code, then testing it until it is correct.

The central skill is **computational thinking**: breaking a big problem into smaller ones (decomposition), ignoring details that do not matter (abstraction), and spotting patterns you have solved before.

## What do computer scientists do?

- **Software engineers** design and build programs, from phone apps to the systems that run airports.
- **Data scientists and AI engineers** build models that find patterns in data, recognise speech and images, or make predictions.
- **Security specialists** protect systems from attack and design encryption.
- **Network and systems engineers** keep the internet, cloud services and company networks running.
- **Researchers** work on faster algorithms, new programming languages, quantum computing and the limits of computation.

## Why it matters

Almost every field now depends on software: medicine, finance, science, transport, entertainment and government. Computer science also raises important questions about privacy, fairness in automated decisions, and the safe use of AI. Understanding how these systems work lets you build them, and also judge them.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level Computer Science (9618)**, the syllabus for exams in 2027–2029. The course in this guide is planned in these parts:

- **AS theory:** information representation, communication and networks, hardware and logic gates, processor fundamentals, system software, security, ethics and databases.
- **AS programming:** algorithm design, data types and structures, pseudocode, programming and testing.
- **A Level theory:** data representation, communication protocols, hardware and virtual machines, encryption and artificial intelligence.
- **A Level programming:** computational thinking, recursion, further data structures, object-oriented programming and other paradigms.
- **Exam readiness:** paper guides and mock papers.

## Is it for you?

Computer Science suits you if you enjoy logical puzzles, are patient when something does not work the first time, and like building things. You do not need to have programmed before, but you must be willing to practise regularly: programming is learnt by doing. Good Maths helps a lot, especially for algorithms and the theory papers.

It combines well with **Maths** (and Further Maths), Physics, Economics or Business. Many computer science degrees ask for Maths rather than Computer Science itself, so check the requirements.
