## What is economics?

Every society faces the **economic problem**: wants are unlimited, but resources (land, labour, capital and enterprise) are scarce, so choices must be made, and every choice has an **opportunity cost**. Economics studies those choices at two levels:

- **Microeconomics** looks at individual markets: how supply and demand set prices, how firms decide what to produce, why some markets fail (pollution, monopolies) and how governments can step in.
- **Macroeconomics** looks at the whole economy: growth, inflation, unemployment, the balance of payments, exchange rates, and the government and central bank policies that steer them.

Economists build **models**, simplified pictures of how things work (usually as diagrams), and test them against data.

## What do economists do?

- **Government and central banks** use economists to set interest rates, design taxes and benefits, and forecast the economy.
- **Banks, investment firms and consultancies** hire economists to study markets and advise companies.
- **International organisations** work on trade, development and poverty reduction.
- **Researchers** study questions such as what reduces inequality or how to price carbon emissions.
- An economics background is common in business, law, journalism, politics and public policy.

## Why it matters

Economic decisions affect jobs, prices, housing, the environment and how much governments can spend on schools and hospitals. Understanding economics helps you follow the news, judge politicians' promises, and make better decisions with your own money.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level Economics (9708)**, the syllabus for exams in 2026–2028. The course in this guide is planned in these parts:

- **AS microeconomics:** the basic economic problem, the price system, demand and supply, elasticity and government intervention.
- **AS macroeconomics:** national income, aggregate demand and supply, inflation, unemployment, international trade and government policy.
- **A Level microeconomics:** consumer behaviour and behavioural economics, costs and revenue, market structures, the growth of firms, efficiency, labour markets, and inequality and redistribution.
- **A Level macroeconomics:** the circular flow and the multiplier, money and banking, unemployment and inflation in depth, policy aims and conflicts, economic development and globalisation.
- **Exam readiness:** diagram practice, essay and data-response skills, paper guides and mock papers.

## Is it for you?

Economics suits you if you follow the news, like explaining why things happen, and enjoy a mix of diagrams, numbers and argument. You will write essays that build a chain of reasoning and then **evaluate**: weighing up when a policy works and when it does not. Comfortable numeracy helps (percentages, index numbers, reading graphs).

It combines well with **Maths** (essential for many top economics degrees), Business, Accounting, History, Geography or Politics.
