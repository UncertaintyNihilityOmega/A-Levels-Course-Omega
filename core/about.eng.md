## What is engineering?

Engineering is **applied science with a purpose**. A scientist asks how the world works; an engineer uses that knowledge to solve a practical problem, within limits of cost, safety, materials and time. The main branches are:

- **Mechanical engineering:** machines, engines, vehicles, robots.
- **Civil and structural engineering:** buildings, bridges, roads, dams, water supply.
- **Electrical and electronic engineering:** power grids, circuits, chips, communication systems.
- **Chemical engineering:** turning raw materials into fuels, plastics, medicines and food at industrial scale.
- **Aerospace, biomedical, software and environmental engineering**, among others.

Every branch follows the same **design process**: define the problem, research, design and model, build a prototype, test, and improve.

## What do engineers do?

Engineers calculate loads and stresses, simulate designs on computers, choose materials, manage projects and teams, test prototypes, and make sure products are safe and meet regulations. They work in industry, consultancy, construction, energy, transport, healthcare and research. Engineering degrees lead to some of the best-paid and most in-demand graduate jobs.

## Why it matters

Clean energy, safe water, transport, housing, communication and healthcare all depend on engineering. Problems such as climate change will be solved partly by engineers designing better batteries, grids, buildings and materials.

## How to study it at A Level

There is **no Cambridge International Engineering A Level**. Engineering degrees almost always ask for:

- **Mathematics** (essential), and often **Further Mathematics**;
- **Physics** (essential for most branches);
- a third subject such as Chemistry (needed for chemical engineering), Computer Science, Design and Technology or Electronics.

Other routes include OCR Design and Technology: Design Engineering, Eduqas Electronics, and vocational qualifications such as BTECs and T Levels.

This guide's Engineering course is not an exam course. It shows how A Level Maths and Physics become engineering, in these parts:

- **Foundations:** units, estimation, the design process, and the maths engineers use most.
- **Mechanics and structures:** forces, stress and strain, beams and simple structures.
- **Thermofluids:** energy, heat engines and fluid flow.
- **Electrical and electronic:** circuits, power and simple electronics.
- **Design and making:** materials, manufacturing and testing.
- **Next steps:** degrees, apprenticeships and how to prepare.

## Is it for you?

Engineering suits you if you enjoy maths and physics and like seeing them used on real objects, solving open problems, and working in teams. The **Mathematics** and **Physics** courses in this guide are complete, and they are the best preparation you can do now.
