## How the guide is organised

Open the menu (the ☰ button on a phone, or the sidebar on a larger screen) to see every subject: the six main subjects (Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Engineering and Business), Economics and Accounting, short guides to other subjects, the preparation road, the toolbox and practice quizzes. Each subject is split into groups of chapters in a sensible order of study, usually starting with a course map.

The top of each chapter shows a level label, a short summary and, for subject chapters, the exams that test it and a realistic study time for a beginner. The level labels mean:

| Label | What it means |
|---|---|
| Foundations | GCSE/IGCSE skills that A Level assumes. Start here if you are rusty. |
| AS Level | First-year content. |
| A Level (A2) | Second-year content, needed for the full A Level. |
| Guide, Reference, Summary | Course maps, the preparation road, formula sheets and short subject summaries. |
| Interactive | Tools such as the graph plotter and calculators. |
| Beyond A Level | Extension material, such as the tour of Further Mathematics. |

Most chapters begin with **Before you start** links to the chapters you need first, and end with a **Next** link. On a wide screen, the **On this page** panel lists the sections and gives an estimated reading time.

## The boxes, and how to use them

| Box | What it holds | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Key term | An exact definition in examiner-quality wording | Learn it word for word: definitions are easy marks |
| Worked example | A full solution, with marks shown in exam-style ones | Cover the solution and try it first |
| Common mistake | An error examiners see every year | Check your own work against it |
| Exam technique | What a question wants and where the marks are | Apply it when you practise past papers |
| Board differences | Where Edexcel International, AQA or OCR differ from Cambridge | Read the parts for your board |
| Tip, Think of it like this | Shortcuts and everyday analogies | Use them to understand, then learn the precise version |
| Key facts, Formula | Facts and formulas to memorise | Turn them into flashcards |
| Practical skills | Methods, apparatus, safety and errors in experiments | Essential for practical and planning papers |
| Try it yourself | Practice questions, each with a hidden full answer | Attempt first; only then press **Show answer** |
| Quick check | Multiple-choice questions marked instantly | Read the explanation every time, even when you are right |
| Summary | The facts to remember from the chapter | Test yourself on them a week later |

Here is what three of them look like.

:::def Mark scheme
The document examiners use to award marks. It lists the accepted answers and shows exactly where each mark is earned, including method (M), accuracy (A) and independent (B) marks in Mathematics.
:::

:::eg A rectangle is \((x + 3)\) cm long and \(x\) cm wide. Its area is \(40\,\mathrm{cm^2}\). Find \(x\).
Form the equation: \(x(x + 3) = 40\), so \(x^2 + 3x - 40 = 0\). [B1]

Factorise: \((x + 8)(x - 5) = 0\). [M1]

So \(x = 5\); reject \(x = -8\), because a length cannot be negative. [A1]

**Check:** a rectangle 8 cm long and 5 cm wide has area \(40\,\mathrm{cm^2}\).
:::

The mark labels follow the system used in Cambridge and Edexcel mathematics mark schemes:

- **M (method)**: for a correct method applied to the problem, even if you then make a slip.
- **A (accuracy)**: for a correct answer or step. You can only earn it if you have earned the method mark it depends on.
- **B (independent)**: for a correct result or statement that does not depend on a method.

So a correct answer with no working may not earn full marks, while a wrong answer with a sound method can still earn some. You will meet problems like this one in [Quadratics](#math.p1.quadratics).

:::warn Reading is not the same as doing
Following a worked solution feels easy because every step is already there. The real test is whether you can produce it yourself with the page covered. Always try first, then compare.
:::

## Track your progress

At the end of every chapter there is a **Mark as learned** button. Press it when you can do the practice questions without help. Learned chapters get a tick in the menu, the progress bar fills up, and the home page shows how many chapters you have learned in each subject, with a link to pick up where you left off.

:::warn Progress is saved in this browser only
Your ticks, theme and text size are stored on this device, in this browser. They do not move between your phone and your computer, and clearing your browsing data or using a private window removes them. Choose one main device for studying.
:::

## Search, glossary, tools and display

- **Search**: press **/** from anywhere (or tap the search box) and type a topic, key term or formula name. Use the arrow keys and Enter to open a result, or Esc to close the list.
- **Glossary**: every key-term card in the guide, in one alphabetical list that you can filter by subject: [Glossary](#tools.glossary).
- **Tools**: the [graph plotter](#tools.grapher), the [periodic table](#tools.periodic), [calculators](#tools.calc) for UCAS points, formula masses, break-even and more, and formula sheets such as the [maths formula sheet](#tools.f.math).
- **Practice**: [Practice quizzes](#practice.quiz) mix questions from every chapter and tell you which chapters to revise.
- **Planning**: [Build your personal study plan](#road.planner) turns your exam date into a weekly timetable.
- **Display**: the buttons at the top right switch between light and dark mode and cycle the text size from normal to large to extra large.

:::steps
1. **Read the chapter** with pen and paper. Write each key term in your own words, then compare it with the card.
2. **Study the worked examples.** Cover each solution, attempt it, then compare, noting where the marks are.
3. **Try it yourself** with the chapter closed. Open **Show answer** only after a real attempt, and mark your work honestly.
4. **Take the quick check** and read every explanation.
5. **Mark as learned** only when you can do the practice questions without help.
6. **Revisit after a week.** Redo a few questions and the quick check from memory. Each time you pull an idea back out of memory after a gap, you keep it for longer.
:::

:::tip Little and often
A main-subject chapter usually takes several sessions, as its study time shows. End each session by writing down three things you learned, and start the next one by recalling them without notes.
:::

:::try Check you know your way around
Q: In a mark scheme, what is the difference between an M mark and an A mark?
A: An M mark is for a correct method applied to the problem. An A mark is for an accurate answer or step, and it can only be awarded if the method mark it depends on has been earned.
Q: You have just read a chapter and its worked examples. What should you do next, and why?
A: Try the practice questions with the chapter closed, and only then check the answers. Recognising a solution is much easier than producing one, and producing answers from memory is what builds lasting understanding.
:::
