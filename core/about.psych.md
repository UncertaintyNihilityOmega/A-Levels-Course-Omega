:::why
Psychology is the scientific study of the mind and behaviour: why we remember some things and forget others, why people obey or conform, what goes wrong in mental illness and how it can be treated. It is a science, with experiments and statistics, not just opinions about people. This page explains what you would study and whether it fits you.
:::

## What is psychology?

Psychologists try to explain and predict behaviour using **evidence**. They form a hypothesis, design a study (an experiment, observation, interview or questionnaire), collect data, and test whether the data support the idea. Because people are complicated, psychology looks at behaviour from several **approaches**:

- the **biological** approach (the brain, hormones, genes);
- the **cognitive** approach (memory, attention, thinking);
- the **learning** approach (how experience and rewards shape behaviour);
- the **social** approach (how other people and groups affect us).

A big part of the subject is judging research: was the sample representative, was the study ethical, could the result be repeated, does it apply to real life?

## What do psychologists do?

- **Clinical psychologists** assess and treat mental health problems such as depression, anxiety and phobias.
- **Educational psychologists** help children who struggle at school.
- **Health psychologists** study stress, pain and how to help people change habits like smoking.
- **Organisational psychologists** work with companies on motivation, leadership and wellbeing at work.
- **Forensic psychologists** work with courts and prisons; **researchers** run studies in universities.
- Psychology is also used in marketing, user-experience design, sport, policing and the design of public services.

## Why it matters

Mental health problems affect a large share of people at some point in their lives. Psychology research shapes how they are treated, how schools teach, how witnesses are questioned in court, and how products and services are designed. It also helps you understand yourself and others, and spot weak claims about human behaviour.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level Psychology (9990)**, the syllabus for exams in 2028–2030. The course in this guide is planned in these parts:

- **Research methods:** experiments, self-reports, observations, case studies, correlations, sampling, ethics and data analysis.
- **Approaches and core studies:** the biological, cognitive, learning and social approaches, each through key studies you learn and evaluate, and the issues and debates that run through them.
- **A Level options:** clinical psychology, consumer psychology, health psychology and organisational psychology.
- **Exam readiness:** paper guides and mock papers.

## Is it for you?

Psychology suits you if you are curious about people and enjoy arguing from evidence. You will write a lot: describing studies precisely and evaluating them in balanced paragraphs. There is also maths: descriptive statistics, graphs and reading data. Remember that it is a science subject at A Level, and much of it is about how research is done.

It combines well with **Biology**, Maths, Sociology, English or Economics. Psychology degrees rarely require A Level Psychology, but many like a science or Maths.
