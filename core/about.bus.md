Business studies looks at how organisations are set up, how they make decisions, and how they succeed or fail. It covers the people, the marketing, the operations and the money behind every company, from a corner shop to a global brand. This page explains what you would study and whether it fits you.

## What is business studies?

A business takes resources and turns them into goods or services that customers will pay for. Business studies follows that process through the main **functions** of an organisation:

- **People:** organisational structure, leadership, motivation and recruitment.
- **Marketing:** market research, pricing, promotion and how products reach customers.
- **Operations:** producing efficiently, quality, inventory and location.
- **Finance and accounting:** costs, profit, cash flow, budgets and investment decisions.
- **Strategy:** where the business is going, and how it responds to competitors, the economy, technology and the law.

The subject is applied: you are usually given a **case study** of a real or realistic business and asked to analyse its situation and recommend what it should do.

## What do people in business do?

- **Entrepreneurs** start new businesses and take risks to grow them.
- **Managers** lead teams, plan projects and solve problems in every kind of organisation.
- **Marketing, sales and brand specialists** find customers and keep them.
- **Operations and supply-chain managers** make sure products are made and delivered on time.
- **Analysts and consultants** study data and advise companies on strategy.
- Charities, hospitals, schools and governments need the same skills as companies.

## Why it matters

Businesses create most jobs, products and services, and their decisions affect workers, customers, communities and the environment. Understanding business helps you in almost any career, and if you ever start something of your own, it gives you the basic toolkit: a plan, a budget and an understanding of your customers.

## What the A Level covers

The guide follows **Cambridge International AS & A Level Business (9609)**, the syllabus for exams in 2026–2028. The course in this guide is planned in these parts:

- **AS Level:** business and its environment, people in organisations, marketing, operations management and finance and accounting.
- **A Level:** the same five areas in more depth, plus business strategy and decision-making tools.
- **Skills:** business calculations, case-study technique and extended writing.
- **Exam readiness:** paper guides and mock papers.

## Is it for you?

Business suits you if you are interested in how organisations really work, enjoy discussing real companies, and can build a reasoned recommendation. You will write a lot: analysis that links causes and effects to the business in the case, then a judgement. There are also calculations (profit, ratios, break-even, investment appraisal) that need steady numeracy.

It combines well with **Economics** and **Accounting**, Maths, a language, or Psychology. Business degrees rarely require A Level Business, but many like Maths.
