@summary Six phases take you from your first day of study to results day. This page shows what each phase is for, how long it takes on three different timelines, the milestones you must not miss, and how to know when you are ready to move on.

:::why
Two years, or even one, is a long time, and long projects fail in a predictable way: weeks drift past, the hard topics get pushed back, and past papers start far too late. A road with clear phases turns one big, frightening goal into small weekly jobs. It also tells you what to do next on any given day, which is half the battle when you study alone.
:::

## The six phases

Every successful exam preparation, whatever the subject or board, passes through the same six phases. They are not strict boxes. Learning and practice run side by side, and past-paper work starts before the last topic is finished. But the order matters: you cannot practise what you have not learned, and timed papers only help once you know most of the content.

| Phase | What you do | Why it matters | How long |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. [Fix your foundations](#road.foundations) | Check and repair the GCSE/IGCSE skills each subject assumes: algebra, units, graphs, essay writing | Every A Level topic is built on them, and small gaps slow down everything later | 2–6 weeks |
| 2. [Learn the content](#road.learn) | Work through the syllabus topic by topic: read actively, make notes, follow worked examples | You cannot practise or revise what you never understood | About half of your total time |
| 3. [Practise and test yourself](#road.practice) | Retrieval practice, spaced review, flashcards, topic questions, an error log | Understanding fades within days unless you test it; testing makes it stick | Alongside phase 2, then 3–6 weeks of its own |
| 4. [Past papers](#road.pastpapers) | Real papers under timed conditions, marked honestly with the official mark scheme | Teaches the exam's style, its timing and the wording that earns marks | 8–12 weeks |
| 5. [Final revision](#road.final) | Condense notes, target weak topics, sit full mock exams | Turns what you know into marks you can rely on under pressure | The last 6–8 weeks |
| 6. [Exam day](#road.examday) | Prepare the logistics, manage your time in the paper, then deal with results, reviews and resits | Avoids losing marks to nerves, admin or a missing plan B | The exam series and results day |

## Three tracks: how long each phase takes

How long you spend in each phase depends on how much time you have. The three most common situations are below. Find yours, then use the [study planner](#road.planner) to turn it into dated weeks.

| | 2-year school route | 12-month intensive | 5–6-month resit or crash plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Students in Years 12–13 at a school or college | Private candidates studying full-time, often straight after IGCSE | Students resitting, or who already know much of the content |
| Typical dates | September of Year 12 → May/June of Year 13 | June → June, or October → October/November of the next year | June → October/November, or December → May/June |
| 1. Foundations | 3–4 weeks (the summer before, plus September) | 3–4 weeks | 1 week: a diagnostic test only |
| 2. Learn | September of Year 12 to about February of Year 13 | 26–30 weeks | 4–8 weeks, weak topics only |
| 3. Practise | Every week, plus about 4 weeks of consolidation | Every week, plus about 3 weeks | Every week: the main activity |
| 4. Past papers | Topic questions from Year 12; full papers January–April of Year 13 | 8–10 weeks | 8–10 weeks |
| 5. Final revision | 8 weeks (April–May of Year 13) | 6–8 weeks | 3–4 weeks |
| 6. Exam day | May/June; results in August | Your chosen series | Your chosen series |
| Study outside lessons | 10–15 hours a week | 30–36 hours a week | 8–12 hours a week per subject |

:::board Staged, linear and modular
Cambridge International lets you take the AS papers at the end of Year 12 and the rest of the A Level a year later (**staged assessment**). An AS result can be carried forward for up to 13 months. If your school works this way, you run phases 4–6 twice: a short version for AS in Year 12, then the full version in Year 13. AQA and OCR A Levels are **linear**: every paper is sat in May/June at the end of the course. Edexcel International A Levels (IAL) are **modular**, with exams in January, May/June and October, so you can sit some units early and spread the load.
:::

## Milestones you must not miss

| When | Milestone | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Before you start | Choose your subjects and board, and download the syllabus for **your** exam year | [Choosing your subjects](#start.choose), [Exam boards](#start.boards) |
| First month (private candidates) | Find an exam centre; ask about entry deadlines, fees and practical papers | In Turkey: the British Council (Istanbul, Ankara) and Pearson-approved schools. See [Studying as a private candidate](#road.private) |
| Every 6–8 weeks | A timed test on everything learned so far | Record the score in your progress tracker |
| Autumn of your final year | University applications | UCAS deadlines for 2027 entry: 15 October 2026 (Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses), 13 January 2027 (most other courses). See [University applications](#road.university) |
| Several months before the exams | Exam entries close | Schools enter you; private candidates enter through their centre. Late entries cost extra |
| 2–4 months before the first paper | Mock exams | A full set of papers in exam conditions |
| Start of phase 5 | Past-paper target | At least 5 full papers per component done and marked; 8–12 by exam day |
| August (for June exams) | Results day | Cambridge results for the June 2026 series came out on 11 August 2026 |

## How many hours a week?

Cambridge designs each A Level around **360 guided learning hours** of teaching (about 180 for an AS Level). That is class time with a teacher; practice and revision come on top. As a rough guide:

- **At school:** lessons plus 3–5 hours of your own study per subject per week, so 10–15 hours a week for three subjects. Raise this to 20 or more in the final 8 weeks.
- **Private candidate, full-time:** 10–12 hours per subject per week, so 30–36 hours for three subjects. Treat it like a job: a fixed start time, fixed breaks, and one day a week off.
- **Resitting one or two subjects:** 8–12 hours per subject per week.

:::tip Hours are not the goal
Two hours of testing yourself beat five hours of rereading. Track your hours to make sure the time goes in, but judge your progress by your scores on questions you have not seen before.
:::

## Are you ready to move on?

Use these checks at the end of each phase. If you fail one, do not panic: spend another week or two on that phase, then check again.

| Phase | You are ready to move on when… |
|---|---|
| 1. Foundations | You score at least 8 out of 10 on each foundation self-check without looking anything up |
| 2. Learn | For every syllabus learning outcome, you can explain the idea aloud and answer a basic question without notes |
| 3. Practise | You score 70% or more on mixed topic questions a week after learning them, and the same mistakes have stopped appearing in your error log |
| 4. Past papers | Your last three full papers, timed and honestly marked, reach your target grade |
| 5. Final revision | Every topic in your tracker is green, and you can write out the key formulas and definitions from memory |

:::warn The commonest way to fall behind: living in phase 2
Many students spend months rereading notes and making them prettier, then reach the exam having answered almost no questions. Rereading feels productive because everything looks familiar, but recognising an idea is not the same as being able to use it. From the first week, end every study session with questions you answer without looking.
:::

:::exam Start from the syllabus, finish with the mark scheme
Two documents define what you need: the **syllabus** (what can be asked) and the **mark schemes** (what earns marks). Download the syllabus for your exam year before you study anything. Read your first mark scheme early, during phase 2, so you know what examiners look for from the start.
:::

:::tip Already part-way through?
You do not have to start at phase 1. If your exams are four months away, do one past paper per subject this week, mark it, and study the results. Then jump to the phase that fixes your biggest problem, and use the 5–6-month track for timing.
:::

- Pick your track: the 2-year school route, the 12-month intensive, or the 5–6-month resit or crash plan.
- Download the syllabus for your exam year first, and read a mark scheme early.
- Private candidates: find an exam centre and check its entry deadlines in the first month.
- Cambridge designs an A Level around 360 guided learning hours of teaching; your own practice comes on top.
- Judge progress by scores on unseen questions, not by hours or pages of notes.
- Move on from a phase only when you pass its "ready" check.
:::
