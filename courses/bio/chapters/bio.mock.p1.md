Q: [1 mark] (#bio.microscopy) Which statement correctly compares a transmission electron microscope with a light microscope?

**A** The electron microscope has a lower resolution because electrons are larger than photons.

**B** The electron microscope has a higher magnification but the same resolution.

**C** The electron microscope has a higher resolution because electrons have a shorter wavelength than light.

**D** The electron microscope can be used to look at living cells at high magnification.
A: **C** [B1] The shorter wavelength of the electron beam allows two close points to be seen as separate (higher resolution); specimens in a TEM are dead because they are in a vacuum.

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.prokaryotes) Which statement about a bacterial cell is correct?

**A** It has a nucleus surrounded by a double membrane.

**B** It has a wall made of peptidoglycan and ribosomes that are smaller than those in the cytoplasm of a eukaryotic cell.

**C** It is generally 10 µm to 50 µm in diameter.

**D** It makes ATP in mitochondria.

**A** secreting a protein such as a digestive enzyme

**B** storing lipid as droplets

**C** contracting to produce movement

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.carbs) Which statement about cellulose is correct?

**A** It is made of α-glucose joined by 1,4 and 1,6 glycosidic bonds.

**B** It is highly branched, so it has many ends from which glucose can be removed.

**C** Hydrogen bonds between parallel chains hold them together in microfibrils.

**D** It is a soluble storage molecule in plant cells.

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.enzymes) How does an enzyme increase the rate of a reaction?

**A** It increases the kinetic energy of the substrate molecules.

**B** It supplies energy to the reaction from the enzyme.

**C** It changes the energy of the products so that they are lower.

**D** It lowers the activation energy by forming enzyme–substrate complexes.
A: **D** [B1] The enzyme provides an active site that holds the substrate in a position that makes the reaction easier, which lowers the activation energy.

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.enzymes) The table shows the initial rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction at different substrate concentrations. The temperature and the enzyme concentration were constant.

| substrate / mol dm⁻³ | rate / au |
|---|---|
| 0.2 | 3 |
| 0.4 | 6 |
| 0.6 | 8 |
| 0.8 | 9 |
| 1.0 | 9 |

Which statement explains why the rate does not increase between 0.8 and 1.0 mol dm⁻³?

**A** The high substrate concentration has denatured the enzyme.

**B** All of the active sites are occupied, so the enzyme concentration is the limiting factor.

**C** All of the substrate has been used up.

**D** The product has become a non-competitive inhibitor.
A: **B** [B1] At a high substrate concentration every active site is occupied at any moment, so adding substrate cannot increase the rate; the rate is limited by the number of enzyme molecules.

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.enzymes2) The Michaelis–Menten constant (Km) of enzyme P is 2 mmol dm⁻³ and the Km of enzyme Q is 8 mmol dm⁻³. Both enzymes act on the same substrate. Which statement is correct?

**A** Enzyme Q has a higher affinity for the substrate than enzyme P.

**B** Enzyme P has a higher Vmax than enzyme Q.

**C** Enzyme P has a higher affinity for the substrate than enzyme Q.

**D** Enzyme P needs a higher substrate concentration than enzyme Q to reach half of its Vmax.
A: **C** [B1] A lower Km means that half of Vmax is reached at a lower substrate concentration, which shows a higher affinity; Km says nothing about Vmax.
:::

**B** Ions move down a concentration gradient through a channel protein, without using ATP.

**C** Small non-polar molecules move down a concentration gradient through the phospholipid bilayer.

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.planttransport) In a potometer, an air bubble moves 45 mm in 5.0 minutes along a capillary tube of radius 0.40 mm. What is the rate of water uptake in mm³ per minute?

**B** 18

**C** 23

**D** 9.0
A: **A** [B1] Volume \(= \pi r^2 l = \pi \times 0.40^2 \times 45 = 22.6\,\mathrm{mm^3}\) in 5.0 minutes, so the rate is \(22.6 \div 5.0 = 4.5\,\mathrm{mm^3\,min^{-1}}\) (C forgets the time, D forgets the area).

Q: [1 mark] (#bio.translocation) Which statement about the movement of sucrose in the phloem is correct?

**A** Sucrose diffuses along the sieve tube from the source to the sink.

**B** Sucrose moves by mass flow from a source to a sink, down a hydrostatic pressure gradient.

**C** The pressure is lowest at the source because water leaves the sieve tube there.

**D** ATP is used by the sieve tube elements to push the sap along the tube.
