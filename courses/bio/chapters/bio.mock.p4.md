Q: [9 marks] (#bio.respiration) A student measured the respiration of blowfly larvae at 25 °C in two respirometers. Each held 20 larvae. In respirometer A a small tube of potassium hydroxide solution, which absorbs carbon dioxide, was placed under the larvae. In respirometer B the same tube held water. A coloured drop in each capillary tube showed the change in gas volume. After 10 minutes the gas volume had decreased by 0.50 cm³ in A and by 0.10 cm³ in B.

(a) Use the results to calculate the respiratory quotient (RQ) of the larvae. Show your working. [3]

(b) State what the RQ suggests about the respiratory substrate. [1]

(c) State the role of oxygen in aerobic respiration. [2]

(d) Explain how the energy released by the electron transport chain is used to make ATP in a mitochondrion. [3]
A: (a) The volume of oxygen taken in is 0.50 cm³, as the potassium hydroxide absorbed the carbon dioxide in A [B1]. In B the decrease (0.10 cm³) is oxygen taken in minus carbon dioxide produced, so carbon dioxide produced \(= 0.50 - 0.10 = 0.40\ \mathrm{cm^3}\) [M1]. \(\mathrm{RQ} = \dfrac{0.40}{0.50} = 0.80\) [A1] (no units).

(b) A mixture of substrates, such as lipid and carbohydrate, or protein, is being respired [B1].

(c) Oxygen is the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain [B1]; it combines with electrons and protons (hydrogen ions) to form water [B1].

(d) The energy released as electrons pass along carriers in the inner membrane [B1] is used to pump protons across the inner membrane from the matrix to the intermembrane space, making a proton gradient [B1]. The protons flow back through ATP synthase (by facilitated diffusion), which uses the energy to make ATP from ADP and phosphate [B1].

Q: [10 marks] (#bio.photosynthesis) A chloroplast suspension was mixed with DCPIP, a blue dye that becomes colourless when it is reduced. The tubes were set up as shown and the time for the blue colour to disappear was recorded (invented data).

| tube | contents and conditions | time for blue colour to disappear / min |
|---|---|---|
| A | chloroplasts, DCPIP, bright light | 4.0 |
| B | chloroplasts, DCPIP, dim light | 11.0 |
| C | chloroplasts, DCPIP, darkness | no change after 30 |
| D | boiled chloroplasts, DCPIP, bright light | no change after 30 |

(a) Explain why the DCPIP becomes colourless in tube A. [2]

(b) Explain the results for tubes C and D. [2]

(c) Calculate how many times faster the reaction was in tube A than in tube B. [2]

(d) Explain why the reaction was slower in tube B than in tube A. [2]

(e) The supply of carbon dioxide to the Calvin cycle in a leaf is stopped. State and explain the effect on the concentrations of glycerate 3-phosphate (GP) and ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP). [2]
A: (a) DCPIP accepts electrons (is reduced) in place of the reduced NADP that would normally accept them [B1]; the electrons come from the photolysis of water, driven by light [B1].

(b) Tube C: no light, so the electrons of chlorophyll are not excited (no photoactivation) and no electrons are passed on [B1]. Tube D: boiling denatured the proteins (photosystems and electron carriers), so no electron transfer takes place [B1].

(c) The rate is proportional to \(1/\text{time}\), so A is faster by \(\dfrac{11.0}{4.0}\) [M1] \(= 2.75\), which is 2.8 times to 2 significant figures [A1].

(d) Less light is absorbed in dim light, so fewer electrons are photoactivated (less photolysis of water) [B1]; light intensity is the limiting factor, so electron flow and the reduction of DCPIP are slower [B1].

(e) GP falls because less carbon dioxide is fixed by rubisco to RuBP, so less GP is made [B1]. RuBP rises because it is still regenerated from triose phosphate (using ATP) but is no longer used up [B1].

Q: [12 marks] (#bio.homeostasis) The graph above shows the volume of urine produced by a volunteer who drank 1.0 dm³ of water at time 0.

(a) Describe the changes in the volume of urine produced after the water was drunk. [3]

(b) Explain these changes, with reference to antidiuretic hormone (ADH). [4]

(c) When blood glucose concentration is low, glucagon causes liver cells to release glucose. Describe how glucagon has this effect. [5]
A: (a) The volume rises from 20 cm³ per 30 minutes at time 0 to a peak of 210 cm³ per 30 minutes at 90 minutes [B1]; it is above 100 cm³ per 30 minutes between about 60 and 120 minutes [B1]; it then falls and is back to about 20 cm³ per 30 minutes by about 170 minutes [B1].

(b) The water raises the water potential of the blood plasma, which is detected by osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus [B1]. Less ADH is released from the posterior pituitary gland [B1]. The collecting duct cells have fewer aquaporins, so the collecting duct is less permeable to water and less water is reabsorbed [B1]. A large volume of dilute urine is produced, which returns the water potential of the blood to normal (negative feedback) [B1].

(c) Glucagon binds to a specific receptor on the surface membrane of the liver cell [B1]; the receptor changes its shape (conformational change), which activates a G-protein [B1]; the G-protein activates adenylyl cyclase, which converts ATP to cyclic AMP (cAMP), the second messenger [B1]; cAMP activates protein kinase A, which starts an enzyme cascade of phosphorylation, amplifying the signal [B1]; the enzymes break down glycogen to glucose, which leaves the cell and enters the blood [B1].
:::

Q: [10 marks] (#bio.synapses) The figure above shows a relaxed sarcomere in a muscle.

(a) Name the parts labelled P, Q, R and S. [4]

(b) Describe the changes in the appearance of the sarcomere when the muscle contracts. [3]

(c) Describe how the arrival of an action potential at the neuromuscular junction leads to the contraction of the sarcomere. [3]
A: (a) P: thin filament (actin) [B1]; Q: thick filament (myosin) [B1]; R: Z line [B1]; S: H zone [B1].

(b) The sarcomere gets shorter (the Z lines move closer) [B1]. The I band becomes narrower and the H zone becomes narrower, and it may disappear [B1]. The A band stays the same length [B1].

(c) Acetylcholine is released from the neurone, crosses the gap and binds to receptors on the sarcolemma, causing depolarisation that spreads along the T-tubules [B1]. Calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum [B1]. Calcium ions bind to troponin, which moves tropomyosin to expose the binding sites on actin; myosin heads bind to actin, form cross-bridges and pull the actin filaments inwards using energy from the hydrolysis of ATP [B1].

Q: [10 marks] (#bio.hormones) Half-seeds of barley were cut so that some had an embryo and some did not. They were kept on starch agar for 48 hours. The activity of amylase in each half-seed was measured (invented data).

| treatment | amylase activity / arbitrary units |
|---|---|
| half-seed with embryo | 82 |
| half-seed without embryo | 6 |
| without embryo, with gibberellin | 79 |
| without embryo, with gibberellin and a chemical that stops transcription | 7 |

(a) Describe and interpret the results. [3]

(b) Explain the importance of amylase during germination. [2]

(c) In a drought, abscisic acid causes the stomata of leaves to close. Describe how. [5]
A: (a) Amylase activity is high with an embryo (82 units) but very low without one (6 units), so the embryo produces something that stimulates amylase production [B1]. Gibberellin restores the activity almost fully (79 units), so the embryo's signal is gibberellin [B1]. A chemical that stops transcription almost cancels the effect (7 units), so gibberellin acts by switching on the transcription of the amylase gene [B1].

(b) Amylase hydrolyses starch in the seed to maltose [B1], which is converted to glucose and respired to release energy for growth of the embryo (or used for cell wall synthesis) [B1].

(c) Abscisic acid binds to receptors on the guard cells [B1]. The concentration of calcium ions in the cytoplasm increases (calcium ions are a second messenger) [B1]. Ion channels open, and potassium ions (and anions) leave the guard cells, raising the water potential of the guard cells [B1]. Water leaves the guard cells by osmosis [B1]. The guard cells lose turgor and become flaccid, so the stomatal pore closes [B1].

Q: [9 marks] (#bio.inheritance) In cats, the gene for fur colour is on the X chromosome. The allele \(X^B\) gives black fur and \(X^O\) gives orange fur. The alleles are codominant, and a female with one of each allele has tortoiseshell fur.

(a) Explain why almost all tortoiseshell cats are female. [2]

(b) Draw a genetic diagram to show the cross between a black male and a tortoiseshell female. Give the genotypes and phenotypes of the kittens and the ratio. [5]

(c) The cross produced 80 kittens. Calculate the number expected to be orange and the number expected to be tortoiseshell. [2]
A: (a) Males are XY and have only one X chromosome, so they carry only one allele of the gene [B1]; tortoiseshell needs both alleles (heterozygous), which only females (XX) can have [B1].

(b) Parental genotypes: male \(X^B Y\), female \(X^B X^O\) [B1]. Gametes: \(X^B\), \(Y\) and \(X^B\), \(X^O\) [B1]. Kittens: \(X^B X^B\), \(X^B X^O\), \(X^B Y\), \(X^O Y\) [B1]. Phenotypes: black female, tortoiseshell female, black male, orange male [B1]. Ratio 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 (or 1 black female : 1 tortoiseshell female : 1 black male : 1 orange male) [B1].

(c) One quarter of 80 is 20, so 20 orange (male) kittens [B1] and 20 tortoiseshell (female) kittens [B1].

Q: [8 marks] (#bio.dihybrid) A plant heterozygous for two genes on the same chromosome pair, AaBb, was self-pollinated. If the genes were on different chromosomes, the offspring would be expected in the ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. There were 320 offspring, with phenotype numbers 210, 31, 28 and 51 (in the order of the ratio). The chi-squared formula is \(\chi^2 = \sum \dfrac{(O - E)^2}{E}\). Critical values at \(p = 0.05\): 1 degree of freedom 3.84; 2, 5.99; 3, 7.81; 4, 9.49; 5, 11.07.

(a) State the null hypothesis. [1]

(b) Calculate the expected numbers for a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio. [1]

(c) Calculate \(\chi^2\). [2]

(d) State the degrees of freedom and the conclusion. [2]

(e) Suggest an explanation for the results. [2]
A: (a) There is no significant difference between the observed numbers and the numbers expected for a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio (any difference is due to chance) [B1].

(b) 180, 60, 60 and 20 [B1]

(c) \(\chi^2 = \dfrac{(210-180)^2}{180} + \dfrac{(31-60)^2}{60} + \dfrac{(28-60)^2}{60} + \dfrac{(51-20)^2}{20}\) [M1] \(= 5.0 + 14.0 + 17.1 + 48.1 = 84.1\) [A1] (ecf from wrong expected numbers)

(d) Degrees of freedom \(= 4 - 1 = 3\) [B1]. \(84.1 > 7.81\), so the difference is significant: reject the null hypothesis; the results do not fit 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 [B1].

(e) The genes are linked (on the same chromosome), so the alleles on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together as the parental combinations [B1]. The new combinations form only if crossing over happens between the genes, so these offspring are fewer than expected [B1].

Q: [14 marks] (#bio.variation) Two islands, X and Y, are home to ground beetles. A scientist measured the wing length of 15 beetles from each island. Island X: mean 18.6 mm, standard deviation 1.9 mm. Island Y: mean 17.3 mm, standard deviation 2.2 mm. The \(t\)-test formula is \(t = \dfrac{|\bar x_1 - \bar x_2|}{\sqrt{\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}\). Critical values of \(t\) at \(p = 0.05\): 26 degrees of freedom 2.06; 27, 2.05; 28, 2.05; 29, 2.05; 30, 2.04.

(a) (i) State the number of degrees of freedom. [1]

(ii) Calculate \(t\). [3]

(iii) State the conclusion. [1]

(iv) Explain why the \(t\)-test is suitable for these data. [1]

(b) In island X, a recessive allele for wingless beetles has \(q^2 = 0.09\) among 500 beetles. Use the Hardy–Weinberg equations, \(p + q = 1\) and \(p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1\), to calculate the number of heterozygous beetles. [3]

(c) The beetles on the two islands look similar. Discuss whether they should be classified as the same species or as two species. [5]
H: In (c), name the species concepts and say what evidence each would need.
A: (a) (i) \(15 + 15 - 2 = 28\) [B1]

(ii) \(t = \dfrac{|18.6 - 17.3|}{\sqrt{\dfrac{1.9^2}{15} + \dfrac{2.2^2}{15}}}\) [M1] \(= \dfrac{1.3}{\sqrt{0.2407 + 0.3227}}\) [M1] \(= 1.73\) [A1]

(iii) \(1.73 < 2.05\), so there is no significant difference in mean wing length; accept the null hypothesis [B1].

(iv) The data are continuous measurements of two samples (with similar standard deviations), and there are fewer than 30 in each sample [B1]. (Accept: wing length is normally distributed.)

(b) \(q = \sqrt{0.09} = 0.30\) [M1]; \(p = 0.70\) and \(2pq = 2 \times 0.70 \times 0.30 = 0.42\) [M1]; number of heterozygotes \(= 0.42 \times 500 = 210\) [A1]

(c) Any five from: the biological species concept defines a species as organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring [B1]; this could be tested by crossing beetles from X and Y and checking that the offspring are fertile [B1]; the morphological species concept uses similarity of structure [B1]; the \(t\)-test showed no significant difference in wing length, so this evidence suggests one species [B1]; the ecological species concept uses the niche (habitat, food), so differences in diet or habitat between the islands could suggest two species [B1]. (Also credit: similar appearance does not prove one species, since different species can look alike and one species can vary; a reasoned conclusion that more evidence, such as breeding or DNA sequence data, is needed.)
:::

Q: [9 marks] (#bio.biodiversity) Bank voles live in a nature reserve. In a mark-release-recapture study, 50 voles were caught, marked and released. A week later, 40 were caught, of which 8 were marked.

(a) Calculate the estimated population size, using \(N = \dfrac{n_1 \times n_2}{m_2}\). [2]

(b) Two meadows were sampled. The numbers of individuals of four plant species were: meadow A, 40, 30, 20 and 10; meadow B, 85, 8, 4 and 3. Calculate Simpson's index of diversity, \(D = 1 - \sum \left(\dfrac{n}{N}\right)^2\), for each meadow and state which is more diverse. [4]

(c) The voles are endangered. Outline how a zoo could use in vitro fertilisation (IVF), embryo transfer and surrogacy to increase their numbers. [3]
A: (a) \(N = \dfrac{50 \times 40}{8}\) [M1] \(= 250\) [A1]

(b) Meadow A: \(\sum (n/N)^2 = 0.4^2 + 0.3^2 + 0.2^2 + 0.1^2 = 0.30\) [M1], so \(D = 1 - 0.30 = 0.70\) [A1]. Meadow B: \(0.85^2 + 0.08^2 + 0.04^2 + 0.03^2 = 0.7314\), so \(D = 0.27\) [A1]. Meadow A is more diverse because it has the higher \(D\) (the individuals are spread more evenly between the species) [B1].

(c) Eggs are collected from females and sperm from males and fertilised outside the body (in vitro) [B1]; the embryos are implanted into surrogate mothers (females of the same or a closely related species) [B1]; this produces more offspring than natural breeding, and gametes or embryos from different zoos can be used to keep genetic diversity (they can be frozen) [B1].
:::

Q: [9 marks] (#bio.genetech) A crop plant was genetically modified to carry a gene for herbicide resistance. A scientist used PCR to test two plants (GM1 and GM2) and a non-modified plant (WT). Two pairs of primers were used in each tube: one pair for the inserted gene (500 base pairs) and one pair for a gene present in every plant (250 base pairs). Lane W contained water instead of DNA. The products were separated by gel electrophoresis, as shown in the figure above. Lane M holds fragments of known size.

(a) Describe the three stages of one cycle of PCR. [3]

(b) Explain why the DNA polymerase used in PCR is Taq polymerase. [1]

(c) Use the gel to state which plant has the inserted gene, and explain the results in the other lanes. [3]

(d) Explain why the DNA fragments move towards the positive electrode, and why smaller fragments travel further than larger fragments. [2]
A: (a) Denaturation: heated to about 95 °C, which breaks the hydrogen bonds and separates the two strands [B1]. Annealing: cooled to about 55 to 65 °C so that primers bind to complementary sequences on the single strands [B1]. Extension: heated to about 72 °C, when Taq polymerase adds nucleotides to the primers to build new complementary strands [B1].

(b) Taq polymerase is not denatured by the high temperature of the denaturation stage (it works at about 72 °C), so it need not be added again each cycle [B1].

(c) GM1 has the inserted gene, as it has the 500 base pair band [B1]. WT and GM2 have no 500 base pair band, so they lack the gene (transformation failed in GM2); the 250 base pair band in lanes WT, GM1 and GM2 shows that DNA was present and the PCR worked [B1]. Lane W has no bands, so the reagents were not contaminated with DNA [B1].

(d) DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate groups, so it moves to the positive electrode [B1]. The gel is a mesh through which small fragments move more easily than large fragments, so they travel further in the same time [B1].
