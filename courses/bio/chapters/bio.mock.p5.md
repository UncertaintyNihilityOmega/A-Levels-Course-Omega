Q: [18 marks] (#bio.planning) Fungicide **F** is sprayed on grapes to stop spoilage by yeast, which is a single-celled fungus. A company wants to know how the concentration of F affects the growth of yeast.

Yeast grows in a liquid nutrient broth that contains glucose. As the yeast cells multiply, the broth becomes cloudy, so the absorbance of light by the broth, measured with a colorimeter in arbitrary units, is greater when there are more cells.

You are provided with: F as a powder; sterile distilled water; a culture of yeast in sterile nutrient broth; sterile test tubes with bungs; sterile 10 cm³ syringes; a balance; a stirrer; labels; an incubator set at 30 °C; a colorimeter with cuvettes and a stopwatch; and the apparatus normally found in a school laboratory.

(a) Write a hypothesis for this investigation and sketch a graph of your prediction. [3]

(b) State the independent variable and the dependent variable, and describe one variable that must be standardised and how you would control it. [3]

(c) You make a stock solution of 4.0% (w/v) F. Calculate the mass of F needed to make 200 cm³ of the stock solution. [1]

(d) Describe how you would use the stock solution to make 10 cm³ of each of 2.0, 1.0, 0.50 and 0.25% (w/v) F by serial dilution. [2]

(e) Describe a method for the investigation, using the stock solution, your four dilutions and sterile water. Include how you would set up the tubes, measure the dependent variable and make your results reliable. [4]

(f) F can irritate the skin and the eyes, and yeast is a microorganism. Assess the risks in your method by stating one hazard, how serious the harm could be and how likely it is, and one precaution that reduces the risk. [2]

In a pilot investigation, five replicate tubes were set up for the control (no F) and for 2.0% (w/v) F. After 24 hours the mean absorbance of the control was 0.88 (standard deviation 0.04) and the mean absorbance with 2.0% F was 0.31 (standard deviation 0.06).

(g) Calculate the percentage decrease in mean absorbance caused by the 2.0% F compared with the control. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]

(h) Use the standard deviations to state whether the results of the pilot investigation are reliable. [1]
A: (a) Hypothesis: increasing the concentration of F decreases the growth of yeast (so the absorbance of the broth after a fixed time falls), because F damages yeast cells [B1]. Sketch: the x-axis is labelled concentration of F / % (w/v) and the y-axis is labelled absorbance / arbitrary units [B1]; a line or curve that starts high at 0% F and falls as the concentration rises (it may level off at low absorbance) [B1]. (A line that rises, or an axis with no label or unit, does not score.)

(b) The independent variable is the concentration of F (% (w/v)) [B1]. The dependent variable is the absorbance of the broth after a fixed time (24 hours) [B1]. A variable to standardise, and how: the volume of yeast culture in each tube, using the same syringe volume from a well mixed culture so every tube starts with the same number of cells; or the temperature, by keeping all tubes in the same incubator at 30 °C; or the volume of nutrient broth and the incubation time [B1]. (The variable must be named and the way of controlling it described.)

(c) 4.0% (w/v) means 4.0 g in 100 cm³, so 200 cm³ needs \(4.0 \times \dfrac{200}{100}\) [B1] = 8.0 g (the mark is for 8.0 g with the unit; a working line is shown).

(d) Put 10 cm³ of the stock solution (4.0%) and 10 cm³ of sterile water into a tube and mix: this halves the concentration to give 2.0% [B1]. Transfer 10 cm³ of this 2.0% solution into 10 cm³ of sterile water and mix to make 1.0%; repeat to make 0.50% and then 0.25%, using a clean syringe each time, and discard 10 cm³ from the last tube so that every tube holds 10 cm³ [B1]. (Also credit: label each tube; mix thoroughly at each step.)

(e) Marking points:

- put the same volume of the yeast culture in broth (for example 9.0 cm³, from a well mixed culture) into every tube, so each starts with the same number of cells [B1];
- add the same volume (for example 1.0 cm³) of each F solution (0.25, 0.50, 1.0, 2.0 and 4.0%) to separate tubes, and the same volume of sterile water to a control tube with no F [B1];
- put all the tubes together in the incubator at 30 °C for the same time (for example 24 hours); then mix each tube and measure its absorbance with the colorimeter, set to zero with a tube of nutrient broth with no yeast [B1];
- set up at least three tubes (replicates) for each concentration and the control, and calculate the mean absorbance for each concentration, treating any anomalous result separately [B1].

(Also credit: use sterile technique, bung the tubes, shake gently before sampling, and a graph of mean absorbance against concentration.)

(f) The hazard is F (a powder or solution), which is irritating to skin and eyes, so harm to the eyes is serious but unlikely if care is taken (severity moderate, likelihood low) [B1]. Precaution: wear eye protection (goggles) and gloves, and wash hands after use; or for the yeast, wash hands and disinfect the bench after work and keep the tubes bunged [B1]. (The precaution must match the hazard named.)

(g) \[\begin{aligned} \text{decrease} &= 0.88 - 0.31 = 0.57 \\ \text{percentage decrease} &= \frac{0.57}{0.88} \times 100 \end{aligned}\]
The decrease is divided by the control value, then multiplied by 100 [M1]. The answer is 65% (unrounded 64.77%; 2 significant figures, like the data) [A1].

(h) The standard deviations (0.04 and 0.06) are small compared with the difference between the means (0.57), so the five replicates in each group were close together and the difference is unlikely to be due to variation between tubes; the results are reliable (repeatable) [B1].

Marks by skill in Question 1: planning 15 ((a) to (f)), analysis, conclusions and evaluation 3 ((g) and (h)).

Q: [12 marks] (#bio.evaluation) An extract of seaweed is used as an organic fertiliser. A grower made the hypothesis that seaweed extract increases the growth of lettuce seedlings.

Thirty-six lettuce seedlings of the same variety and age were divided into three groups of 12. Each group was watered every day with the same volume of one solution: water only (the control), 1.0% seaweed extract or 2.0% seaweed extract. The seedlings were kept in the same light and at the same temperature. After 21 days all the seedlings were dried and the dry mass of each was found. The results are in the table and in Fig. 2.1, where the error bars show the standard error.

| treatment | mean dry mass / mg | standard deviation / mg | standard error / mg |
|---|---|---|---|
| control (water) | 41.2 | 5.8 | 1.7 |
| 1.0% extract | 43.0 | 6.4 | 1.8 |
| 2.0% extract | 47.9 | 6.5 | 1.9 |

(a) Calculate the percentage increase in the mean dry mass with the 2.0% extract compared with the control. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]

(b) The grower decides to use a statistical test to find out whether the difference between the mean dry mass of the control and of the 2.0% extract is significant. Name a suitable test and justify your choice. [2]

(c) State the null hypothesis for this test. [1]

The formula for the test is

\[ t = \frac{|\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2|}{\sqrt{\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}}} \]

where \(\bar{x}\) is the mean, \(s\) is the standard deviation and \(n\) is the number of seedlings in a group. Group 1 is the control and group 2 is the 2.0% extract.

(d) The working so far is \(|\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2| = 6.7\) mg and \(\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} = 2.803\). Complete the calculation of \(t\). Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]

Some critical values of \(t\) for a two-tailed test at \(p = 0.05\) are:

| degrees of freedom | critical value |
|---|---|
| 18 | 2.10 |
| 19 | 2.09 |
| 20 | 2.09 |
| 21 | 2.08 |
| 22 | 2.07 |
| 23 | 2.07 |
| 24 | 2.06 |
| 25 | 2.06 |
| 26 | 2.06 |

(e) Calculate the degrees of freedom and use the table to state what the value of \(t\) shows about the null hypothesis. [2]

(f) Discuss the extent to which the results support the hypothesis that seaweed extract increases the growth of lettuce seedlings. Use your answer to (e) and Fig. 2.1. [2]
A: (a) \[\begin{aligned} \text{increase} &= 47.9 - 41.2 = 6.7\ \mathrm{mg} \\ \text{percentage increase} &= \frac{6.7}{41.2} \times 100 \end{aligned}\]
The increase is divided by the control mean, then multiplied by 100 [M1]. The answer is 16.3% (unrounded 16.26%) [A1].

(b) A t-test [B1]. Justification (any two of): the data (dry mass) are continuous; two samples are being compared by their means; the standard deviations are similar (5.8 and 6.5); there are fewer than 30 values in each group (12), and the values are assumed to be normally distributed [B1]. (Chi-squared is wrong, as the data are not frequencies in categories. A correlation test is wrong, as there is no pairing of two variables.)

(c) There is no significant difference between the mean dry mass of seedlings grown with 2.0% seaweed extract and of seedlings grown with water (any difference is due to chance) [B1].

(d) \(\dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2} = \dfrac{6.5^2}{12} = 3.521\) [B1].
\[\begin{aligned} \text{sum} &= 2.803 + 3.521 = 6.324 \\ \sqrt{6.324} &= 2.515 \end{aligned}\]
Adding the two terms and taking the square root [M1]. \(t = \dfrac{6.7}{2.515} = 2.66\) [A1].

(e) Degrees of freedom \(= (12 + 12) - 2 = 22\) [B1]. The critical value for 22 degrees of freedom is 2.07; the calculated \(t\) (2.66) is greater than 2.07, so the difference is significant at \(p = 0.05\) and the null hypothesis is rejected [B1].

(f) The hypothesis is supported for the 2.0% extract: the mean dry mass is 16.3% greater and the t-test shows that the difference is significant (2.66 is greater than 2.07) [B1]. It is less clearly supported for the 1.0% extract: the error bars (standard error) of the control and the 1.0% extract overlap (39.5 to 42.9 mg and 41.2 to 44.8 mg), so the small difference between them may be due to chance; the data support the hypothesis only partly, for the higher concentration (and only for one variety of lettuce, over 21 days) [B1].
