Q: [23 marks] (#bio.practical) A student investigated the effect of the concentration of sodium chloride solution on the mass of carrot tissue. The student was given solution **S**, which is sodium chloride solution of concentration 0.60 mol dm⁻³, distilled water **W**, one carrot, a cork borer, a knife, a white tile, paper towels, six labelled beakers, measuring cylinders, a stopwatch and a balance that reads to 0.01 g.

The student carried out this method.

1. Prepare 30 cm³ of each of six solutions, 0.00, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40 and 0.50 mol dm⁻³, by diluting S with W, and put each into a labelled beaker.
2. On the tile, push the cork borer into the carrot and cut 18 cylinders. Cut each cylinder to a length of 30 mm.
3. Blot three cylinders on a paper towel, find their total mass, and put them into the beaker of 0.00 mol dm⁻³. Repeat for each beaker.
4. After 40 minutes, remove the three cylinders from each beaker, blot them in the same way, and find their total mass again.

(a) State the independent variable and the dependent variable in this investigation. [2]

(b) The student prepares 30 cm³ of each solution. Complete the table to show the volumes of S and W needed for 0.20, 0.40 and 0.50 mol dm⁻³. [3]

| concentration / mol dm⁻³ | volume of S / cm³ | volume of W / cm³ |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0 | 30 |
| 0.10 | 5 | 25 |
| 0.20 | … | … |
| 0.30 | 15 | 15 |
| 0.40 | … | … |
| 0.50 | … | … |

(c) The student used a cork borer and a knife to cut the carrot. Identify the main hazard, state whether the risk is low, medium or high, and describe one precaution. [2]

(d) State one observation, other than mass, that would show that the cylinders in the 0.50 mol dm⁻³ solution had lost water. [1]

The student recorded the mass of the three cylinders together, before and after the 40 minutes:

- 0.00 mol dm⁻³: before 3.12 g, after 3.55 g
- 0.10 mol dm⁻³: before 3.28 g, after 3.54 g
- 0.20 mol dm⁻³: before 3.15 g, after 3.21 g
- 0.30 mol dm⁻³: before 3.25 g, after 3.04 g
- 0.40 mol dm⁻³: before 3.20 g, after 3.12 g
- 0.50 mol dm⁻³: before 3.22 g, after 2.60 g

(e) Construct a table to record these results. Include a column for the change in mass. [3]

(f) Calculate the percentage change in mass of the cylinders in the 0.30 mol dm⁻³ solution. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]

(g) The percentage changes in mass for 0.00 to 0.50 mol dm⁻³ are +13.8, +7.9, +1.9, −6.5, −2.5 and −19.3 %. Identify the anomalous result and give a reason for your choice. [1]

(h) The student plots a graph of the percentage change in mass (y-axis) against the concentration of sodium chloride solution (x-axis). Describe how the student should draw the axes, the scales, the points and the line. [2]

(i) The graph on this page (Fig. 1.1) shows the student's results and line of best fit. Use the graph to determine the concentration of sodium chloride solution at which the carrot cylinders show no change in mass. Show on the graph how you found your answer. [2]

(j) State a conclusion from the results and explain it in terms of water potential. [2]

(k) The student blotted each cylinder before weighing it. Explain why differences in how well the cylinders were blotted are a random error and not a systematic error, and suggest one improvement to the method. [2]

(l) Suggest how the investigation could be extended to give a more precise value for your answer to (i). [1]
A: (a) The independent variable is the concentration of sodium chloride solution [B1]. The dependent variable is the percentage change in mass of the carrot cylinders (accept the change in mass) [B1].

(b) The volume of S needed is \(\dfrac{c}{0.60} \times 30\) cm³, and W makes the total up to 30 cm³. For 0.20: S 10 cm³ and W 20 cm³ [B1]. For 0.40: S 20 cm³ and W 10 cm³ [B1]. For 0.50: S 25 cm³ and W 5 cm³ [B1].

(c) The hazard is the sharp knife or cork borer, which can cut or stab the skin (especially if the carrot is held in the hand while it is cut); the risk is medium (accept low, with a reason such as: an injury is unlikely and would be minor) [B1]. Precaution: cut on a tile, with the blade pointing away from the body and hands, or push the cork borer into the carrot while it is on the tile and not held in the hand [B1]. (Also credit: wear eye protection; use a sharp blade, which needs less force.)

(d) The cylinders in the 0.50 mol dm⁻³ solution are limp, soft or flexible and bend easily (they are firm and rigid in water), or they are shorter than at the start [B1]. (Credit: a described change in texture or length. Not credited: "they look different".)

(e) Model table:

| concentration of NaCl / mol dm⁻³ | mass before / g | mass after / g | change in mass / g |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 3.12 | 3.55 | +0.43 |
| 0.10 | 3.28 | 3.54 | +0.26 |
| 0.20 | 3.15 | 3.21 | +0.06 |
| 0.30 | 3.25 | 3.04 | −0.21 |
| 0.40 | 3.20 | 3.12 | −0.08 |
| 0.50 | 3.22 | 2.60 | −0.62 |

The independent variable is in the left column, and every column heading has a quantity and a unit, with units only in the headings and not in the body [B1]. All the masses are recorded to the same number of decimal places (2), as the balance reads [B1]. The change in mass is calculated correctly for every row and has a sign (+ for a gain, − for a loss) [B1].

(f) \[\begin{aligned} \text{change} &= 3.04 - 3.25 = -0.21\ \mathrm{g} \\ \text{percentage change} &= \frac{-0.21}{3.25} \times 100 \end{aligned}\]
Working: change in mass divided by the starting mass, times 100 [M1]. The answer is \(-6.46\%\) (unrounded \(-6.4615\)), a decrease of 6.46%, to 3 significant figures like the masses [A1].

(g) The result at 0.40 mol dm⁻³ is anomalous [B1], because its change (−2.5%) is smaller than at 0.30 (−6.5%), whereas the trend is a steady fall, so about −12% was expected. (A reason is needed with the choice for the mark.)

(h) The concentration of sodium chloride / mol dm⁻³ (the independent variable) goes on the x-axis and the percentage change in mass / % on the y-axis, each labelled with its unit; the y-axis must include negative values; each scale is even (for example 2% per large square) and uses more than half of the grid in both directions [B1]. Each point is plotted as a small cross or a dot in a circle, exactly at its value, and one straight line of best fit (or a smooth line) is drawn with a ruler or a clear, continuous line, ignoring the anomalous point [B1]. (Also credit: the line is not extended beyond the data unless justified; the origin need not be shown.)

(i) A vertical line is drawn from the point where the line of best fit crosses 0% change (the x-axis) down to the concentration scale, or the crossing point is marked [B1]. The concentration is 0.21 mol dm⁻³ (accept 0.20 to 0.22) [B1]. (This is the concentration of a solution with the same water potential as the carrot cells.)

(j) As the concentration of sodium chloride solution increases, the mass of the carrot decreases: from a gain of 13.8% in 0.00 mol dm⁻³ to a loss of 19.3% in 0.50 mol dm⁻³ [B1]. At low concentrations the solution has a higher water potential than the cells, so water enters the cells by osmosis (down a water potential gradient, through the partially permeable cell surface membrane) and the mass rises; at high concentrations the solution has a lower water potential, so water leaves the cells and the mass falls; at about 0.21 mol dm⁻³ the water potentials are equal, so there is no net movement [B1].

(k) Random errors vary unpredictably: some cylinders keep a little more solution on the surface and read too high, and others are blotted harder and read too low, so the errors are in no one direction [B1]. A systematic error would change every reading in the same direction by the same amount. Improvement: blot every cylinder in the same way, for example for 10 seconds with the same pressure on a fresh dry paper towel, or use more cylinders per beaker and take a mean [B1]. (Also credit: use a timer for blotting.)

(l) Use more concentrations between 0.10 and 0.30 mol dm⁻³, at smaller intervals such as 0.05 mol dm⁻³, because the line crosses zero in this range [B1]. (Also credit: repeat the whole investigation and calculate means.)

Marks by skill in Question 1: manipulation, measurement and observation 8 ((a) to (d)), presentation 5 ((e) and (h)), analysis, conclusions and evaluation 10 ((f), (g), (i) to (l)).

Q: [17 marks] (#bio.practical2) In this question you use a microscope drawing and photomicrographs. The microscope questions are on slide **S1**, a transverse section (TS) of a plant organ. Fig. 2.1 shows what is seen with the ×10 objective, with the eyepiece graticule scale below it. In the section there is a ring around the central tissue: this ring is the endodermis. Fig. 2.2 is photomicrograph **P**, a ×40 view of cells from the cortex of S1. Fig. 2.3 is photomicrograph **S2**, a section of a different plant organ at the same magnification as S1. All three are drawings that stand for what you would see.

(a) State one visible feature in Fig. 2.1 that shows that S1 is a root and not a stem. [1]

(b) Make a plan diagram of the section in Fig. 2.1 on paper. Draw the outline of the section, the endodermis and the xylem. Label the xylem, the cortex and the endodermis. [4]

(c) The student calibrated the eyepiece graticule with a stage micrometer at ×10. The divisions on the stage micrometer scale are 0.10 mm apart. Four divisions of the stage micrometer scale lined up exactly with 32 divisions of the eyepiece graticule. Calculate the actual length in µm of one eyepiece graticule division. [2]

(d) In Fig. 2.1, the dashed lines C and D mark the width of the endodermis. Read the width of the endodermis in graticule divisions from the scale, and calculate its actual width in µm. [2]

(e) Make a drawing of the two cells in the dashed box in Fig. 2.2. Label one cell wall and one nucleus. [3]

(f) On the printed photomicrograph the scale bar is 20 mm long and represents 50 µm. The line XY across cell Z is 28 mm long. Calculate the actual width of cell Z in µm. Show your working. [2]

(g) Compare S1 (Fig. 2.1) with S2 (Fig. 2.3) by completing a table of three differences that you can see. [3]
A: (a) The xylem forms a star or plus shape (cross) in the centre of the section [B1]. (Also credit: there is no pith; the vascular tissue is in one central core and not in separate bundles in a ring; the cortex is wide.)

(b) Marking points for the plan diagram:

- the drawing is large (it uses more than half of the space), and made with a sharp pencil in single, clear, unbroken lines and with no shading [B1];
- the outer outline and the endodermis are drawn as circles in the correct proportions, the endodermis being about 0.4 of the diameter of the section (16 divisions out of 40) [B1];
- the xylem is drawn as a four-armed star or plus inside the endodermis, as an outline, and no individual cells are drawn anywhere [B1];
- three labels (xylem, cortex, endodermis) are written horizontally beside the drawing with ruled label lines that touch the correct tissue and do not cross [B1].

(c) The four stage micrometer divisions are \(4 \times 0.10 = 0.40\ \mathrm{mm} = 400\ \mathrm{\mu m}\), and this equals 32 graticule divisions [M1].
\[\begin{aligned} 1\ \text{division} &= \frac{400}{32} \\ &= 12.5\ \mathrm{\mu m} \end{aligned}\]
The answer is 12.5 µm (accept 0.0125 mm) [A1].

(d) C is at 17 on the scale and D is at 33, so the endodermis is 16 divisions wide (accept 15 to 17) [B1]. \(16 \times 12.5 = 200\ \mathrm{\mu m}\) (accept 0.20 mm, or the candidate's reading multiplied by 12.5) [A1].

(e) Marking points for the cell drawing:

- the drawing is large, in single, sharp, unbroken lines, with no shading [B1];
- two touching cells, drawn as six-sided shapes of about the same size and proportions as the photomicrograph, with the wall between them drawn once and the cells touching (no gap, and no extra cells outside the box) [B1];
- only observable features are drawn: a small, round nucleus in the correct position, and starch grains if they are in the box; one cell wall and one nucleus are labelled with ruled label lines that touch them [B1].

(f) Scale: 20 mm on the paper represents 50 µm. Cell Z is 28 mm, so its width is \(\dfrac{28}{20} \times 50\ \mathrm{\mu m}\) [M1].
\[\begin{aligned} \text{width of cell Z} &= \frac{28}{20} \times 50 \\ &= 70\ \mathrm{\mu m} \end{aligned}\]
The answer is 70 µm (2 significant figures, like the measurements) [A1].

|---|---|---|
| xylem or vascular tissue | one central star-shaped group of xylem | eight separate oval vascular bundles in a ring |

| cortex | wide: about 60% of the radius | narrow band near the outer edge |
