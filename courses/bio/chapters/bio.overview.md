## What A Level Biology is

Biology is the science of living things, and A Level Biology follows life up a ladder of size: **molecules** (water, glucose, proteins, DNA) build **cells**; cells build **tissues, organs and organisms**; and organisms live together in **populations** and **ecosystems** and change over generations by **evolution**. Almost every topic asks the same three questions. What is the structure? How does that structure let it do its job? And what goes wrong when it fails?

The course rewards three habits:
- **Precise explanation.** You are marked on creditworthy ideas written in the correct scientific terms. "The enzyme and substrate fit" earns less than "the substrate has a shape complementary to the active site".
- **Data handling.** Most questions give you a graph, a table or an unfamiliar experiment. You describe it with figures and units, calculate from it, and explain it using what you know.
- **Practical thinking.** You must be able to plan an investigation, name variables, spot sources of error and judge the evidence. Two of the five papers test this directly.

Biology links to medicine and nursing (immunity, homeostasis, disease), to agriculture and food (photosynthesis, selection, genetic technology), to conservation and ecology, and to biotechnology (enzymes, DNA technology). It suits you if you like understanding how things work, can remember and use precise vocabulary, and are happy reading a graph. You do not need to be brilliant at mathematics: the maths is arithmetic, percentages, ratios, rates from graphs, and (at A Level) a few statistics tests whose formulae are printed in the question.

If you did little or no science before this, start with [Biology from zero](#bio.f.basics). It gives you the chemistry, the cell basics and the number skills every later chapter assumes.

## The exam papers

This guide follows the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology syllabus (9700) for exams in 2025, 2026 and 2027. There are five papers.

| Paper | Name | Time | Marks | AS weight | A Level weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multiple Choice | 1 h 15 min | 40 | 31% | 15.5% |
| 2 | AS Level Structured Questions | 1 h 15 min | 60 | 46% | 23% |
| 3 | Advanced Practical Skills | 2 h | 40 | 23% | 11.5% |
| 4 | A Level Structured Questions | 2 h | 100 | – | 38.5% |
| 5 | Planning, Analysis and Evaluation | 1 h 15 min | 30 | – | 11.5% |

What each paper contains:
- **Paper 1**: 40 four-option multiple-choice questions (A–D) on AS content.
- **Paper 2**: structured questions (several parts, short written answers and some calculations) on AS content.
- **Paper 3**: a laboratory practical on AS content and unfamiliar contexts, with two or three questions. Half the candidates start with the microscope work and half with the investigation.
- **Paper 4**: structured questions on A Level content. AS knowledge is required, so Paper 4 can ask about any topic from 1 to 19.
- **Paper 5**: a written paper on planning, analysis and evaluation, with no laboratory work. It can test AS and A Level content.

### Three routes

| Route | Papers | Result |
|---|---|---|
| AS Level only | 1, 2 and 3 in one exam series | AS grade a–e |
| A Level, staged | AS papers first, then Papers 4 and 5 in a later series (the AS result is carried forward) | A Level grade A*–E |
| A Level, one series | all five papers together | A Level grade A*–E |

AS grades are lower-case letters, a to e. A Level grades are capital letters, A* to E. AS Level is a stand-alone qualification covering topics 1–11, and also the first half of A Level. Exams are held in June and November (and March in India only). The syllabus estimates about 180 guided learning hours for AS and 360 for A Level.

## What the papers give you

:::key What is printed, and what is not
- **A calculator is allowed in every paper.** You should show all your working and use appropriate units.
- **There is no data booklet, no formula sheet and no periodic table in any paper.** You must remember every definition, equation and unit yourself. For the few equations you need (magnification, percentage change, rate), this course teaches them early and uses them often.
- **Statistics formulae (A Level only) are printed inside the question that needs them**, and a table of critical values is given when needed. You do not have to remember the formulae. **Degrees of freedom are never given**, so you must be able to work them out.
- **Paper 1 gives one mark per correct answer and takes nothing away for a wrong answer.** Never leave a multiple-choice question blank: a guess costs nothing.
- Papers 2 to 5 are answered on the question paper in black or dark blue pen, with HB pencil for diagrams and graphs. The marks for each part are shown in brackets, and they tell you how many separate points to make.
:::

## What the examiners test

Every question tests one or more **assessment objectives** (AOs).

| Objective | What it tests | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding: recall the facts, terms, definitions and processes | 40% |
| AO2 | Handling, applying and evaluating information: use data, apply ideas to new contexts, calculate | 40% |
| AO3 | Experimental skills and investigations: plan, carry out, analyse and evaluate practical work | 20% |

Papers 1, 2 and 4 are half AO1 and half AO2. Papers 3 and 5 are entirely AO3. The practical papers therefore reward skills that no amount of memorising can replace, so this course teaches them in their own chapters and uses them in every topic chapter.

:::tip Unfamiliar contexts are normal
The contexts in Papers 3 and 5 may be outside the syllabus content, and any new information you need is given in the question. The same is true of many AO2 questions in Papers 2 and 4: the organism or disease may be one you have never met. Do not panic. The biology underneath is always content you have learned. Read the stem, name the idea from the syllabus that applies, and then answer in mark-scheme language.
:::

## Study order and pace

The chapters are already in a sensible teaching order: follow the course from the top. Each topic chapter starts with a three-question warm-up taken from earlier chapters, so revision is built in.

:::steps
1. **Foundation.** Read [Biology from zero](#bio.f.basics) unless you have a strong GCSE or IGCSE in biology and chemistry. Even then, skim its units and command-word sections.
2. **Cells and molecules** (topics 1 to 4): microscopy, cells, the four groups of biological molecule, food tests, enzymes, membranes. Everything later uses these.
3. **Genetics, transport, infection** (topics 5 to 11): the cell cycle, DNA and protein synthesis, transport in plants and mammals, gas exchange, disease and immunity. This ends AS content.
4. **Work through the AS exam readiness chapters** if you are taking AS only: the three paper guides, the AS mixed practice and Mocks 1 to 3.
5. **A Level topics** (12 to 19) in the order given, then the Paper 4 and Paper 5 guides, mixed practice and Mocks 4 and 5.
:::

Three adjustments make a real difference:
- **Do the skills chapters alongside the AS topics, not at the end.** Work through [Maths skills for biology](#bio.maths) once you have finished the first few topic chapters, so you can use it on every later calculation. Read [Practical skills: microscopy and drawing (Paper 3)](#bio.practical2) after [Eukaryotic cells and organelles](#bio.cells), and [Practical skills: investigations (Paper 3)](#bio.practical) after [Movement into and out of cells](#bio.movement), by which point you have met the food tests, the enzyme investigations and the osmosis practicals it builds on.
- **Statistics early if you wish.** The chi-squared test is first taught in [Dihybrid crosses and the chi-squared test](#bio.dihybrid) and the t-test in [Variation and the t-test](#bio.variation). If you want the statistics in one place first, read [Statistics for biologists](#bio.stats) before those two chapters. The A Level skills chapters can be done in any order once you have reached topic 12.
- **Mix old and new.** Revisit a chapter's checklist a week after you finish it. Spacing your practice works far better than reading a chapter twice in one evening.

Pace depends on your hours. These are guides, not promises:

| Chapters per week | AS (24 topic + 3 skills chapters) | A Level extra (22 topic + 3 skills chapters) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | about 14 weeks | about 13 weeks |
| 3 | about 9 weeks | about 9 weeks |
| 4 | about 7 weeks | about 7 weeks |

Each chapter shows a realistic study time in its heading, so you can plan your own weeks. Leave extra time at the end for exam readiness: past papers and the mocks are where knowledge turns into marks.

## How to reach an A*

:::key Five habits of A* biology answers
1. **Write in mark-scheme language.** Most marks are single creditworthy points. Use the exact terms ("active site", "complementary shape", "water potential gradient", "partially permeable membrane") and build cause-and-effect chains: change, so effect, so result.
2. **Obey the command word.** "Describe" means say what happens (for a graph, the trend with figures and units). "Explain" means give the biological reason. "Suggest" means apply your knowledge to something unfamiliar. Count the marks: three marks means at least three separate points.
3. **Quote the data.** Use the numbers and the units from the graph or table, name the turning point, and do any calculation with working shown. "Increases" alone is never enough.
4. **Know the practical skills as lists.** Variables (independent, dependent, standardised), control, repeats and mean, sources of error, improvements and risk assessment appear in every practical question. Learn them as checklists and practise them.
5. **Practise with real past papers.** Use the chapters to learn, then answer real questions under time. Mark yourself against the mark scheme and write down every point you missed.
:::

:::exam Past papers are the best practice
Work through Physics & Maths Tutor's [Paper 1](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/a-level-biology/cie-paper-1/), [Paper 2](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/a-level-biology/cie-paper-2/), [Paper 3](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/a-level-biology/cie-paper-3/), [Paper 4](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/a-level-biology/cie-paper-4/) and [Paper 5](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/a-level-biology/cie-paper-5/) pages. These are unofficial copies, so check the official Cambridge site for the current version. Do a chapter's own questions first, then the matching past-paper questions, and keep a list of the marks you lost and why.
:::

:::warn Three habits that cost marks
- **Everyday words instead of scientific ones.** "Goes through", "the enzyme is killed", "the water moves to where there is more salt" are all marked wrong or vague. Each chapter has a warning box for the words to avoid.
- **Describing when asked to explain** (and the reverse). Read the command word before you start writing.
- **Ignoring the marks.** A one-mark question needs one clear point. A six-mark question needs six, and a few lines will not carry them.
:::

:::board Board difference you may meet
AQA A Level Biology (7402) assesses practical skills in its written papers, plus a separately reported practical endorsement, with no timed hands-on practical exam. Cambridge has a timed hands-on exam, Paper 3, which is why this guide teaches the laboratory practical skills in their own chapters.
:::

:::recap
- A Level Biology runs from molecules to cells to organisms to ecosystems. Marks reward precise explanation, data handling and practical thinking.
- There are five papers. AS = Papers 1 (40 marks, multiple choice), 2 (60 marks, structured) and 3 (40 marks, practical). A Level adds Paper 4 (100 marks, structured) and Paper 5 (30 marks, planning, analysis and evaluation).
- AS covers topics 1 to 11; A Level covers topics 1 to 19. AS grades are a to e; A Level grades are A* to E.
- A calculator is allowed in every paper. There is no data booklet or formula sheet. Statistics formulae are printed in the question; degrees of freedom are not given.
- Paper 1 gives one mark per question and no penalty for a wrong answer: answer every question.
- Assessment objectives: AO1 40%, AO2 40%, AO3 20%. Papers 3 and 5 are all AO3.
- Study in course order, do the skills chapters alongside the AS topics, and finish with past papers and mocks.
- A* answers use mark-scheme language, obey the command word, quote the data with units, and use the practical checklists.
:::
