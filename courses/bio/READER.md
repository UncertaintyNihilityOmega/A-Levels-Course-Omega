# READER.md: what each chapter teaches (course order)

## bio.overview
Taught: the five CAIE 9700 papers (times, marks, what each holds), AS against A Level and the three routes, grades (a–e, A*–E), what is and is not printed in the exams (calculator yes, no formula sheet, Paper 1 no negative marking), assessment objectives AO1/AO2/AO3, the topic and chapter map, study order, the five habits of A* answers, command words (describe, explain, suggest) in outline.
Assumed: "stem" (of a question), "synoptic", "creditworthy", "degrees of freedom" and "critical values" (named only), the variable words independent, dependent, standardised and control (named only).

## bio.f.basics
Taught: the seven characteristics of living things; levels of organisation (molecule to ecosystem); animal and plant cell parts in one page; atom, element, molecule, compound, the six main elements, formulae; covalent bond, ion, ionic bond; polar molecule, water polarity, hydrogen bond, hydrophilic and hydrophobic, solute and solvent; acid, alkali, pH (logarithmic); catalyst, enzyme (name only); monomer, polymer, condensation, hydrolysis, the four groups of large molecule; photosynthesis and respiration equations, ATP (name); diffusion, concentration gradient, surface area to volume ratio; mm, µm, nm and the 1000 ladder, SI prefixes, size order, standard form, significant figures; percentage and percentage change, mean, anomalous result; reading graphs, gradient, tangent, initial rate, mean rate; command words state, describe, explain, suggest.
Assumed: the exam mark tokens [B1], [M1], [A1], [B0] and the ★ ratings (used from the first worked example, never explained); kinetic energy; chlorophyll, cell sap, cellulose (named in the cell table); osmosis and partially permeable (used in the potato example, taught later).

## bio.microscopy
Taught: light microscope parts and use, total magnification = eyepiece × objective, field of view, temporary slide, stain, M = I/A (with the triangle), scale bars (ratio and magnification methods), eyepiece graticule and stage micrometer and calibration per objective, magnification against resolution, resolution limits (light about 200 nm, TEM about 0.5 nm), TEM against SEM, artefact, false colour, rules for biological drawings.
Assumed: "photomicrograph" and "micrograph" (used without a definition); "vesicle" (practice question); "epidermis", "section" (thin section); "cilia" (SEM bullet); the meaning of "evaluate".

## bio.cells
Taught: eukaryotic cell, organelle, cell surface membrane (partially permeable), nucleus (envelope, pores, chromatin, nucleolus), ribosomes (80S and 70S, S value), rough and smooth ER, Golgi body, lysosome, secretory pathway and exocytosis, mitochondrion (cristae, matrix), chloroplast (grana, thylakoids, stroma), microtubule, centriole, cilium, microvillus, cell wall, plasmodesma, large permanent vacuole and tonoplast, plant against animal cell comparison, ATP as the energy currency, reading electron micrographs.
Assumed: "vesicle" (used in the RER definition before it is explained); "spindle" and "cytoskeleton"; "assimilates"; "protoctists"; "glycogen"; "active transport" (named in the ATP box and in a practice question); "genes" (nuclear pore sentence); turgid; "linear" DNA (stretch answer); binary fission (stretch answer).

## bio.prokaryotes
Taught: prokaryotic cell and the structure of a typical bacterium (peptidoglycan wall, circular DNA, nucleoid, 70S ribosomes, optional plasmid, capsule, flagellum, pili), bacterium against animal and plant cell, size of bacteria, virus (nucleic acid core, capsid, optional envelope, non-cellular), why antibiotics do not act on viruses.
Assumed: "histone proteins"; "metabolism" (used to say viruses have none); "gene" (plasmid genes); "mutate" and "evolve"; bursting by osmosis (penicillin example).

## bio.carbs
Taught: monosaccharide, disaccharide, polysaccharide, macromolecule, (CH2O)n, triose, pentose, hexose; α and β glucose rings and how to draw them; glycosidic bond (1,4 and 1,6) by condensation, n units give n − 1 bonds; maltose, sucrose, lactose; reducing and non-reducing sugars and the acid-hydrolysis test; amylose, amylopectin, glycogen and why they suit storage; cellulose (β-glucose, straight chains, hydrogen bonds between chains, microfibrils); Mr calculations from condensation.
Assumed: "water potential", "osmotic effect" and "osmosis" (used in the key box and the spot-the-error answer); "active site", "complementary", "specific", "enzyme–substrate complex" (cow, pea seed and lactase examples); "substrate" (glucose as the substrate for respiration); "turgor pressure"; "tensile strength"; "relative molecular mass".

## bio.lipids
Taught: water's hydrogen bonding, solvent action, high specific heat capacity, high latent heat of vaporisation and their roles; glycerol, fatty acid, saturated and unsaturated (C=C, kinks, melting point), triglyceride by three condensations with ester bonds, not a polymer; functions (energy store 39 against 16 kJ g⁻¹, insulation, buoyancy, protection, metabolic water); phospholipid (hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails) and the bilayer.
Assumed: "no osmotic effect" and "water potential" (functions table, stretch answer); "denatured" (leaf question); "hydrocarbon" and "carboxyl group" (used in the definition of a fatty acid, explained in the next lines); "enzymes ... optimum".

## bio.proteins
Taught: amino acid (amine, carboxyl, H, R group; 20 types), peptide bond by condensation, polypeptide with n − 1 bonds; primary, secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet), tertiary and quaternary structure and the bonds that hold each (peptide, hydrogen, hydrophobic interactions, ionic, disulfide); globular against fibrous; haemoglobin (2 α, 2 β, four haem groups with Fe²⁺, binds O2 reversibly); collagen (triple helix, glycine every third, cross-links, fibrils, fibres, tensile strength, not elastic).
Assumed: "active site", "substrate", "complementary", "enzyme–substrate complex" (real-context example and exam question); "backbone" (used for secondary structure without a definition); "gene" and "a change in a gene" (primary-structure definition, sickle cell, stretch); "denatured" (named, explained later in enzymes); "physiological"; "keratin" and "reducing chemical" (stretch).

## bio.foodtests
Taught: reagent, positive and negative result, qualitative against semi-quantitative, negative and positive controls; Benedict's test (heat, water bath, blue to green/yellow/orange/brick-red ladder, "reducing sugar" not "glucose"); standardise, colour standards, time to first colour change, serial dilution (halving, 5 cm³ into 10 cm³), reading an unknown from a graph, rate = 1/t; non-reducing sugar test (negative Benedict's, boil fresh sample with dilute HCl, neutralise with sodium hydrogencarbonate, retest); iodine test (amylose helix, blue-black); emulsion test (ethanol then water, cloudy white); biuret test (peptide bonds, blue to purple, no heat); identifying unknowns from a results table; hazard, risk (low/medium/high), precaution.
Assumed: "colorimeter", "absorbance" and "filter" (used in the Improvements paragraph and two ★★★ questions, taught only in Enzymes); "carbonyl group" (the test table); "substrate", "product", "amylase", "protease", "denatured" (the ★★★ questions, taught in Enzymes); "serial dilution" (named in lab step 1 before the worked example); "glucose test strips".

## bio.enzymes
Taught: catalyst, enzyme (globular protein), active site, substrate, enzyme–substrate complex, complementary (not "same"), specificity, intracellular against extracellular enzymes, lock-and-key and induced fit (and how to draw them), activation energy and how an enzyme lowers it, rate of reaction, progress curve, initial rate from a tangent, mean rate, rate = 1/t for a time, catalase gas-syringe method, amylase and iodine end-point method, colorimeter (filter, blank, calibration curve, absorbance), optimum temperature and denaturation, optimum pH and buffer solution, effect of enzyme, substrate (saturation, Vmax) and inhibitor concentration, limiting factor, Q10 (defined in the stretch), describe against explain.
Assumed: "arbitrary units" (graph axes and tables); "limiting factor" (used in the enzyme-concentration section, explained only in the tip after it); "complementary colour" (colorimeter filter step); "buffer" (named in the catalase Variables line before it is defined in the pH section); "pepsin", "lipase", "lysozyme", "polymerase chain reaction" (named); the ionising of -COO⁻ and -NH₃⁺ groups on R groups.

## bio.enzymes2
Taught: Vmax, Michaelis–Menten constant Km (a concentration, half of Vmax read across then down), affinity (low Km = high affinity), reading Vmax and Km from a graph or table, reversible against irreversible inhibitors, competitive inhibitor (active site, Vmax same, Km up, more substrate helps), non-competitive inhibitor (allosteric site, Vmax down, Km same), telling them apart from graphs and tables, end-product inhibition, immobilised enzyme, alginate beads (sodium alginate dripped into calcium chloride), advantages and disadvantages of immobilised enzymes, glucose oxidase test strips for glucose (not Benedict's, since lactose is reducing).
Assumed: "[S]" as the symbol for substrate concentration (table headings from the first worked table on); "metabolic pathway" (end-product inhibition note); "allosteric site" (named only); "µmol" and "mmol dm⁻³" units; how a glucose test strip is read.

## bio.membranes
Taught: five jobs of a membrane, partially permeable, phospholipid bilayer and self-assembly, 7 nm thickness, why ions and polar molecules cannot cross and small non-polar molecules can, fluid mosaic model (fluid, mosaic), intrinsic and extrinsic proteins, transmembrane protein, leaflet, cholesterol (regulates fluidity both ways, reduces permeability), glycolipid, glycoprotein, glycocalyx, channel against carrier protein, receptor against carrier, ligand, cell surface receptor, four stages of cell signalling (secrete, transport, bind, respond), target cell, beetroot permeability practical (betalain, colorimeter green filter, temperature and ethanol), describe-then-explain with figures.
Assumed: "tissue fluid" (first bilayer section; the fluid that surrounds cells); "antigen", "self/non-self", "immune system" and "ABO blood group" (glycolipid and glycoprotein section); "cytoskeleton" (protein table); "passive", "facilitated diffusion", "down a concentration gradient" and "ATP" for transport (channel against carrier columns, taught in the next chapter); "hormone" (first meets it in the signalling section as an example); "protease" and "denatured" (earlier chapter).

## bio.movement
Taught: passive against active against bulk transport, the six processes, diffusion (net, random, dynamic equilibrium), concentration gradient, factors affecting the rate of diffusion, facilitated diffusion (channel, carrier, gated, maximum rate), surface area to volume ratio (cube 6/L, cylinder, cuboid, sphere in a stretch question), why large organisms need exchange surfaces and transport systems, agar-block practical, water potential (kPa, psi, pure water 0, solutions negative), osmosis, aquaporins, turgid, flaccid, incipient plasmolysis, plasmolysis, crenation, lysis, percentage change in mass and the x-intercept method, Visking tubing, active transport (carrier, ATP hydrolysed, sodium–potassium pump 3 out 2 in), how to recognise active transport in data (oxygen, cyanide, temperature), endocytosis (phagocytosis, pinocytosis), exocytosis, vesicle, clue words for naming the process.
Assumed: "mol dm⁻³" and how to dilute from a stock (potato lab step 1); "aerobic respiration" and "respiratory inhibitor" (active transport data section and the ★★★ root question); "aquaporins" (used in the six-process table before the water potential section); "steroid hormones" (list of lipid-soluble molecules); "macrophage", "neutrophil" (named in the phagocytosis bullet; the macrophage is glossed only in the exam question); "epidermis" (onion strip question); how to look at onion cells under the microscope; where the table of water potentials in the potato example comes from.

## bio.mitosis
Taught: chromatin and chromosome, histone (positively charged), sister chromatids, centromere, telomere, the counting rule (chromosomes, chromatids, DNA molecules at each stage), mitosis (genetically identical daughter nuclei), its uses (growth, replacement, repair, asexual reproduction/clones), mitotic cell cycle (interphase G1, S, G2; mitosis; cytokinesis), DNA content graph of a cycle, the four stages and what happens to chromosomes, nuclear envelope, spindle and membrane, spindle, equator and poles, cleavage furrow against cell plate, plant cells have no centrioles, identifying stages from drawings and slides, root tip squash, mitotic index, time in a stage, telomere shortening, stem cell, differentiation (in the stem-cell definition), mutation, uncontrolled division and tumour, benign and malignant, metastasis, radiotherapy and chemotherapy.
Assumed: "gene", "base" and "base sequence" (the mitosis definition, "Telomere" definition, telomere data question); "specialised cell" and "differentiate" (used in the Growth bullet and the opening; defined in the stem-cell section); why the copying machinery cannot reach the end of a chromosome ("DNA polymerase works in only one direction": pointed to the next chapter); "lymph" and "carcinogen" (note on tumours); the negative charge of DNA's phosphate groups; "kb" (given in the question).

## bio.dna
Taught: nucleic acid, nucleotide (pentose sugar, phosphate, nitrogenous base), deoxyribose against ribose, the numbering 1′ to 5′, bases A, G, C, T, U, purine against pyrimidine, ATP as a phosphorylated nucleotide (ADP), phosphodiester bond (5′ phosphate to 3′ OH, condensation, n − 1 bonds), polynucleotide, sugar–phosphate backbone, 5′ and 3′ ends, DNA double helix, complementary base pairing (A–T two hydrogen bonds, C–G three), antiparallel, writing the complementary strand with ends labelled, base ratios and hydrogen-bond counts, mRNA (single strand, ribose, uracil, codon named), comparison of DNA and mRNA, semi-conservative replication, template, replication fork, DNA polymerase (adds to 3′ end), leading and lagging strand, DNA ligase, Okazaki fragments (name only), Meselson and Stahl (heavy and light nitrogen, density bands, generation 1, 2, 3 predictions).
Assumed: "gene" (mRNA definition, the opening of the mRNA section); "codon" and "amino acid sequence" (mRNA bullets, taught in the next chapter); "genome" (the ★★★ viral drug question); "centrifuge" and "density" (the Meselson–Stahl steps, partly explained in a sentence); why the copying machinery cannot copy the very end of a chromosome (promised by bio.mitosis, not delivered here); why a polymerase moving with the fork reads the template 3′ to 5′.

## bio.synthesis
Taught: gene (nucleotide sequence coding for a polypeptide), why a triplet code (4, 16, 64), genetic code (triplet, degenerate, non-overlapping, universal, start AUG, stop UAA UAG UGA), codon, anticodon, reading a printed codon table, template (transcribed) and non-transcribed strand, transcription (RNA polymerase, A to U, 5′ to 3′), RNA polymerase against DNA polymerase, primary transcript, exon, intron, splicing, calculating transcript, mRNA and polypeptide lengths, translation, tRNA (clover-leaf, anticodon, one amino acid), ribosome holds two tRNAs, peptide bond, stop codon releases, template triplet → codon → anticodon → amino acid, gene mutation, substitution (silent, missense, nonsense), insertion and deletion, reading frame, frameshift, 3-base indels keep the frame, harmful/neutral/beneficial.
Assumed: "gametes", "inherited" and "offspring" (the sentence on mutations in body cells against gamete-forming cells); "frameshift" and "reading frame" (used in the substitution worked example before the section that defines them); "primary transcript" for prokaryotes (only the eukaryote case is explained); how RNA polymerase finds the start of a gene (not needed); "haemoglobin molecules interact" (sickle cell example).

## bio.planttransport
Taught: why large plants need transport systems, xylem (water and mineral ions) against phloem (sucrose and amino acids), vascular bundle, herbaceous dicotyledon, transverse section, plan diagram and its rules, where xylem, phloem, cambium, cortex, pith, pericycle and endodermis lie in a stem, root and leaf, magnification of a drawing, xylem vessel element (dead, lignin, pits, no end walls), sieve tube element and companion cell, root hair cell, apoplast and symplast pathways, endodermis and Casparian strip (suberin), transpiration (evaporation from mesophyll walls, diffusion out of stomata), cohesion, adhesion, tension, cohesion-tension theory, factors affecting transpiration, potometer and V = πr²d, xerophyte adaptations (rolled leaf, hairs, sunken stomata, cuticle, fewer stomata, spines, water storage), annotated drawings.
Assumed: "lumen", "TS", "mesophyll" (palisade and spongy), "epidermis", "cortex", "pith", "pericycle" and "stoma/stomata/guard cells" in the plan-diagram sections (before the transpiration section defines the stomata); "tension (negative pressure)" with no link to water potential; "loading sucrose" and "sources" in the phloem passages (explained in the next chapter); "turgor" (movement); "aerobic respiration" (the ★★★ root question).
