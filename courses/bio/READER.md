# READER.md: what each chapter teaches (course order)

## bio.overview
Taught: the five CAIE 9700 papers (times, marks, what each holds), AS against A Level and the three routes, grades (a–e, A*–E), what is and is not printed in the exams (calculator yes, no formula sheet, Paper 1 no negative marking), assessment objectives AO1/AO2/AO3, the topic and chapter map, study order, the five habits of A* answers, command words (describe, explain, suggest) in outline.
Assumed: "stem" (of a question), "synoptic", "creditworthy", "degrees of freedom" and "critical values" (named only), the variable words independent, dependent, standardised and control (named only).

## bio.f.basics
Taught: the seven characteristics of living things; levels of organisation (molecule to ecosystem); animal and plant cell parts in one page; atom, element, molecule, compound, the six main elements, formulae; covalent bond, ion, ionic bond; polar molecule, water polarity, hydrogen bond, hydrophilic and hydrophobic, solute and solvent; acid, alkali, pH (logarithmic); catalyst, enzyme (name only); monomer, polymer, condensation, hydrolysis, the four groups of large molecule; photosynthesis and respiration equations, ATP (name); diffusion, concentration gradient, surface area to volume ratio; mm, µm, nm and the 1000 ladder, SI prefixes, size order, standard form, significant figures; percentage and percentage change, mean, anomalous result; reading graphs, gradient, tangent, initial rate, mean rate; command words state, describe, explain, suggest.
Assumed: the exam mark tokens [B1], [M1], [A1], [B0] and the ★ ratings (used from the first worked example, never explained); kinetic energy; chlorophyll, cell sap, cellulose (named in the cell table); osmosis and partially permeable (used in the potato example, taught later).

## bio.microscopy
Taught: light microscope parts and use, total magnification = eyepiece × objective, field of view, temporary slide, stain, M = I/A (with the triangle), scale bars (ratio and magnification methods), eyepiece graticule and stage micrometer and calibration per objective, magnification against resolution, resolution limits (light about 200 nm, TEM about 0.5 nm), TEM against SEM, artefact, false colour, rules for biological drawings.
Assumed: "photomicrograph" and "micrograph" (used without a definition); "vesicle" (practice question); "epidermis", "section" (thin section); "cilia" (SEM bullet); the meaning of "evaluate".

## bio.cells
Taught: eukaryotic cell, organelle, cell surface membrane (partially permeable), nucleus (envelope, pores, chromatin, nucleolus), ribosomes (80S and 70S, S value), rough and smooth ER, Golgi body, lysosome, secretory pathway and exocytosis, mitochondrion (cristae, matrix), chloroplast (grana, thylakoids, stroma), microtubule, centriole, cilium, microvillus, cell wall, plasmodesma, large permanent vacuole and tonoplast, plant against animal cell comparison, ATP as the energy currency, reading electron micrographs.
Assumed: "vesicle" (used in the RER definition before it is explained); "spindle" and "cytoskeleton"; "assimilates"; "protoctists"; "glycogen"; "active transport" (named in the ATP box and in a practice question); "genes" (nuclear pore sentence); turgid; "linear" DNA (stretch answer); binary fission (stretch answer).

## bio.prokaryotes
Taught: prokaryotic cell and the structure of a typical bacterium (peptidoglycan wall, circular DNA, nucleoid, 70S ribosomes, optional plasmid, capsule, flagellum, pili), bacterium against animal and plant cell, size of bacteria, virus (nucleic acid core, capsid, optional envelope, non-cellular), why antibiotics do not act on viruses.
Assumed: "histone proteins"; "metabolism" (used to say viruses have none); "gene" (plasmid genes); "mutate" and "evolve"; bursting by osmosis (penicillin example).

## bio.carbs
Taught: monosaccharide, disaccharide, polysaccharide, macromolecule, (CH2O)n, triose, pentose, hexose; α and β glucose rings and how to draw them; glycosidic bond (1,4 and 1,6) by condensation, n units give n − 1 bonds; maltose, sucrose, lactose; reducing and non-reducing sugars and the acid-hydrolysis test; amylose, amylopectin, glycogen and why they suit storage; cellulose (β-glucose, straight chains, hydrogen bonds between chains, microfibrils); Mr calculations from condensation.
Assumed: "water potential", "osmotic effect" and "osmosis" (used in the key box and the spot-the-error answer); "active site", "complementary", "specific", "enzyme–substrate complex" (cow, pea seed and lactase examples); "substrate" (glucose as the substrate for respiration); "turgor pressure"; "tensile strength"; "relative molecular mass".

## bio.lipids
Taught: water's hydrogen bonding, solvent action, high specific heat capacity, high latent heat of vaporisation and their roles; glycerol, fatty acid, saturated and unsaturated (C=C, kinks, melting point), triglyceride by three condensations with ester bonds, not a polymer; functions (energy store 39 against 16 kJ g⁻¹, insulation, buoyancy, protection, metabolic water); phospholipid (hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails) and the bilayer.
Assumed: "no osmotic effect" and "water potential" (functions table, stretch answer); "denatured" (leaf question); "hydrocarbon" and "carboxyl group" (used in the definition of a fatty acid, explained in the next lines); "enzymes ... optimum".

## bio.proteins
Taught: amino acid (amine, carboxyl, H, R group; 20 types), peptide bond by condensation, polypeptide with n − 1 bonds; primary, secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet), tertiary and quaternary structure and the bonds that hold each (peptide, hydrogen, hydrophobic interactions, ionic, disulfide); globular against fibrous; haemoglobin (2 α, 2 β, four haem groups with Fe²⁺, binds O2 reversibly); collagen (triple helix, glycine every third, cross-links, fibrils, fibres, tensile strength, not elastic).
Assumed: "active site", "substrate", "complementary", "enzyme–substrate complex" (real-context example and exam question); "backbone" (used for secondary structure without a definition); "gene" and "a change in a gene" (primary-structure definition, sickle cell, stretch); "denatured" (named, explained later in enzymes); "physiological"; "keratin" and "reducing chemical" (stretch).
