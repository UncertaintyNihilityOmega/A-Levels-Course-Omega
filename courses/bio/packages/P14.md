## bio.nerves: Neurones and action potentials (`@time 5–6 hours`)
Syllabus 15.1.3–15.1.8. Candidates should be able to:
3. describe the structure and function of a sensory neurone and a motor neurone and state that intermediate neurones
   connect sensory neurones and motor neurones
4. outline the role of sensory receptor cells in detecting stimuli and stimulating the transmission of impulses in
   sensory neurones
5. describe the sequence of events that results in an action potential in a sensory neurone, using a chemoreceptor
   cell in a human taste bud as an example
6. describe and explain changes to the membrane potential of neurones, including: how the resting potential is
   maintained; the events that occur during an action potential; how the resting potential is restored during the
   refractory period
7. describe and explain the rapid transmission of an impulse in a myelinated neurone with reference to saltatory
   conduction
8. explain the importance of the refractory period in determining the frequency of impulses
Cover: reflex arc as the frame (receptor → sensory → intermediate (relay) → motor → effector); neurone structures
(cell body, dendrites, axon, myelin sheath of Schwann cells, nodes of Ranvier, axon terminals); sensory v motor (cell
body position, long dendron v long axon); receptors as transducers; taste bud chemoreceptor: e.g. salt (Na⁺ diffuses
in through channels → depolarisation → receptor potential → if threshold reached, neurotransmitter released → action
potential in the sensory neurone); resting potential about −70 mV: Na⁺/K⁺ pump 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, membrane more
permeable to K⁺ (K⁺ leaks out), large anions inside; action potential: stimulus opens some voltage-gated Na⁺ channels,
threshold (about −50 mV: state "about"), many Na⁺ channels open, Na⁺ floods in (depolarisation to about +30 to +40
mV), Na⁺ channels close, voltage-gated K⁺ channels open, K⁺ out (repolarisation), overshoot (hyperpolarisation), K⁺
channels close, pump restores; refractory period (Na⁺ channels cannot reopen) → one-way transmission, discrete
impulses, maximum frequency; all-or-nothing; stimulus strength coded as frequency; local circuits; saltatory
conduction (myelin insulates, depolarisation only at nodes, impulse jumps, faster; also larger diameter faster);
calculate conduction speed (distance ÷ time) and maximum frequency (1 ÷ refractory period).
Pitfalls: "sodium pump causes the action potential"; "the impulse is a flow of electrons"; "bigger stimulus → bigger
action potential"; "myelin conducts electricity"; potassium "pumped out" during repolarisation (it diffuses out through
channels).
Examples: label phases on an action potential graph and explain each (exam 6 marks); speed calculation; maximum
impulse frequency from a refractory period of e.g. 2 ms (500 Hz); explain why myelinated neurones conduct faster (4
marks); real context: multiple sclerosis damages myelin (qualitative).
Figures: motor and sensory neurones (fig, viewBox ~600, two rows); resting membrane with pump and channels (fig
small); action potential graph (SVG schematic with phases labelled, data/fig_ap.py; not `:::plot`).
Widget **bio-actionpot** after the action potential section.
Links: PhET neuron (explore); PMT control-and-coordination; OpenStax ch 35.

## bio.synapses: Synapses and muscle contraction (`@time 5–6 hours`)
Syllabus 15.1.9–15.1.12. Candidates should be able to:
9. describe the structure of a cholinergic synapse and explain how it functions, including the role of calcium ions
10. describe the roles of neuromuscular junctions, the T-tubule system and sarcoplasmic reticulum in stimulating
   contraction in striated muscle
11. describe the ultrastructure of striated muscle with reference to sarcomere structure using electron micrographs and
   diagrams
12. explain the sliding filament model of muscular contraction including the roles of troponin, tropomyosin, calcium
   ions and ATP
Cover: synapse structure (presynaptic knob with mitochondria and vesicles of acetylcholine, synaptic cleft about 20 nm,
postsynaptic membrane with receptor proteins/ligand-gated Na⁺ channels); sequence: action potential arrives →
voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ diffuses in → vesicles fuse with presynaptic membrane, exocytosis of ACh →
ACh diffuses across cleft → binds receptors → Na⁺ channels open → depolarisation → if threshold reached, action
potential; acetylcholinesterase hydrolyses ACh (choline recycled, ATP used to resynthesise ACh); roles of synapses
(one-way transmission, integration, summation as a `:::note`); neuromuscular junction (motor end plate; one action
potential always enough); muscle fibre structure (sarcolemma, T-tubules, sarcoplasmic reticulum storing Ca²⁺, many
mitochondria, myofibrils, multinucleate); sarcomere (Z lines, I band (actin only), A band (myosin, overlap), H zone
(myosin only), M line); during contraction: I band and H zone shorten, A band stays the same, sarcomere shortens;
sliding filament: action potential along sarcolemma and down T-tubules → Ca²⁺ released from SR → Ca²⁺ binds troponin
→ troponin changes shape and moves tropomyosin off the myosin-binding sites on actin → myosin heads bind (cross
bridges) → power stroke (ADP + Pi released, head tilts, actin pulled towards M line) → ATP binds, head detaches → ATP
hydrolysed, head recocks → repeat; relaxation: Ca²⁺ pumped back into SR (active transport, ATP).
Pitfalls: "myosin filaments shorten"; "A band shortens"; "ATP is needed for the power stroke itself" (ATP detaches
the head; hydrolysis recocks it); "calcium binds tropomyosin"; "acetylcholine enters the postsynaptic neurone".
Examples: describe synaptic transmission (exam 6 marks); predict effects of a drug that inhibits
acetylcholinesterase or blocks receptors (data in the stem; real context: organophosphate insecticides inhibit
acetylcholinesterase, qualitative); band length changes from measurements (calculate sarcomere length change);
explain why rigor mortis happens (no ATP to detach heads) as stretch.
Figures: cholinergic synapse (fig, viewBox ~560, data/fig_synapse.py); muscle fibre with T-tubules and SR (fig small);
sarcomere relaxed and contracted with band labels (fig, viewBox ~600); cross-bridge cycle (fig, four panels).
Widget **bio-sarcomere** after the sarcomere section.

## Widget spec: bio-actionpot
Name `bio-actionpot`, file `widgets/bio-actionpot.js`. Attributes: `data-mode="single|train"` (default single).
- `single`: model membrane potential v time (0–5 ms): resting −70 mV, threshold −50 mV (say "about" in the note, as the chapter does),
  peak +35 mV, hyperpolarisation to about −80 mV, back to −70 mV. A `W.stepper` (Back / Next) walks five stages
  (resting, depolarisation, repolarisation, hyperpolarisation, restoring); for each, the trace is drawn up to that
  point and a membrane diagram under the graph shows which channels are open (Na⁺ voltage-gated, K⁺ voltage-gated, the
  Na⁺/K⁺ pump) with arrows of ion movement and one-sentence caption. Stimulus slider: below threshold gives only a small
  local depolarisation that decays (all-or-nothing).
- `train`: stimulus strength slider (weak to strong) → action potentials of the same height at increasing frequency,
  capped by the refractory period (model refractory period 2 ms → maximum 500 per second). Readout: impulses per
  second.
- Survive zero size; channel symbols labelled with text, not colour.

## Widget spec: bio-sarcomere
Name `bio-sarcomere`, file `widgets/bio-sarcomere.js`. Attributes: `data-len="2.5"` (µm).
- Canvas: one sarcomere between two Z lines; thin filaments (actin, 1.0 µm each from each Z line) and thick filaments
  (myosin, 1.6 µm, centred on the M line); bands I, A, H and the M line labelled with brackets above.
- Slider: sarcomere length 1.8–3.4 µm (contract/relax). Readout: lengths of sarcomere, A band (constant 1.6 µm), I band
  (sarcomere − A band), H zone (max(0, sarcomere − 2 × actin length)), and the overlap. These geometric values follow
  from the stated model filament lengths: say so in the note ("model filament lengths").
- Checkbox "show calcium": draws tropomyosin covering binding sites when off, uncovered with cross bridges when on;
  contraction slider only moves below the relaxed length when calcium is on (and ATP: a second checkbox; with calcium
  on but ATP off, heads stay attached: "rigor").
- Survive zero size; fits at 375 px.
