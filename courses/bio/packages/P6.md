## bio.mitosis: Chromosomes and the mitotic cell cycle (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Syllabus 5.1 and 5.2. Candidates should be able to:
5.1.1 describe the structure of a chromosome, limited to: DNA; histone proteins; sister chromatids; centromere;
  telomeres
5.1.2 explain the importance of mitosis in the production of genetically identical daughter cells during: growth of
  multicellular organisms; replacement of damaged or dead cells; repair of tissues by cell replacement; asexual
  reproduction
5.1.3 outline the mitotic cell cycle, including: interphase (growth in G1 and G2 phases and DNA replication in S
  phase); mitosis; cytokinesis
5.1.4 outline the role of telomeres in preventing the loss of genes from the ends of chromosomes during DNA
  replication
5.1.5 outline the role of stem cells in cell replacement and tissue repair by mitosis
5.1.6 explain how uncontrolled cell division can result in the formation of a tumour
5.2.1 describe the behaviour of chromosomes in plant and animal cells during the mitotic cell cycle and the associated
  behaviour of the nuclear envelope, the cell surface membrane and the spindle (names of the main stages of mitosis
  are expected: prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase)
5.2.2 interpret photomicrographs, diagrams and microscope slides of cells in different stages of the mitotic cell
  cycle and identify the main stages of mitosis
Cover: chromosome structure (DNA wound round histones, condensed in division; two sister chromatids joined at the
centromere after replication; telomeres = repeated non-coding sequences at the ends); the cycle with G1, S, G2,
mitosis, cytokinesis and what happens to DNA mass (graph of DNA content per cell: a `:::plot` step graph or SVG);
stages with behaviour of chromosomes, nuclear envelope, spindle (centrioles in animal cells; plant cells have no
centrioles; cell plate in plants v cleavage furrow in animals); identifying stages from drawings; mitotic index
(number of cells in mitosis ÷ total cells, as a Paper 3 skill: count from a field of view); telomeres shorten at
each replication because DNA polymerase cannot copy the very end of the lagging strand: keep to "the end of the
chromosome is not fully copied each time; telomeres are lost instead of genes"; stem cells (unspecialised,
can divide by mitosis and differentiate; replace cells, e.g. bone marrow → blood cells, skin); cancer: mutations in
genes controlling division, uncontrolled mitosis, tumour (benign v malignant in a `:::note`, metastasis one line).
Pitfalls: chromatid v chromosome counts (46 chromosomes in human G2 still = 46 chromosomes, 92 chromatids); "DNA
replicates in prophase"; "spindle fibres pull chromosomes in metaphase" (they line up in metaphase, separate in
anaphase); "cytokinesis is part of mitosis"; plant cells "pinch in".
Examples: identify stages from five drawings; calculate a mitotic index and the time spent in each stage when the
cycle time is known (proportion of cells × cycle time); exam 6 marks: describe behaviour of chromosomes from
prophase to telophase; real context: chemotherapy drugs that disrupt spindle formation (one paragraph, qualitative).
Figures: chromosome structure (fig small); the cell cycle circle (fig small); four stages (fig, viewBox ~600, four
panels, drawn in data/fig_mitosis.py).
Widget **bio-mitosis** after the stages section.
Links: PMT mitotic-cell-cycle; SME 5-the-mitotic-cell-cycle; OpenStax ch 10; Amoeba Sisters.

## bio.dna: Nucleic acids and DNA replication (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Syllabus 6.1. Candidates should be able to:
1. describe the structure of nucleotides, including the phosphorylated nucleotide ATP (structural formulae are not
   expected)
2. state that the bases adenine and guanine are purines with a double ring structure, and that the bases cytosine,
   thymine and uracil are pyrimidines with a single ring structure (structural formulae for bases are not expected)
3. describe the structure of a DNA molecule as a double helix, including: the importance of complementary base pairing
   between the 5′ to 3′ strand and the 3′ to 5′ strand (antiparallel strands); differences in hydrogen bonding between
   C–G and A–T base pairs; linking of nucleotides by phosphodiester bonds
4. describe the semi-conservative replication of DNA during the S phase of the cell cycle, including: the roles of DNA
   polymerase and DNA ligase (knowledge of other enzymes in DNA replication in cells and different types of DNA
   polymerase is not expected); the differences between leading strand and lagging strand replication as a
   consequence of DNA polymerase adding nucleotides only in a 5′ to 3′ direction
5. describe the structure of an RNA molecule, using the example of messenger RNA (mRNA)
Cover: nucleotide = pentose + phosphate + base; deoxyribose v ribose; ATP = adenine + ribose + 3 phosphates;
purines and pyrimidines; condensation forms phosphodiester bonds (sugar–phosphate backbone); 5′ and 3′ ends
(carbon numbers of the sugar); antiparallel strands; A–T two hydrogen bonds, C–G three; purine always pairs with a
pyrimidine so the helix has a constant width; base ratios (Chargaff-style data: if A = 30%, T = 30%, C = G = 20%)
worked; semi-conservative replication: hydrogen bonds broken (say "the double helix unwinds and the strands
separate"; helicase is not required: you may name it in a `:::note` as extra), free nucleotides pair, DNA polymerase
joins nucleotides in the 5′ → 3′ direction, leading strand continuous, lagging strand in fragments joined by DNA
ligase; Meselson–Stahl evidence as a data question (density bands after 1 and 2 generations); mRNA single strand,
ribose, uracil, shorter, codons.
Pitfalls: "hydrogen bonds join nucleotides in one strand"; "DNA polymerase unzips DNA"; "uracil pairs with thymine";
"ligase joins the two strands"; mixing 3′/5′ direction; "semi-conservative = half the molecule is new and half old
on each strand" (each new molecule has one old strand and one new strand).
Examples: base percentages; predict Meselson–Stahl bands; exam 5 marks: explain why the lagging strand is made in
fragments; compare DNA and mRNA table.
Figures: nucleotide (fig small); DNA ladder with antiparallel labels and hydrogen bond counts (fig); replication fork
with leading and lagging strands (fig, coordinates in data/fig_dna.py).
Links: Learn.Genetics basics; PMT nucleic-acids-protein-synthesis; OpenStax ch 14.

## bio.synthesis: Protein synthesis and gene mutation (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Syllabus 6.2. Candidates should be able to:
1. state that a polypeptide is coded for by a gene and that a gene is a sequence of nucleotides that forms part of a
   DNA molecule
2. describe the principle of the universal genetic code in which different triplets of DNA bases either code for
   specific amino acids or correspond to start and stop codons
3. describe how the information in DNA is used during transcription and translation to construct polypeptides,
   including the roles of: RNA polymerase; messenger RNA (mRNA); codons; transfer RNA (tRNA); anticodons; ribosomes
4. state that the strand of a DNA molecule that is used in transcription is called the transcribed or template strand
   and that the other strand is called the non-transcribed strand
5. explain that, in eukaryotes, the RNA molecule formed following transcription (primary transcript) is modified by the
   removal of non-coding sequences (introns) and the joining together of coding sequences (exons) to form mRNA
6. state that a gene mutation is a change in the sequence of base pairs in a DNA molecule that may result in an
   altered polypeptide
7. explain that a gene mutation is a result of substitution or deletion or insertion of nucleotides in DNA and
   outline how each of these types of mutation may affect the polypeptide produced
Cover: gene → polypeptide; the code: triplet, degenerate (several triplets per amino acid), non-overlapping,
universal, start codon AUG (methionine), stop codons UAA, UAG, UGA (mRNA); give a small code table extract in the
question when an exam would (the exam always provides it); transcription (RNA polymerase, template strand,
complementary RNA nucleotides with U opposite A, mRNA leaves via nuclear pores); splicing (introns removed, exons
joined); translation (ribosome, tRNA with anticodon and amino acid, codon–anticodon pairing, peptide bonds formed,
ribosome moves along, stop codon); mutations: substitution (may be silent because of degeneracy, missense, or
nonsense = stop), insertion and deletion cause frameshift (all codons after change), effect on polypeptide and
protein function; sickle cell anaemia as the substitution example only at AS level of detail (one base change → one
amino acid change → different R group; the HBB detail is A Level in bio.genecontrol).
Pitfalls: "the code is the amino acid sequence"; anticodons on mRNA; "tRNA carries codons"; transcribing T into
mRNA; deleting three bases "always" frameshifts (it removes one amino acid and keeps the frame); "mutation always
harmful".
Examples: transcribe and translate a short sequence using a given code table (always give the table in the stem, as
the exam does); find the effect of a substitution v an insertion; exam 6 marks describing translation; real context:
a nonsense mutation in a disease gene (described in the stem, no unverified named mutations).
Genetic code data: generate the standard code table in data/codon_table.py (standard RNA codon table, double-checked
by script: 64 codons, 61 sense, 3 stop) and use it in the widget and checks; never type it by hand.
Figures: transcription (fig small); translation at the ribosome (fig); splicing (fig small).
Widget **bio-codon** after translation and again in the mutation section (data attributes for a preset mutation).

## Widget spec: bio-codon
Name `bio-codon`, file `widgets/bio-codon.js`. Attributes: `data-dna="TACCGAAAGTTTCCGATT"` (template strand, 3′→5′
as read; this one gives mRNA AUG GCU UUC AAA GGC UAA: Met Ala Phe Lys Gly STOP), `data-mutate="none|sub|ins|del"`, `data-pos="4"`.
- Shows: template strand (labelled 3′ … 5′), mRNA (5′ … 3′) by complementary pairing, codons grouped in threes
  with alternating background, anticodons, amino acids (three-letter abbreviations) using the standard code table
  generated into the widget file from data/codon_table.py (keep the generator). Translation starts at the first AUG
  and stops at a stop codon (shown "STOP").
- Controls: text field for the template strand (A, T, C, G only; friendly `.w-err` otherwise), mutation tabs
  (none / substitution / insertion / deletion) with a position slider and a base select; the mutated sequence and
  polypeptide are shown under the original with changes highlighted (bold + `.hi` class) and a verdict line: "silent",
  "missense: 1 amino acid changed", "nonsense: early stop", or "frameshift: every codon after position n changed".
- Start: a template whose mRNA starts AUG and gives a 5-amino-acid chain ending in a stop codon, with a substitution
  preset so the verdict shows at once. Fits at 375 px (wrap the strands in rows of 12 bases).
