## bio.photosynthesis: Photosynthesis (`@time 5–6 hours`)
Syllabus 13.1. Candidates should be able to:
1. describe the relationship between the structure of chloroplasts, as shown in diagrams and electron micrographs, and
   their function
2. explain that energy transferred as ATP and reduced NADP from the light-dependent stage is used during the
   light-independent stage (Calvin cycle) of photosynthesis to produce complex organic molecules
3. state that within a chloroplast, the thylakoids (thylakoid membranes and thylakoid spaces), which occur in stacks
   called grana, are the site of the light-dependent stage and the stroma is the site of the light-independent stage
4. describe the role of chloroplast pigments (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotene and xanthophyll) in light
   absorption in thylakoids
5. interpret absorption spectra of chloroplast pigments and action spectra for photosynthesis
6. describe and use chromatography to separate and identify chloroplast pigments (reference should be made to Rf
   values in identification of chloroplast pigments)
7. state that cyclic photophosphorylation and non-cyclic photophosphorylation occur during the light-dependent stage
   of photosynthesis
8. explain that in cyclic photophosphorylation: only photosystem I (PSI) is involved; photoactivation of chlorophyll
   occurs; ATP is synthesised
9. explain that in non-cyclic photophosphorylation: photosystem I (PSI) and photosystem II (PSII) are both involved;
   photoactivation of chlorophyll occurs; the oxygen-evolving complex catalyses the photolysis of water; ATP and
   reduced NADP are synthesised
10. explain that during photophosphorylation: energetic electrons release energy as they pass through the electron
   transport chain (details of carriers are not expected); the released energy is used to transfer protons across the
   thylakoid membrane; protons return to the stroma from the thylakoid space by facilitated diffusion through ATP
   synthase, providing energy for ATP synthesis (details of ATP synthase are not expected)
11. outline the three main stages of the Calvin cycle: rubisco catalyses the fixation of carbon dioxide by combination
   with a molecule of ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP), a 5C compound, to yield two molecules of glycerate 3-phosphate
   (GP), a 3C compound; GP is reduced to triose phosphate (TP) in reactions involving reduced NADP and ATP; RuBP is
   regenerated from TP in reactions that use ATP
12. state that Calvin cycle intermediates are used to produce other molecules, limited to GP to produce some amino acids
   and TP to produce carbohydrates, lipids and amino acids
Cover: overall equation; chloroplast structure (double envelope, thylakoids, grana, stroma, starch grains, lipid
droplets, circular DNA, 70S ribosomes) to function; pigments: chlorophyll a (primary pigment, reaction centre),
chlorophyll b, carotene, xanthophyll (accessory pigments widen the range of wavelengths absorbed and pass energy to
the reaction centre) in light-harvesting complexes; absorption v action spectra (chlorophylls absorb mainly
blue-violet and red, reflect green; action spectrum matches the combined absorption; describe by wavelength in nm:
say "around 430–450 nm and 650–680 nm"); chromatography `:::lab` (grind leaf with solvent, spot, run in a
non-polar solvent, mark the front; Rf = distance moved by pigment ÷ distance moved by solvent; identify pigments by
comparing Rf with given values: always give the reference Rf values in the question as an exam would; carotene
usually highest); light-dependent stage: photoactivation (light excites electrons in chlorophyll to a higher
energy level, they leave the photosystem), non-cyclic (PSII → ETC → PSI → reduces NADP; PSII's lost electrons
replaced from photolysis of water by the oxygen-evolving complex: \(\ce{2H2O -> 4H+ + 4e- + O2}\)); cyclic (PSI
electrons return to PSI via the ETC; only ATP); chemiosmosis across the thylakoid membrane (protons pumped into the
thylakoid space; also protons from photolysis; back through ATP synthase into the stroma); Calvin cycle with carbon
numbers (3 CO₂ + 3 RuBP → 6 GP → 6 TP; 1 TP leaves, 5 TP regenerate 3 RuBP); per CO₂ fixed: 3 ATP and 2 reduced
NADP (state as the standard textbook bookkeeping, check in the script); uses of GP and TP; compare chemiosmosis in
mitochondria and chloroplasts (table).
Pitfalls: "light-independent stage happens in the dark"; "oxygen comes from CO₂" (from water); "chlorophyll absorbs
green"; Rf above 1; "NADP is reduced in cyclic"; protons pumped "into the stroma"; RuBP 6C.
Examples: Rf calculation and identification (given values); interpret absorption and action spectra (exam 4 marks);
explain the effect of stopping light on GP and RuBP levels (classic data: GP rises, RuBP falls; exam 5 marks);
explain the role of water (4 marks).
Figures: chloroplast (fig small); absorption and action spectra (fig, schematic curves drawn in data/fig_ps.py, axes
labelled, no colour words); Z-scheme simplified non-cyclic flow (fig, viewBox ~600); Calvin cycle (fig small).
Links: PMT photosynthesis; SME 13-photosynthesis; OpenStax ch 8.

## bio.limiting: Limiting factors in photosynthesis (`@paper CAIE 9700 Papers 4 and 5`, `@time 3–4 hours`)
Syllabus 13.2. Candidates should be able to:
1. state that light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature are examples of limiting factors of
   photosynthesis
2. explain the effects of changes in light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature on the rate of
   photosynthesis
3. describe and carry out investigations using redox indicators, including DCPIP and methylene blue, and a suspension
   of chloroplasts to determine the effects of light intensity and light wavelength on the rate of photosynthesis
4. describe and carry out investigations using whole plants, including aquatic plants, to determine the effects of
   light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature on the rate of photosynthesis
Cover: limiting factor definition (the factor in shortest supply that limits the rate; increasing it increases the
rate); the classic graph: rate v light intensity at two CO₂ concentrations and two temperatures; reading which factor
limits on each part of the curve; explanations in terms of stages (light → ATP and reduced NADP; CO₂ → fixation by
rubisco; temperature → enzyme-controlled Calvin cycle, denaturation at high temperature); compensation point as an
optional `:::note`; inverse square law for light intensity from a lamp distance (light intensity ∝ 1/d²) as a
Paper 5 tool; DCPIP with isolated chloroplasts (Hill reaction idea: DCPIP accepts electrons from the light-dependent
stage and turns from blue to colourless; colorimeter or time to decolourise; controls: chloroplasts in the dark,
DCPIP without chloroplasts; keep cold, isotonic buffer); coloured filters for wavelength; whole-plant `:::lab` with
Elodea or Cabomba (count bubbles or measure gas volume with a capillary or syringe; sodium hydrogencarbonate for CO₂;
heat-shield water bath; acclimatise; limitations of bubble counting); glasshouse economics (add CO₂, heat, light up to
the point where another factor limits; cost v yield).
Pitfalls: "temperature affects the light-dependent stage most"; reading the plateau as "photosynthesis stops";
"increasing light always increases rate"; bubble counting gives O₂ volume precisely (no: bubble size varies);
inverse square law misused (doubling distance → quarter intensity).
Examples: identify the limiting factor at points A–C on a graph; light intensity from distances (1/d² relative values);
plan a whole-plant investigation (Paper 5 style 8 marks); interpret DCPIP absorbance data.
Figures: the limiting factors graph (`:::plot` with two series per plot: two plots, e.g.
`--f "8*x/(20+x)"` and `--f "4*x/(10+x)"`, model curves, stated); Elodea apparatus (fig small).
Widget **bio-limiting** after the graph explanation.

## Widget spec: bio-limiting
Name `bio-limiting`, file `widgets/bio-limiting.js`. Attributes: `data-co2="0.04"`, `data-temp="25"`.
- Model: rate = min(light-limited, CO₂-limited, temperature-limited) with a smooth minimum, e.g.
  P = Pmax(T, CO₂) · I/(I + K). Pmax rises with CO₂ (0.01–0.15%, saturating) and with temperature up to about 30 °C,
  then falls (enzyme denaturation). State "model, arbitrary units" in the note.
- Canvas: rate v light intensity (0–100 arbitrary units) for the current CO₂ and temperature, plus a faint reference
  curve at the starting settings. A draggable point on the light axis shows which factor is limiting there (readout:
  "light is limiting: raising light raises the rate" or "CO₂ (or temperature) is limiting: the curve has levelled
  off").
- Sliders: CO₂ % (0.01–0.15), temperature (5–45 °C). Axes follow the values (y max = 1.1 × highest Pmax shown).
- Survive zero size.
