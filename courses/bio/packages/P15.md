Chapters: `bio.meiosis`, `bio.inheritance`, `bio.dihybrid` (all `@level a2`, `@paper CAIE 9700 Paper 4`;
dihybrid also Paper 5 for chi-squared). Widgets: **bio-punnett**, **bio-chisq**. Read COURSE.md, kit/STYLE.md and the

## bio.meiosis: Meiosis and genetic variation (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Syllabus 16.1. Candidates should be able to:
1. explain the meanings of the terms haploid (n) and diploid (2n)
2. explain what is meant by homologous pairs of chromosomes
3. explain the need for a reduction division during meiosis in the production of gametes
4. describe the behaviour of chromosomes in plant and animal cells during meiosis and the associated behaviour of the
   nuclear envelope, the cell surface membrane and the spindle (names of the main stages of meiosis, but not the
   sub-divisions of prophase I, are expected: prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I, telophase I, prophase II,
   metaphase II, anaphase II and telophase II)
5. interpret photomicrographs and diagrams of cells in different stages of meiosis and identify the main stages of
   meiosis
6. explain that crossing over and random orientation (independent assortment) of pairs of homologous chromosomes and
   sister chromatids during meiosis produces genetically different gametes
7. explain that the random fusion of gametes at fertilisation produces genetically different individuals
Cover: haploid/diploid with human numbers (46, 23); homologous pairs (same genes at the same loci, maybe different
alleles; one maternal one paternal); why halving is needed (fertilisation restores 2n, keeps number constant across
generations); all eight stages with chromosome, envelope, spindle behaviour (bivalents, chiasmata in prophase I;
pairs on the equator in metaphase I; homologues separate in anaphase I; chromatids separate in anaphase II); four
genetically different haploid cells; crossing over (non-sister chromatids exchange sections at chiasmata → new
combinations of alleles); random orientation in metaphase I (2ⁿ combinations: 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million in humans) and of
chromatids in metaphase II (because crossing over made them different); random fertilisation (2²³ × 2²³ before
crossing over); mitosis v meiosis table.
Pitfalls: "homologous chromosomes are identical"; "crossing over between sister chromatids"; "meiosis II halves the
chromosome number"; counting chromatids v chromosomes at each stage; "independent assortment = crossing over".
Examples: number of combinations for n = 3 pairs (8) and for a species with given 2n; identify stage from drawings;
explain how meiosis produces variation (exam 6 marks); chromosome and DNA content table through meiosis.
Figures: homologous pair with loci and alleles (fig small); meiosis I and II stages (fig, viewBox ~600, two rows of four
panels, data/fig_meiosis.py); crossing over (fig small).
Links: PMT inherited-change; SME 16-inheritance; OpenStax ch 11.

## bio.inheritance: Monohybrid inheritance (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Syllabus 16.2.1, 16.2.2 (monohybrid parts), 16.2.4. Candidates should be able to:
1. explain the terms gene, locus, allele, dominant, recessive, codominant, linkage, test cross, F1, F2, phenotype,
   genotype, homozygous and heterozygous
2. interpret and construct genetic diagrams, including Punnett squares, to explain and predict the results of
   monohybrid crosses and dihybrid crosses that involve dominance, codominance, multiple alleles and sex linkage (this
   chapter: the monohybrid crosses; dihybrid ones are in bio.dihybrid)
4. interpret and construct genetic diagrams, including Punnett squares, to explain and predict the results of test
   crosses
Also: genetic ratios as probabilities (Mathematical requirements, A Level).
Cover: the terms as `:::def` cards (linkage defined here, used in bio.dihybrid); the full genetic diagram layout that
examiners expect (parental phenotypes, parental genotypes, gametes in circles, Punnett square, offspring genotypes,
phenotypes, ratio) as a `:::key`; dominance (3:1 and 1:1); codominance (notation with a gene letter and superscript
alleles, e.g. \(C^R C^W\) for flower colour, or the ABO system); multiple alleles (ABO: \(I^A\), \(I^B\), \(I^o\);
\(I^A\) and \(I^B\) codominant, both dominant to \(I^o\)); sex linkage (X and Y; genes on the non-homologous part of
X; notation \(X^H X^h\), \(X^h Y\); haemophilia as the example, linking bio.genecontrol for the F8 gene; carrier
females; males cannot be carriers); test cross with a homozygous recessive to find an unknown genotype; pedigree
diagrams (interpreting family trees, as exams use them); ratios as probabilities (each child is an independent
event).
Pitfalls: gametes not circled or containing two alleles of one gene; using different letters for alleles of one gene;
ratios given as phenotypes without naming them; "probability of 3:1 means exactly 3 of 4 children"; X-linked notation
on the Y.
Examples: a monohybrid cross to F2; a codominance cross; ABO cross with possible blood groups; X-linked haemophilia
cross giving probabilities for sons and daughters (exam 5 marks); test cross; pedigree analysis (stretch).
Figures: genetic diagram layout (the Punnett square as a Markdown table is fine); pedigree symbols and one pedigree
(fig small).
Widget **bio-punnett** after the first full genetic diagram, and again for sex linkage (data attributes).

## bio.dihybrid: Dihybrid crosses and the chi-squared test (`@paper CAIE 9700 Papers 4 and 5`, `@time 5–6 hours`)
Syllabus 16.2.2 (dihybrid), 16.2.3, 16.2.5. Candidates should be able to:
2. interpret and construct genetic diagrams, including Punnett squares, to explain and predict the results of
   monohybrid crosses and dihybrid crosses that involve dominance, codominance, multiple alleles and sex linkage
3. interpret and construct genetic diagrams, including Punnett squares, to explain and predict the results of dihybrid
   crosses that involve autosomal linkage and epistasis (knowledge of the expected ratios for different types of
   epistasis is not expected)
5. use the chi-squared test to test the significance of differences between observed and expected results (the formula
   for the chi-squared test will be provided, as shown in the Mathematical requirements)
Cover: dihybrid with unlinked genes (AaBb × AaBb → 9:3:3:1; four gametes AB, Ab, aB, ab; test cross AaBb × aabb →
1:1:1:1); a dihybrid with one sex-linked gene; autosomal linkage (genes on the same chromosome inherited together;
parental gametes predominate; recombinants appear only via crossing over; ratio deviates from 9:3:3:1 or 1:1:1:1);
epistasis (one gene affects the expression of another; work from the given information each time, e.g. a gene whose
recessive homozygote blocks pigment: interpret, do not memorise ratios); chi-squared: null hypothesis (no significant
difference between observed and expected), expected from the ratio, χ² = Σ(O − E)²/E (formula given in the exam),
degrees of freedom = classes − 1 (not given in the exam), compare with the critical value at p = 0.05 from the table
(given in the exam; copy from FACTS.md); conclusion in words ("the difference is not significant; it is due to chance;
the null hypothesis is accepted; the results fit a 9:3:3:1 ratio"); when a significant result suggests linkage.
Pitfalls: using percentages or ratios instead of numbers in χ²; df = number of classes (not minus 1); "accept the null
hypothesis" when χ² > critical; claiming χ² "proves" a ratio; expected values not summing to the total.
Examples: a 9:3:3:1 cross with χ² (data from a described plant cross, 4 classes, df 3, critical 7.81); a linkage
example where χ² is large (significant) and explained by linkage; an epistasis interpretation; Spot the error: df
taken as 4. Practice uses different organisms and numbers from the examples. Check scripts import data/crit.py.
Figures: chromosomes showing linked v unlinked genes (fig small); a χ² working table (Markdown table).
Widget **bio-chisq** after the first χ² worked example.
Links: PMT inherited-change; SME 16-inheritance; Learn.Genetics basics.
`Next: [Genes, phenotype and gene control](#bio.genecontrol)`.

## Widget spec: bio-punnett
Name `bio-punnett`, file `widgets/bio-punnett.js`. Attributes: `data-type="mono|di|xlinked|codom"`,
`data-p1="Aa"`, `data-p2="Aa"` (for di: `AaBb`; for xlinked: `XHXh` and `XHY`; for codom: `CRCW`).
- Tabs for the four types. Inputs: each parent's genotype by selects (per gene: AA, Aa, aa; X-linked female XᴴXᴴ,
  XᴴXʰ, XʰXʰ, male XᴴY, XʰY; codominant two alleles R and W).
- Output: gametes (each shown in a circle, with their probabilities), the Punnett square (2×2 or 4×4; gamete headers
  on top and left), offspring genotype ratio and phenotype ratio (simplest whole numbers and as fractions or
  percentages), with phenotype labels editable for dominant/recessive (default "dominant phenotype"/"recessive
  phenotype"; for X-linked "unaffected"/"affected"/"carrier", split by sex).
- A 4×4 square must fit at 375 px: small font, cells min 40 px, wrap labels; or scroll inside the widget box only.
- Start: mono Aa × Aa. Survive zero size (no canvas needed: DOM table).

## Widget spec: bio-chisq
Name `bio-chisq`, file `widgets/bio-chisq.js`. Attributes: `data-obs="315,108,101,32"`, `data-ratio="9:3:3:1"`.
- Inputs: observed counts (comma-separated) and the expected ratio (e.g. 9:3:3:1 or 1:1) or explicit expected values.
  Validation: same number of classes; positive numbers; friendly messages.
- Working table: class, O, E, O − E, (O − E)², (O − E)²/E; then χ² (2 d.p.), df = classes − 1, critical value at
  p = 0.05 for df 1–10 (3.84 5.99 7.81 9.49 11.07 12.59 14.07 15.51 16.92 18.31: from FACTS.md) and a conclusion
  sentence in exam wording. Optional bar chart of O v E on a canvas (two bars per class, labelled O and E, not by colour).
- Start state as the attributes above (Mendel-style data). The chapter's worked examples use different data.
