## bio.planttransport: Plant tissues and water transport (`@time 5–6 hours`)
Syllabus 7.1 and 7.2.1–7.2.5. Candidates should be able to:
7.1.1 draw plan diagrams of transverse sections of stems, roots and leaves of herbaceous dicotyledonous plants from
  microscope slides and photomicrographs
7.1.2 describe the distribution of xylem and phloem in transverse sections of stems, roots and leaves of herbaceous
  dicotyledonous plants
7.1.3 draw and label xylem vessel elements, phloem sieve tube elements and companion cells from microscope slides,
  photomicrographs and electron micrographs
7.1.4 relate the structure of xylem vessel elements, phloem sieve tube elements and companion cells to their functions
7.2.1 state that some mineral ions and organic compounds can be transported within plants dissolved in water
7.2.2 describe the transport of water from the soil to the xylem through the: apoplast pathway, including reference to
  lignin and cellulose; symplast pathway, including reference to the endodermis, Casparian strip and suberin
7.2.3 explain that transpiration involves the evaporation of water from the internal surfaces of leaves followed by
  diffusion of water vapour to the atmosphere
7.2.4 explain how hydrogen bonding of water molecules is involved with movement of water in the xylem by
  cohesion-tension in transpiration pull and by adhesion to cellulose in cell walls
7.2.5 make annotated drawings of transverse sections of leaves from xerophytic plants to explain how they are adapted
  to reduce water loss by transpiration
Cover: why plants need transport (SA:V, link bio.movement); distribution of vascular tissue: stem (vascular bundles in
a ring, phloem outside xylem, cambium between), root (central xylem star with phloem between arms, endodermis,
pericycle), leaf (midrib bundle, xylem upper side); how to draw a plan diagram (no cells, correct proportions, tissue
boundaries only; link bio.practical2); xylem vessel elements (dead, no end walls, lignified walls with pits, continuous
tube, narrow lumen helps cohesion) and phloem structure briefly (full detail in bio.translocation; draw sieve tube
element and companion cell here as 7.1.3 requires); root hair uptake by osmosis; apoplast (through cellulose cell
walls, water-filled spaces) and symplast (cytoplasm via plasmodesmata; vacuolar route may be mentioned as part of the
cell-to-cell route); endodermis with Casparian strip of suberin blocks the apoplast, so water and ions must cross
membranes (control of what enters the xylem); transpiration (evaporation from mesophyll cell walls into air spaces,
diffusion of water vapour out through stomata down a water potential gradient); cohesion (hydrogen bonds between
water molecules), adhesion (hydrogen bonds to cellulose/lignin), tension (transpiration pull), continuous water
columns; factors affecting transpiration (light, temperature, humidity, wind) with a potometer `:::lab` (measures
water uptake, not loss; seal under water; air bubble or meniscus distance per unit time → rate; calculate volume
from capillary radius: \(\pi r^2 \times\) distance); xerophytes: annotated drawing of a marram grass leaf TS (rolled
leaf, sunken stomata, hairs, thick waxy cuticle on the outer surface) and others (cacti, reduced leaves); explain
each as "traps moist air → reduces water potential gradient → less diffusion of water vapour".
Pitfalls: "water evaporates from the stomata" (it evaporates from cell walls inside the leaf); "xylem is living";
"the Casparian strip is in the root hair"; potometer "measures transpiration"; "cohesion is between water and xylem
wall" (that is adhesion); xerophyte adaptations without the explanation.
Examples: potometer calculation (bubble moves 24 mm in 5 min in a capillary of radius 0.5 mm → volume and rate);
plan diagram of a stem from a drawing; exam 6 marks: explain water movement up the xylem; real context: crops in dry
regions and irrigation.
Figures: TS stem, root, leaf plan diagrams (three fig small, drawn as plans in data/fig_plant.py); xylem vessel and
phloem (fig small); apoplast and symplast pathways across the root (fig, viewBox ~600); potometer (fig small); marram
grass TS annotated (fig small).

## bio.translocation: Translocation in phloem (`@time 2–3 hours`)
Syllabus 7.2.6–7.2.8 (and 7.1.4 for phloem). Candidates should be able to:
6. state that assimilates dissolved in water, such as sucrose and amino acids, move from sources to sinks in phloem
   sieve tubes
7. explain how companion cells transfer assimilates to phloem sieve tubes, with reference to proton pumps and
   cotransporter proteins
8. explain mass flow in phloem sieve tubes down a hydrostatic pressure gradient from source to sink
Cover: sources (photosynthesising leaves, storage organs being used) and sinks (roots, growing points, fruits,
storage organs filling); a storage organ can be either at different times; sieve tube elements (living, few
organelles, no nucleus, sieve plates with pores) and companion cells (many mitochondria for ATP, plasmodesmata to the
sieve tube); loading: proton pumps (active transport of H⁺ out of companion cells using ATP), H⁺ diffuses back in
through cotransporter proteins carrying sucrose with it; sucrose passes into the sieve tube via plasmodesmata;
water enters by osmosis (lower water potential) → high hydrostatic pressure at source; at sinks sucrose is removed
(used or stored as starch) → water leaves → lower pressure; mass flow down the pressure gradient; flow can be in
either direction in different sieve tubes; evidence: aphid stylets and ringing experiments as data questions;
compare xylem and phloem transport (table: living or dead, direction, driving force, energy needed).
Pitfalls: "phloem transports glucose" (sucrose); "companion cells pump sucrose directly by active transport" (the
proton pump is active; sucrose enters by cotransport using the H⁺ gradient); "mass flow is diffusion"; "phloem flow
is only downwards".
Examples: explain loading (exam 5 marks); interpret a ringing experiment; compare xylem and phloem (table, 5 marks);
real context: aphids as crop pests feeding on phloem sap.
Figures: companion cell loading with proton pump and cotransporter (fig, viewBox ~560); mass flow source→sink (fig
small).
Links: PMT transport-in-plants; OpenStax ch 30.
