## bio.f.basics: Biology from zero (`@level pre`, `@time 3–4 hours`, no `@paper`)
For a student who did little or no GCSE biology or chemistry. Every later chapter assumes these ideas. Topic chapter
format (all STYLE sections; exam practice here means GCSE-to-AS bridging questions, marked).
Cover:
1. Living things: the characteristics of life; levels of organisation (molecule → organelle → cell → tissue → organ →
   organ system → organism → population → community → ecosystem) with one example per level.
2. Cells in one page: animal and plant cell basics (nucleus, cytoplasm, membrane, wall, chloroplast, vacuole) as
   GCSE knows them; the AS version is in bio.cells.
3. Chemistry for biologists: atoms, elements, molecules; the elements of life C, H, O, N, P, S; covalent bonds (shared
   electrons), ions and ionic bonds; polar molecules and the hydrogen bond (link chem.bonding); acids, alkalis and pH
   scale (link chem.acids); a catalyst speeds up a reaction and is not used up.
4. Big biological molecules in outline: monomers and polymers, condensation (water removed) and hydrolysis (water
   added), with a picture of beads on a string. Name carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids; details later.
5. Energy: respiration releases energy from glucose; photosynthesis stores light energy; ATP mentioned by name only.
6. Diffusion in one paragraph (net movement down a concentration gradient) and why a cell needs exchange.
7. Units and numbers: SI prefixes G, M, k, m, µ, n; converting mm ↔ µm ↔ nm; standard form; significant figures;
   percentages and percentage change; reading a graph (axes, units, gradient as rate). (bio.maths goes further.)
8. How biology is examined: command words state, describe, explain, suggest with a two-line example each.
Pitfalls: confusing µm and mm; "diffusion of water" wrongly used for osmosis (say osmosis is taught in bio.movement);
mixing up atoms, molecules and cells in size order (include a size ladder figure: atom 0.1 nm, small molecule ~1 nm,
protein ~5–10 nm, virus ~20–300 nm, bacterium 1–5 µm, animal cell ~10–30 µm, plant cell ~10–100 µm).
Figures: size ladder (fig small, log scale ticks), levels of organisation (fig small), condensation and hydrolysis
of a dimer (fig small).
Examples: convert 0.025 mm to µm; percentage change in mass of a potato cylinder; is hydrolysis or condensation
happening; size ordering.
Use the `units` widget (physics) for unit conversion if it helps; text must make sense without it.
`Next: [Microscopy and magnification](#bio.microscopy)`.
Links: Amoeba Sisters (watch), OpenStax ch 3 intro (read), Khan Academy root.
