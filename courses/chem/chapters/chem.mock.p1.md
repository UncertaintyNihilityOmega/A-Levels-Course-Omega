Q: [1 mark] (#chem.atoms) Which ion has the electronic configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶?

**A** \(\ce{Mn^2+}\)

**B** \(\ce{Fe^3+}\)

**C** \(\ce{Fe^2+}\)

**D** \(\ce{Co^2+}\)

A: **C**. An iron atom is [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²; the two 4s electrons are lost first, so \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) is [Ar] 3d⁶. \(\ce{Mn^2+}\) and \(\ce{Fe^3+}\) are both 3d⁵ (B is the tempting wrong option: it has lost one 3d electron too many), and \(\ce{Co^2+}\) is 3d⁷.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.moles) A sample of magnesium contains three isotopes: ²⁴Mg (79.0%), ²⁵Mg (10.0%) and ²⁶Mg (11.0%). What is the relative atomic mass of magnesium in this sample, to one decimal place?

**A** 24.0

**B** 24.3

**C** 25.0

**D** 26.0

A: **B**. \(A_r = (24 \times 79.0 + 25 \times 10.0 + 26 \times 11.0)/100 = 24.3\), which agrees with the Periodic Table. C is the unweighted mean of 24, 25 and 26. A is the mass of the most abundant isotope.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.atoms) The first four ionisation energies of an element Z are 590, 1150, 4940 and 6480 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the formula of the oxide of Z?

**A** \(\ce{Z2O3}\)

**B** \(\ce{Z2O}\)

**C** \(\ce{ZO2}\)

**D** \(\ce{ZO}\)

A: **D**. The big jump comes between the second and third ionisation energies, so the atom has two outer electrons and forms \(\ce{Z^2+}\). The oxide is \(\ce{ZO}\). A would need the jump after the third electron; B after the first.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.moles) A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass. Its relative molecular mass is 84.0. What is its molecular formula?

**A** \(\ce{C6H12}\)

**B** \(\ce{CH2}\)

**C** \(\ce{C3H6}\)

**D** \(\ce{C7H14}\)

A: **A**. Moles of C : H = 85.7/12.0 : 14.3/1.0 = 7.14 : 14.3 = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is \(\ce{CH2}\) (14.0). The molecular formula is 84.0/14.0 = 6 times that, \(\ce{C6H12}\). B stops at the empirical formula. C has \(M_r\) 42.0.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.moles) 25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium carbonate of concentration 0.100 mol dm⁻³ reacts exactly with hydrochloric acid of concentration 0.150 mol dm⁻³: \(\ce{Na2CO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2}\). What volume of the acid is needed?

**A** 75.0 cm³

**B** 50.0 cm³

**C** 33.3 cm³

**D** 16.7 cm³

A: **C**. \(n(\ce{Na2CO3}) = 0.0250 \times 0.100 = 2.50 \times 10^{-3}\) mol; \(n(\ce{HCl}) = 2 \times 2.50 \times 10^{-3} = 5.00 \times 10^{-3}\) mol; \(V = 5.00 \times 10^{-3}/0.150 = 0.0333\) dm³ = 33.3 cm³. D ignores the 1 : 2 ratio. B divides by 0.100 instead of 0.150. A inverts the ratio of the concentrations.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.bonding) How many σ bonds and how many π bonds are there in a molecule of propenenitrile, \(\ce{CH2=CH-C#N}\)?

**A** 6 σ and 3 π

**B** 6 σ and 2 π

**C** 5 σ and 4 π

**D** 7 σ and 3 π

A: **A**. The three C–H bonds, the C–C bond, and one bond in each of C=C and C≡N are σ: 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6. The extra bond in C=C gives 1 π and the C≡N bond gives 2 π: 3 π in all. B forgets that a triple bond has two π bonds.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.shapes) Which statements are correct?

1  \(\ce{BF3}\) is a trigonal planar molecule and is non-polar.
2  Hydrogen bonds form between molecules of \(\ce{NH3}\).
3  The ammonium ion, \(\ce{NH4+}\), contains one coordinate (dative covalent) bond.

**A** 1, 2 and 3

**B** 1 and 2 only

**C** 2 and 3 only

**D** 1 only

A: **A**. All three are correct. In \(\ce{BF3}\) the three polar B–F bonds point symmetrically and their dipoles cancel (1). Q: [1 mark] (#chem.states) 0.320 g of a gas occupies 125 cm³ at 100 kPa and 300 K. What is its relative molecular mass? (\(R = 8.31\) J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)

**A** \(6.38 \times 10^{-5}\)

**B** 0.0638

**C** 63.8

**D** \(6.38 \times 10^{4}\)

A: **C**. The other options are powers-of-ten slips: B uses kPa with cm³, D uses kPa with m³, and A uses Pa with cm³.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.states) The table gives properties of four solids.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | high | yes | yes | no |
| B | low | no | no | no |

Which solid is most likely to be silicon(IV) oxide?

**A** solid A

**B** solid B

**C** solid C

**D** solid D

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.equilibria) 0.100 mol of \(\ce{N2O4}\) is placed in a closed 1.00 dm³ vessel and the equilibrium \(\ce{N2O4(g) <=> 2NO2(g)}\) is set up. At equilibrium \([\ce{NO2}] = 0.0400\) mol dm⁻³. What is \(K_c\)?

A: **B**. \(\ce{N2O4}\) used = 0.0400/2 = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³, so \([\ce{N2O4}]\) at equilibrium = 0.0800. \(K_c = (0.0400)^2/0.0800 = 0.0200\) mol dm⁻³. A uses the initial 0.100 instead of the equilibrium value. C multiplies by the coefficient 2 instead of squaring. D is the inverted expression.

Which indicator is suitable?

**A** Both methyl orange and bromophenol blue

**B** Methyl orange only

**C** Bromophenol blue only

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.kinetics) The rate of a reaction increases when the temperature is raised by 10 K. Which statements help to explain this?

**A** 1, 2 and 3

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.periodicity) Silicon has a much higher melting point than phosphorus (\(\ce{P4}\)). Which statement explains this?

**A** Silicon has a giant metallic structure with delocalised electrons.

**B** Silicon has a giant covalent structure, so many strong covalent bonds must be broken, whereas melting \(\ce{P4}\) overcomes only weak forces between molecules.

**C** The Si–Si bonds are stronger than the P–P bonds inside a \(\ce{P4}\) molecule.

**D** Silicon has a larger relative formula mass, so its id-id forces are stronger.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.org.polymers) A section of an addition polymer is –CH₂–C(CH₃)₂–CH₂–C(CH₃)₂–. What is the monomer?

**A** propene

**B** but-1-ene

**C** but-2-ene

**D** 2-methylpropene

A: **D**. The repeat unit is –CH₂–C(CH₃)₂–, which came from \(\ce{CH2=C(CH3)2}\), 2-methylpropene: the double bond opened and the carbons joined into the chain. A would leave a –CH₂–CH(CH₃)– unit. B and C would leave a different pattern of side groups.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.org.synthesis) 1.00 mol of \(\ce{HOCH2CH=CHCO2H}\) reacts completely with an excess of sodium metal. What amount of hydrogen gas is released?

**A** 1.00 mol

**B** 0.50 mol

**C** 2.00 mol

**D** 1.50 mol

A: **A**. Sodium reacts with both the alcohol –OH and the carboxylic acid –COOH groups. Each group releases one hydrogen atom, so the two groups release 2 H, which make 1 \(\ce{H2}\): 1.00 mol. B counts only the acid group. The C=C does not react with sodium.

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.analysis) The infrared spectrum of a compound shows a strong absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ and a very broad absorption between 2500 and 3000 cm⁻¹. Use the Data booklet. Which compound is it?

**A** propan-1-ol

**B** propanone

**C** propanoic acid

**D** methyl ethanoate

A: **C**. The absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ is C=O; the very broad band from 2500 to 3000 cm⁻¹ is the O–H of a carboxylic acid. Propanone and methyl ethanoate have C=O but no O–H. Propan-1-ol has an O–H absorption at higher wavenumber (3200–3600) and no C=O.
:::

Q: [1 mark] (#chem.analysis) In the mass spectrum of a hydrocarbon the molecular ion peak M has an abundance of 32.0 and the [M+1] peak has an abundance of 1.76. How many carbon atoms are in one molecule? Use

\[n = \dfrac{100 \times \text{abundance of [M+1]}}{1.1 \times \text{abundance of M}}\]

**A** 4

**B** 5

**C** 6

**D** 18

A: **B**. \(n = 100 \times 1.76/(1.1 \times 32.0) = 5.0\), so five carbon atoms. C comes from leaving out the 1.1 factor (5.5, rounded up). D inverts the ratio of the peaks.
