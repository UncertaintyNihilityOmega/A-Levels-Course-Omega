Q: [16 marks] (#chem.practical) **Titration.**

FA 1 is an acidified aqueous solution containing 13.90 g dm⁻³ of hydrated iron(II) sulfate, \(\ce{FeSO4.xH2O}\). FA 2 is 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII), \(\ce{KMnO4}\).

A student used a pipette to place 25.0 cm³ of FA 1 into a conical flask and titrated it with FA 2 from a burette until the first permanent pink colour. The reaction is

\[\ce{MnO4- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O}\]

The student's burette readings were:

- Rough titration: initial 0.00 cm³, final 25.90 cm³.
- Titration 1: initial 0.00 cm³, final 25.55 cm³.
- Titration 2: initial 1.00 cm³, final 26.10 cm³.
- Titration 3: initial 0.50 cm³, final 25.70 cm³.

(a) Record these results in a single table. Include headings with units and the titre for each titration. (4 marks)

(b) State which titres you will use to calculate the mean titre, explain your choice, and calculate the mean. (2 marks)

(c) Calculate the concentration of \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) in FA 1 in mol dm⁻³. (3 marks)

(d) Use your answer to (c) to calculate the relative formula mass of \(\ce{FeSO4.xH2O}\), and deduce the value of \(x\). (3 marks)

(e) Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mean titre. Each burette reading has an uncertainty of ±0.05 cm³. (2 marks)

(f) The student suggests that the percentage uncertainty can be reduced by changing the concentration of FA 2. State the change, and show by calculation that it works. (2 marks)
A: (a) one table with headings including units, e.g. final reading / cm³, initial reading / cm³, titre / cm³, and a column or row for each titration (rough, 1, 2, 3) [B1]
all burette readings recorded to two decimal places, with the last figure 0 or 5 (e.g. 0.00, 1.00) [B1]
titres correctly subtracted: rough 25.90, 1: 25.55, 2: 25.10, 3: 25.20 [B1]
units in the headings only, not repeated in the body of the table; the rough titre identified [B1]

(b) Titres 2 and 3 (25.10 and 25.20 cm³) are used, because they are within 0.10 cm³ of each other (concordant); the rough titre and titration 1 (25.55 cm³) are not concordant with them. [B1]
mean \(= (25.10 + 25.20)/2 = 25.15\) cm³ [A1]

(c) \(n(\ce{MnO4-}) = 0.0100 \times 25.15/1000 = 2.515 \times 10^{-4}\) mol [M1]
\(n(\ce{Fe^2+}) = 5 \times 2.515 \times 10^{-4} = 1.2575 \times 10^{-3}\) mol in 25.0 cm³ [M1]
\([\ce{Fe^2+}] = 1.2575 \times 10^{-3} / 0.0250 = 0.0503\) mol dm⁻³ [A1]

(d) \(M_r(\ce{FeSO4}) = 55.8 + 32.1 + 4(16.0) = 151.9\) [B1]
relative formula mass of the hydrate \(= 13.90 / 0.0503 = 276\) (276.3) [M1]
\(x = (276.3 - 151.9)/18.0 = 6.9\), so \(x = 7\) [A1]

(e) uncertainty in the titre \(= 2 \times 0.05 = 0.10\) cm³ [M1]
percentage uncertainty \(= 0.10 / 25.15 \times 100 = 0.398\%\) (0.40%) [A1]

(f) use a more dilute FA 2, for example 0.00600 mol dm⁻³, so the titre is larger (about 42 cm³) [B1]
titre \(= 2.515 \times 10^{-4} / 0.00600 \times 1000 = 41.9\) cm³, so the percentage uncertainty is \(0.10/41.9 \times 100 = 0.24\%\), smaller than 0.40% [B1]

Q: [10 marks] (#chem.energetics) **Thermometric experiment.**

A student measured the enthalpy change for the reaction

\[\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}\]

She placed 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ aqueous copper(II) sulfate in a polystyrene cup. She measured the temperature every 0.5 min. At 2.0 min she added an excess of zinc powder and stirred. The temperature at 2.0 min was not recorded.

| time / min | 0.0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 4.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| temperature / °C | 21.5 | 21.5 | 21.5 | 21.5 | zinc added | 30.0 | 31.5 | 31.0 | 30.5 | 30.0 | 29.5 | 29.0 |

Assume the solution has density 1.00 g cm⁻³ and specific heat capacity 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹, and that zinc is in excess.

(a) Describe how the maximum temperature change at the moment of mixing is found from a graph of these results. (2 marks)

(b) The correct extrapolation gives a temperature of 32.5 °C at 2.0 min. Calculate the temperature change, \(\Delta T\). (1 mark)

(c) Calculate the enthalpy change of reaction in kJ mol⁻¹, including its sign. (4 marks)

(d) The thermometer is read to the nearest 0.5 °C. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in \(\Delta T\). (1 mark)

(e) The volume of copper(II) sulfate was measured with a 50 cm³ measuring cylinder read to ±0.5 cm³. Identify which measurement, the volume or the temperature change, has the greater percentage uncertainty, justifying your choice with a calculation, and suggest an improvement to that measurement. (2 marks)
A: (a) plot temperature (y-axis) against time (x-axis) and draw a line of best fit through the points after the maximum temperature (the cooling points) [B1]
extrapolate this line back to 2.0 min, the time the zinc was added, and read the temperature there [B1]

(b) \(\Delta T = 32.5 - 21.5 = 11.0\) °C [B1]

(c) \(n(\ce{Cu^2+}) = 0.200 \times 50.0/1000 = 0.0100\) mol (copper(II) is the limiting reactant because zinc is in excess) [M1]
\(q = mc\Delta T = 50.0 \times 4.18 \times 11.0 = 2299\) J [M1]
\(\Delta H = -q/n = -2299 / 0.0100 = -229\,900\) J mol⁻¹ [M1]
\(\Delta H = -230\) kJ mol⁻¹ (negative because the temperature rises; 3 s.f.) [A1]

(d) two readings, so the uncertainty is \(2 \times 0.5 = 1.0\) °C; \(1.0/11.0 \times 100 = 9.09\%\) [B1]

(e) the temperature change: its percentage uncertainty (9.09%) is much greater than that of the 50.0 cm³ of solution measured with the measuring cylinder (\(0.5/50.0 \times 100 = 1.0\%\)) [B1]
use a thermometer reading to 0.1 °C: the uncertainty of \(2 \times 0.05 = 0.10\) °C gives \(0.10/11.0 \times 100 = 0.91\%\) [B1]

Q: [14 marks] (#chem.practical) **Qualitative analysis.**

FB 1 and FB 2 are two salts. Each contains one cation and one anion. A student dissolved a sample of each in water and carried out the tests below. The observations are given.

| test | observations with FB 1 | observations with FB 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Add NaOH(aq) dropwise, then in excess | white ppt., soluble in excess giving a colourless solution | green ppt. which turns brown on contact with air, insoluble in excess |
| 2. Add NH₃(aq) dropwise, then in excess | white ppt., insoluble in excess | green ppt. which turns brown on contact with air, insoluble in excess |
| 3. Add dilute HNO₃, then Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) | white ppt. | no ppt. |
| 4. Add dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq); then add NH₃(aq) | no ppt. | white ppt.; dissolves on adding NH₃(aq) |

FB 3 and FB 4 are propanal and propanone, in some order. Samples were warmed with the following reagents.

| test | observations with FB 3 | observations with FB 4 |
|---|---|---|
| 5. Add Fehling's solution and warm | orange/red ppt. | no change (solution stays blue) |
| 6. Add alkaline aqueous iodine and warm | no yellow ppt. | yellow ppt. |

(a) Identify the cation and the anion in FB 1. Give the evidence for each. (4 marks)

(b) Identify the cation and the anion in FB 2. Give the evidence for each. (4 marks)

(c) Describe a further test, using a different reagent, to confirm the cation in FB 2, and state the expected observation. (2 marks)

(d) Write an ionic equation, with state symbols, for the formation of the green precipitate in test 1 with FB 2. (2 marks)

(e) Identify FB 3 and FB 4, using the results of tests 5 and 6. (2 marks)
A: (a) the cation is \(\ce{Al^3+}\) [B1], because the white ppt. dissolves in excess NaOH(aq) but does not dissolve in excess NH₃(aq) (this rules out \(\ce{Zn^2+}\), whose ppt. dissolves in both) [B1]
the anion is \(\ce{SO4^2-}\) [B1], because the white ppt. with barium nitrate is formed in the presence of dilute nitric acid, so it is insoluble in acid (BaSO₄) [B1]

(b) the cation is \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) [B1], because the green ppt. turns brown on contact with air, and is insoluble in excess NaOH(aq) and in excess NH₃(aq) [B1]
the anion is \(\ce{Cl-}\) [B1], because the white ppt. with silver nitrate dissolves in NH₃(aq) (and no ppt. forms with barium nitrate, so no sulfate) [B1]

(c) add a few drops of FB 2 to dilute sulfuric acid, then add acidified potassium manganate(VII) (acidified \(\ce{KMnO4}\)) [B1]
the purple colour disappears (decolourised), as \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) is oxidised [B1]
(acidify with dilute sulfuric acid, not hydrochloric acid, which would add chloride ions that the \(\ce{KMnO4}\) can also oxidise)

(d) \(\ce{Fe^2+(aq) + 2OH-(aq) -> Fe(OH)2(s)}\): correct species [B1], balanced with state symbols [B1]

(e) FB 3 is propanal, because the orange/red ppt. with Fehling's solution shows an aldehyde [B1]
FB 4 is propanone, because it gives a yellow ppt. with alkaline aqueous iodine (the \(\ce{CH3CO}{-}\) group) and does not react with Fehling's solution [B1]
