Q: [11 marks] (#chem.atoms) **Atomic structure and moles.**

(a) Define the term *isotopes*. (2 marks)

(b) The mass spectrum of a sample of silicon shows three peaks:

| m/z | 28 | 29 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| relative abundance / % | 92.2 | 4.7 | 3.1 |

Calculate the relative atomic mass of this sample of silicon. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (2 marks)

(c) Write the full electron configuration of (i) the chloride ion, \(\ce{Cl-}\), and (ii) the iron(II) ion, \(\ce{Fe^2+}\). (2 marks)

(d) The first four ionisation energies of element Q, in kJ mol⁻¹, are 590, 1150, 4940 and 6480.

(i) State the group of the Periodic Table that contains Q, and explain how the data show this. (2 marks)

(ii) Write the equation, with state symbols, that represents the second ionisation energy of Q. (1 mark)

(e) An oxide of copper contains 3.17 g of copper combined with 0.400 g of oxygen. Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide. (2 marks)
A: (a) atoms of the same element (with the same number of protons / same proton number) [B1]
with different numbers of neutrons (different nucleon numbers / masses) [B1]

(b) \(A_r = (28 \times 92.2 + 29 \times 4.7 + 30 \times 3.1)/100\) [M1]
\(= 2810.9/100 = 28.1\) [A1]

(c) (i) 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ [B1]
(ii) 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ [B1] (24 electrons in total: the 4s electrons are lost first)

(d) (i) Group 2 [B1], because there is a very large increase between the second and third ionisation energies, so the third electron is removed from an inner shell (closer to the nucleus) and Q has two outer electrons [B1]
(ii) \(\ce{Q+(g) -> Q^2+(g) + e-}\) [B1] (the state symbols and the charge must both be correct)

(e) \(n(\ce{Cu}) = 3.17/63.5 = 0.0499\) mol and \(n(\ce{O}) = 0.400/16.0 = 0.0250\) mol, giving Cu : O = 2 : 1 [M1]
the empirical formula is \(\ce{Cu2O}\) [A1]

Q: [13 marks] (#chem.energetics) **Energetics and equilibria.**

(a) 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid at 20.5 °C was mixed with 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide, also at 20.5 °C, in a polystyrene cup. The highest temperature reached was 27.3 °C. Assume that the density of the solutions is 1.00 g cm⁻³ and that their specific heat capacity is 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation, in kJ mol⁻¹ of water formed, including its sign. (4 marks)

(b) The standard enthalpy changes of combustion of carbon, hydrogen and methanol, \(\ce{CH3OH(l)}\), are −394, −286 and −726 kJ mol⁻¹ respectively. Use these data to calculate the standard enthalpy change of formation of methanol. (3 marks)

(c) Hydrogen and iodine react reversibly:

\[\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}\]

0.500 mol of hydrogen and 0.500 mol of iodine were sealed in a 2.00 dm³ vessel. At equilibrium the vessel contained 0.790 mol of hydrogen iodide. Write the expression for \(K_c\), and calculate its value, stating its units if it has any. (4 marks)

(d) The forward reaction in (c) is exothermic. State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the position of equilibrium and on the value of \(K_c\), and state the effect of increasing the pressure on the position of equilibrium. (2 marks)
A: (a) \(\Delta T = 27.3 - 20.5 = 6.8\) K and the total mass is 100 g [B1]
\(q = mc\Delta T = 100 \times 4.18 \times 6.8 = 2842\) J [M1]
\(n(\ce{H2O}) = 1.00 \times 50.0/1000 = 0.0500\) mol [M1]
\(\Delta H = -2842/0.0500 = -56\,800\) J mol⁻¹ \(= -56.8\) kJ mol⁻¹ (negative because the temperature rose) [A1]

(b) \(\ce{C(s) + 2H2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) -> CH3OH(l)}\); the enthalpy change of formation is the sum of the combustion values of the elements minus that of methanol (a Hess's law cycle) [M1]
\(\Delta H_{\mathrm{f}} = [-394 + 2(-286)] - (-726)\) [M1]
\(= -240\) kJ mol⁻¹ [A1]

(c) \(K_c = \dfrac{[\ce{HI}]^2}{[\ce{H2}][\ce{I2}]}\) [B1]
\(n(\ce{H2}) = n(\ce{I2}) = 0.500 - 0.790/2 = 0.105\) mol; \([\ce{HI}] = 0.395\) and \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.0525\) mol dm⁻³ [M1]
\(K_c = 0.395^2/(0.0525 \times 0.0525)\) [M1] \(= 56.6\), with no units (they cancel) [A1]

(d) the position moves to the left and \(K_c\) decreases, because the reverse reaction is endothermic and absorbs the extra heat, opposing the change [B1]
a change in pressure has no effect on the position, because there are 2 mol of gas on each side [B1]

Q: [12 marks] (#chem.periodicity) **Inorganic chemistry.**

(a) Magnesium oxide and sulfur dioxide are each added to separate samples of water. For each oxide, write an equation for the reaction and state the approximate pH of the resulting mixture. (4 marks)

(b) Aluminium oxide is amphoteric. State what this term means. (1 mark)

(c) Magnesium carbonate decomposes on heating.

(i) Write the equation for the thermal decomposition of magnesium carbonate. (1 mark)

(ii) 4.21 g of magnesium carbonate is heated until the decomposition is complete. Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed, measured at room conditions. (2 marks)

(iii) Barium carbonate decomposes only at a much higher temperature than magnesium carbonate. Explain why. (2 marks)

(d) A colourless solution of a sodium halide gives a cream precipitate when acidified aqueous silver nitrate is added.

(i) Name the halide ion present. (1 mark)

(ii) Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for the formation of the precipitate. (1 mark)
A: (a) \(\ce{MgO(s) + H2O(l) -> Mg(OH)2(s)}\) [B1]; pH about 9 to 10 (weakly alkaline, as the hydroxide is only slightly soluble) [B1]
\(\ce{SO2(g) + H2O(l) -> H2SO3(aq)}\) [B1]; pH about 2 to 3 (acidic) [B1]

(b) it reacts with both acids and bases (it dissolves in dilute acid and in aqueous sodium hydroxide) [B1]

(c) (i) \(\ce{MgCO3(s) -> MgO(s) + CO2(g)}\) [B1]
(ii) \(n(\ce{MgCO3}) = 4.21/84.3 = 0.0499\) mol, and \(n(\ce{CO2}) = 0.0499\) mol [M1]
\(V = 0.0499 \times 24.0 = 1.20\) dm³ [A1]
(iii) the \(\ce{Ba^2+}\) ion is larger than the \(\ce{Mg^2+}\) ion, so it has a lower charge density and polarises the carbonate ion much less [B1]
the C–O bond in the carbonate ion is weakened less, so more energy (a higher temperature) is needed to decompose it [B1]

(d) (i) bromide, \(\ce{Br-}\) [B1]
(ii) \(\ce{Ag+(aq) + Br-(aq) -> AgBr(s)}\) [B1]

Q: [12 marks] (#chem.org.hydrocarbons) **Organic chemistry.**

Propene can be converted into propanone in three steps.

\[\ce{CH3CH=CH2 ->[\text{step 1}] CH3CHBrCH3 ->[\text{step 2}] CH3CH(OH)CH3 ->[\text{step 3}] CH3COCH3}\]

(a) Give the reagent and conditions for step 1. (2 marks)

(b) Describe the mechanism of step 1. Name the mechanism, describe the curly arrows and the intermediate, and explain why \(\ce{CH3CHBrCH3}\) is the major product rather than 1-bromopropane. (4 marks)

(c) Give the reagent and conditions for step 2. (2 marks)

(d) Give the reagent and conditions for step 3, and state the colour change seen. (3 marks)

(e) Name the aldehyde that is a structural isomer of propanone. (1 mark)
A: (a) hydrogen bromide, HBr(g) [B1], at room temperature [B1]

(b) electrophilic addition [B1]
a curly arrow from the C=C double bond to the hydrogen of HBr (the δ+ end), and a curly arrow from the H–Br bond to the bromine atom [B1]
the intermediate is the secondary carbocation \(\ce{CH3CH^+CH3}\), which then reacts with \(\ce{Br-}\) (lone pair arrow from \(\ce{Br-}\) to \(\ce{C+}\)) [B1]
the secondary carbocation is more stable than the primary carbocation that would give 1-bromopropane, because two alkyl groups push electron density towards the positive carbon (inductive effect) [B1]

(c) aqueous sodium hydroxide, NaOH(aq) [B1], heated (under reflux) [B1]

(d) acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (\(\ce{K2Cr2O7}\) and dilute \(\ce{H2SO4}\)) [B1], heat [B1] (allow acidified potassium manganate(VII))
orange to green [B1] (allow purple to colourless if acidified manganate(VII) is given)

(e) propanal [B1]

Q: [12 marks] (#chem.analysis) **Analysis and synthesis.**

Compound W has the molecular formula \(\ce{C_xH_yO}\). Its properties are:

- the mass spectrum has a molecular ion peak at m/z 72 (relative abundance 100) and a peak at m/z 73 (relative abundance 4.4)
- the mass spectrum also has peaks at m/z 57 and m/z 43
- the infrared spectrum has a strong absorption at 1700 cm⁻¹ and C–H absorptions at 2850–2950 cm⁻¹, but no other absorption between 2500 and 3600 cm⁻¹
- it does not react with Fehling's solution

(a) Use the peak at m/z 73 to calculate the number of carbon atoms in one molecule of W. (2 marks)

(b) Deduce the molecular formula of W. (2 marks)

(c) Use the infrared data and the absorption ranges below to explain what the infrared spectrum shows about W. (3 marks)

Infrared absorption ranges in cm⁻¹: C=O (carbonyl and carboxyl) 1670–1740; C=O (ester) 1710–1750; O–H (carboxylic acid) 2500–3000; O–H (alcohol) 3200–3600; C–H (alkane) 2850–2950.

(d) Use the other information to deduce the structure of W. (2 marks)

(e) Suggest a two-step synthesis of W from but-1-ene. For each step, give the reagent and conditions, and name the intermediate organic compound. (3 marks)
A: (a) number of carbon atoms \(= 100 \times 4.4/(1.1 \times 100)\) [M1] \(= 4\) [A1]

(b) four carbon atoms have a mass of 48 and one oxygen atom (from the C=O seen in (c)) has a mass of 16, so the hydrogen atoms account for \(72 - 48 - 16 = 8\) [M1]
\(\ce{C4H8O}\) [A1]

(c) the absorption at 1700 cm⁻¹ is a C=O bond (carbonyl, 1670–1740 cm⁻¹) [B1]
there is no broad O–H absorption (2500–3000 or 3200–3600 cm⁻¹), so W is not a carboxylic acid or an alcohol [B1]
W has only one oxygen atom, so it cannot be an ester (which needs two, and whose C=O range 1710–1750 cm⁻¹ does not include 1700 cm⁻¹): W is an aldehyde or a ketone [B1]

(d) it does not react with Fehling's solution, so it is a ketone and not an aldehyde [B1]
the peak at m/z 57 is \(\ce{CH3CH2CO+}\) and the peak at m/z 43 is \(\ce{CH3CO+}\), so W is butanone, \(\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}\) [B1]

(e) step 1: steam, \(\ce{H3PO4}\) catalyst (high temperature and pressure) [B1], giving butan-2-ol, \(\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}\) [B1]
step 2: acidified potassium dichromate(VI), heat (under reflux) [B1], which oxidises the secondary alcohol to the ketone, butanone
