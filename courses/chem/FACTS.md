## 9701 papers in detail (syllabus section 4, verified)
- P1 Multiple Choice: 40 four-choice questions, AS content, AO1 50% / AO2 50%.
- P2 AS Level Structured Questions: AS content, AO1/AO2.
- P3 Advanced Practical Skills: practical test, AO3 only. Mark grid (minimum): manipulation, measurement and observation 12; presentation of data and observations 6; analysis, conclusions and evaluation 10; the remaining 12 vary from paper to paper.
- P4 A Level Structured Questions: A Level content, AS knowledge required, AO1/AO2.
- P5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation: two or more questions totalling 30 marks, AO3 only, no laboratory; context may be outside the syllabus. Mark grid (minimum): planning (defining the problem, method) 12; analysis, conclusion and evaluation 12; remaining 6 vary.
- Routes: AS only (P1, P2, P3); staged A Level (AS in year 1, then P4 and P5); all five in one series. AS grades a–e; A Level A*–E.
- Assessment objectives: AO1 knowledge and understanding 40%, AO2 handling, applying and evaluating information 40%, AO3 experimental skills and investigations 20% (both AS and A Level).
- Command words (syllabus list): analyse, calculate, compare, consider, contrast, deduce, define, demonstrate, describe, determine, discuss, evaluate, examine, explain, give, identify, justify, predict, show (that), sketch, state, suggest.
- Units and s.f.: misuse of units or significant figures, units on ratios, or wrong s.f. may be penalised. Answers to the same number of s.f. as (or one more than) the least precise data.
- Decimal marker is a dot on the line. Sulfur spelt with f; the traditional names sulfite, nitrite, sulfur trioxide, sulfurous acid and nitrous acid are used.

## Practical skills facts (syllabus section 5, verified)
- Burette readings to the nearest 0.05 cm³; a thermometer at 1 °C intervals read to the nearest 0.5 °C; a measuring cylinder at 1.0 cm³ read to the nearest 0.5 cm³.
- Concordant titres: two titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other. A rough titration first.
- Maximum uncertainty = half the difference between the closest calibrations (thermometer ±0.5 °C); % error in a temperature change of 14.0 °C = (2 × 0.5)/14.0 × 100 = 7.14%.
- Calculated values to the same number of s.f. as, or one more than, the least precise data (e.g. titre 23.45 cm³ → 4 s.f.; 0.100 mol dm⁻³ given → 3 or 4 s.f.).
- Tables: one table, headings with units as volume / cm³, volume (cm³) or volume in cm³; raw data to the same precision.
- Graphs: scales of 1, 2 or 5 units to a 20 mm square; points fill at least half the grid each way; plot × or ⊙; gradient from two points more than half the line apart; mark anomalies.
- Errors: "human error" is not acceptable; distinguish systematic (zero error on a balance) from random (room temperature changes).
- Required titrations: acid–alkali (strong/weak), KMnO₄ with H₂O₂, Fe²⁺ or ethanedioic acid/its salts, thiosulfate–iodine. Also rates (thiosulfate + acid, cross obscured), gravimetric (heating a hydrated salt to constant mass in a crucible), thermometric (ΔH, Hess's law), gas collection over water (carbonate + acid).
- Indicators in the materials list: methyl orange (pH 2.9–4.6), bromophenol blue (3.0–4.5), thymol blue (8.0–9.6), thymolphthalein (9.3–10.5). (Phenolphthalein is not on the 9701 list; do not state its range as a Cambridge fact.)
- Organic tests in P3: Fehling's (orange/red ppt., aldehyde); Tollens' (silver mirror/black ppt., aldehyde); alkaline aqueous iodine (yellow ppt., CH₃CO or CH₃CH(OH)); acidified KMnO₄ purple → colourless (oxidisable compound).

## Syllabus learning outcomes
One line per sub-topic; numbers are the syllabus outcome numbers. Topics 1–22 AS, 23–37 A Level.

### 1 Atomic structure
- 1.1 Particles, atomic radius: 1 atom mostly empty space, small dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, electrons in shells; 2 relative charges and masses of p, n, e; 3 atomic/proton number, mass/nucleon number; 4 distribution of mass and charge in an atom; 5 behaviour of beams of p, n, e at the same velocity in an electric field; 6 numbers of p, n, e in atoms and ions; 7 state and explain qualitatively variations in atomic and ionic radius across a period and down a group.
- 1.2 Isotopes: 1 define isotope (protons and neutrons); 2 notation ᴬ_Z X (x = nucleon number, y = proton number); 3 same chemical properties, explained; 4 different physical properties, limited to mass and density.
- 1.3 Electrons, energy levels, orbitals (ground state, H to Kr only): 1 shells, sub-shells, orbitals, principal quantum number n, ground state; 2 number of orbitals in s, p, d and electrons they hold; 3 order of increasing energy of sub-shells in the first three shells and 4s, 4p; 4 configurations: electrons per shell, sub-shell, orbital; 5 explain configurations by energy and inter-electron repulsion; 6 full (1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d⁶4s²) and shorthand ([Ar] 3d⁶4s²) configurations of atoms and ions; 7 electrons-in-boxes notation; 8 describe and sketch shapes of s and p orbitals; 9 free radical = species with one or more unpaired electrons.
- 1.4 Ionisation energy (H to Kr): 1 define and use first IE; 2 equations for first, second and subsequent IEs; 3 trends across a period and down a group; 4 variation in successive IEs; 5 IE due to attraction between nucleus and outer electron; 6 factors: nuclear charge, atomic/ionic radius, shielding by inner shells and sub-shells, spin-pair repulsion; 7 deduce configurations from successive IE data; 8 deduce position in the Periodic Table from successive IE data.

### 2 Atoms, molecules and stoichiometry
- 2.1 Relative masses: 1 unified atomic mass unit = 1/12 mass of a carbon-12 atom; 2 define Ar, relative isotopic mass, Mr, relative formula mass in terms of the unified atomic mass unit.
- 2.2 Mole: 1 define and use the mole in terms of the Avogadro constant.
- 2.3 Formulas: 1 formulas of ionic compounds from ionic charges and oxidation numbers (Roman numerals), (a) predict ionic charge from position in the Periodic Table, (b) recall NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻, OH⁻, NH₄⁺, Zn²⁺, Ag⁺, HCO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻; 2 (a) balanced equations including ionic equations without spectator ions, (b) state symbols; 3 empirical and molecular formula; 4 anhydrous, hydrated, water of crystallisation; 5 calculate empirical and molecular formulas.
- 2.4 Reacting masses and volumes: 1 mole calculations (a) reacting masses incl. percentage yield, (b) volumes of gases (e.g. burning hydrocarbons), (c) volumes and concentrations of solutions, (d) limiting and excess reagent, (e) deduce stoichiometric relationships; answers to the s.f. given or asked for.

### 3 Chemical bonding
- 3.1 Electronegativity: 1 define electronegativity (power of an atom to attract electrons to itself); 2 factors: nuclear charge, atomic radius, shielding; 3 trends across a period and down a group; 4 use Pauling differences to predict ionic or covalent bonding (covalent character in ionic compounds not assessed).
- 3.2 Ionic bonding: 1 define (electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions); 2 describe NaCl, MgO, CaF₂.
- 3.3 Metallic bonding: 1 define (electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons).
- 3.4 Covalent and coordinate bonding: 1 define covalent bond (electrostatic attraction between the nuclei of two atoms and a shared pair of electrons); (a) H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, HCl, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄; (b) Period 3 expanded octets: SO₂, PCl₅, SF₆; (c) coordinate bonding in NH₄⁺ (NH₃ + HCl gases) and Al₂Cl₆; 2 (a) σ bonds (direct overlap) and π bonds (sideways overlap of adjacent p orbitals above and below the σ bond), (b) σ and π in H₂, C₂H₆, C₂H₄, HCN, N₂, (c) hybridisation sp, sp², sp³; 3 (a) define bond energy (energy required to break one mole of a particular covalent bond in the gaseous state) and bond length (internuclear distance of two covalently bonded atoms), (b) use bond energy and bond length to compare reactivity.
- 3.5 Shapes: 1 VSEPR shapes and angles: BF₃ trigonal planar 120°, CO₂ linear 180°, CH₄ tetrahedral 109.5°, NH₃ pyramidal 107°, H₂O non-linear 104.5°, SF₆ octahedral 90°, PF₅ trigonal bipyramidal 120° and 90°; 2 predict shapes and angles of analogous molecules and ions.
- 3.6 Intermolecular forces: 1 (a) hydrogen bonding limited to N–H and O–H, NH₃ and H₂O, (b) anomalous properties of water: high m.p./b.p., high surface tension, ice less dense than water; 2 bond polarity and dipole moments from electronegativity; 3 (a) van der Waals' forces = generic term for all intermolecular forces, (b) id-id (London dispersion) and pd-pd including hydrogen bonding, (c) hydrogen bonding as a special case of pd-pd where H is bonded to a highly electronegative atom; 4 ionic, covalent and metallic bonding generally stronger than intermolecular forces.
- 3.7 Dot-and-cross: 1 dot-and-cross diagrams for ionic, covalent and coordinate bonding, all compounds in 3.4 and 3.5, including expanded octets and odd-electron species.

### 4 States of matter
- 4.1 Gases: 1 pressure from collisions of molecules with the container walls; 2 ideal gas: zero particle volume, no intermolecular forces; 3 pV = nRT in calculations, including finding Mr.
- 4.2 Bonding and structure: 1 lattice structures: (a) giant ionic NaCl, MgO, (b) simple molecular I₂, C₆₀, ice, (c) giant molecular SiO₂, graphite, diamond, (d) giant metallic Cu; 2 effect of structure and bonding on m.p., b.p., electrical conductivity, solubility; 3 deduce structure and bonding from given information.

### 5 Chemical energetics
- 5.1 Enthalpy change: 1 exothermic (ΔH negative), endothermic (ΔH positive); 2 reaction pathway diagrams with ΔH and activation energy; 3 (a) standard conditions 298 K and 101 kPa, symbol ⦵, (b) ΔH of reaction ΔHr, formation ΔHf, combustion ΔHc, neutralisation ΔHneut; 4 energy changes from breaking and making bonds; 5 bond energies (ΔH positive for bond breaking) to calculate ΔHr; 6 some bond energies exact, some averages; 7 ΔH from experimental results, q = mcΔT and ΔH = −mcΔT/n.
- 5.2 Hess's law: 1 construct simple energy cycles; 2 calculations with cycles: (a) ΔH that cannot be found by experiment, (b) bond energy data.

### 6 Electrochemistry (AS: redox)
- 6.1 Redox: 1 oxidation numbers of elements in compounds and ions; 2 use changes in oxidation number to balance equations; 3 redox, oxidation, reduction, disproportionation by electron transfer and oxidation number; 4 oxidising agent, reducing agent; 5 Roman numerals for oxidation numbers.

### 7 Equilibria
- 7.1 Chemical equilibria: 1 (a) reversible reaction, (b) dynamic equilibrium: rates of forward and reverse reactions equal, concentrations constant, (c) need for a closed system; 2 Le Chatelier's principle: if a change is made to a system at dynamic equilibrium, the position of equilibrium moves to minimise this change; 3 qualitative effects of temperature, concentration, pressure, catalyst; 4 Kc expressions; 5 mole fraction and partial pressure; 6 Kp expressions (Kp–Kc relationship not required); 7 Kc and Kp calculations (no quadratics); 8 quantities at equilibrium from data; 9 which changes affect the value of K (only temperature); 10 conditions of the Haber and Contact processes.
- 7.2 Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases: 1 HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, CH₃COOH; 2 NaOH, KOH, NH₃; 3 Brønsted–Lowry theory; 4 strong = fully dissociated, weak = partially dissociated in aqueous solution; 5 water pH 7, acids below 7, alkalis above 7; 6 strong vs weak acids: reaction with a reactive metal, pH (pH meter, universal indicator), conductivity; 7 neutralisation H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l); 8 salts form in neutralisation; 9 sketch pH titration curves for strong/weak acid with strong/weak alkali; 10 choose indicators from data (no pKa).

### 8 Reaction kinetics
- 8.1 Rate: 1 rate of reaction, frequency of collisions, effective and non-effective collisions; 2 effect of concentration and pressure (frequency of effective collisions); 3 calculate rate from experimental data.
- 8.2 Temperature and activation energy: 1 EA = minimum energy required for a collision to be effective; 2 sketch and use the Boltzmann distribution; 3 effect of temperature by Boltzmann distribution and collision frequency.
- 8.3 Catalysts: 1 (a) catalyst gives a different mechanism of lower activation energy, (b) explain with the Boltzmann distribution, (c) reaction pathway diagram with and without a catalyst.

### 9 The Periodic Table: chemical periodicity
- 9.1 Physical properties (Period 3): 1 atomic radius, ionic radius, m.p., electrical conductivity; 2 explain m.p. and conductivity by structure and bonding.
- 9.2 Chemical properties (Period 3): 1 reactions with oxygen (Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂), chlorine (NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅) and water (Na and Mg only); 2 oxidation numbers of oxides (Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃) and chlorides (NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅) from valence electrons; 3 oxides Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃ with water and pHs; 4 acid/base behaviour of oxides Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃ and hydroxides NaOH, Mg(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃, amphoterism (with NaOH only); 5 chlorides NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅ with water and pHs; 6 explain trends by bonding and electronegativity; 7 suggest bonding in chlorides and oxides from properties.
- 9.3 Other elements: 1 predict properties of an element from its group; 2 deduce nature, position and identity of unknown elements from data.

### 10 Group 2 (AS)
- 10.1 Mg to Ba: 1 reactions of the elements with oxygen, water, dilute HCl and H₂SO₄; 2 reactions of oxides, hydroxides and carbonates with water and dilute HCl and H₂SO₄; 3 thermal decomposition of nitrates and carbonates and the trend in thermal stability; 4 describe and predict trends in properties of these elements and compounds; 5 variation in solubility of the hydroxides and sulfates.

### 11 Group 17

### 12 Nitrogen and sulfur
- 12.1: 1 lack of reactivity of N₂ (triple bond strength, lack of polarity); 2 (a) basicity of NH₃ (Brønsted–Lowry), (b) structure of NH₄⁺ and its formation by acid–base reaction, (c) displacement of NH₃ from ammonium salts; 3 natural and man-made oxides of nitrogen and their catalytic removal from exhaust gases; 4 NO and NO₂ with unburned hydrocarbons form PAN (photochemical smog); 5 NO and NO₂ in acid rain, directly and as catalysts in oxidation of atmospheric SO₂.

### 13 Introduction to AS organic chemistry
- Conventions: X = halogen; R, R′ alkyl groups (or H).
