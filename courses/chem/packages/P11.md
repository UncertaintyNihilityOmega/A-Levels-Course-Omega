## chem.org.carbonyl · Aldehydes and ketones (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 17. Warm-up from [Alcohols](#chem.org.alcohols) (oxidation) and [Introduction to organic chemistry](#chem.org.intro).
Outcomes (checklist):
- 17.1 1 made by oxidation with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄ and distillation of (a) primary alcohols → aldehydes,
  (b) secondary alcohols → ketones; 2 (a) reduction by NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄ to alcohols (aldehyde → primary, ketone →
  secondary; [H] in equations), (b) HCN with KCN catalyst and heat → hydroxynitriles (ethanal → 2-hydroxypropanenitrile;
  propanone → 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile); 3 mechanism of nucleophilic addition of HCN: CN⁻ (from KCN) attacks the
  δ+ carbonyl carbon (arrow from the lone pair on C of CN⁻ to C; arrow from the C=O π bond to O), giving an alkoxide
  intermediate O⁻; then the O⁻ takes H⁺ from HCN (arrow from the O⁻ lone pair to the H of H–CN and from the H–CN bond to C),
  regenerating CN⁻ (so KCN is a catalyst); 4 2,4-DNPH test: orange (or yellow-orange) precipitate with any carbonyl;
  5 aldehyde vs ketone by Fehling's (blue solution → orange/red precipitate with an aldehyde on warming), Tollens'
  (silver mirror / black precipitate with an aldehyde), and ease of oxidation by acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange → green);
  6 CH₃CO– group by alkaline I₂(aq): yellow tri-iodomethane and RCO₂⁻ (ethanal and methyl ketones such as propanone).
Sections: structure (C=O polar, planar, sp², 120°); naming; physical properties briefly (pd-pd; no H-bonding between
carbonyl molecules, but they H-bond with water); preparation (link the distillation figure in chem.org.alcohols);
reduction (NaBH₄ in water/ethanol; LiAlH₄ in dry ether — dry ether is the standard condition; state it only as
"in dry ether" if you include it); nucleophilic addition: the mechanism figure (hand SVG `fig`, two rows like the gold
SN1 figure, propanone + CN⁻ → intermediate; intermediate + HCN → product + CN⁻); a note that addition to an aldehyde
other than methanal creates a chiral centre and the product is racemic because the planar C=O is attacked equally
from both sides (link [Isomerism](#chem.org.isomerism)); the tests as a table (reagent, aldehyde result, ketone result)
and `:::lab` boxes; the iodoform reaction for CH₃CO–.
Worked examples (≥6): products of reducing butanone and butanal; product of HCN + propanal (name, chiral?);
mechanism with marks; identify an unknown C₄H₈O from test results (2,4-DNPH positive, Tollens' negative, iodoform
positive → butanone); safety context: HCN is extremely toxic, so it is generated in situ from KCN and acid (keep it
brief and factual).
Pitfalls: "ketones give a silver mirror"; arrow from the O of C=O; forgetting to regenerate CN⁻; calling NaBH₄ an
oxidising agent; Fehling's colour change described as "blue to red solution".

## chem.org.acids · Carboxylic acids, esters and nitriles (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topics 18 and 19 (AS). The A Level parts (acyl chlorides, benzoic acid, further oxidation, relative acidities) are in
[Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids](#chem.org.acyl) and [Phenol and the acidity of organic compounds](#chem.org.phenol).
Warm-up from [Alcohols](#chem.org.alcohols), [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen) and [Acids and bases](#chem.acids). Outcomes:
- 18.1 1 carboxylic acids made by (a) oxidation of primary alcohols and aldehydes with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄ under
  reflux, (b) hydrolysis of nitriles with dilute acid or dilute alkali, then acidification, (c) hydrolysis of esters with
  dilute acid or dilute alkali and heat, then acidification; 2 (a) with reactive metals → salt + H₂ (redox),
  (b) with alkalis → salt + H₂O, (c) with carbonates → salt + H₂O + CO₂ (effervescence: test for COOH), (d) esterification
  with alcohols, concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst, (e) reduction by LiAlH₄ to a primary alcohol.
- 18.2 1 esters made by condensation of an alcohol and a carboxylic acid with concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst;
  2 hydrolysis by dilute acid (reversible, gives acid + alcohol) and by dilute alkali and heat (goes to completion, gives
  the carboxylate salt + alcohol; acidify to obtain the acid).
- 19.1 1 primary amines from halogenoalkane + NH₃ in ethanol heated under pressure (classification not tested at AS).
- 19.2 1 nitriles from halogenoalkane + KCN in ethanol and heat; 2 hydroxynitriles from aldehydes/ketones + HCN, KCN
  catalyst, heat; 3 hydrolysis of nitriles with dilute acid (→ RCOOH + NH₄⁺) or dilute alkali (→ RCOO⁻ + NH₃), then
  acidification → carboxylic acid.
Sections: structure and naming (ethanoic acid, propanoic acid, methanoic acid; carboxylate ions; esters named alkyl
alkanoate from the alcohol and acid parts: a figure of methyl propanoate showing which part comes from which);
acidity as weak acids (link [Acids and bases](#chem.acids); the full explanation of relative acidity is A Level);
reactions with equations; esterification as a reversible condensation (Le Chatelier link: excess alcohol, removing
water); ester uses (flavourings, solvents, perfumes) with care; hydrolysis acid vs alkali (soap context: alkaline
hydrolysis of fats is saponification — mention briefly and accurately); nitriles and hydroxynitriles as routes that
add a carbon; amines from halogenoalkanes (link the gold chapter).
Worked examples (≥6): name and draw the ester from propan-1-ol + ethanoic acid (propyl ethanoate); products of alkaline
hydrolysis of ethyl ethanoate; route from bromoethane to propanoic acid (link the gold chapter); equation for ethanoic
acid + sodium carbonate; percentage yield of an esterification; deduce a carboxylic acid from a titration with NaOH
(Mr from moles; link [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles)).
Pitfalls: naming esters the wrong way round; "esters are acidic"; forgetting acidification after alkaline hydrolysis;
H₂SO₄ described as a reactant; nitrile hydrolysis products swapped between acid and alkali conditions.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
