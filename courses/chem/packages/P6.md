## chem.acids · Acids and bases (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 7.2 (AS). pH calculations, Ka and buffers are A Level: [pH, buffers, solubility product and partition](#chem.acids2)
(link forward, do not teach them here). Warm-up from [Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria) and
[Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles). Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 7.2 1 common acids HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, CH₃COOH; 2 common alkalis NaOH, KOH, NH₃; 3 Brønsted–Lowry theory (acid =
  proton donor, base = proton acceptor); 4 strong acids and bases fully dissociated in aqueous solution, weak ones
  partially; 5 water pH 7, acids below 7, alkalis above 7; 6 strong vs weak acids: reaction with a reactive metal
  (rate of H₂ bubbles, e.g. magnesium), pH (pH meter or universal indicator), electrical conductivity, at the same
  concentration; 7 neutralisation H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l); 8 salts form in neutralisation; 9 sketch pH titration
  curves for strong/weak acid with strong/weak alkali; 10 choose indicators from data (pKa not used).
Sections: Brønsted–Lowry with equations (HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻; NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻; water acting as both);
strong vs weak with equilibrium arrows (CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺, position far to the left); "strong" vs "concentrated"
(`:::warn`); comparing strong and weak acids by experiment (same concentration: weak gives higher pH, slower H₂,
lower conductivity, but the same total volume of H₂ with excess metal — a good exam point); neutralisation and salts
(sulfates, chlorides, nitrates, ethanoates; ammonium salts from ammonia); titration curves: four sketches (strong
acid–strong base, strong–weak, weak–strong, weak–weak) with the vertical section and equivalence point labelled —
embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-titration" data-acid="strong" data-base="strong"></div>` and also give one
hand SVG or `:::plot` of the strong acid–strong base curve so the page works without it; indicators: an indicator
changes colour over a range; choose one whose range lies within the vertical part. Use only the 9701 list in FACTS.md
(methyl orange 2.9–4.6, bromophenol blue 3.0–4.5, thymol blue 8.0–9.6, thymolphthalein 9.3–10.5); phenolphthalein is
not on the 9701 materials list, so do not give its range as a Cambridge fact. Weak acid–weak base: no sharp end
point, no suitable indicator (use a pH meter).
Worked examples (≥6, one real context such as antacid tablets neutralising stomach acid or vinegar titration):
identify the Brønsted–Lowry acid and base in an equation (conjugate pairs are A Level, 25.1: leave them for chem.acids2); explain a pH
difference at equal concentration; choose an indicator for NaOH + ethanoic acid (vertical section about pH 7–11:
thymol blue or thymolphthalein); a titration calculation (link back to moles) with a diprotic acid.
Pitfalls: "weak acid = dilute acid"; H₂SO₄ as a weak acid; choosing methyl orange for weak acid–strong base;
neutral at the equivalence point for weak/strong cases (it is not 7).
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.kinetics · Reaction kinetics (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 8. Warm-up from [Chemical energetics](#chem.energetics) (pathway diagrams) and [Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria).
Outcomes:
- 8.1 1 rate of reaction, frequency of collisions, effective and non-effective collisions; 2 effect of concentration and
  pressure by the frequency of effective collisions; 3 calculate rate from experimental data (rate = change in amount
  or concentration ÷ time; gradient of a tangent on a concentration–time or volume–time graph; 1/time for clock reactions).
- 8.2 1 activation energy Eₐ = the minimum energy required for a collision to be effective; 2 sketch and use the
  Boltzmann distribution; 3 effect of temperature by the Boltzmann distribution and the frequency of effective collisions.
- 8.3 1 catalyst and catalysis: (a) a catalyst provides a different mechanism of lower activation energy, (b) explain with
  the Boltzmann distribution, (c) pathway diagrams with and without a catalyst.
Sections: measuring rates (gas syringe, mass loss, colour change, the thiosulfate cross-disappearing experiment from
FACTS.md practical facts; link [Practical skills and qualitative analysis](#chem.practical)); rate from a graph (a
`:::plot` of volume of gas against time, e.g. `--f "60*(1-exp(-x/40))" --x 0 200 --y 0 80` with a tangent drawn as a
second `--f` line at one point, labelled; compute the gradient in the check script); collision theory: frequency,
orientation, energy; concentration and pressure; surface area (more collisions); the Boltzmann distribution (figure via
`:::plot` of `x**0.5*exp(-x)`-like shape is acceptable; better: embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-boltzmann" data-t1="300" data-t2="310"></div>`
and give one hand SVG or plot with Eₐ marked and the area to the right shaded (`--shade`)); key features: starts at
the origin, no maximum energy, asymptotic to the axis, area = total number of molecules, at a higher T the peak moves to
higher energy and lower height with the same area; temperature: more molecules with E ≥ Eₐ (the main reason) and more
frequent collisions; catalysts: pathway diagram with and without (reuse `chem-profile` with `data-cat="1"`), Boltzmann
with the lower Eₐ marked (more molecules exceed it); homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts in A Level
([Rate equations and catalysis](#chem.rates2)) — mention only.
Worked examples (≥6): rate from a table of gas volumes; initial rate from a tangent; explain doubling concentration
qualitatively; why a 10 °C rise can roughly double the rate (state only as "often", qualitatively, no rule as fact);
sketch a Boltzmann curve at two temperatures (describe); catalyst explanation for full marks.
Pitfalls: "molecules collide more often" as the main reason for the temperature effect (the main reason is the larger
fraction with E ≥ Eₐ); the higher-T curve drawn with a larger area or crossing the axis; "a catalyst gives the molecules
more energy"; a catalyst "is not used up" without mentioning the different pathway with lower Eₐ.
Board box: leave it out (the Arrhenius-equation difference belongs in [Rate equations and catalysis](#chem.rates2)).
`Next: [Periodicity: Period 3](#chem.periodicity)`.

## Widget `chem-titration` (widgets/chem-titration.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-titration" data-acid="strong" data-base="strong" data-ca="0.100" data-cb="0.100" data-va="25.0"></div>`.
Also used by A Level ([pH, buffers, solubility product and partition](#chem.acids2)) with `data-pka="4.76"`.
- Controls: acid strong/weak, base strong/weak, acid concentration and volume in the flask, base concentration in the
  burette (0.010–1.00 mol dm⁻³), optional pKa for a weak acid (default 4.76, labelled "pKa" only on the A Level page:
  when `data-pka` is absent show "weak acid (like ethanoic acid)") and pKb-equivalent strength for a weak base (fixed
  like ammonia; do not display a Kb value, Kb is not on the syllabus).
- Curve: pH (0–14) against volume of base added (0 to 2 × equivalence), computed properly: strong/strong exact charge
  balance with Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴; weak acid by solving the charge-balance cubic numerically (bisection on [H⁺]); never
  the "half-way = pKa" shortcut alone. Mark the equivalence volume and pH; mark the half-equivalence point when weak
  acid (pH = pKa, A Level).
- Indicator select (none, methyl orange 2.9–4.6, bromophenol blue 3.0–4.5, thymol blue 8.0–9.6, thymolphthalein
  9.3–10.5): its range drawn as a shaded band; a note says whether it is suitable (its range lies within the steep part).
- Readout: equivalence volume (cm³, 3 s.f.), pH at equivalence (2 d.p.), initial pH.
- Probe defaults, weak/strong, weak/weak, `data-pka="9"`, `data-ca="0"` (friendly message).

## Widget `chem-boltzmann` (widgets/chem-boltzmann.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-boltzmann" data-t1="300" data-t2="320" data-ea="50"></div>`.
- Curves of the energy distribution of molecules (shape f(E) ∝ √E · e^(−E/RT), normalised to the same area) at T₁ and
  (checkbox) T₂ (250–600 K sliders); Eₐ slider (kJ mol⁻¹, 10–100) drawn as a vertical line; area with E ≥ Eₐ shaded for
  each curve; checkbox "catalyst" adds a second line at a lower Eₐ (slider or 60% of Eₐ).
- Readout: fraction of molecules with E ≥ Eₐ for each curve (computed by numerical integration; show as % to 3 s.f.)
  and the ratio between T₂ and T₁. Axes "energy" and "number of molecules", no y numbers. Axis range follows the values.
- `.w-note`: "Raise T₂ by 10 K and compare the shaded areas."
- Probe defaults, `data-ea="100"`, `data-t1="600" data-t2="250"`, `data-ea="x"`.
