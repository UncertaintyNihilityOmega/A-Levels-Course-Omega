## chem.redox · Redox and oxidation numbers (`@time 4–6 hours`)
Topic 6 (AS electrochemistry). Warm-up from [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles) and [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding).
Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 6.1 1 oxidation numbers of elements in compounds and ions; 2 use changes in oxidation number to balance equations;
  3 redox, oxidation, reduction and disproportionation in terms of electron transfer and changes in oxidation number;
  4 oxidising agent and reducing agent; 5 Roman numerals for the magnitude of the oxidation number.
Sections: oxidation and reduction as electron transfer (OIL RIG as an `:::analogy`/tip) and as change in oxidation
number; the oxidation-number rules (`:::key`: elements 0; simple ions = charge; sum = 0 in compounds, = charge in ions;
O −2 except peroxides −1 and with F; H +1 except metal hydrides −1; F always −1; Group 1 +1, Group 2 +2); worked
oxidation numbers in MnO₄⁻ (+7), Cr₂O₇²⁻ (+6), SO₄²⁻ (+6), S₂O₃²⁻ (+2 average), NH₄⁺ (−3), H₂O₂ (−1); naming with
Roman numerals (iron(III) chloride, manganate(VII), chlorate(I) for ClO⁻, chlorate(V) for ClO₃⁻); half-equations
(balance atoms, then O with H₂O, H with H⁺, then charge with electrons) and combining them; balancing a full equation
by oxidation-number change (e.g. MnO₄⁻ with Fe²⁺ in acid: Mn +7 → +2 (gain 5), Fe +2 → +3 (lose 1), so 1 : 5);
oxidising and reducing agents (the oxidising agent is reduced); disproportionation (Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O:
Cl 0 → −1 and +1; link [Group 17: the halogens](#chem.group17); 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂).
Worked examples (≥6): oxidation numbers in five species; name compounds with Roman numerals; write the half-equation
for Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺; combine with Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ to give Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O + 6Fe³⁺; balance the
reaction of copper with concentrated nitric acid by oxidation numbers; identify disproportionation.
Pitfalls: oxidation number written "2+" instead of "+2" (charges are 2+, oxidation numbers +2); calling the species
that is oxidised the oxidising agent; forgetting electrons on the correct side; changing coefficients of H₂O but not H⁺.
Board box: leave it out unless sure. (E⦵ values come in A Level: link [Electrochemistry: electrolysis and electrode potentials](#chem.electrochem).)
`Next: [Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria)`.

## chem.equilibria · Chemical equilibria (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topic 7.1. Warm-up from [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles), [States of matter](#chem.states) (gases) and
[Chemical energetics](#chem.energetics) (sign of ΔH). Outcomes:
- 7.1 1 (a) reversible reaction, (b) dynamic equilibrium: rates of forward and reverse reactions equal, concentrations
  of reactants and products constant, (c) need for a closed system; 2 Le Chatelier's principle, exactly: "if a change is
  made to a system at dynamic equilibrium, the position of equilibrium moves to minimise this change"; 3 qualitative
  effects of temperature, concentration, pressure and a catalyst; 4 Kc expressions; 5 mole fraction and partial
  pressure; 6 Kp expressions (the Kp–Kc relationship is not required); 7 Kc and Kp calculations (no quadratic
  equations); 8 equilibrium quantities from data; 9 only temperature changes the value of K; a catalyst does not
  change K or the position, only the time to reach equilibrium; 10 conditions of the Haber process (iron catalyst,
  about 400–450 °C, about 20 MPa / 200 atm, ΔH negative: the compromise between yield and rate, unreacted gases
  recycled) and the Contact process (V₂O₅ catalyst, about 400–450 °C, about 1–2 atm: the yield is already high, so high
  pressure is not worth its cost).
Sections: reversible reactions and the idea of a dynamic state (concentration–time graph: `:::plot` with two curves
levelling off, e.g. `--f "1-0.6*(1-exp(-x))"` and `--f "0.6*(1-exp(-x))"` with labels, `--legend tr`, headroom);
closed system; Le Chatelier with each change (concentration, pressure with gas moles counted, temperature with ΔH
sign, catalyst) and a summary table; embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-equilibrium" data-system="haber"></div>`;
Kc expressions (homogeneous; pure solids and liquids are left out of Kc when they are not solvents/aqueous — keep to
the Cambridge convention: heterogeneous examples with solids omit the solid); units of Kc worked out from the
expression; ICE tables (COURSE.md format) for calculations from initial amounts and one equilibrium amount; the
volume matters when Δn ≠ 0 (divide moles by V); mole fraction, partial pressure (p_A = x_A × p_total), Kp expressions
and units (Pa or atm as given); Haber and Contact process sections with an industry context box.
Worked examples (≥8): Kc for the esterification CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O from equilibrium moles (no unit);
Kc for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI from initial amounts and the equilibrium amount of HI; Kc with a volume (N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ in 2.00 dm³,
unit mol dm⁻³); Kp from partial pressures (total pressure and mole fractions given); the effect of each change on the
Haber process; why a catalyst does not change Kc; predict the effect of temperature on Kc for an exothermic reaction.
Pitfalls: Le Chatelier answers that say "equilibrium shifts to the right" without saying why (state: "the forward
reaction is exothermic, so increasing T moves the position to the left, in the endothermic direction, to absorb the
added heat; Kc decreases"); using initial instead of equilibrium amounts; forgetting the coefficients as powers;
including a solid in K; "the rate of forward reaction is zero at equilibrium"; mole fraction not summing to 1.
Board box: leave it out unless you are sure of one board and one content difference.
Acids and bases (7.2) are in the next chapter: `Next: [Acids and bases](#chem.acids)`.

## Widget `chem-equilibrium` (widgets/chem-equilibrium.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-equilibrium" data-system="haber"></div>`; `data-tab="kc"` opens the calculator.
Two tabs (W `.w-tabs`):
1. **Le Chatelier**: system select: haber (\(\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}\), forward exothermic, 4 → 2 gas moles), contact
   (\(\ce{2SO2 + O2 <=> 2SO3}\), forward exothermic, 3 → 2), hi (\(\ce{H2 + I2 <=> 2HI}\), 2 → 2 gas moles; show the
   forward reaction as "slightly exothermic" only qualitatively), ester (\(\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH <=> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}\),
   liquids). Buttons: raise/lower temperature, raise/lower pressure (gases only), add/remove a reactant, add/remove a
   product, add catalyst. Show: an arrow for the shift of position (left/right/none), whether Kc changes (only T),
   a bar chart of relative equilibrium amounts before and after (qualitative bars computed from a simple model; label
   "relative amounts (not to scale)"), and one sentence of explanation in Cambridge wording.
2. **Kc from data**: the reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD with a, b, c, d (1–3; set d = 0 to drop D), initial moles of each,
   the equilibrium moles of one chosen species, and the volume in dm³. It builds the ICE table (initial, change,
   equilibrium; moles and concentrations), then Kc (3 s.f.) and its unit (mol dm⁻³ to the power Δn). Validate: no
   negative equilibrium amounts (friendly message).
- Never hard-code colours; bars use W.palette.
- Probe defaults, `data-system="contact"`, `data-tab="kc"`, `data-system="xyz"` (falls back to haber with a note).
