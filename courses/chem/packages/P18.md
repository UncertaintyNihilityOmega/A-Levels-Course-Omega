## chem.org.polymers · Polymerisation (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 35, starting with a short recap of addition polymerisation (topic 20, taught in
[Hydrocarbons: alkanes and alkenes](#chem.org.hydrocarbons)). Warm-up from [Carboxylic acids, esters and nitriles](#chem.org.acids),
[Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids](#chem.org.acyl) and [Amines, amides and amino acids](#chem.org.nitrogen).
Outcomes (checklist):
- 35.1 1 polyesters from (a) a diol + a dicarboxylic acid or a dioyl chloride (e.g. ethane-1,2-diol + benzene-1,4-
  dicarboxylic acid → PET (Terylene), losing H₂O; with a dioyl chloride HCl is lost), (b) a hydroxycarboxylic acid
  (e.g. poly(lactic acid) from 2-hydroxypropanoic acid); 2 polyamides from (a) a diamine + a dicarboxylic acid or a dioyl
  chloride (e.g. 1,6-diaminohexane + hexanedioic acid or hexanedioyl dichloride → nylon-6,6), (b) an aminocarboxylic acid,
  (c) amino acids (proteins; link 34.4); 3 repeat unit from monomer(s); 4 monomer(s) from a section of polymer.
- 35.2 1 predict the type (addition: C=C monomers; condensation: two reactive groups per monomer, small molecule lost);
  2 deduce the type from a section of polymer (all-carbon backbone → addition; ester or amide links → condensation).
- 35.3 1 poly(alkenes) are chemically inert and hard to biodegrade; 2 some polymers can be degraded by light
  (photodegradable); 3 polyesters and polyamides are biodegradable by acidic and alkaline hydrolysis (the ester/amide
  link hydrolyses).
Sections: addition recap; condensation polymerisation idea; polyesters (figure: repeat unit of PET in skeletal form with
the ester link labelled, `fig`; use the hexagon-with-circle for the benzene ring); polyamides (nylon-6,6 repeat unit;
Kevlar can be a stretch only if drawn correctly from benzene-1,4-diamine + benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid);
proteins as polyamides; drawing repeat units `:::steps` (remove H/OH or Cl from the ends, join with the new link, put
square brackets through the link bonds, n outside); identifying monomers from a section (split at each ester or
amide link, add back H and OH); predicting the type; disposal and degradation (landfill, incineration and its
harmful products, recycling as text only; no statistics); hydrolysis of a polyester by alkali (products as salts).
Worked examples (≥6): repeat unit from a diol + a dioyl chloride; monomers from a polyamide section; type of
polymerisation from a section; the polyester from 6-hydroxyhexanoic acid; hydrolysis products of nylon-6,6 with acid and with alkali; explain why PVC is not
biodegradable but a polyester can be hydrolysed.
Pitfalls: repeat units with the lost atoms still there; two repeat units drawn as one; "condensation polymers keep the
double bond"; amide link drawn as –CO–NH₂–.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
`Next: [Organic synthesis (A Level)](#chem.org.synthesis2)`.

## chem.org.synthesis2 · Organic synthesis (A Level) (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 36: the outcomes of 21.1 using all syllabus reactions. Warm-up from [Organic synthesis (AS)](#chem.org.synthesis)
and two A Level organic chapters. Outcomes:
- 36.1 1 for a molecule with several functional groups: (a) identify the groups using syllabus reactions, (b) predict
  properties and reactions; 2 devise multi-step routes; 3 analyse a given route (reaction types, reagents, by-products).
Sections: the full reaction map (embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-orgmap" data-level="a2"></div>`, plus a
Markdown table of the A Level reactions grouped by starting group: arenes, phenol, acyl chlorides, amines, amides,
nitriles; take every reagent and condition word for word from FACTS.md topics 30–35 (`grep -n "^- 3[0-5]" FACTS.md`));
aromatic routes and the order of steps (directing effects; protecting groups are not required by 9701); adding carbon (CN⁻, Friedel–Crafts); reducing agents
compared (NaBH₄, LiAlH₄, H₂/Ni, Sn/HCl); chirality in synthesis (racemic products from planar intermediates; chiral
catalysts: link [Isomerism](#chem.org.isomerism)); identifying unknowns by tests plus spectra (link
[Chromatography and NMR spectroscopy](#chem.analysis2) and [Infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry](#chem.analysis));
analysing a given route (Paper 4 style: name each reaction type and reagent; suggest a by-product).
Worked examples (≥6, B1 per reagent/condition): benzene → phenylamine → azo dye; methylbenzene → 4-nitrobenzoic acid
(nitrate first: CH₃ directs 2,4; then oxidise) vs 3-nitrobenzoic acid (oxidise first); ethanoyl chloride + an amine →
amide → reduction to a secondary amine; propanal → 2-hydroxybutanoic acid (HCN then hydrolysis; racemic); a
multifunctional molecule's reactions with five reagents; a route analysis with by-products.
Pitfalls: reagents without conditions; steps that ignore directing effects (–NO₂ deactivates the ring: say only "less
reactive" and "directs to the 3-position"); using NaBH₄ on acids, esters or amides; temperatures for diazotisation.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
`Next: [Chromatography and NMR spectroscopy](#chem.analysis2)`.
