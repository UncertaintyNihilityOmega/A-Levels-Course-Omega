## chem.org.intro · Introduction to organic chemistry (`@level as`, `@time 6–8 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Papers 1 and 2 (AS); assumed knowledge in Paper 4`. Topic 13.1–13.3. Warm-up from
[Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding) (σ/π, hybridisation) and [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles) (empirical formula).
Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- Conventions: X = halogen; R and R′ alkyl groups (or H). The AS functional-group table: alkene (C=C), halogenoalkane
  (primary, secondary, tertiary), alcohol (1°, 2°, 3°; hydroxyl), aldehyde (carbonyl, –CHO), ketone (carbonyl, C=O
  between two carbons), carboxylic acid (carboxyl, –COOH), ester (–COO–), amine (primary only, –NH₂), nitrile (–C≡N);
  examples: propene, 1-chloropropane, propan-1-ol, propanal, propanone, propanoic acid, methyl propanoate, propylamine,
  propanenitrile.
- 13.1 1 hydrocarbon = compound of C and H only; 2 alkanes are simple hydrocarbons with no functional group;
  3 the functional group dictates physical and chemical properties; 4 general, structural, displayed and skeletal
  formulas; 5 systematic names up to six carbons (six plus six for esters; straight chains only for esters and
  nitriles); 6 molecular/empirical formula from structural, displayed or skeletal formulas.
- 13.2 1 terms: homologous series; saturated/unsaturated; homolytic/heterolytic fission; free radical, initiation,
  propagation, termination; nucleophile, electrophile, nucleophilic, electrophilic; addition, substitution,
  elimination, hydrolysis, condensation; oxidation and reduction ([O] and [H] in equations); 2 mechanism names:
  free-radical substitution, electrophilic addition, nucleophilic substitution, nucleophilic addition; curly arrows
  start at a bond or a lone pair.
- 13.3 1 straight-chained, branched, cyclic; 2 shapes and bond angles around sp (180°), sp² (120°), sp³ (109.5°) atoms;
  3 arrangement of σ and π bonds; 4 "planar" (ethene: all six atoms in one plane).
Sections: why carbon (four bonds, chains, rings); homologous series (same functional group, same general formula,
differ by CH₂, similar chemistry, gradual trend in physical properties); the four kinds of formula for one molecule
(propan-1-ol: general CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH, structural CH₃CH₂CH₂OH, displayed, skeletal) with a figure (displayed + skeletal side
by side: COURSE.md conventions); the functional-group table as a Markdown table plus one figure of skeletal formulas
of all nine examples (two rows, `fig wide`); naming rules `:::steps` (longest chain containing the functional group,
number from the end nearer the group, prefixes for branches alphabetical, di/tri, suffixes -ene, -ol, -al, -one,
-oic acid, -oate, -amine, -nitrile; the nitrile carbon counts in the chain; esters named alkyl alkanoate);
worked naming examples (2-methylbut-2-ene, 3-methylbutan-2-ol, ethyl propanoate, 2-methylpropanenitrile); bond fission
(homolytic gives radicals, heterolytic gives ions; curly-arrow and fish-hook conventions: Cambridge uses full curly
arrows for pairs; radical steps are written as equations); reagent types (nucleophile = electron-pair donor,
electrophile = electron-pair acceptor; examples); reaction types with one example each; mechanism names (forward links
to [Hydrocarbons: alkanes and alkenes](#chem.org.hydrocarbons), [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen),
[Aldehydes and ketones](#chem.org.carbonyl)); shapes and hybridisation in organic molecules (ethane, ethene, ethyne
or a nitrile; count σ and π bonds in propenal or but-1-ene).
Worked examples (≥6): name five structures; draw (describe) the displayed formula of methyl propanoate; molecular and
empirical formula from a skeletal formula; classify reactions; σ/π count; identify sp/sp²/sp³ carbons in
CH₂=CHCH₂C≡N.
Pitfalls: numbering from the wrong end; naming esters backwards (propyl methanoate vs methyl propanoate); skeletal
formulas with H atoms on carbon drawn (do not) or missing H on O/N (draw OH, NH₂); "ethene is tetrahedral".
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.org.isomerism · Isomerism (`@level as a2`, `@time 5–7 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Papers 1 and 2 (AS); Paper 4 (A Level section)`. Topics 13.4 (AS) and 29.4 (A Level: final `##`
section "Optical isomers in medicine and the lab (A Level)", starting with a note that AS students can stop before it).
Warm-up from [Introduction to organic chemistry](#chem.org.intro). Outcomes:
- 13.4 1 structural isomerism: chain, positional, functional group; 2 stereoisomerism: geometrical (cis/trans) and
  optical (E/Z acceptable, not required); 3 cis/trans from restricted rotation about the π bond (each carbon of C=C
  needs two different groups); 4 chiral centre (a carbon with four different groups) gives two optical isomers
  (enantiomers, non-superimposable mirror images); more than one chiral centre possible (no meso or diastereoisomers);
  5 identify chiral centres and cis/trans isomerism in a given structure, including cyclic compounds (e.g.
  1,2-dimethylcyclohexane cis/trans; a ring carbon can be chiral); 6 deduce isomers from a molecular formula.
- 29.4 1 enantiomers have identical physical and chemical properties except rotation of plane-polarised light and
  potential biological activity; 2 optically active, racemic mixture (equal amounts: no net rotation); 3 the two
  isomers rotate plane-polarised light by equal amounts in opposite directions; 4 chirality in drug synthesis: (a)
  different biological activity of the two enantiomers, (b) the need to separate a racemic mixture, (c) chiral
  catalysts to make a single isomer (benefits: smaller doses, fewer side effects, less waste).
Sections: structural isomers with C₄H₁₀ (chain), C₃H₇Cl/C₄H₉OH (positional), C₃H₆O propanal vs propanone and
C₂H₆O ethanol vs methoxymethane (functional group); a systematic method for listing isomers (`:::steps`, e.g. all
C₄H₉Br isomers: link the gold chapter's figure); geometrical isomerism: but-2-ene cis and trans figure (skeletal,
`fig`), why rotation is restricted (π bond overlap must break), cis/trans in rings; optical isomerism: chiral centres
marked with an asterisk; figure of the two enantiomers of butan-2-ol with wedge/dash bonds and a mirror line
(`ln-m dash`), plus embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-chiral" data-mol="butan-2-ol"></div>`; counting chiral
centres in larger molecules (2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid), an amino acid such as alanine); A Level: polarimeter
idea, racemic mixtures from planar intermediates (link the SN1 stretch in the gold chapter and nucleophilic addition
to aldehydes in [Aldehydes and ketones](#chem.org.carbonyl)), drug examples stated carefully (thalidomide is the
standard example: one enantiomer was a sedative, the other caused birth defects; note that the isomers interconvert
in the body — keep it brief and accurate, or choose a safer example such as one enantiomer being more active).
Worked examples (≥6): list the structural isomers of C₅H₁₂ (3) and of C₃H₈O (3); decide whether pent-2-ene and
2-methylbut-2-ene show cis/trans; find the chiral centres in a given structure; total stereoisomers of a molecule with
one chiral centre and one C=C able to show cis/trans (2 × 2 = 4); A Level: explain why a racemic product is optically
inactive.
Pitfalls: "but-1-ene has cis/trans isomers" (C1 has two H); missing the chiral carbon in a ring; enantiomers described
as "mirror images" without "non-superimposable"; saying enantiomers have different boiling points.
Board box (sure): leave it out unless sure (E/Z naming is acceptable in 9701 but not required: say this in the text).

## Widget `chem-chiral` (widgets/chem-chiral.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-chiral" data-mol="butan-2-ol"></div>`.
- Presets (`data-mol`): `butan-2-ol` (H, OH, CH₃, C₂H₅), `lactic` (2-hydroxypropanoic acid: H, OH, CH₃, COOH),
  `alanine` (H, NH₂, CH₃, COOH), `chfclbr` (bromochlorofluoromethane: H, F, Cl, Br), `propan-2-ol` (H, OH, CH₃, CH₃: not
  chiral, the "superimpose" test succeeds).
- Two 3D tetrahedral models side by side (W.view3d, W.orbit on each, or one shared rotation), the second the mirror
  image of the first, a dashed mirror line between. Groups as labelled spheres in four W.palette series colours, the
  label text on each sphere; bonds as lines. Bond angle 109.5°.
- Buttons: "Rotate right model to match two groups" (animates with W.animate, respects reduced motion) then reports
  "Two groups match, the other two are swapped: not superimposable" (or "all four match: superimposable, not chiral");
  "Reset".
- Readout: chiral centre yes/no, number of different groups.
- Probe defaults, each preset, `data-mol="nope"` (falls back with a note). Never name colours in notes.
