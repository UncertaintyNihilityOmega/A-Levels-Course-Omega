## chem.org.arenes · Arenes: benzene and its compounds (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topics 29.1.4, 29.2, 29.3, 30 and 31. Warm-up from [Hydrocarbons: alkanes and alkenes](#chem.org.hydrocarbons)
(electrophilic addition), [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding) (sp², π bonds) and [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen).
Outcomes (checklist):
- 29.1.4 names of simple aromatics with one ring and simple substituents (e.g. 3-nitrobenzoic acid,
  2,4,6-tribromophenol; methylbenzene, chlorobenzene, nitrobenzene, phenylamine, benzoic acid, 1-phenylethanone).
- 29.2.1 terms: electrophilic substitution, addition–elimination (the latter is used in [Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids](#chem.org.acyl)).
- 29.3.1 shape of benzene: planar, sp² carbons, σ framework (120°), the six p orbitals overlap to give a delocalised π
  system above and below the ring; all C–C bonds equal in length (between single and double).
- 30.1 1 reactions of benzene and methylbenzene: (a) Cl₂ or Br₂ with AlCl₃ or AlBr₃ catalyst → halogenoarenes;
  (b) nitration: concentrated HNO₃ + concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C; (c) Friedel–Crafts alkylation: CH₃Cl + AlCl₃, heat;
  (d) Friedel–Crafts acylation: CH₃COCl + AlCl₃, heat; (e) side-chain oxidation: hot alkaline KMnO₄ then dilute acid →
  benzoic acid; (f) hydrogenation: H₂, Pt or Ni, heat → cyclohexane ring; 2 mechanism of electrophilic substitution
  (nitrobenzene: NO₂⁺ generated by HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻ (or the simpler HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ form); bromobenzene:
  Br⁺ from Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻; arrow from the delocalised ring to the electrophile, intermediate with a partial
  ring (horseshoe) and + charge, arrow from the C–H bond back into the ring, H⁺ lost (regenerating the catalyst));
  delocalisation (aromatic stabilisation) explains substitution rather than addition; 3 side-chain halogenation (UV
  light, free radical) vs ring halogenation (AlCl₃ catalyst, no UV) in methylbenzene; 4 directing effects: –NH₂, –OH, –R
  direct to 2- and 4- positions (and activate); –NO₂, –COOH, –COR direct to 3- (and deactivate).
- 31.1 1 halogenoarenes from benzene → chlorobenzene and methylbenzene → 2-chloromethylbenzene and
  4-chloromethylbenzene; 2 chlorobenzene is much less reactive than chloroethane towards nucleophiles: a lone pair on Cl
  overlaps with the ring's π system, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character (stronger), and the electron-rich
  ring repels nucleophiles.
Sections: benzene's structure (the Kekulé model and why it fails: no addition with bromine water, equal bond lengths,
less exothermic hydrogenation than three C=C would predict — give the qualitative argument; enthalpy values only as
"data for this question" unless sure); naming; the reactions table; the electrophilic substitution mechanism figure
(hand SVG `fig`, two rows: benzene + NO₂⁺ → intermediate (horseshoe drawn as a `ln` arc over five carbons, + in the
middle, H and NO₂ on the sp³ carbon); intermediate → nitrobenzene + H⁺); the catalyst regeneration equations;
halogenation in the side chain vs ring with methylbenzene; directing effects table and an example (nitration of
methylbenzene gives 2- and 4-nitromethylbenzene; nitration of nitrobenzene gives 1,3-dinitrobenzene); Friedel–Crafts
reactions; side-chain oxidation; halogenoarene reactivity.
Worked examples (≥7): name four aromatic compounds; mechanism of bromination with marks; predict products of
methylbenzene + Cl₂ under two sets of conditions; directing effects on a disubstituted benzene; a two-step synthesis
(benzene → 3-nitrobenzoic acid? order matters: methylbenzene → benzoic acid → 3-nitrobenzoic acid, because COOH directs
to 3); explain why chlorobenzene does not react with NaOH(aq) under the conditions that hydrolyse chloroethane.
Pitfalls: drawing the intermediate with a full ring; the arrow from the electrophile to the ring; saying benzene has
three double bonds; forgetting the catalyst regeneration; side-chain vs ring conditions mixed up.

## chem.org.phenol · Phenol and the acidity of organic compounds (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topics 32.2 and the acidity outcomes 33.1.4–33.1.5. Warm-up from [Arenes: benzene and its compounds](#chem.org.arenes),
[Alcohols](#chem.org.alcohols) and [pH, buffers, solubility product and partition](#chem.acids2) (Ka, pKa). Outcomes:
- 32.2 1 phenol made from phenylamine + HNO₂ (or NaNO₂ and dilute acid) below 10 °C → benzenediazonium salt; warming
  the diazonium salt with water gives phenol + N₂ (link [Amines, amides and amino acids](#chem.org.nitrogen));
  2 (a) NaOH(aq) → sodium phenoxide + water, (b) Na(s) → sodium phenoxide + H₂, (c) in NaOH(aq) with diazonium salts →
  azo compounds (coupling at the 4-position, orange/yellow dyes), (d) nitration with dilute HNO₃(aq) at room temperature →
  2-nitrophenol and 4-nitrophenol, (e) Br₂(aq) → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate, bromine water decolourised);
  3 acidity of phenol (the phenoxide ion is stabilised: the O⁻ lone pair delocalises into the ring); 4 relative acidities:
  phenol > water > ethanol; 5 why nitration and bromination of phenol need milder conditions than benzene (an O lone
  pair overlaps with the ring, increasing its electron density and activating it towards electrophiles; no catalyst
  needed); 6 OH directs to 2-, 4- and 6-positions; 7 apply to other phenols, e.g. naphthol (2-naphthol couples with
  diazonium salts to give azo dyes).
- 33.1.4 relative acidities of carboxylic acids, phenols and alcohols (carboxylic acid > phenol > water > alcohol:
  carboxylate charge delocalised over two O atoms; phenoxide over the ring; alkoxide destabilised by the alkyl group's
  inductive effect); 33.1.5 chlorine-substituted carboxylic acids (more Cl and closer to COOH → stronger: electron-
  withdrawing Cl spreads the negative charge: ClCH₂COOH < Cl₂CHCOOH < Cl₃CCOOH in acid strength).
Sections: phenol's structure and naming; acidity: reactions that show it (phenol reacts with NaOH but not with
Na₂CO₃ to give CO₂ — a useful distinguishing test from carboxylic acids; state it as the standard result), the
explanation with a resonance-free delocalisation figure (hand SVG of phenoxide with curly arrow from O⁻ lone pair into the
ring, or the overlap of the O p orbital with the ring's π system); the acidity ranking table (with pKa values given as
"typical values" only if you are sure — otherwise only the order); ring reactions with conditions compared to benzene
in a table (bromine: Br₂(aq), room temperature, no catalyst vs Br₂ + AlBr₃; nitration: dilute HNO₃, room temperature vs
concentrated mixed acids 25–60 °C); azo coupling and dyes (structure of the azo group –N=N–); naphthol.
Worked examples (≥6): equations for phenol + NaOH and + Na; product and observation with bromine water; explain why
phenol reacts with Br₂(aq) but benzene does not; rank four compounds by acidity with reasons; compare chloroethanoic
acids; mass of 2,4,6-tribromophenol from a given mass of phenol (Mr from the table; check).
Pitfalls: "phenol is an alcohol so it is neutral"; phenol + carbonates giving CO₂; tribromophenol drawn with Br at 3 and 5;
explaining acidity by "OH is more polar" instead of the stability of the anion.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
