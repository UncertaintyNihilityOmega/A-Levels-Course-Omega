## chem.org.synthesis · Organic synthesis (AS) (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 21. Brings together every AS organic chapter; the A Level version is [Organic synthesis (A Level)](#chem.org.synthesis2).
Warm-up from three earlier organic chapters. Outcomes (checklist):
- 21.1 1 for a molecule with several functional groups: (a) identify the functional groups using the reactions in the
  syllabus, (b) predict properties and reactions; 2 devise multi-step synthetic routes using syllabus reactions;
  3 analyse a given route: type of reaction, reagents for each step, possible by-products.
Sections: the reaction map — a big Markdown table (from → to, reagents and conditions, type) built from the AS edge
list below, and the interactive map: embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-orgmap" data-level="as"></div>` and say
what to try (start at an alkene, find a route to a carboxylic acid with one more carbon); a step-by-step method
(`:::steps`: compare carbon numbers (does the chain grow? use CN⁻), compare functional groups, work backwards one
step, check conditions do not destroy another group); tests to identify groups (table: bromine water, 2,4-DNPH,
Tollens', Fehling's, acidified dichromate, iodoform, Na₂CO₃ effervescence, Na metal, AgNO₃ after hydrolysis) — these
match the P3 tests in FACTS.md; multifunctional molecules (e.g. predict the products of a molecule with C=C, OH and
CHO groups with each reagent); by-products (HCl from SOCl₂/PCl₅; mixtures from free-radical substitution; isomeric
products from Markovnikov addition or elimination; over-oxidation).
Worked examples (≥6, with B1 per step): ethene → ethyl ethanoate (two routes from ethene to both parts); propan-1-ol →
butanoic acid? (needs a carbon: propan-1-ol → 1-bromopropane → butanenitrile → butanoic acid); 2-bromopropane →
propanone; identify reagents in a given four-step route; predict the reactions of a given bifunctional molecule with
NaBH₄, Br₂(aq), acidified K₂Cr₂O₇; identify an unknown from a set of test results.
Pitfalls: steps that change the carbon count without CN⁻; reagents without conditions; reduction with NaBH₄ of a
carboxylic acid (only LiAlH₄); oxidising a tertiary alcohol.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.analysis · Infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 22. Chromatography and NMR are A Level: [Chromatography and NMR spectroscopy](#chem.analysis2).
Warm-up from [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms) (isotopes) and [Introduction to organic chemistry](#chem.org.intro).
Outcomes:
- 22.1 1 analyse an IR spectrum of a simple molecule to identify functional groups, using the Data-section table
  (FACTS.md IR line, copy it as a Markdown table in the chapter).
- 22.2 1 analyse mass spectra in terms of m/e values and isotopic abundances (the working of the instrument is not
  required); 2 calculate Ar from relative isotopic abundances or a mass spectrum; 3 molecular mass from the molecular
  ion peak M⁺; 4 identify simple fragments (CH₃⁺ 15, C₂H₅⁺ 29, CHO⁺ 29, CH₂OH⁺ 31, CH₃CO⁺ 43, C₃H₇⁺ 43, COOH⁺ 45 —
  compute every m/e in the check script); 5 number of carbons from [M+1]⁺: n = 100 × abundance of [M+1]⁺ / (1.1 ×
  abundance of M⁺); 6 Br and Cl from the [M+2]⁺ peak (one Cl: M : M+2 about 3 : 1; one Br: about 1 : 1; two Cl:
  9 : 6 : 1 for M, M+2, M+4 — derive it from 3 : 1 in a worked example).
Sections: IR basics (bonds absorb at characteristic wavenumbers; the fingerprint region below about 1500 cm⁻¹ is
unique to a compound: say "below about 1500 cm⁻¹" without other numbers); reading spectra: hand SVG spectra (`fig`,
600 × 240; transmittance (%) on y from 0 to 100, wavenumber on x decreasing from 4000 to 500 left to right, absorptions
as downward dips drawn with a `c1` path; label each dip with its bond) for ethanol (broad O–H 3200–3600, C–O), ethanoic
acid (very broad O–H 2500–3000, C=O 1670–1740) and propanone (C=O, no O–H); the wavenumber ranges only from FACTS.md;
a `:::steps` method (look for C=O, then O–H, then N–H, C≡N, C–O); mass spectra: molecular ion, fragments
(fragmentation gives a cation and a radical; only the cation is detected), a hand SVG mass spectrum (vertical lines,
m/e on x, relative abundance on y) for a simple molecule, and embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-ms" data-formula="C2H5Br"></div>`;
Ar calculations from isotopic data (e.g. bromine 79 and 81 with given abundances; the result must match 79.9 from the
table only if you use real abundances: otherwise call it "a sample of element X"); [M+1] carbon counting; [M+2] for
Cl and Br; combined problems (IR + MS to identify a compound).
Worked examples (≥7): identify a compound from an IR spectrum with three absorptions; Ar of an element from a spectrum
with three isotopes; n from [M+1] (e.g. M⁺ 25.0%, [M+1]⁺ 1.10% → n = 4.0, so 4 carbons); recognise a chlorine compound
from a 3 : 1 pair; identify fragments in the spectrum of propanal or propanone; a ★★★ problem combining IR, M⁺ and a
fragment.
Pitfalls: reading the x-axis the wrong way; quoting a peak as "3000" when the table gives ranges; forgetting the charge
in fragment formulas (CH₃⁺, not CH₃); "the M⁺ peak is the tallest" (the tallest is the base peak).
Board box (sure): AQA's course explains how a time-of-flight mass spectrometer works (ionisation, acceleration, flight
time), which 9701 states is not required.

## Widget `chem-orgmap` (widgets/chem-orgmap.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-orgmap" data-level="as" data-focus="alkene"></div>`; `data-level="a2"` shows AS + A Level.
Used by many organic chapters (P10, P11, P16, P17, P18 embed it).
- Nodes (functional groups; AS): alkane, alkene, halogenoalkane, alcohol (primary), alcohol (secondary), alcohol
  (tertiary), diol, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid, carboxylate salt, ester, nitrile, hydroxynitrile, amine,
  poly(alkene). A Level adds: arene, halogenoarene, nitroarene, alkylbenzene, aryl ketone, benzoic acid, phenylamine,
  diazonium salt, azo compound, phenol, phenoxide, acyl chloride, amide, secondary amine, cyclohexane, polyester,
  polyamide, CO₂ + H₂O (from methanoic/ethanedioic acid).
- Edges (reagents and conditions, type) — copy exactly from FACTS.md topics 14–21 and 29–36:
  AS: alkane → halogenoalkane (Cl₂ or Br₂, UV light; free-radical substitution); long alkane → shorter alkane + alkene
  (heat with Al₂O₃; cracking); alkene → alkane (H₂, Pt or Ni catalyst, heat; addition); alkene → halogenoalkane (HX(g) at
  room temperature, or X₂; electrophilic addition); alkene → alcohol (steam, H₃PO₄ catalyst; electrophilic addition);
  alkene → diol (cold dilute acidified KMnO₄; oxidation); alkene → ketone / carboxylic acid / CO₂ (hot concentrated
  acidified KMnO₄; oxidation, C=C broken); alkene → poly(alkene) (addition polymerisation); halogenoalkane → alcohol
  (NaOH(aq), heat; nucleophilic substitution); halogenoalkane → nitrile (KCN in ethanol, heat; nucleophilic
  substitution); halogenoalkane → amine (NH₃ in ethanol, heated under pressure); halogenoalkane → alkene (NaOH in
  ethanol, heat; elimination); alcohol → halogenoalkane (HX(g); KCl + concentrated H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄; PCl₃ and heat; PCl₅;
  SOCl₂; substitution); primary alcohol → aldehyde (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄, distil; oxidation); primary alcohol →
  carboxylic acid (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄, reflux); secondary alcohol → ketone (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄, distil);
  alcohol → alkene (heated Al₂O₃ or concentrated H₂SO₄; dehydration/elimination); alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester
  (concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat; condensation); aldehyde → carboxylic acid (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄, reflux);
  aldehyde → primary alcohol and ketone → secondary alcohol (NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄; reduction); aldehyde/ketone →
  hydroxynitrile (HCN, KCN catalyst, heat; nucleophilic addition); carboxylic acid → primary alcohol (LiAlH₄;
  reduction); carboxylic acid → carboxylate salt (reactive metal, alkali or carbonate); nitrile and hydroxynitrile →
  carboxylic acid (dilute acid or dilute alkali, heat, then acidify; hydrolysis); ester → carboxylic acid + alcohol
  (dilute acid or dilute alkali and heat; hydrolysis).
  A Level: arene → halogenoarene (Cl₂ or Br₂ with AlCl₃ or AlBr₃; electrophilic substitution); arene → nitroarene
  (concentrated HNO₃ + concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C); arene → alkylbenzene (CH₃Cl + AlCl₃, heat; Friedel–Crafts
  alkylation); arene → aryl ketone (CH₃COCl + AlCl₃, heat; Friedel–Crafts acylation); arene → cyclohexane (H₂, Pt or
  Ni, heat); alkylbenzene → benzoic acid (hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid; oxidation); alkylbenzene → side-chain
  halogenation (Cl₂ or Br₂, UV light; free-radical substitution); nitroarene → phenylamine (hot Sn + concentrated HCl,
  then NaOH(aq); reduction); phenylamine → diazonium salt (HNO₂, or NaNO₂ + dilute acid, below 10 °C); diazonium salt →
  phenol (warm with water); diazonium salt + phenol → azo compound (in NaOH(aq); coupling); phenol → phenoxide (NaOH(aq),
  or Na(s) giving H₂); phenol → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (Br₂(aq)); phenol → 2- and 4-nitrophenol (dilute HNO₃, room
  temperature); phenylamine → 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine (Br₂(aq), room temperature); carboxylic acid → acyl chloride
  (PCl₃ and heat, PCl₅, or SOCl₂); acyl chloride → carboxylic acid (water, room temperature; hydrolysis); acyl chloride →
  ester (alcohol or phenol, room temperature; addition–elimination); acyl chloride → amide (NH₃ or a primary/secondary
  amine, room temperature); amide → amine (LiAlH₄; reduction); amide → carboxylic acid (aqueous acid or alkali, heat;
  hydrolysis); nitrile → amine (LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni; reduction); halogenoalkane + primary amine → secondary amine (in
  ethanol, heated in a sealed tube); methanoic acid → CO₂ + H₂O (Fehling's, Tollens', acidified KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇);
  ethanedioic acid → CO₂ (warm acidified KMnO₄); diol + dicarboxylic acid (or dioyl chloride) → polyester; diamine +
  dicarboxylic acid (or dioyl chloride) → polyamide; amino acids → polypeptide (condensation).
- View: nodes laid out in a fixed readable arrangement (DOM grid of buttons, not a free graph: it must work at 375 px;

## Widget `chem-ms` (widgets/chem-ms.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-ms" data-formula="C2H5Br"></div>`; `data-tab="ar"` opens the Ar calculator.
- Tab "Molecular ion": a formula of C, H, N, O, Cl, Br (parse like `molar`, integer masses C 12, H 1, N 14, O 16,
  ³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl, ⁷⁹Br/⁸¹Br). Draw the molecular-ion cluster: M from the lightest isotopes; [M+1] height = 1.1% × number of
  carbons of M (the syllabus rule; ignore other isotopes and say so); Cl and Br patterns from the ratios 3 : 1 and 1 : 1
  combined binomially for several halogen atoms (M, M+2, M+4 …). Readout: m/e and relative heights (3 s.f.), the
  carbon count recovered with n = 100 × [M+1] / (1.1 × M).
- Tab "Ar from isotopes": up to four rows of isotope mass and abundance (% or relative), Ar = Σ(mass × abundance) /
  Σ abundance to 1 d.p. and 3 s.f.; a bar chart of the spectrum.
- Clearly labelled "simplified: only the molecular ion region is drawn".
- Probe defaults, `data-formula="CH2Cl2"`, `data-formula="C6H5Br"`, `data-tab="ar"`, `data-formula="Qx"` (friendly error).
