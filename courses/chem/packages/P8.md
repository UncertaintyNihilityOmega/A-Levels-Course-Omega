## chem.group17 · Group 17: the halogens (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 11. Warm-up from [Redox and oxidation numbers](#chem.redox), [Shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces](#chem.shapes)
and [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding). Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 11.1 1 colours and volatility trend of Cl₂ (pale green / yellow-green gas), Br₂ (red-brown liquid, orange vapour),
  I₂ (grey-black solid, purple vapour); volatility decreases down the group; 2 bond strength trend of X₂ (Data booklet:
  Cl–Cl 242 > Br–Br 193 > I–I 151; F–F 158 is anomalously weak, a stretch point: lone-pair repulsion in the short bond);
  3 volatility by id-id forces (more electrons down the group).
- 11.2 1 relative reactivity as oxidising agents (Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂; displacement reactions, e.g. Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂,
  observed as colour changes; in water bromine is orange/yellow, iodine brown; in a hydrocarbon solvent iodine is
  purple — state only colours you are sure of); 2 reactions with hydrogen and relative reactivity (Cl₂ explosive in
  sunlight, Br₂ needs heating, I₂ slow and reversible on heating with a catalyst — describe qualitatively); 3 thermal
  stability of HX from bond energies (H–Cl 431 > H–Br 366 > H–I 299: HI decomposes to purple iodine vapour with a hot wire).
- 11.3 1 relative reactivity of halide ions as reducing agents (I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻); 2 (a) Ag⁺(aq) then NH₃(aq): exactly the
  FACTS.md QA notes (AgCl white, soluble in NH₃(aq); AgBr cream, partially soluble; AgI pale yellow, insoluble; the
  [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ formula is not required); (b) concentrated H₂SO₄ with balanced equations: NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl
  (steamy fumes, no redox); NaBr gives HBr then 2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O (orange-brown fumes); NaI gives HI then
  reduction of H₂SO₄ further: 8HI + H₂SO₄ → 4I₂ + H₂S + 4H₂O (also SO₂ and S; purple fumes/black solid, smell of bad eggs);
  use oxidation numbers of S (+6 → +4, 0, −2) to show the increasing reducing power.
- 11.4 1 Cl₂ with cold NaOH(aq): Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O; with hot NaOH(aq): 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O;
  both disproportionation (0 → −1 and +1, or 0 → −1 and +5); 2 water purification: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl;
  HOCl ⇌ H⁺ + ClO⁻; HOCl and ClO⁻ kill bacteria.
Sections follow the outcomes. Figures: a hand SVG of the silver halide test as three test tubes (outline `ln`,
precipitate as `fm2` fill at the bottom, labels "white", "cream", "pale yellow" — colours in words only), and a table
for concentrated sulfuric acid reactions (halide, products, observations, S oxidation number change). Optional
embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-trend" data-mode="en" data-set="group17"></div>`.
Worked examples (≥6): explain the volatility trend (id-id); displacement equation and observation; explain the
reducing-power trend with ionic radius/shielding (larger ion, outer electron further from the nucleus, more easily
lost); balance the NaBr + concentrated H₂SO₄ equation using oxidation numbers; disproportionation oxidation numbers in
hot NaOH; a water-treatment context question.
Pitfalls: "Cl⁻ is a stronger reducing agent than I⁻"; confusing halogen (Cl₂) with halide (Cl⁻); writing ClO⁻ as
chlorate(V) (it is chlorate(I)); stating the [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ formula as required (it is not, but it is fine to mention).
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.nitrogen · Nitrogen and sulfur (`@time 4–5 hours`)
Topic 12. Warm-up from [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding), [Acids and bases](#chem.acids) and
[Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria). Outcomes:
- 12.1 1 lack of reactivity of N₂: triple bond strength (N≡N 944 kJ mol⁻¹, Data booklet) and lack of polarity;
  2 (a) basicity of NH₃ (Brønsted–Lowry: accepts a proton using its lone pair), (b) the ammonium ion: tetrahedral,
  formed by an acid–base reaction (dative bond), (c) displacement of NH₃ from ammonium salts by a base on warming
  (NH₄Cl + NaOH → NaCl + NH₃ + H₂O; test: damp red litmus turns blue); 3 natural (lightning, N₂ + O₂ → 2NO) and man-made
  (internal combustion engines, high temperature) oxides of nitrogen and their catalytic removal (catalytic converters:
  2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ on Pt/Pd/Rh; link the A Level mechanism in [Rate equations and catalysis](#chem.rates2));
  4 NO and NO₂ react with unburned hydrocarbons to form peroxyacetyl nitrate, PAN, a component of photochemical smog;
  5 NO and NO₂ in acid rain: directly (2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ + HNO₂; or 4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃) and as catalysts for
  oxidising SO₂: SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO, then NO + ½O₂ → NO₂ (NO₂ regenerated), SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄.
Sections: nitrogen's unreactivity; ammonia and ammonium (with a dot-and-cross or displayed NH₄⁺ link); ammonia in
industry (link the Haber process in [Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria)); fertilisers context (ammonium nitrate,
ammonium sulfate) with care: no environmental statistics; oxides of nitrogen sources; catalytic converters; acid rain
mechanism with NO₂ as a catalyst (`:::key` box); photochemical smog and PAN (structure not required; describe as a
secondary pollutant formed in sunlight). Sulfur: the syllabus heading is "Nitrogen and sulfur"; the sulfur content is
SO₂ in acid rain and (from 7.1) the Contact process: link it, do not invent extra outcomes.
Figures: a hand SVG flow figure of the catalytic oxidation of SO₂ by NO₂ as a cycle (NO₂ → NO → NO₂, with SO₂ in and
SO₃ out) using COURSE.md "Energy cycles" text-and-arrow style.
Worked examples (≥4): explain why N₂ is unreactive (two marks: bond energy, non-polar); equation for NH₃ + HCl and the
bond formed; show NO₂ acts as a catalyst in the oxidation of SO₂; equations for the catalytic converter.
Pitfalls: "N₂ is unreactive because it is a gas"; NO₂ "used up" in the SO₂ oxidation; ammonium as "NH₄" without charge.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
