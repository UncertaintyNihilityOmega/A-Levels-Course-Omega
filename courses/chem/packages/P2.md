## chem.atoms · Atomic structure (`@time 6–8 hours`)
Warm-up from [Chemistry from zero](#chem.f.basics). Outcomes (FACTS.md topic 1, copy into the checklist):
- 1.1 Particles, atomic radius: 1 atom mostly empty space, small dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, electrons in
  shells; 2 relative charges and masses of p (+1, 1), n (0, 1), e (−1, 1/1836); 3 atomic/proton number, mass/nucleon
  number; 4 distribution of mass and charge; 5 beams of p, n, e at the same velocity in an electric field;
  6 numbers of p, n, e in atoms and ions; 7 state and explain qualitatively atomic and ionic radius across a period
  and down a group.
- 1.2 Isotopes: 1 define isotope (same number of protons, different numbers of neutrons); 2 notation \({}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\);
  3 same chemical properties (same electron configuration); 4 different physical properties, limited to mass and density.
- 1.3 Orbitals (ground state, H to Kr only): 1 shells, sub-shells, orbitals, principal quantum number n, ground state;
  2 orbitals in s (1), p (3), d (5) and electrons they hold (2, 6, 10); 3 order of energy: 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s
  < 3d < 4p; 4 configurations by shell, sub-shell and orbital; 5 explain by electron energy and inter-electron
  repulsion (Hund's idea: electrons occupy separate orbitals of a sub-shell before pairing, with parallel spins; the
  word "Hund" is optional); 6 full and shorthand configurations of atoms and ions (Fe 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²,
  [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²; Cr [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ exceptions; transition-metal ions lose 4s first: Fe²⁺ [Ar] 3d⁶,
  Fe³⁺ [Ar] 3d⁵); 7 electrons-in-boxes notation; 8 describe and sketch s (sphere) and p (dumbbell) orbitals;
  9 free radical = species with one or more unpaired electrons.
- 1.4 Ionisation energy (H to Kr): 1 define first IE: the energy required to remove one electron from each atom in one
  mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions; 2 equations \(\ce{Na(g) -> Na+(g) + e-}\),
  \(\ce{Na+(g) -> Na^2+(g) + e-}\); 3 trends across a period and down a group; 4 successive IEs; 5 attraction between
  nucleus and outer electron; 6 nuclear charge, atomic/ionic radius, shielding, spin-pair repulsion; 7 configurations
  from successive IE data; 8 position in the Periodic Table from successive IE data.
Sections and ideas:
1. Inside the atom: Rutherford-style picture (mostly empty space), table of particles, a `:::def` for proton number
   and nucleon number; electric-field deflection figure (hand SVG, `fig`, 560 × 260): beams of p, n, e entering
   between + and − plates; n straight, p bends towards −, e bends towards + and much more (smaller mass);
   explain the angle qualitatively (deflection ∝ charge/mass).
2. Counting particles in atoms and ions (worked example with \({}^{56}_{26}\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}\): 26 p, 30 n, 23 e).
3. Isotopes: chlorine-35 and chlorine-37; same chemistry, different mass/density (e.g. heavy water D₂O is denser).
   Ar from isotopic abundance is taught in [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles) and mass spectra in
   [Infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry](#chem.analysis): link forward.
4. Shells, sub-shells, orbitals: an energy-level diagram figure (hand SVG): 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p levels as
   short `ln thick` lines with boxes; orbital shapes figure (s sphere, p dumbbells along x, y, z; COURSE.md style).
   Embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-config" data-z="26"></div>` and say what to try (Cr and Cu, then Fe³⁺).
5. Configurations of ions, free radicals (Cl• has one unpaired electron).
6. Atomic and ionic radius: across a period (more protons, same shielding, radius falls), down a group (more shells);
   ions: cations smaller than their atoms, anions larger; isoelectronic ions Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ (more protons, smaller).
   No numerical radii (not in FACTS): describe qualitatively.
7. Ionisation energy: definition card, equations with state symbols, the four factors. Figure: first IE against Z for
   Z = 1–20 from the Data booklet values in FACTS.md (H 1310 … Ca 590) as a hand SVG line/point chart generated by a
   Python script (points labelled with symbols, axis "first ionisation energy / kJ mol⁻¹"); explain the dips at B
   (2p electron, higher energy, more shielded) and O (spin-pair repulsion in 2p⁴), the peaks at noble gases, the drop
   to Na. Successive IEs: figure of the four successive IEs of Al (577, 1820, 2740, 11600) as bars (log scale optional,
   say so on the axis); the big jump after the third shows 3 outer electrons → Group 13. Worked example: deduce the group
   of an element from 1st–4th IEs (e.g. Mg: 736, 1450, 7740, 10500 → jump after 2nd → Group 2). The chapter may embed
   `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-trend" data-mode="first" data-set="1-36"></div>` (built in P7; shows a
   placeholder until then); the text and SVG figures must teach without it.
Worked examples (≥5, one exam-style with marks): particles in ions; configuration of Cu and Cu²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁹);
electrons in boxes for N (3 unpaired); explain why first IE of Mg > Al; group from successive IEs.
Pitfalls: writing Cu as [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s²; removing 3d before 4s for ions; "IE decreases down a group because of more
protons" (wrong: more shells and shielding outweigh the larger nuclear charge); missing (g) state symbols in IE
equations; saying electrons are "in" the nucleus.
Stretch ideas: deduce an unknown element from data in a new context; explain why Ga's first IE (577) is lower than Zn's
(908) using 4p vs 4s (both values in FACTS).
Board box: leave it out in chem.atoms (the time-of-flight mass spectrometer difference belongs to
[Infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry](#chem.analysis)).

## chem.moles · Moles and stoichiometry (`@time 8–10 hours`)
Warm-up from [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms) and [Chemistry from zero](#chem.f.basics). Outcomes (FACTS.md topic 2):
- 2.1 1 unified atomic mass unit = 1/12 of the mass of a ¹²C atom; 2 define Ar (weighted mean mass of an atom of an
  element compared with 1/12 of the mass of a ¹²C atom), relative isotopic mass, Mr, relative formula mass.
- 2.2 1 mole in terms of the Avogadro constant (L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, Data booklet).
- 2.3 1 ionic formulas from charges and oxidation numbers (Roman numerals, e.g. iron(III) oxide Fe₂O₃),
  (a) charges from the Periodic Table, (b) recall NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻, OH⁻, NH₄⁺, Zn²⁺, Ag⁺, HCO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻;
  2 (a) balanced equations and ionic equations without spectator ions, (b) state symbols; 3 empirical and molecular
  formula; 4 anhydrous, hydrated, water of crystallisation; 5 calculate empirical and molecular formulas.
- 2.4 1 calculations: (a) reacting masses incl. percentage yield, (b) gas volumes (24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room
  conditions; burning hydrocarbons), (c) solution volumes and concentrations (mol dm⁻³, g dm⁻³), (d) limiting and
  excess reagents, (e) deduce stoichiometric relationships (e.g. from titration or gas-volume data). Answers to the
  s.f. of the data.
Sections and ideas:
1. Relative masses: definitions; calculating Ar from isotopic abundances (Cl: 75% ³⁵Cl, 25% ³⁷Cl → 35.5; Mg isotopes
   24, 25, 26 with given abundances; boron from 10 and 11) — give the abundances in the question.
2. The mole: n = m/M; number of particles N = nL; worked examples with water and with ions in Al₂(SO₄)₃.
3. Formulas: ionic formulas with Roman numerals; ionic equations (precipitation of AgCl, neutralisation
   \(\ce{H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)}\), metal + acid), spectator ions crossed out in words.
4. Empirical and molecular formula: percentage by mass → empirical formula table (Markdown); Mr → molecular formula;
   water of crystallisation from heating a hydrated salt (e.g. hydrated magnesium sulfate MgSO₄·xH₂O, find x; link
   [Practical skills and qualitative analysis](#chem.practical)). Embed `<div class="widget" data-w="molar" data-formula="CuSO4.5H2O"></div>`.
5. Reacting masses and percentage yield.
6. Gas volumes: V = n × 24.0 dm³ at room conditions; combustion of hydrocarbons and deducing a formula from gas volumes
   (e.g. 10 cm³ of a hydrocarbon + excess O₂ → 30 cm³ CO₂ → 3 carbons; volumes measured at the same T and p).
   Link [States of matter](#chem.states) for pV = nRT.
7. Solutions: c = n/V with V in dm³; titration calculations (acid–alkali), back-titration as a ★★★ example;
   g dm⁻³ ↔ mol dm⁻³.
8. Limiting reagent: compare moles to the equation ratio; mass of product and excess left.
9. Stoichiometry from data: e.g. find x in a reaction from moles of reactants, or the equation from a titration ratio.
Worked examples (≥8 here; this chapter is the calculation foundation). One real context: e.g. the mass of CO₂ made
by burning 1.00 kg of octane, or the dose in a tablet from a titration. Every calculation with [B1]/[M1]/[A1].
Figures: a "mole map" flow figure (hand SVG): mass ⇄ moles ⇄ particles, moles ⇄ gas volume, moles ⇄ concentration ×
volume, with the operation on each arrow (× M, ÷ M, × L, × 24.0, × V(dm³)). Keep it to five boxes.
Pitfalls: cm³ not converted to dm³; using the wrong ratio from the equation; rounding mid-calculation; confusing
empirical with molecular formula; giving Mr a unit (it has none; molar mass M has g mol⁻¹); spectator ions left in
ionic equations; "mole of chlorine" ambiguity (atoms vs molecules).
Board box (sure): AQA asks for percentage atom economy calculations; 9701 does not.

## Widget `chem-config` (widgets/chem-config.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-config" data-z="26" data-charge="0"></div>`.
- Element select for Z = 1–36 (names and symbols generated by Python from data/elements.json into the JS file;
  never type them) and a charge select from −3 to +3 (only charges that leave ≥ 0 electrons).
- Configuration computed in JS by the filling rule (order 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p, capacities 2/6/10) with the Cr and
  Cu exceptions; for positive ions of Z 21–30 remove 4s electrons before 3d; for anions add electrons by the same order.
- Output: electrons-in-boxes (one box per orbital, sub-shells labelled, arrows ↑ ↓ drawn on canvas or as text,
  sub-shells placed at heights by energy order), full configuration with superscripts, shorthand ([He]/[Ne]/[Ar]),
  number of unpaired electrons, and a note when the species is a free radical (unpaired electrons) or has a noble-gas
  configuration. The 3d line is written before 4s (Cambridge order).
- `.w-note`: "Try Cr and Cu, then Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺."
- Probe with defaults, `data-z="24"`, `data-z="29" data-charge="2"`, `data-z="99"` (friendly error), `data-z="9" data-charge="-1"`.

## Widget `molar` (widgets/molar.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="molar" data-formula="CuSO4.5H2O"></div>` (also used without attributes on tools.calc).
- Formula box: parses element symbols (case-sensitive), numbers, brackets (nested), hydrate dot (`.` or `·` or `*`) with a
  leading coefficient (`5H2O`), and a leading coefficient for the whole formula (`2H2O` → 2 × Mr shown as "2 × 18.0").
- Ar values: generated by Python from data/elements.json into the JS (1 d.p., the 9701 values). Elements with no Ar
  in the table: friendly message.
- Output: Mr (1 d.p.), a table: element, number of atoms, Ar, mass contribution, % by mass (3 s.f.); and a
  mass ⇄ moles line: type a mass in g → moles (3 s.f.), or moles → mass.
- Friendly errors: unknown symbol ("Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide: check the capital letters"), unbalanced
  bracket, empty input. Never throw.
- Probe with defaults, `data-formula="Ca(OH)2"`, `data-formula="(NH4)2SO4"`, `data-formula="Xx2"`, `data-formula="Al2(SO4"`.
