## chem.acids2 · pH, buffers, solubility product and partition (`@time 8–10 hours`)
Topic 25. Warm-up from [Acids and bases](#chem.acids), [Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria) and
[Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles). Outcomes (checklist):
- 25.1 1 conjugate acid and conjugate base; 2 conjugate acid–base pairs in reactions; 3 define pH = −log₁₀[H⁺],
  Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA], pKa = −log₁₀ Ka, Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 298 K, and use them (Kb and
  Kw = Ka × Kb are not tested); 4 [H⁺] and pH of (a) strong acids, (b) strong alkalis (via Kw), (c) weak acids
  ([H⁺] = √(Ka × c), assuming [HA] ≈ c and [H⁺] from water negligible); 5 (a) define a buffer (a solution that
  minimises changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added), (b) how a buffer is made (weak acid + its
  salt, e.g. CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa; or a weak acid partly neutralised by a strong base), (c) how buffers control pH, with
  equations (added H⁺ removed by CH₃COO⁻; added OH⁻ removed by CH₃COOH), (d) uses, including HCO₃⁻ in blood
  (H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H₂O + CO₂); 6 pH of buffers: [H⁺] = Ka × [HA]/[A⁻]; 7 solubility product Ksp; 8 Ksp expressions;
  9 Ksp from concentrations and vice versa; 10 (a) common ion effect, (b) calculations with Ksp and a common ion.
- 25.2 1 partition coefficient Kpc = [X] in solvent 1 / [X] in solvent 2 at equilibrium (state which solvent is on top
  of the fraction); 2 calculate and use Kpc (same physical state in both solvents), including amounts extracted in one
  or two extractions; 3 factors: polarities of solute and solvents ("like dissolves like").
Sections: conjugate pairs; pH scale and calculations (strong acids: [H⁺] = c for monoprotic; strong alkalis: [H⁺] =
Kw/[OH⁻]); weak acids, Ka and pKa (table of example Ka values given as data); the approximations and when they hold;
pH after dilution; buffers with equations and worked pH calculations (including one made by adding NaOH to excess
ethanoic acid); blood buffer context; titration curves revisited with pKa: embed
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-titration" data-acid="weak" data-base="strong" data-pka="4.76"></div>` (half-
equivalence pH = pKa); solubility product (Ksp of AgCl, of Ca(OH)₂ (s = cube-root type), units; predicting precipitation
by comparing the ionic product with Ksp); common ion effect calculation (solubility of AgCl in 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaCl);
partition coefficients (worked: ammonia or iodine between water and an organic solvent, with Kpc given as data;
compare one extraction with two smaller ones).
Worked examples (≥10, this is calculation-heavy): pH of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ HCl (1.30); pH of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH (12.30);
pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid with Ka = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ (2.88; check); Ka from a measured pH; buffer pH from amounts;
pH change when acid is added to a buffer; Ksp from solubility; solubility from Ksp; common ion; Kpc extraction.
Pitfalls: pH to 2 d.p.; using [H⁺] = c for weak acids; forgetting to square in Ksp for 1:2 salts (Ca(OH)₂: Ksp = 4s³);
Ksp units; buffer "keeps pH constant" (minimises changes); conjugate base of H₂O is OH⁻ and conjugate acid is H₃O⁺.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.rates2 · Rate equations and catalysis (`@time 8–10 hours`)
Topic 26. Warm-up from [Reaction kinetics](#chem.kinetics) and [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen) (SN1/SN2 rate-determining
steps). Outcomes:
- 26.1 1 rate equation, order, overall order, rate constant, half-life, rate-determining step, intermediate;
  2 (a) rate = k[A]^m[B]^n with m, n = 0, 1 or 2, (b) order from concentration–time graphs, initial rates and half-life
  methods, (c) concentration–time and rate–concentration graphs (shapes for orders 0, 1, 2), (d) initial rate from
  concentration data (tangent at t = 0), (e) construct a rate equation; 3 (a) first-order half-life independent of
  concentration, (b) half-life calculations; 4 k from (a) initial rates and the rate equation (with units), (b) k =
  0.693/t½; 5 multi-step reactions: (a) suggest a mechanism consistent with the rate equation and the overall equation,
  (b) predict the order from a mechanism and rate-determining step, (c) deduce a rate equation from a mechanism,
  (d) identify an intermediate or catalyst, (e) identify the rate-determining step; 6 temperature raises k
  (qualitative; no Arrhenius equation).
- 26.2 1 homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts; 2 heterogeneous: adsorption of reactants, bond weakening, desorption
  of products, e.g. (a) Fe in the Haber process, (b) Pd, Pt and Rh in catalytic converters removing oxides of nitrogen;
  3 homogeneous: used in one step and regenerated in a later step, e.g. (a) atmospheric oxides of nitrogen in the
  oxidation of SO₂ (link [Nitrogen and sulfur](#chem.nitrogen)), (b) Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺ in the I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction
  (S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺, then 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂; both steps involve oppositely charged ions, unlike the
  uncatalysed reaction between two negative ions; E⦵ values from FACTS.md show each step is feasible).
Sections: definitions; orders and the rate equation (cannot be deduced from the stoichiometric equation); graphs: three
`:::plot` figures (or one with two series and a second plot) of [A] against t for orders 0, 1, 2 (e.g. `--f "1-0.1*x"`,
`--f "exp(-0.2*x)"`, `--f "1/(1+0.5*x)"` with sensible domains and labels), and rate against [A] (horizontal line,
straight line through the origin, upward curve); embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-order" data-order="1"></div>`;
half-life method (constant half-lives → first order); initial-rates method with a three-experiment table (the
standard exam format: change one concentration at a time); calculating k and its units (s⁻¹, mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹,
mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹); mechanisms and rate-determining steps (examples: SN1 vs SN2 rate equations; 2NO₂ + F₂ type; propanone
+ iodine in acid: rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺], zero order in I₂, so I₂ is not in the slow step; H⁺ is a catalyst);
catalysis sections with a heterogeneous-catalysis figure (hand SVG: a metal surface row of atoms with molecules
adsorbing, reacting and desorbing in three panels, `fig wide`) and the Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ cycle (energy profile with two humps:
reuse `chem-profile` only if it fits, otherwise a hand SVG).
Worked examples (≥9): orders from an initial-rates table and k with units; t½ to k (k = 0.693/t½); concentration after
three half-lives; order from a concentration–time graph (constant half-life); a rate equation from a mechanism; a
mechanism consistent with a given rate equation; identify intermediate and catalyst; explain how Fe²⁺ catalyses the
I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction with E⦵ (S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ +2.01, Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77, I₂/I⁻ +0.54).
Pitfalls: orders from the equation's coefficients; units of k; "zero order means the substance is not involved" (it is
not in the rate-determining step or before it); intermediates vs transition states; "the catalyst is not involved".
Board box (sure): AQA's A Level uses the Arrhenius equation k = Ae^(−Eₐ/RT) and a graph of ln k against 1/T to find
Eₐ; 9701 treats the effect of temperature on k only qualitatively. Name AQA only.

## Widget `chem-order` (widgets/chem-order.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-order" data-order="1" data-k="0.10" data-a0="1.00"></div>`.
- Controls: order 0, 1 or 2 (tabs), k slider (units change with order and are shown: mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹, s⁻¹,
  mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹), [A]₀ slider (0.10–2.00 mol dm⁻³).
- Two canvases: [A] against t (time axis long enough for about four half-lives of the chosen case; for order 0 until
  [A] = 0), with successive half-lives marked by dashed guides and labelled (constant for order 1, doubling for order 2,
  halving for order 0); rate against [A] (line, straight line through the origin, parabola).
- Readout: first half-life (3 s.f., s), rate at t = 0, and the rate equation as text ("rate = k[A]").
- `.w-note`: "For first order, change [A]₀: the half-life stays the same."
- Probe each order, `data-k="0"` (friendly message), `data-a0="5"` (clamped with a note).
