## chem.f.basics · Chemistry from zero (`@level pre`, no `@paper`)
`@time 6–8 hours`. For a reader who has never studied chemistry (or forgot it). This is IGCSE-level groundwork, at
the depth needed to start [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms). Full topic structure (why, warm-up from maths:
[Numbers, fractions and percentages](#math.f.numbers), [Algebra from scratch](#math.f.algebra) or
[Indices, surds and standard form](#math.f.powers), all written), defs, worked examples, try, quiz, checklist, recap). Sections:
1. **Matter and particles**: elements, compounds, mixtures; atoms and molecules; the three states and changes of
   state in particle terms (melting, boiling, sublimation). `:::def` element, compound, mixture, molecule.
2. **The atom in one picture**: protons, neutrons, electrons (charge and mass, simply: details in chem.atoms),
   atomic number and mass number, simple shell filling 2, 8, 8 for Z ≤ 20. Figure: a hand SVG of a sodium atom
   as shells (Bohr model) with 2, 8, 1 electrons (`fig small`), labelled nucleus, shells.
3. **The Periodic Table**: groups and periods, metals and non-metals, the group number = outer electrons for
   groups 1, 2, 13–17 (group 13 has 3, … group 17 has 7; noble gases full). Embed the widget:
   `<div class="widget" data-w="periodic"></div>` and say what to try (search for an element, colour by block).
4. **Ions and ionic formulas**: why atoms gain/lose electrons; charges from group number (Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, O²⁻,
   Cl⁻); the common ions to learn (from FACTS 2.3.1(b): NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻, OH⁻, NH₄⁺, Zn²⁺, Ag⁺, HCO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻);
   writing formulas by balancing charges (\(\ce{Al2(SO4)3}\)), with brackets. `:::key` "charges add to zero".
5. **Covalent molecules**: sharing electrons simply (H₂, H₂O, CH₄, CO₂), formulas of common molecules.
6. **Equations**: word equations → symbol equations → balancing (count atoms each side, change only coefficients),
   state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq). Worked examples: \(\ce{Mg + O2 -> MgO}\) balanced, combustion of methane,
   \(\ce{Al + O2}\), a neutralisation. `:::warn` never change a formula to balance.
7. **Acids, bases and salts in a nutshell**: acids give H⁺(aq), alkalis give OH⁻(aq), pH scale, neutralisation gives a
   salt and water; acid + metal, acid + carbonate. (Full theory in [Acids and bases](#chem.acids).)
8. **Numbers chemists use**: standard form, significant figures (with the Cambridge rule: answer to the same s.f. as
   the least precise data, or one more), units cm³ and dm³ (1 dm³ = 1000 cm³), g and kg, kJ; rearranging
   \(n = m/M\) as a preview (link [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles)).
9. **Safety and the lab**: hazard symbols named in words only (corrosive, toxic, flammable, oxidising, irritant), basic
   apparatus names (beaker, conical flask, measuring cylinder, pipette, burette, test tube, Bunsen burner).
Worked examples (≥4): writing \(\ce{Ca(OH)2}\), \(\ce{(NH4)2SO4}\) from ions; balancing \(\ce{C3H8 + O2}\); converting
250 cm³ to dm³; rounding 0.0042516 to 3 s.f. Try ≥8 (formulas, balancing, s.f.), exam practice ★ only (6 short
questions, "[2 marks]" style), quiz ≥5 with misconceptions (e.g. "NaCl₂", "balancing by changing subscripts",
"Mg⁺"). Board box: none needed (pre chapter) unless sure. Checklist: 6–8 "I can" lines covering the sections.
`Next: [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms)`.
Pitfalls: do not teach orbitals (1s 2s 2p) here beyond a forward link; keep definitions consistent with later
chapters (e.g. "relative atomic mass" only as a forward reference).

## Widget `periodic` (widgets/periodic.js)
The Toolbox page and any chapter embed `<div class="widget" data-w="periodic"></div>`. Optional attributes:
`data-color="category|block"` (start colouring, default category), `data-highlight="Na,Mg,Al"` (outline these),
`data-select="Fe"` (open this element's details).
- **Data**: run `py data/elements.py` (writes data/elements.json from the 9701 syllabus table). Then write a
  small Python script (in scratch/) that writes widgets/periodic.js with the JSON embedded as
  `const EL = [...]` at the top of the IIFE. Never type element data. Fields: z, sym, name, ar (1 d.p. or null),
  group (1–18 or null for f-block), period, block, cat, config/short (Z ≤ 36 only).
- **Layout**: the 18-column grid (periods 1–7) with lanthanoids (57–71) and actinoids (89–103) in two rows below,
  and a "57–71" / "89–103" placeholder cell in group 3 of periods 6 and 7, as in the Cambridge table. Each cell shows
  Z (small), symbol (bold) and Ar (small, "–" when null). Group numbers 1–18 above the columns, period numbers at the left.
- **Colour** by category (alkali metal, alkaline earth metal, transition metal, d-block metal, post-transition metal,
  metalloid, non-metal, halogen, noble gas, lanthanoid, actinoid) or by block (s, p, d, f), using theme colours
  only (W.palette(el).series and CSS variables; repeat the six series colours with a pattern of solid fill vs
  outline if more than six classes); a legend below the grid lists the classes in words.
- **Details panel** (click or keyboard Enter on a cell): name, symbol, Z, Ar, group, period, block, category, and for
  Z ≤ 36 the full and shorthand configurations (`config`, `short`, shown with superscripts: 1s² 2s² …). Note that
  scandium and zinc are d-block metals but not transition elements in the 9701 definition (show this line for Sc and Zn).
- **Search** box: symbol, name or Z; matching cells highlighted, the first selected. Friendly message if none.
- **Phones**: the grid keeps its 18 columns and scrolls horizontally inside its own frame (`overflow-x: auto` on
  the grid wrapper, never the page); minimum cell width about 34 px. Controls wrap.
- Cells are buttons with `aria-label` "Iron, Fe, atomic number 26"; arrow keys move between cells.
- Probe: `node kit/zta.js widget periodic "" --probe`, then with `data-select="Cu" data-color="block"`, then with
  `data-select="Xx"` (must show a friendly message, not throw). Look once at widget.html?dark&phone.
