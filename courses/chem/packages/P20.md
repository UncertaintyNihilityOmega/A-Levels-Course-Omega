## chem.planning · Planning, analysis and evaluation (`@level a2`, `@time 6–8 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Paper 5`. The Paper 5 guide and skills chapter. Warm-up from [Practical skills and qualitative analysis](#chem.practical)
(uncertainty, tables), [Rate equations and catalysis](#chem.rates2) and [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles).
Outcomes for the checklist (the syllabus Paper 5 skills, FACTS.md): define the problem (independent, dependent and
control variables; a prediction as a hypothesis or a sketched graph); plan a method in full (apparatus, quantities and
concentrations, how to vary and measure, control experiments, risks and precautions, results table, graph to plot);
standard procedures (making a standard solution, weighing by difference, concordant titres, heating to constant mass,
more readings near an inflexion point); deal with data (means, percentages, % gain or loss, graphs of y against x to
find m and c, % error, s.f.); draw conclusions and explain them; evaluate (anomalies, replication, range, control of
variables, weaknesses, effect of changed conditions, reliability and confidence).
Sections:
1. What Paper 5 is (FACTS.md: 1 h 15 min, 30 marks, two or more questions, no laboratory, contexts may be outside the
   syllabus; mark grid planning 12, analysis/conclusion/evaluation 12, 6 variable).
2. Variables and predictions (`:::def` cards: independent variable, dependent variable, control variable, hypothesis;
   a worked prediction: "as the concentration of HCl increases, the time for the magnesium to dissolve decreases,
   because…", plus a sketch graph described or drawn with `:::plot`).
3. Writing a method that scores: a model plan built step by step (e.g. investigate how the rate of the reaction between
   calcium carbonate chips and hydrochloric acid depends on the acid concentration, by gas volume against time), with
   the apparatus figure (hand SVG: conical flask, bung, delivery tube, gas syringe; COURSE.md apparatus rules), the
   dilution table to make five concentrations from a stock solution (volumes of acid and water to 50.0 cm³ total),
   measuring instruments and precision, the control variables and how each is controlled, a control experiment where
   relevant, and a results table with headings.
4. Safety and risk: specific hazards and precautions (FACTS.md examples: fume cupboard, face mask, no naked flames for
   flammable solvents, chemically resistant gloves); "wear goggles" alone scores little.
5. Standard procedures: standard solution, weighing by difference, heating to constant mass, concordant titres, extra
   readings near an inflexion point.
6. Dealing with data: processed values (1/t, ln values when given as a method, means, %), choosing axes, plotting,
   a straight-line graph y = mx + c from data (worked: a plot of a quantity that gives the rate constant or a Ksp, with the
   gradient and intercept found and their units), % error in a measurement and in a final result.
7. Conclusions and evaluation: does the data support the hypothesis? anomalies (identify, explain, deal with: repeat
   or exclude from the mean, not from the table), adequacy of replication and range, control of variables, the most
   significant source of error and an improvement that fixes it; reliability vs accuracy wording as in the syllabus.
Worked examples (≥7, model answers built up mark by mark with [B1]): variables for a given aim; a dilution table;
a full plan for a gravimetric determination of x in a hydrated salt; % error of a titre; gradient and intercept with
units from a data table; identify the anomaly in a table and justify it; evaluate a method and suggest two improvements.
Try ≥5 and exam practice ★–★★★ including one complete 12-mark planning question with a mark-by-mark scheme.
Pitfalls: vague variables ("the amount"); no quantities; "repeat to make it accurate" (repeats improve reliability:
use the syllabus word "replicate" and say what it achieves); safety without the specific hazard; plotting 1/t vs
concentration without saying why.
Board box (sure): AQA has no separate planning paper; its written papers include practical-skills questions. Name AQA only.
`Next: [Paper 1 survival guide: multiple choice](#chem.exam.p1)`.

## chem.mock.p3 · Mock paper: Paper 3 practical skills (written) (`@level mock`)
`@time 2 h`, `@paper CAIE 9701 Paper 3 (written version)`, total **40 marks** exactly. Paper 3 is a laboratory exam;
this mock is a written paper with supplied readings, so readers practise everything except handling apparatus. An
`:::exam` box first: time allowed, total marks, "use the data supplied as your own readings", Data booklet values allowed,
"show working; give answers to an appropriate number of significant figures".
Three questions, each a bare `:::try` (STYLE.md "Mock papers"), each `Q: [n marks] (#chapter) …` with parts (a), (b), …
(blank line before each part) and `A:` the full mark scheme with marks per point:
- **Question 1 [16 marks] (#chem.practical)**: a titration to find the concentration of an unknown (e.g. FA 1, a
  solution of ethanedioic acid or iron(II) ions, titrated with KMnO₄; or a hydroxide with HCl). Supply a rough titre and
  three accurate burette tables (initial and final readings to 0.05 cm³; two concordant within 0.10 cm³). Parts: record
  the titres in a table with correct headings (marks for headings, precision, concordance), choose and calculate the mean,
  calculate moles and concentration (M1/A1 lines), a further deduction (Mr or x in a formula), the % uncertainty in the
  titre, and an improvement.
- **Question 2 [10 marks] (#chem.energetics)**: a thermometric or gravimetric experiment with supplied readings (e.g.
  temperatures every 30 s before and after mixing, or crucible masses through heating to constant mass). Parts: process
  the data (ΔT by extrapolation described in words with the value given, or mass of water lost), calculate ΔH or x, a
  % error from the thermometer or balance, identify the most significant source of error and a specific improvement.
- **Question 3 [14 marks] (#chem.practical)**: qualitative analysis with supplied observations for two unknown solids
  (FB 1 and FB 2). Give a table of tests with observations (wording exactly as in FACTS.md notes), then ask: identify the
  cation and anion of each with the evidence; name a further test to confirm one ion and its expected observation;
  write an ionic equation with state symbols for one precipitate; one organic test question (e.g. distinguish an aldehyde
  from a ketone with Tollens' or Fehling's, or identify CH₃CO– with alkaline iodine).
Marks add to 40 (16 + 10 + 14). checks/chem.mock.p3.py recomputes every titre, mean, concentration, ΔH and %.

## chem.mock.p5 · Mock paper: Paper 5 planning, analysis and evaluation (`@level mock`)
`@time 1 h 15 min`, `@paper CAIE 9701 Paper 5`, total **30 marks** exactly. `:::exam` instructions box first.
Two questions (STYLE.md "Mock papers"):
- **Question 1 [14 marks] (#chem.planning)**: a planning task in a context possibly outside the syllabus, with the
  background information supplied (e.g. determine the percentage by mass of calcium carbonate in eggshell by back
  titration, or investigate how temperature affects the rate of a reaction followed by colorimetry described in the
  question). Parts: prediction with a reason; independent, dependent and control variables; a full method (apparatus,
  quantities, procedure, how to vary and measure, a control experiment if relevant); a hazard and precaution; a results
  table; how the results will be processed to reach the answer.
- **Question 2 [16 marks] (#chem.planning)**: data analysis and evaluation. Supply a table of raw data (8–10 rows, with
  one anomalous row and inconsistent precision in one column as traps). Parts: complete a processed column (e.g. 1/t, or
  log values given the formula); describe the graph to draw (axes, scale choice) and give the best-fit line's gradient
  and intercept from two stated points (the answer must be checkable from the data: build the data from an exact line
  plus small offsets in the check script); identify and explain the anomaly; a conclusion with the mathematical
  relationship; an evaluation of the method (replication, range, control) and a % error; an improvement.
Marks add to 30 (14 + 16). checks/chem.mock.p5.py recomputes every processed value, gradient and intercept.
