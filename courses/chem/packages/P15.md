## chem.transition · Transition elements (`@time 8–10 hours`)
Topic 28.1 and 28.2. Warm-up from [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms) (configurations of ions), [Redox and oxidation numbers](#chem.redox)
and [Electrochemistry: electrolysis and electrode potentials](#chem.electrochem). Outcomes (checklist):
- 28.1 1 transition element = a d-block element which forms one or more stable ions with incomplete d orbitals (so Sc
  and Zn are not: Sc³⁺ is 3d⁰, Zn²⁺ is 3d¹⁰); 2 sketch 3dxy (four lobes between the x and y axes) and 3dz² (two lobes on z
  with a ring around the middle); 3 variable oxidation states, catalysts, complex ions, coloured compounds;
  4 variable oxidation states because 3d and 4s are similar in energy; 5 catalysts: more than one stable oxidation state
  and vacant d orbitals that are energetically accessible and can form dative bonds with ligands; 6 complex ions: vacant
  accessible d orbitals.
- 28.2 1 complexes of Cu(II) and Co(II) with H₂O, NH₃, OH⁻ and Cl⁻; 2 ligand = a species that contains a lone pair of
  electrons that forms a dative covalent bond to a central metal atom/ion; 3 (a) monodentate H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻,
  (b) bidentate en (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂) and ethanedioate C₂O₄²⁻, (c) polydentate EDTA⁴⁻; 4 complex = a molecule or ion formed by
  a central metal atom/ion surrounded by one or more ligands; 5 geometries: linear (180°, e.g. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺),
  square planar (90°, e.g. cisplatin), tetrahedral (109.5°, e.g. [CuCl₄]²⁻, [CoCl₄]²⁻), octahedral (90°, e.g.
  [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺); 6 (a) coordination number (number of dative bonds to the metal), (b) predict formula and charge of a
  complex; 7 ligand exchange (Cu(II), Co(II) with H₂O, NH₃, OH⁻, Cl⁻) — the observations below; 8 feasibility of redox
  reactions of transition elements from E⦵ (FACTS.md values, e.g. vanadium oxidation states with zinc; Fe²⁺ oxidised
  by MnO₄⁻); 9 reactions and calculations: (a) MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻ in acid (2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O;
  warm), (b) MnO₄⁻/Fe²⁺ in acid (MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O; self-indicating: end point first permanent
  pale pink), (c) Cu²⁺/I⁻ (2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂, then I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ with starch added near the end point:
  blue-black → colourless); 10 other redox systems from data.
Observations to use (state exactly these; they are standard 9701 descriptions — if unsure of any, describe less):
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ blue; with a little NH₃(aq) or NaOH(aq): pale blue precipitate Cu(OH)₂ (as [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄]); with excess
NH₃(aq): dark (deep) blue solution [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ (matches the FACTS.md QA note); with concentrated HCl: yellow(-green)
[CuCl₄]²⁻, tetrahedral. [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ pink; with concentrated HCl: blue [CoCl₄]²⁻, tetrahedral; with NaOH(aq): blue
precipitate of Co(OH)₂; with excess NH₃(aq): [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺, a brown (yellow-brown) solution that darkens in air as
Co(II) is oxidised.
Sections: where the transition elements are; configurations of the elements and ions Ti–Cu (link `chem-config`); the
definition and why Sc and Zn are excluded; d-orbital shapes figure (hand SVG of 3dxy and 3dz² with axes; COURSE.md orbital
style); the four properties with explanations; ligands and complexes (dative bonds; figures of an octahedral
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with wedge/dash bonds, tetrahedral [CuCl₄]²⁻, square planar, linear; en and EDTA as chelating ligands,
coordination number 6 with three en); formula and charge prediction `:::steps`; ligand exchange equations with
observations (a table); redox chemistry with E⦵ (e.g. VO₂⁺ → VO²⁺ → V³⁺ → V²⁺ with zinc in acid, using E⦵ values from
FACTS.md: Zn/Zn²⁺ −0.76 reduces all of them; colours of vanadium species only if sure: yellow VO₂⁺, blue VO²⁺, green V³⁺,
violet V²⁺); the three titrations with full worked calculations (percentage of iron in a tablet or wire; mass of
ethanedioate; percentage of copper in brass from a thiosulfate titre).
Worked examples (≥9): configuration of Cr³⁺ and Mn²⁺; is Zn a transition element?; formula and charge of a complex
of Fe³⁺ with six CN⁻ ([Fe(CN)₆]³⁻); coordination number of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ (6); ligand exchange observations; feasibility of
Fe³⁺ oxidising I⁻; MnO₄⁻/Fe²⁺ titration calculation ★★; Cu²⁺/I⁻/thiosulfate calculation ★★★; MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻.
Pitfalls: removing 3d before 4s for ions; coordination number = number of ligands (not for bidentate ligands);
"transition metals are coloured because of d electrons" without the splitting (next chapter); MnO₄⁻ titration ratio 1 : 5;
I₂ : S₂O₃²⁻ ratio 1 : 2 and Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ ratio 1 : 1.

## chem.complexes · Complexes: colour, isomerism and stability (`@time 6–8 hours`)
Topic 28.3–28.5. Warm-up from [Transition elements](#chem.transition), [Isomerism](#chem.org.isomerism) and
[Chemical equilibria](#chem.equilibria). Outcomes:
- 28.3 1 degenerate (same energy) and non-degenerate d orbitals; 2 splitting into two sets with energy gap ΔE: (a)
  octahedral: two higher (dz², dx²−y²) and three lower; (b) tetrahedral: three higher and two lower (smaller ΔE);
  3 colour: light of a particular frequency is absorbed as an electron is promoted from a lower to a higher d orbital
  (ΔE = hf); the colour seen is the complementary colour of the light absorbed; no colour if d⁰ or d¹⁰ (Sc³⁺, Zn²⁺, Cu⁺);
  4 different ligands give different ΔE, so a different frequency absorbed and a different colour; 5 the Cu(II) and
  Co(II) complexes with H₂O, NH₃, OH⁻ and Cl⁻ as examples of ligand exchange changing colour.
- 28.4 1 stereoisomerism in complexes: (a) cis/trans: square planar [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] (cisplatin is the cis isomer, an
  anticancer drug), octahedral [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ and [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺; (b) optical: [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ and the cis isomer of
  [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺; 2 overall polarity of these complexes (trans-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] non-polar, cis polar).
- 28.5 1 Kstab = the equilibrium constant for the formation of the complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or
  molecules; 2 Kstab expression without [H₂O] (e.g. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ ⇌ [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O,
  Kstab = [[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺] / ([[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺][NH₃]⁴)); 3 calculations (with Kstab values given as data); 4 ligand
  exchange explained by Kstab: the ligand giving the larger Kstab replaces the other; a large Kstab means a stable complex
  (EDTA⁴⁻ complexes are very stable, partly because one ligand replaces six water molecules: entropy increase — link
  [Lattice energy, entropy and Gibbs free energy](#chem.lattice)).
Sections: degenerate d orbitals and splitting diagrams (hand SVG `fig`, octahedral and tetrahedral side by side: five
degenerate levels in the free ion, split levels with ΔE labelled, `ln thick` level lines; COURSE.md); colour and
complementary colours (a colour wheel figure is optional: if drawn, use text labels for colour names and grey
segments, never coloured fills — the colours are named in words); embed
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-dsplit" data-geom="oct" data-ion="Cu2+"></div>`; ligand strength (qualitative:
Cl⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻ for ΔE, give it as "in these examples"); the Cu(II)/Co(II) colour changes table again with the
reason; isomerism figures (cis/trans square planar; cis/trans octahedral; the two optical isomers of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ drawn with
en as curved `ln thick` arcs between adjacent positions, mirror line); polarity; Kstab with worked calculations and a
ranking question (given log Kstab or Kstab values for several ligands, predict exchanges).
Worked examples (≥7): explain why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is blue (absorbs in the red-orange region: say "absorbs red/orange light
and transmits/reflects blue" only qualitatively); explain why Zn²⁺ is colourless; predict a colour change on ligand
exchange; draw/describe isomers of [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺; is cis-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ chiral? (yes); Kstab expression and
calculation; predict which ligand exchange happens from Kstab values.
Pitfalls: "the electron emits light when it falls back" (colour is from absorption); the octahedral splitting upside down
(two higher in octahedral, three higher in tetrahedral); including water in Kstab; cis/trans for a tetrahedral complex
(not possible).

## Widget `chem-dsplit` (widgets/chem-dsplit.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-dsplit" data-geom="oct" data-ion="Cu2+" data-ligand="H2O"></div>`.
- Selects: geometry (octahedral / tetrahedral), metal ion (Ti³⁺, V³⁺, Cr³⁺, Mn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Co²⁺, Ni²⁺, Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺, Sc³⁺;
  d-electron count computed from Z and charge with the 4s-first rule, not typed), ligand (Cl⁻, H₂O, NH₃, CN⁻) as a
  qualitative ΔE scale (relative units: say "relative ΔE, not to scale").
- Canvas: free-ion five degenerate levels on the left, the split levels on the right (oct: 3 lower, 2 higher; tet: 2 lower,
  3 higher, smaller gap), electrons as arrows filled by the rule "lower set first, one per orbital before pairing" for
  oct with weak-field ligands (do not teach high/low spin: show only this simple filling and say "electrons fill the
  lower set first"); an arrow showing one electron promoted.
- Readout: d-electron count; "coloured: yes/no" (no for d⁰ and d¹⁰); "a larger ΔE means higher-frequency light is absorbed".
  Light absorbed and colour seen are given in words only from a small mapping by relative ΔE band (red, orange, yellow,
  green, blue, violet absorbed → complementary colour seen) and labelled "illustrative". No hard-coded colours anywhere.
- Probe defaults, `data-ion="Zn2+"`, `data-geom="tet" data-ion="Co2+" data-ligand="Cl-"`, `data-ion="Xx"`.
