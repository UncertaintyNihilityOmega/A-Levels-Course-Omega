## chem.periodicity · Periodicity: Period 3 (`@level as`, `@time 6–8 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Papers 1 and 2 (AS); assumed knowledge in Paper 4`. Topic 9. Warm-up from
[Atomic structure](#chem.atoms), [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding) and [States of matter](#chem.states).
Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 9.1 1 describe qualitatively (and show the periodicity in) atomic radius, ionic radius, melting point and electrical
  conductivity of the elements; 2 explain m.p. and conductivity by structure and bonding.
- 9.2 1 reactions of the elements with oxygen (to Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂), chlorine (to NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃,
  SiCl₄, PCl₅) and water (Na and Mg only), with equations; 2 oxidation numbers of the oxides (Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀,
  SO₂, SO₃) and chlorides (NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅) from outer-shell electrons; 3 the oxides Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃,
  SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃ with water and the likely pH; 4 acid/base behaviour of the oxides Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂,
  SO₃ and hydroxides NaOH, Mg(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃, including amphoteric behaviour (with NaOH only), with equations;
  5 the chlorides NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅ with water and the likely pH; 6 explain these trends by bonding and
  electronegativity; 7 suggest bonding from properties.
- 9.3 1 predict properties of an element in a given group; 2 deduce the nature, position and identity of unknown
  elements from data.
Sections: physical trends (qualitative; describe the m.p. shape: rises Na → Al (stronger metallic bonding: more
delocalised electrons, smaller ions), maximum at Si (giant covalent), sharp drop to the simple molecular P₄, S₈, Cl₂,
Ar, with S₈ > P₄ > Cl₂ > Ar by number of electrons and id-id forces; conductivity high for Na, Mg, Al, low for Si
(semiconductor), none for the rest). Do not give numerical m.p. or radii (not in FACTS): a hand SVG sketch with no
y-numbers is fine, labelled "melting point (not to scale)". First IE across Period 3 uses Data-booklet values: embed
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-trend" data-mode="first" data-set="period3"></div>` and link back to
[Atomic structure](#chem.atoms). Electronegativity across Period 3 (Na 0.9 → Cl 3.0) explains the change from ionic to
covalent oxides and chlorides.
Reactions with equations (state symbols): 2Na + ½O₂ → Na₂O (or 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O, burns with a yellow-orange flame),
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (bright white flame), 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃, P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀, S + O₂ → SO₂ (blue flame; SO₃ needs a
catalyst: link the Contact process); chlorides similarly; Na + water (2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂, vigorous), Mg + cold
water (very slow) and steam (Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂). Oxides with water: Na₂O → NaOH (pH about 13–14), MgO slightly
soluble → Mg(OH)₂ (pH about 9–10), Al₂O₃ and SiO₂ insoluble (pH 7), P₄O₁₀ → H₃PO₄ (pH about 2), SO₂ → H₂SO₃ (pH about
2–3), SO₃ → H₂SO₄ (pH about 1); give pH values as "about". Acid/base: Al₂O₃ and Al(OH)₃ amphoteric with equations:
Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O; Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH + 3H₂O → 2NaAl(OH)₄; Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → NaAl(OH)₄. SiO₂ reacts with hot
concentrated NaOH (SiO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + H₂O) — mention as acidic behaviour. Chlorides with water: NaCl dissolves
(pH 7), MgCl₂ dissolves (slightly acidic, about 6.5), AlCl₃ hydrolyses (about 3; [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ explanation can be a
stretch), SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl (white fumes, pH about 1–2), PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl (pH about 1–2).
Oxidation numbers equal the number of outer electrons used (Na +1 … S +6 in SO₃, P +5, Cl in chlorides −1).
A summary table: formula, bonding, structure, behaviour with water, pH, acid/base nature.
Worked examples (≥6): explain the m.p. of Si vs P; explain why MgCl₂ solution is nearly neutral but SiCl₄ gives pH 1–2;
write the amphoteric equations; deduce an unknown element from data (e.g. its chloride hydrolyses giving white fumes,
its oxide is a giant structure insoluble in water and acidic: Si); predict properties of germanium or selenium
(9.3.1) carefully and qualitatively.
Pitfalls: "SiO₂ dissolves to make an acid" (it is insoluble); confusing P₄O₁₀ with P₂O₅ in equations (use P₄O₁₀);
"AlCl₃ is ionic" (covalent, Al₂Cl₆ dimer, link [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding)); writing pH values as exact facts.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.group2 · Group 2 (`@level as a2`, `@time 5–7 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Papers 1 and 2 (AS); Paper 4 (A Level section)`. Topics 10 (AS) and 27 (A Level). The A Level
part is the last `##` section, "Explaining the trends (A Level)", and starts by saying AS students can stop before it;
it links back from [Lattice energy, entropy and Gibbs free energy](#chem.lattice) ideas (link forward to it).
Outcomes:
- 10.1 1 the elements with oxygen, water and dilute HCl and H₂SO₄ (equations); 2 oxides, hydroxides and carbonates
  with water and dilute HCl and H₂SO₄; 3 thermal decomposition of nitrates and carbonates and the trend in thermal
  stability; 4 describe and predict trends; 5 solubility of hydroxides (increases down the group) and sulfates
  (decreases down the group).
- 27.1 1 thermal stability of nitrates and carbonates explained by the ionic radius of the cation and polarisation of
  the large anion (smaller cation, higher charge density, distorts the CO₃²⁻/NO₃⁻ electron cloud more, weakening a C–O
  or N–O bond, so decomposes more easily: MgCO₃ decomposes at a lower temperature than BaCO₃); 2 solubility and
  ΔHsol of the hydroxides and sulfates explained by the relative sizes of ΔHhyd and lattice energy (both become less
  exothermic down the group; for sulfates the large anion means the lattice energy changes little while ΔHhyd of the
  cation falls a lot, so ΔHsol becomes more endothermic and solubility falls; for hydroxides the lattice energy falls
  faster than ΔHhyd, so solubility rises).
Sections: physical trends (atomic radius increases, first IE decreases: data from FACTS.md Mg 736, Ca 590, Sr 548,
Ba 502; embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-trend" data-mode="first" data-set="group2"></div>` optionally);
reactions with oxygen (2M + O₂ → 2MO), water (Mg with steam; Ca, Sr, Ba with cold water, M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂,
more vigorous down the group), dilute acids (M + 2HCl → MCl₂ + H₂; with H₂SO₄ the reaction of Ca, Sr, Ba slows
because an insoluble sulfate coats the metal); oxides and hydroxides with water and acids; carbonates with acids;
thermal decomposition: MCO₃ → MO + CO₂; 2M(NO₃)₂ → 2MO + 4NO₂ + O₂ (brown gas); solubility trends with uses (Mg(OH)₂
as an antacid; BaSO₄ insoluble, used to test for sulfate ions and as a "barium meal" for X-ray imaging; Ca(OH)₂ to
neutralise acidic soil). A Level section with a polarisation figure (hand SVG: a small cation distorting a large
carbonate ion's electron cloud, drawn as a distorted ellipse; `fig small`) and the solubility cycle explanation
(link the energy cycle from chem.lattice).
Worked examples (≥6): equations for Sr + water and Ba + HCl; predict the decomposition of radium carbonate relative to
barium carbonate; mass of CaO from heating CaCO₃ (moles link); explain the trend in thermal stability (A Level, marks);
explain the trend in sulfate solubility (A Level, marks).
Pitfalls: "Mg reacts vigorously with cold water"; nitrate decomposition equation unbalanced; saying thermal stability
"decreases down the group"; the A Level answers must name the cation's charge density and the polarisation of the anion.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## Widget `chem-trend` (widgets/chem-trend.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-trend" data-mode="first" data-set="period3"></div>`.
- **Data**: generate the JS data with a Python script from FACTS.md lines "First IE / kJ mol⁻¹", "Successive IE (1st
  2nd 3rd 4th)" and "Pauling electronegativity" (parse the text; never type the numbers), plus symbols/Z from
  data/elements.json. Elements missing from the booklet (Ge, As, Se, Kr for IE) are simply absent; say so in a note.
- **Modes** (tabs): `first` (first IE against Z), `en` (electronegativity against Z), `successive` (1st to 4th IE of one
  chosen element as bars; log₁₀ scale toggle; the largest jump highlighted and the group deduced: "big jump after the
  2nd: 2 outer electrons, Group 2").
- **Sets** (`data-set`): `1-20`, `1-36`, `period2`, `period3`, `group1` (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs), `group2` (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba),
  `group17` (F, Cl, Br, I). Points labelled with symbols, joined in Z order; axis labels with units; the y-range follows
  the data (min 0).
- Hover/tap a point: value readout. `data-el="Al"` picks the element in successive mode.
- Probe each mode and set, `data-set="zz"` and `data-el="Kr"` (friendly message).
