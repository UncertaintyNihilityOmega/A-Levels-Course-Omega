## chem.bonding · Chemical bonding (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topic 3.1–3.4 and 3.7. Warm-up from [Atomic structure](#chem.atoms) (configurations, IE trends).
Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 3.1 1 electronegativity = the power of an atom to attract electrons to itself; 2 factors: nuclear charge, atomic
  radius, shielding by inner shells and sub-shells; 3 trends: increases across a period, decreases down a group;
  4 use Pauling differences to predict ionic or covalent bonding (Pauling values from FACTS.md; covalent character in
  ionic compounds not assessed).
- 3.2 1 ionic bonding = electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (cations and anions); 2 NaCl, MgO, CaF₂.
- 3.3 1 metallic bonding = electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.
- 3.4 1 covalent bonding = electrostatic attraction between the nuclei of two atoms and a shared pair of electrons;
  (a) H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂, HCl, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄; (b) expanded octets in SO₂, PCl₅, SF₆ (Period 3 can expand);
  (c) coordinate (dative covalent) bonding: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄⁺ (as NH₄Cl) and the Al₂Cl₆ dimer;
  2 (a) σ bonds (direct, end-on overlap) and π bonds (sideways overlap of adjacent p orbitals above and below the σ bond),
  (b) σ and π in H₂, C₂H₆, C₂H₄, HCN, N₂, (c) hybridisation sp, sp², sp³;
  3 (a) bond energy = the energy required to break one mole of a particular covalent bond in the gaseous state; bond
  length = the internuclear distance of two covalently bonded atoms; (b) use bond energy and bond length to compare
  reactivity (e.g. N≡N 944 kJ mol⁻¹ makes N₂ unreactive; the weak F–F bond, 158).
- 3.7 1 dot-and-cross diagrams for ionic, covalent and coordinate bonding, all species in 3.4 and 3.5, including
  expanded octets and odd-electron species (e.g. NO₂ or the methyl radical, with one unpaired electron).
Sections: why atoms bond (lower energy, attraction); electronegativity (with the Pauling table from FACTS.md as a
small Markdown table for Period 2 and 3); ionic bonding (dot-and-cross for NaCl, MgO, CaF₂ — show outer shells only,
square brackets and charges); metallic bonding; covalent bonding (each molecule in 3.4.1(a) by dot-and-cross; double
and triple bonds; lone pairs); expanded octets; coordinate bonds (arrow from donor; NH₄⁺, Al₂Cl₆ structure figure);
σ and π bonds (figure: orbital overlap for σ (s–s, p–p end-on) and π (two p orbitals side by side), with the π
electron density above and below); hybridisation (sp³ in CH₄ 109.5°, sp² in C₂H₄ 120°, sp in HCN/C₂H₂ 180°; count σ
and π: C₂H₄ has 5 σ and 1 π; HCN 2 σ and 2 π; N₂ 1 σ and 2 π); bond energy and bond length (C–C 350, C=C 610,
C≡C 840; shorter bonds are stronger; H–X bond energies from FACTS.md predict HI least stable).
**Dot-and-cross figures**: hand SVG, `fig` (600 wide) with up to three diagrams per figure. Convention: overlapping
circles (`ln`) for covalent shells (radius ~38 px, centres ~52 px apart), electrons as dots (`fi` circles r 3) and crosses
(two short `ln` lines forming × of 6 px), element symbols in the middle (`tl tc`), ionic diagrams in square brackets
(`ln` paths) with the charge at the top right. Draw only outer shells. Plan coordinates with Python. One figure for
H₂O, NH₃, CO₂; one for NaCl and MgO; one for NH₄⁺ showing the dative pair (both electrons from N, e.g. two dots).
Worked examples (≥5): predict ionic or covalent from electronegativity difference (e.g. NaCl 3.0 − 0.9 = 2.1, ionic;
HCl 0.9, polar covalent; use the rule of thumb as "large difference (around 2 or more) → ionic", stated as a guide);
dot-and-cross of SF₆ (12 electrons around S); σ/π count in propene or ethanenitrile; why N₂ is unreactive (bond energy
944 and no polarity); Al₂Cl₆ dative bonds.
Pitfalls: dot-and-cross diagrams with the wrong number of electrons (count total valence electrons first); calling a
dative bond "ionic"; saying π bonds are stronger than σ bonds (a π bond alone is weaker; C=C 610 < 2 × C–C 700);
writing "molecules of NaCl"; drawing inner shells by mistake when asked for outer only (allowed, but must be right).
Board box: leave it out unless you are sure of one board and one content difference.

## chem.shapes · Shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces (`@time 6–8 hours`)
Topic 3.5, 3.6 and the structure-and-properties link (forward to chem.states). Warm-up from
[Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding). Outcomes:
- 3.5 1 VSEPR shapes and angles: BF₃ trigonal planar 120°; CO₂ linear 180°; CH₄ tetrahedral 109.5°; NH₃ pyramidal 107°;
  H₂O non-linear 104.5°; SF₆ octahedral 90°; PF₅ trigonal bipyramidal 120° and 90°; 2 predict shapes and angles of
  analogous molecules and ions (e.g. NH₄⁺ tetrahedral 109.5°, H₃O⁺ pyramidal ~107°, SO₂ non-linear, PCl₅, SiCl₄,
  CO₃²⁻ trigonal planar, NO₃⁻, AlCl₄⁻).
- 3.6 1 (a) hydrogen bonding limited to N–H and O–H (NH₃, H₂O), (b) anomalous properties of water: high m.p. and b.p.,
  high surface tension, ice less dense than water (open hydrogen-bonded lattice); 2 bond polarity and dipole moments
  from electronegativity (symmetrical molecules such as CO₂, BF₃, CH₄ are non-polar overall); 3 (a) van der Waals'
  forces as the generic term for all intermolecular forces, (b) id-id (London dispersion) and pd-pd (including hydrogen
  bonding), (c) hydrogen bonding as a special case of pd-pd where H is bonded to a highly electronegative atom;
  4 ionic, covalent and metallic bonds are generally stronger than intermolecular forces.
Sections: VSEPR rules (electron pairs repel and get as far apart as possible; lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs:
lp–lp > lp–bp > bp–bp, which closes the angle by about 2.5° per lone pair in the tetrahedral family; multiple bonds count
as one region); a method `:::steps` (count valence electrons of the central atom, add/subtract for charge, add one per
bonded atom for single bonds, divide by 2 = pairs, subtract bonding pairs = lone pairs); a summary table (pairs, lone
pairs, shape, angle, example). Embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-vsepr" data-preset="NH3"></div>` and say what
to try. Polarity: dipole arrows, symmetry cancels. Intermolecular forces: id-id (increase with number of electrons and
contact area: boiling points of alkanes and of halogens, chain vs branched isomers), pd-pd (propanone vs butane,
similar Mr), hydrogen bonding (figure: two water molecules with δ+ H, δ− O, lone pair and dashed H-bond in a straight
line O–H···O, labelled; ice lattice described in words). Comparing boiling points across the hydrides of Group 16
(qualitative: H₂O anomalously high) — avoid numerical b.p. values unless you are sure; describe the pattern.
Worked examples (≥5): shape and angle of PCl₃ (pyramidal, about 107°); SO₄²⁻ (tetrahedral, 109.5°); polarity of
CHCl₃ vs CCl₄; explain the b.p. order of methane, ammonia, water; explain why ice floats. Stretch: an unfamiliar
species such as ClF₃ (5 pairs, 2 lone pairs: T-shaped) or ICl₄⁻ (6 pairs, 2 lone pairs: square planar).
Figures: shapes figure (hand SVG wide, 640 × 220): five shapes with wedge/dash bonds — wedge as a filled `fi`
triangle, dash as `ln dash thick` line, plain bonds `ln thick` (state this convention in the text: wedge toward you,
dash away); angles labelled.
Pitfalls: counting a double bond as two regions; "H₂O is linear"; hydrogen bonds drawn to H atoms on C; saying
intermolecular forces are "bonds broken on boiling covalent molecules" (the covalent bonds do not break); confusing
polar bonds with polar molecules.
Board box (sure): none required; leave it out unless sure.

## Widget `chem-vsepr` (widgets/chem-vsepr.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-vsepr" data-preset="NH3"></div>` or `data-bp="3" data-lp="1"`.
- Controls: bonding pairs (2–6) and lone pairs (0–3), total pairs 2–6; preset select: BF3, CO2, CH4, NH3, H2O, SF6, PF5,
  NH4+, H3O+, BeCl2, PCl5, SO2 (two bonding regions, each S=O counted once, plus one lone pair: non-linear, a little
  less than 120°). Double bonds count as one region; the widget models regions, so label presets with double bonds
  accordingly.
- A 3D view (W.view3d + W.orbit, drag to rotate) of the central atom, bonded atoms (labelled spheres) and lone pairs
  (lobes or translucent ellipses using theme colours), with the ideal electron-pair geometry; bond-angle arcs labelled.
- Readout: electron pairs, shape name (Cambridge names: linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, pyramidal, non-linear,
  trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral; for others: see-saw, T-shaped, square pyramidal, square planar), bond angle(s):
  use the syllabus values for the syllabus molecules (107° for 3 bp + 1 lp, 104.5° for 2 bp + 2 lp) and ideal values
  otherwise with "about".
- `.w-note`: "Add a lone pair to CH₄ and watch the angle close." Respect reduced motion (no auto-spin).
- Probe defaults, each preset, `data-bp="7"` (friendly error), `data-bp="2" data-lp="3"` (linear).
