## chem.states · States of matter (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topic 4. Warm-up from [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles) and [Shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces](#chem.shapes).
Outcomes (copy into the checklist):
- 4.1 1 pressure from collisions of gas molecules with the container walls; 2 ideal gases have zero particle volume
  and no intermolecular forces of attraction; 3 pV = nRT in calculations, including finding Mr (Mr = mRT/pV).
- 4.2 1 lattice structures: (a) giant ionic NaCl, MgO; (b) simple molecular I₂, buckminsterfullerene C₆₀, ice;
  (c) giant molecular SiO₂, graphite, diamond; (d) giant metallic Cu; 2 effect of structure and bonding on m.p., b.p.,
  electrical conductivity and solubility; 3 deduce structure and bonding from given information.
Sections: the kinetic picture of gases; ideal gas assumptions and when real gases deviate (high pressure, low
temperature; molecules have volume and attract); pV = nRT with SI units (p in Pa, V in m³, T in K: 1 dm³ = 10⁻³ m³,
1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, °C + 273); finding Mr of a volatile liquid (mass of vapour in a gas syringe at known T, p — a real
practical context); molar volume link (24.0 dm³ at room conditions, check with pV = nRT at 298 K and 101 kPa:
V = 8.31 × 298 / 101000 = 0.0245 m³ → about 24 dm³). Embed the existing physics widget
`<div class="widget" data-w="phys-gas"></div>` only after checking its name in kit/LINKS.md (it is listed) and say what
to try; the text must work without it. Structures: each of the four types with a figure or a clear description
(NaCl lattice drawn as a 2D grid of alternating ions is enough: hand SVG `fig small`; graphite layers vs diamond
tetrahedral network described with one figure of graphite layers (hexagons in parallel layers, delocalised electrons
between) — keep figures simple and accurate); properties table (structure type, example, m.p./b.p., conductivity
solid/molten/aqueous, solubility in water); a deduction method `:::steps` (conducts when solid → metallic or graphite;
conducts only molten/aqueous → ionic; high m.p., non-conductor → giant molecular; low m.p. → simple molecular).
Worked examples (≥5): pV = nRT for a gas volume at given T, p; Mr of a vapour from data (e.g. 0.240 g occupies 81.0 cm³
at 373 K and 101 kPa → Mr = mRT/pV); conversion pitfalls; deduce structure from a property table; explain why
graphite conducts but diamond does not; why ice is less dense (link back).
Pitfalls: T in °C; V in dm³ in pV = nRT; "ionic solids conduct" (only when molten or dissolved: ions must move);
"C₆₀ is giant" (it is simple molecular); breaking covalent bonds when simple molecular substances melt.

## chem.energetics · Chemical energetics (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topic 5. Warm-up from [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles) and [Chemical bonding](#chem.bonding) (bond energy).
Outcomes:
- 5.1 1 exothermic (ΔH negative) and endothermic (ΔH positive); 2 reaction pathway diagrams with ΔH and activation
  energy; 3 (a) standard conditions 298 K and 101 kPa, shown by ⦵ (write `^{\ominus}`), (b) define ΔHr, ΔHf, ΔHc,
  ΔHneut. Standard wording to use: enthalpy change of formation = the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is
  formed from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions; of combustion = when one mole of a
  substance is burnt completely in oxygen under standard conditions; of neutralisation = when one mole of water is
  formed by the reaction of an acid with an alkali under standard conditions; of reaction = the enthalpy change for the
  amounts in the equation as written. 4 energy transfer from breaking (endothermic) and making (exothermic) bonds;
  5 bond energies to calculate ΔHr (ΔH = Σ bonds broken − Σ bonds formed); 6 some bond energies exact (diatomic) and
  some averages (FACTS.md tables 3(a) and 3(b)); 7 ΔH from experiments: q = mcΔT and ΔH = −mcΔT/n.
- 5.2 1 Hess's law energy cycles; 2 calculations: (a) ΔH not found by experiment (e.g. ΔHf of CO, or of a hydrated
  salt from two solution enthalpies), (b) with bond energy data.
Sections: system and surroundings, exo/endo; pathway diagram figure (hand SVG, COURSE.md "Energy profiles", one exo
and one endo side by side, 600 × 240) and embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-profile" data-dh="-90" data-ea="120"></div>`;
standard conditions and the definitions (each a `:::def`); calorimetry (simple polystyrene-cup method for
neutralisation or displacement, with a `:::lab` box; combustion of a fuel using a spirit burner and a copper can,
with heat losses as the main error; temperature–time graph with extrapolation to the time of mixing: `:::plot` or
hand SVG); worked calculation q = mcΔT with m = mass of solution (assume density 1.00 g cm⁻³, c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹),
then ΔH = −q/n in kJ mol⁻¹ with sign; bond energies (methane combustion using FACTS.md values C–H 410, O=O 496,
C=O in CO₂ 805, O–H 460: ΔH = 4(410) + 2(496) − [2(805) + 4(460)] = −818 kJ mol⁻¹; explain why it differs from the data
book ΔHc: average bond energies, water as a gas); Hess's law (cycle figure: elements at the bottom for formation
cycles, combustion products at the bottom for combustion cycles; arrows as in COURSE.md "Energy cycles");
worked examples with ΔHf data and ΔHc data (supply the data in the question: values for real compounds must be
standard data-book values you are sure of, e.g. ΔHf(H₂O(l)) = −286, ΔHf(CO₂(g)) = −394 kJ mol⁻¹; otherwise call them
"data for this question").
Worked examples (≥7, incl. a real context: energy from burning fuels or a hand-warmer): q and ΔH from a cup
experiment; ΔHc from a spirit-burner experiment; ΔHr from bond energies; ΔHr from ΔHf; ΔHf from ΔHc (e.g. ethanol);
an indirect ΔH via a cycle (e.g. CaCO₃ decomposition from two neutralisation-type measurements).
Pitfalls: sign of ΔH from mcΔT; mass of solution vs mass of solid; ΔT in K equals ΔT in °C; using kJ vs J; reversing
arrows without changing sign; multiplying by coefficients; "bond making needs energy".
Board box (sure): leave it out unless sure.

## Widget `chem-profile` (widgets/chem-profile.js)
`<div class="widget" data-w="chem-profile" data-dh="-90" data-ea="120" data-cat="0"></div>`.
- Sliders (W.sliderNum): ΔH from −400 to +400 kJ mol⁻¹ (step 10), Eₐ (forward) from 10 to 400 (step 10; Eₐ must be
  greater than ΔH when ΔH > 0: clamp and say so in a `.w-note`), checkbox "add a catalyst" (Eₐ with catalyst = slider,
  default 50% of Eₐ). Optional `data-labels="reactants,products"` (e.g. `"N2 + 3H2,2NH3"`), plain text.
- Canvas (W.canvas): axes "enthalpy" (y, no numbers) and "progress of reaction" (x); reactant level, product level, the
  smooth hump (cubic curve) peaking at reactants + Eₐ; catalysed hump dashed (sc2); double-headed arrows for ΔH and Eₐ
  with value labels ("ΔH = −90 kJ mol⁻¹", "Eₐ = 120 kJ mol⁻¹", "Eₐ (catalysed)"). The y-range follows the values
  (top = max level + 15%).
- Readout: exothermic/endothermic, Eₐ of the reverse reaction (= Eₐ − ΔH).
- Kinetics ([Reaction kinetics](#chem.kinetics)) reuses this widget with `data-cat="1"`.
- Probe defaults, `data-dh="150" data-ea="80"` (clamp), `data-cat="1"`, `data-dh="abc"` (friendly message).
