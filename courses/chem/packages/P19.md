## chem.analysis2 · Chromatography and NMR spectroscopy (`@level a2`, `@time 7–9 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Paper 4`. Topic 37. Warm-up from [Infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry](#chem.analysis),
[Shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces](#chem.shapes) and [Introduction to organic chemistry](#chem.org.intro).
Outcomes (checklist):
- 37.1 TLC: 1 stationary phase (e.g. aluminium oxide on a solid support, or silica), mobile phase (a polar or non-polar
  solvent), Rf value (distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front), solvent front and baseline
  (pencil line, above the solvent level); 2 interpret Rf values (compare with standards in the same conditions);
  3 differences in Rf by interaction (adsorption) with the stationary phase and solubility in the mobile phase.
- 37.2 GLC: 1 stationary phase (a high boiling point non-polar liquid on a solid support), mobile phase (an unreactive
  gas, e.g. nitrogen or helium), retention time; 2 percentage composition from peak areas (area of one peak ÷ total area
  × 100, assuming the detector responds equally); 3 retention times by interaction with the stationary phase (and
  volatility).
- 37.3 ¹³C NMR: 1 deduce (a) the number of carbon environments, (b) possible structures; 2 predict or explain the number
  of peaks (shifts from FACTS.md table 7).
- 37.4 ¹H NMR: 1 deduce (a) proton environments from chemical shift (FACTS.md table 6), (b) relative numbers from peak
  areas (integration), (c) the number of equivalent protons on the adjacent carbon by the n + 1 rule (singlet, doublet,
  triplet, quartet, multiplet), (d) possible structures; 2 predict shifts and splitting; 3 TMS, Si(CH₃)₄, as the standard
  (δ = 0: one sharp peak, inert, volatile, upfield of most signals); 4 deuterated solvents (CDCl₃) so the solvent gives no
  ¹H signal; 5 identify O–H and N–H protons by D₂O exchange (the peak disappears; O–H/N–H protons usually do not split
  or get split).
Sections: TLC (figure: plate with baseline, solvent front, two spots, distances labelled with arrows; Rf calculation;
visualising colourless spots with UV light or iodine — mention briefly); GLC (figure: a chromatogram with three peaks on
a time axis; percentage composition worked example with triangle-approximation areas given as data); ¹³C NMR (counting
environments with symmetry; worked: propan-1-ol 3 peaks, propan-2-ol 2 peaks, propanone 2, methyl propanoate 4, benzene
1, methylbenzene 5); ¹H NMR (shifts, integration, splitting; the n + 1 rule figure: a triplet and a quartet with
1 : 2 : 1 and 1 : 3 : 3 : 1 heights; spectrum figures as hand SVG: x axis δ from 12 (left) to 0 (right), TMS at 0;
COURSE.md "Graphs"); embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-nmr" data-mol="ethanol"></div>`; D₂O exchange; full
structure-solving method `:::steps` (molecular formula → IR → ¹H shifts → integration → splitting → assemble → check ¹³C count).
Worked examples (≥8): Rf from distances; % composition from GLC peak areas; number of ¹³C peaks for four isomers of
C₄H₁₀O; predict the ¹H NMR of ethyl ethanoate (three signals, quoted as the FACTS.md ranges: CH₃–C=O 2.2–3.0 singlet;
–O–CH₂– 3.2–4.0 quartet; CH₃ 0.9–1.7 triplet; areas 3 : 2 : 3); identify a
compound C₃H₆O₂ from its ¹H spectrum; use D₂O to find the OH peak; a combined IR + MS + NMR ★★★ problem.
Pitfalls: Rf greater than 1 (impossible); counting splitting from protons on the same carbon; using n instead of n + 1;
forgetting that equivalent protons by symmetry give one signal; quoting shift values outside the booklet ranges.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.practical · Practical skills and qualitative analysis (`@level as`, `@time 8–10 hours`)
`@paper CAIE 9701 Paper 3`. The Paper 3 guide and skills chapter; Paper 5 skills are in
[Planning, analysis and evaluation](#chem.planning). Use FACTS.md "Practical skills facts" and the qualitative analysis
notes exactly. Warm-up from [Moles and stoichiometry](#chem.moles), [Chemical energetics](#chem.energetics) and
[Reaction kinetics](#chem.kinetics). Outcomes for the checklist (from the syllabus Paper 3 skill grid): collect data and
observations; make accurate, consistent measurements (concordant titres within 0.10 cm³); decide on numbers of tests,
ranges and repeats; record data in one table with units in headings and consistent precision; show calculations and
use the right s.f.; draw graphs (scales, plotting, lines of best fit, anomalies, gradients); interpret data and draw
conclusions; identify errors (systematic vs random), uncertainties and improvements; carry out and interpret the
qualitative tests.
Sections:
1. What Paper 3 is (FACTS.md: 2 h, 40 marks, the mark grid 12/6/10 + 12 variable); it is a laboratory exam, so this
   chapter teaches the skills and the written parts; link the board's past papers and the PMT topic page.
2. Titration (`:::steps`: rinse and fill the burette, read the bottom of the meniscus, rough titre, accurate titres to
   0.05 cm³, swirl, white tile, dropwise near the end point, concordant results); results table format; mean of
   concordant titres only; the three titration families from FACTS.md (acid–alkali with the listed indicators; KMnO₄
   with H₂O₂, Fe²⁺ or ethanedioate: self-indicating, first permanent pale pink; thiosulfate–iodine with starch near the
   end point); a full worked titration calculation with marks; making a standard solution in a 250 cm³ volumetric
   flask (weighing by difference).
3. Other quantitative experiments from FACTS.md: rates (thiosulfate + acid, cross disappears; rate ∝ 1/t), gravimetric
   (heating a hydrated salt in a crucible on a pipe-clay triangle to constant mass; worked: find x in BaCl₂·xH₂O or
   MgSO₄·xH₂O from masses), thermometric (ΔH from a temperature rise; extrapolation on a temperature–time graph),
   gas collection over water (carbonate + acid → CO₂ volume → composition of a mixture).
4. Measurement uncertainty: half the smallest division (thermometer ±0.5 °C, burette reading ±0.05 cm³ so a titre has
   ±0.10 cm³ from two readings, balance to 0.01 g and so on); percentage uncertainty; the FACTS.md example (2 × 0.5 /
   14.0 × 100 = 7.14%); systematic vs random errors; "human error" is not accepted; heat loss as the major error in
   calorimetry; improvements that address the named error.
5. Tables and graphs: headings "volume / cm³", raw data to the same precision, graph scales 1/2/5 per 2 cm, points over
   half the grid, × or ⊙, best-fit line, anomalous points ringed, gradient from a large triangle. A `:::plot` of a
   straight-line rate graph with a best-fit line and a gradient triangle.
6. Qualitative analysis: the cation table (NaOH(aq) and NH₃(aq), each a few drops then excess), the anion tests, the
   gas tests, iodine with starch: exact wording from FACTS.md as Markdown tables; flow-chart method for an unknown
   (`:::steps`); organic tests from FACTS.md. Embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-qa"></div>` and say what to try.
7. Recording observations: "white precipitate, soluble in excess giving a colourless solution" (state both what you see
   and what changes; "no change" is an observation).
Worked examples (≥8): mean titre from four readings (exclude the rough and any non-concordant); concentration from a
titration to 4 s.f. where justified; x in a hydrated salt; ΔH from a thermometric experiment with the % uncertainty in ΔT;
identify a cation from two observations; identify an anion from a test sequence; choose a test to distinguish two given
ions (e.g. Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺: excess NH₃(aq)); evaluate an experiment and suggest an improvement.
Spot the error ideas: titre mean including the rough titre; burette readings to 0.1 cm³; "the precipitate dissolved"
without saying in excess of which reagent.
Board box (sure): AQA assesses practical skills through the written papers and a separate practical endorsement
(teacher-assessed required practicals), not a timed laboratory exam like Paper 3. Name AQA only.
