## chem.org.hydrocarbons · Hydrocarbons: alkanes and alkenes (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topics 14 and 20 (addition polymerisation is taught here). Warm-up from [Introduction to organic chemistry](#chem.org.intro),
[Chemical energetics](#chem.energetics) (bond energies) and [Isomerism](#chem.org.isomerism). Outcomes (checklist):
- 14.1 1 alkanes made by (a) hydrogenation of alkenes: H₂(g), Pt or Ni catalyst, heat; (b) cracking of longer alkanes:
  heat with Al₂O₃; 2 (a) complete and incomplete combustion, (b) free-radical substitution by Cl₂ or Br₂ in UV light
  (ethane); 3 mechanism: initiation (Cl₂ → 2Cl•, homolytic fission by UV), propagation (Cl• + C₂H₆ → C₂H₅• + HCl;
  C₂H₅• + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + Cl•), termination (two radicals combine: Cl• + Cl•, C₂H₅• + Cl•, C₂H₅• + C₂H₅• → C₄H₁₀);
  further substitution gives a mixture; 4 cracking gives more useful smaller alkanes and alkenes; 5 unreactivity: strong
  C–H bonds (410 kJ mol⁻¹) and low polarity; 6 environmental effects of CO (toxic), oxides of nitrogen and unburnt
  hydrocarbons from engines, and their catalytic removal (catalytic converters; link [Nitrogen and sulfur](#chem.nitrogen)).
- 14.2 1 alkenes made by (a) elimination of HX from a halogenoalkane with NaOH in ethanol, heat (link
  [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen)), (b) dehydration of an alcohol (heated Al₂O₃ or concentrated H₂SO₄),
  (c) cracking; 2 (a) electrophilic addition of (i) H₂, Pt/Ni, heat (hydrogenation; margarine context), (ii) steam,
  H₂O(g), H₃PO₄ catalyst, (iii) HX(g) at room temperature, (iv) X₂ (Br₂ at room temperature); (b) cold dilute acidified
  KMnO₄ → diol (purple → colourless); (c) hot concentrated acidified KMnO₄ splits the C=C: =CH₂ → CO₂ (+ H₂O), =CHR →
  RCOOH, =CRR′ → ketone RCOR′; use the products to locate the double bond; (d) addition polymerisation of ethene and
  propene; 3 bromine water test: orange → colourless; 4 electrophilic addition mechanism: Br₂/ethene (induced dipole in
  Br₂, curly arrow from the C=C π bond to Brδ+, arrow from Br–Br bond to Brδ−, carbocation intermediate, Br⁻ lone pair to
  C⁺) and HBr/propene; 5 inductive effect of alkyl groups on primary, secondary, tertiary carbocation stability →
  Markovnikov addition (major product 2-bromopropane).
- 20.1 1 addition polymerisation: poly(ethene) and poly(chloroethene), PVC; 2 repeat unit from a monomer (draw the
  repeat unit in square brackets with extension bonds and n); 3 monomer from a polymer section; 4 disposal problems:
  non-biodegradable (chemically inert), harmful combustion products (e.g. HCl from PVC, CO from incomplete combustion).
Sections: alkanes (saturated, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, boiling point trend with chain length and branching, link id-id); combustion
equations (complete, incomplete giving CO and C); free-radical substitution as equations (COURSE.md: `\ce{Cl^.}`), with
products of further substitution; cracking (thermal vs catalytic: give only Al₂O₃ and heat as the syllabus states);
engine pollutants; alkenes: structure, π bond as the reactive electron-rich region, electrophiles; each addition
reaction with conditions and equations; the two mechanism figures (hand SVG `fig`, like the gold SN1 figure: two rows,
Br₂ + ethene; and HBr + propene showing the secondary carbocation and the major product, with a note that the primary
carbocation gives the minor product 1-bromopropane); Markovnikov with the inductive-effect explanation; KMnO₄
oxidations with a worked "locate the double bond" example (e.g. an alkene C₆H₁₂ gives propanone and propanoic acid →
2-methylpent-2-ene); polymers with repeat-unit figures (poly(propene): the CH₃ is a side group, not in the chain).
Worked examples (≥8): combustion volumes (link moles); propagation steps for Br₂ + methane; products of cracking
C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄; mechanism of Br₂ + ethene (marks); major/minor products of HBr + but-1-ene; products of hot
KMnO₄ with but-2-ene (2 × ethanoic acid); repeat unit of poly(chloroethene) and the monomer of a given polymer section.
Pitfalls: arrows from the H of HBr or from a C atom instead of the π bond; drawing the carbocation with the positive
charge on the wrong carbon; "alkenes are saturated"; polymer repeat units with the double bond left in; poly(propene)
drawn with the CH₃ in the main chain.

## chem.org.alcohols · Alcohols (`@time 6–8 hours`)
Topic 16 (AS). Phenol and alcohol + acyl chloride are A Level, taught in [Phenol and the acidity of organic compounds](#chem.org.phenol)
and [Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids](#chem.org.acyl): link forward. Warm-up from
[Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen) and [Hydrocarbons: alkanes and alkenes](#chem.org.hydrocarbons). Outcomes:
- 16.1 1 alcohols made by (a) steam + alkene, H₃PO₄ catalyst; (b) cold dilute acidified KMnO₄ on alkenes → diol;
  (c) NaOH(aq) + halogenoalkane, heat; (d) reduction of aldehydes or ketones by NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄; (e) reduction of
  carboxylic acids by LiAlH₄; (f) hydrolysis of esters with dilute acid or dilute alkali and heat;
  2 (a) combustion, (b) substitution to halogenoalkanes (HX(g); KCl + concentrated H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄; PCl₃ and heat; PCl₅;
  SOCl₂), (c) reaction with Na(s) (2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂; sodium ethoxide; less vigorous than with water),
  (d) oxidation with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄: (i) to carbonyl compounds by distillation, (ii) to carboxylic acids by
  refluxing; primary → aldehyde → acid, secondary → ketone, tertiary not oxidised; (e) dehydration to an alkene (heated
  Al₂O₃ or concentrated acid), (f) esterification with a carboxylic acid and concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst (ethanol);
  3 (a) classify primary, secondary and tertiary, including molecules with more than one OH group; (b) distinguishing
  reactions, e.g. acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ orange → green for 1° and 2°, no change for 3°; 4 CH₃CH(OH)– group by alkaline
  I₂(aq): yellow precipitate of tri-iodomethane (CHI₃) and RCO₂⁻ (ethanol and propan-2-ol give it; propan-1-ol does not);
  5 acidity of alcohols compared with water (alcohols are weaker acids: the alkyl group pushes electron density onto
  the O⁻ of the alkoxide, destabilising it; sodium reacts less vigorously).
Sections: structure, naming, classification (figure: skeletal butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol — a new
figure in the gold chapter's style, not a copy), hydrogen bonding and boiling points/solubility (link
[Shapes of molecules and intermolecular forces](#chem.shapes)); making alcohols (table of six routes); reactions in the
order of 16.1.2 with equations ([O] allowed); distillation vs reflux: figure of distillation apparatus for collecting
an aldehyde as it forms (flask, still head, thermometer at the side arm, sloping condenser with water in at the lower
end, receiver; COURSE.md apparatus conventions) and link the gold chapter's reflux figure; tests (dichromate, iodoform
reaction as a `:::lab`); acidity.
Worked examples (≥7, a real context: e.g. ethanol fuel from fermentation vs hydration of ethene, or breathalyser
dichromate): products of oxidising propan-1-ol under distillation and under reflux; which C₄H₉OH isomers give the
iodoform test (butan-2-ol only); dehydration products of butan-2-ol (three alkenes, link the gold chapter); esterification
equation for ethanol + ethanoic acid; percentage yield of an ester; distinguishing three isomeric alcohols by tests.
Pitfalls: "tertiary alcohols are oxidised slowly" (not at all, under these conditions); writing H₂ as a product of
oxidation; distillation vs reflux reversed; iodoform from any secondary alcohol (only those with CH₃CH(OH)–).
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
