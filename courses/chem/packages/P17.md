## chem.org.acyl · Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids (`@time 5–7 hours`)
Topics 32.1, 33.1.1–33.1.3, 33.2, 33.3 (the acidity outcomes 33.1.4–5 are in [Phenol and the acidity of organic compounds](#chem.org.phenol)).
Warm-up from [Carboxylic acids, esters and nitriles](#chem.org.acids), [Alcohols](#chem.org.alcohols) and
[Arenes: benzene and its compounds](#chem.org.arenes). Outcomes (checklist):
- 33.1 1 benzoic acid from an alkylbenzene (methylbenzene) with hot alkaline KMnO₄ then dilute acid; 2 carboxylic acids
  + PCl₃ and heat, PCl₅, or SOCl₂ → acyl chlorides (equations: CH₃COOH + PCl₅ → CH₃COCl + POCl₃ + HCl;
  3CH₃COOH + PCl₃ → 3CH₃COCl + H₃PO₃; CH₃COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃COCl + SO₂ + HCl); 3 further oxidation: (a) methanoic acid by
  Fehling's, Tollens', acidified KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇ → CO₂ + H₂O (it contains an aldehyde-like H–C=O), (b) ethanedioic acid by
  warm acidified KMnO₄ → CO₂ (link the titration in [Transition elements](#chem.transition)).
- 32.1 / 33.2 esters from alcohols + acyl chlorides (ethyl ethanoate: CH₃COCl + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + HCl) and from
  phenol + an acyl chloride (phenyl benzoate from benzoyl chloride; phenols do not esterify well with carboxylic acids).
- 33.3 1 acyl chlorides made as above; 2 reactions at room temperature: (a) water → carboxylic acid + HCl (steamy fumes),
  (b) alcohol → ester + HCl, (c) phenol → ester + HCl, (d) ammonia → primary amide + HCl (with excess NH₃: NH₄Cl),
  (e) primary or secondary amine → substituted amide + HCl; 3 addition–elimination mechanism (lone pair of the
  nucleophile to the δ+ carbonyl carbon, C=O π electrons to O; tetrahedral intermediate; C=O reforms, Cl⁻ leaves; H⁺ lost);
  4 relative ease of hydrolysis: acyl chloride (fast, cold water) > alkyl chloride (heat with NaOH(aq)) > aryl chloride
  (no reaction under these conditions), explained by the carbonyl carbon being very δ+ (two electronegative atoms, O and
  Cl) and by the partial double bond in chlorobenzene.
Sections: acyl chloride structure and naming (ethanoyl chloride, propanoyl chloride, benzoyl chloride); preparation;
reactions as a table and equations; the addition–elimination mechanism figure (hand SVG `fig`, two rows: ethanoyl
chloride + water → tetrahedral intermediate; intermediate → ethanoic acid + HCl); why acyl chlorides are better than
carboxylic acids for making esters and amides (fast, irreversible at room temperature, but HCl fumes and moisture
sensitivity); the hydrolysis ranking with a `:::lab` (AgNO₃(aq) with the three chlorides: acyl chloride immediate white
precipitate, chloroalkane slow on warming, chlorobenzene none); benzoic acid preparation; methanoic and ethanedioic acid
oxidations.
Worked examples (≥6): equations for ethanoyl chloride with each nucleophile; mechanism with marks; route from
methylbenzene to phenyl benzoate (needs phenol from another route: give phenol as a reagent) or ethyl benzoate; why
methanoic acid gives a silver mirror; explain the hydrolysis ranking.
Pitfalls: acyl chloride + water "needs heat"; amide formula with the wrong number of H on N; mechanism arrows from Cl;
forgetting HCl in equations.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.

## chem.org.nitrogen · Amines, amides and amino acids (`@time 7–9 hours`)
Topic 34 (and the AS amine route from 19.1 as revision). Warm-up from [Halogenoalkanes](#chem.org.halogen),
[Acids and bases](#chem.acids) and [Acyl chlorides and A Level carboxylic acids](#chem.org.acyl). Outcomes:
- 34.1 1 primary and secondary amines made by (a) halogenoalkane + NH₃ in ethanol under pressure, (b) halogenoalkane +
  primary amine in ethanol, heated in a sealed tube/under pressure, (c) reduction of amides with LiAlH₄, (d) reduction of
  nitriles with LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni; 2 ammonia or amine + acyl chloride at room temperature → amide (condensation);
  3 basicity of aqueous amines (lone pair on N accepts H⁺: RNH₂ + H₂O ⇌ RNH₃⁺ + OH⁻).
- 34.2 1 phenylamine: nitrate benzene → nitrobenzene, then reduce with hot Sn and concentrated HCl, then NaOH(aq) to
  release the free amine from its salt; 2 (a) phenylamine + Br₂(aq) at room temperature → 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine
  (white precipitate), (b) phenylamine + HNO₂ (NaNO₂ + dilute HCl) below 10 °C → benzenediazonium chloride; warming with
  water → phenol + N₂ + HCl; 3 relative basicities: ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine (alkyl group pushes electron
  density onto N, making the lone pair more available; in phenylamine the lone pair is delocalised into the ring);
  4 (a) coupling of benzenediazonium chloride with phenol in NaOH(aq) → an azo compound (4-hydroxyazobenzene, a yellow-
  orange dye), (b) the azo group –N=N–, (c) azo compounds are often used as dyes, (d) other azo dyes by similar routes
  (e.g. coupling with 2-naphthol).
- 34.3 1 amides from (a) ammonia + acyl chloride, (b) primary amine + acyl chloride, at room temperature;
  2 (a) hydrolysis with aqueous alkali (→ carboxylate salt + NH₃ or amine) or aqueous acid (→ carboxylic acid + NH₄⁺ or
  RNH₃⁺), (b) LiAlH₄ reduces the C=O of an amide → amine (CH₃CONH₂ → CH₃CH₂NH₂); 3 amides are much weaker bases than
  amines: the N lone pair is delocalised over the C=O group.
- 34.4 1 acid/base properties of amino acids, zwitterions (H₃N⁺–CHR–COO⁻), isoelectric point (the pH at which the
  amino acid has no overall charge); in acid → cation, in alkali → anion; 2 peptide (amide) bonds between amino acids:
  dipeptides and tripeptides (number of possible dipeptides from two different amino acids: 4, including the same pair
  twice); 3 electrophoresis of amino acids and dipeptides at different pHs (cations move to the negative electrode,
  anions to the positive; the larger the charge and the smaller the species, the faster; at the isoelectric point it
  does not move; apparatus assembly not tested).
Sections: amine structure, classification (primary, secondary, tertiary: classification is A Level here) and naming
(primary amines only need names); preparation routes (table); basicity with the ranking explanation (a `:::key` box);
salts with acids (C₂H₅NH₂ + HCl → C₂H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻); phenylamine preparation (a `:::lab` box with the reflux figure link) and
reactions; diazonium salts and azo dyes (figure: skeletal benzenediazonium ion and the azo product with the –N=N–
highlighted by a label); amides: structure, formation, hydrolysis, reduction, weak basicity; amino acids: general
structure, chirality (all except glycine are chiral: link [Isomerism](#chem.org.isomerism)), zwitterion figure, behaviour
in acid and alkali with equations, peptide bond figure (displayed formula of a dipeptide with the –CONH– link labelled),
hydrolysis of peptides; electrophoresis: embed `<div class="widget" data-w="chem-electrophoresis" data-ph="7"></div>`
and give a hand SVG of a strip with the two electrodes and three spots at one pH so the page works without it.
Worked examples (≥8): rank three bases with reasons; route benzene → phenylamine → an azo dye with conditions;
amide hydrolysis products under acid and alkali; structure of glycine at pH 1, at its isoelectric point and at pH 12;
draw/describe the two dipeptides of glycine and alanine; predict electrophoresis movement of three amino acids at pH 6
(given isoelectric points as data); an amine preparation from a nitrile by reduction.
Pitfalls: "phenylamine is a stronger base than ammonia"; diazotisation above 10 °C (the salt decomposes); the azo
coupling done in acid (it needs NaOH(aq)); zwitterion drawn as H₂N–CHR–COOH; amino acid charges at the wrong pH.
Board box: leave it out unless sure.
