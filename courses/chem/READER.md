# Chemistry: what each chapter teaches (written by the Reader, in course order)

## chem.overview
Taught: the three branches (physical, inorganic, organic) and the two cross-cutting skills; the five Cambridge 9701 papers, their marks, durations and weightings; AS (topics 1–22) versus A Level (23–37); the three assessment objectives AO1/AO2/AO3; the chapter map and study order; study rhythm and A* habits; the command words; the Data booklet.
Assumed: nothing. (It names many later terms, such as mechanisms, Kc and Nernst, only as labels in the map.)

## chem.f.basics
Taught: matter, element, compound, molecule, mixture and the physical separation methods; solid/liquid/gas and the changes of state; atom, nucleus, proton/neutron/electron with charges and masses; proton number (Z), nucleon number (A) and the notation A/Z X; shells 2, 8, 8; the Periodic Table (periods, groups 1–18, outer electrons, metals/non-metals, d-block); ions, cations/anions, ion charge from group; ionic bonding and the lattice; the polyatomic ions (hydroxide, nitrate, hydrogencarbonate, carbonate, sulfate, phosphate, ammonium, Zn2+, Ag+, Cu2+, H+); writing ionic formulas with brackets; covalent bond as a shared pair, valency (H 1, Cl 1, O 2, N 3, C 4), double bond, the seven diatomic elements; balancing equations by coefficients only; state symbols; acids, bases, alkalis, pH 0–14, salts, neutralisation; the three acid reactions; the H+ + OH- equation; tests for H2 and CO2; standard form, significant figures and the Cambridge rounding rule; unit conversions (cm3/dm3, kg/g, kJ/J, degC/K); a first look at n = m/M and molar mass; lab hazards and common apparatus. The mark codes [B1], [M1], [A1] appear but are not explained.
Assumed: what the mole is as a number (6.02 x 10^23 appears only inside the calculator tip); relative atomic mass; "precipitate"; the mark-scheme codes B1/M1/A1; Roman numerals such as iron(III), dichromate(VI) in the stretch questions.

## chem.atoms
Taught: Rutherford's gold-foil result and the nuclear atom; relative charge/mass table (electron 1/1836); proton and nucleon number (and the names atomic and mass number); counting protons, neutrons and electrons in atoms and ions (electrons = Z - charge); deflection of proton, neutron and electron beams in an electric field (charge/mass); isotopes, their same chemistry and different mass and density; shell (n), sub-shell (s, p, d), orbital, spin; orbital counts and capacities; s sphere and p dumbbell shapes; ground state; the order of filling 1s to 4p with 4s below 3d; the three filling rules (lowest first, paired opposite spins, singly before pairing) and the reason (repulsion); electrons-in-boxes diagrams; full and shorthand configurations; the Cr and Cu exceptions; configurations of ions (4s lost before 3d); isoelectronic; free radical (unpaired electron, Cl.); shielding; atomic radius trends; ionic radius, cation smaller and anion larger, isoelectronic ion sizes; first and second ionisation energy definitions and equations with state symbols; the four factors (nuclear charge, radius, shielding, spin-pair repulsion); trends down a group and across a period with the dips at B/Al and O/S; successive ionisation energies and finding the group from the big jump; the log plot for sodium; an answer pattern for ionisation energy.
Assumed: the mole and kJ mol-1 (used in the ionisation energy definition before the mole chapter); "endothermic"; "precipitate"; the mark codes.

## chem.moles
Taught: unified atomic mass unit; relative isotopic, atomic, molecular and formula mass (no units) and Ar from isotope abundances (both directions); molar mass versus Mr; the mole, the Avogadro constant L, n = m/M, N = nL, atoms versus molecules; ionic formulas from charges, Roman numerals, the recall ion list; balanced and ionic equations, spectator ions; empirical and molecular formulas from percentages, masses and combustion data; percentage by mass; water of crystallisation, hydrated/anhydrous, heating to constant mass; reacting masses and the mole map; theoretical and actual yield, percentage yield; molar gas volume 24.0 dm3 (room) and 22.4 dm3 (s.t.p.); volume ratio equals mole ratio; hydrocarbon combustion gas-volume problems; concentration (mol dm-3 and g dm-3); titration, end-point, indicator, titre, concordant; back titration; limiting and excess reagent; deducing stoichiometry from data; calculation technique.
Assumed: which ionic compounds are soluble or insoluble (needed to decide which product is a solid in ionic-equation questions); "strong acid" (used in the ionic equation steps); "dibasic acid"; the algebra of the boron abundance step.

## chem.bonding
Taught: why atoms bond (lower energy, electrostatic attraction); electronegativity, the Pauling scale, its trends and causes; predicting ionic, polar or non-polar covalent from the difference; polar bond and delta+/delta-; ionic bonding and dot-and-cross diagrams for NaCl, MgO, CaF2; metallic bonding and delocalised electrons; covalent bonding, single/double/triple bonds, lone pairs, the 6-step dot-and-cross method (H2, Cl2, HCl, O2, N2, H2O, NH3, CO2, CH4, C2H4, C2H6); expanded octets (PCl5, SF6, SO2) and the period restriction; coordinate (dative) bonds (NH4+, Al2Cl6, electron-deficient Al); odd-electron species (CH3, NO2); sigma and pi bonds and counting them; hybridisation sp3, sp2 and sp with 109.5, 120, 180 degrees and the region-counting rule; bond energy and bond length and their trends; using bond energy to compare reactivity (N2, F2, hydrogen halides); estimating the pi bond energy; a bond-energy enthalpy estimate.
Assumed: "nucleophile" and "electrophile" (used to explain N2 and alkene reactivity); halogenoalkanes, alkenes, hydrolysis (the chapter says "you met this" for C-Cl but they have not been taught yet); "thermal stability"; "exothermic/endothermic".

## chem.shapes
Taught: electron-pair repulsion (VSEPR); bonding and lone pairs, regions of electron density, a multiple bond as one region; the repulsion order; the pair-counting method (outer electrons + H/halogen - charge, O adds 0); shapes and angles: linear 180, trigonal planar 120, non-linear (SO2), tetrahedral 109.5, pyramidal 107, non-linear 104.5, trigonal bipyramidal 120/90, octahedral 90; lone pairs in unfamiliar species (see-saw, T-shaped, square pyramidal, square planar); wedge/dash drawing; shapes at carbon in organic molecules; dipole moment, polar versus non-polar molecules and cancelling dipoles; intermolecular forces versus covalent bonds; van der Waals' as the umbrella term; id-id forces (electrons, surface area); pd-pd forces; hydrogen bonding (N-H, O-H, lone pair, straight line) and how to draw it; boiling-point explanations; the hydride boiling-point anomaly; water's high boiling point, surface tension and less dense ice; what is and is not broken when a molecular substance boils.
Assumed: "R" in R-OH and the names alcohol, amine, carboxylic acid, ketone-type formulas (CH3COCH3); the dot product used in the optional 109.5 degree derivation.

## chem.states
Taught: the three states and the idea of energy versus attraction; gas pressure from collisions with the walls; temperature and kinetic energy; ideal gas (zero volume, no forces) and when real gases deviate (high pressure, low temperature); pV = nRT with SI units and the unit-conversion table; molar volume check; Mr = mRT/pV and the density form; p/T and pV/T ratio shortcuts; Mr of a volatile liquid by gas syringe; gas volume from a reaction; the four structure types (giant ionic, simple molecular, giant molecular, giant metallic) with NaCl, MgO, I2, C60, ice, diamond, graphite, SiO2, Cu; structure explaining melting point, conductivity and solubility; deducing structure from data; answer pattern for "explain the property".
Assumed: "lattice energy" (forward link); nothing else.

## chem.energetics
Taught: system and surroundings; enthalpy change ΔH and its sign; exothermic/endothermic (temperature of surroundings, products lower/higher); reaction pathway diagrams, activation energy Ea and transition state, a catalyst lowers Ea but not ΔH; standard conditions (101 kPa, 298 K, 1.00 mol dm-3), standard states and the standard sign; the definitions and equations of the standard enthalpy changes of reaction, formation, combustion and neutralisation (about -57 kJ mol-1 for strong acid with strong alkali) and the four traps; q = mcΔT and ΔH = -mcΔT/n in kJ mol-1; calorimetry in a polystyrene cup and with a spirit burner, temperature-time graph with the cooling line extrapolated back, sources of heat loss; bond breaking endothermic and making exothermic, bond energy, exact versus average bond energies, ΔH = Σ(broken) - Σ(formed), finding an unknown bond energy; Hess's law, energy cycles from formation data (products minus reactants), from combustion data (reactants minus products) and from experimental data, finding ΔHf of CO; energy per gram of fuels.
Assumed: the symbol Σ ("the sum of"); what the enthalpy H itself is (heat content); "Data section" for the bond energy table; the Haber process by name; "oxidised" for iron (used in the everyday sense).

## chem.redox
Taught: oxidation and reduction as loss/gain of electrons (OIL RIG) and as change in oxidation number; redox reaction; half-equations; oxidation number definition and the seven rules; written +2 (sign first) versus charge 2+; oxidation numbers in organic carbon (CH4 to CO2); Roman numerals in names (iron(III), manganate(VII), chlorate(I)/(V), nitrate(III)/(V)); deciding whether a reaction is redox; combustion, rusting, displacement and the blast furnace as redox; oxidising and reducing agents and the table of common ones; hydrogen peroxide as both; the five-step method for half-equations in acid and combining them by equalising electrons; balancing by total increase = total decrease; disproportionation (Cl2 with cold/hot NaOH, H2O2, Cu+); redox titrations with acidified manganate(VII) (Fe2+, ethanedioate, H2O2), why sulfuric not hydrochloric acid, the end-point colour, iodine-thiosulfate (stretch).
Assumed: how to find carbon's oxidation number in HCHO and HCOOH from their structures (the table gives answers, no working); "acidified" and "warmed" used without comment; "the practical course" (which practicals).

## chem.equilibria
Taught: reversible reaction and the double arrow; dynamic equilibrium, closed system, position of equilibrium left/right; the two wrong ideas (stopped, equal); Le Chatelier's principle for concentration, pressure (and inert gas), temperature and catalyst; the three-part exam answer; Kc expression, heterogeneous equilibria (solids left out), units of Kc as (mol dm-3)^Δn, what the size of K means; Kc from equilibrium amounts and with ICE tables; equilibrium amount from Kc by square root (no quadratics); reversing the equation inverts K; mole fraction and partial pressure; Kp and its units; only temperature changes K (exothermic forward: K falls as T rises); a catalyst does not change K; conditions of the Haber and Contact processes and recycling.
Assumed: the pressure unit atm (and its size); that the endothermic direction has the larger activation energy (it points to kinetics for the reason, which that chapter does not give); "phase".

## chem.acids
Taught: formulas and strengths of the common acids and alkalis, monoprotic/diprotic, alkali versus base; the pH definition, pH 7 neutral, lower pH means ten times more H+ per step, universal indicator and pH meter; Brønsted-Lowry acid (proton donor) and base (acceptor), oxonium ion, amphoteric water, NH3 as a base; strong and weak acids and bases (full/partial dissociation, arrows), strong is not concentrated; comparing strong and weak acids (pH, conductivity, rate with Mg, same final volume of H2); salts and the four ways to make them, ammonia forming only a salt; pH titration curves for the four combinations, equivalence point versus end point, steep section, pH at equivalence above/below/at 7 and why; indicators and their ranges, choosing one; titration calculations including diprotic acids and dilution.
Assumed: "lg" (base-10 logarithm) in the pH definition; Lewis acid (mentioned once in a quiz explanation); the ethanoate and ammonium ions acting as bases/acids is stated, not derived.

## chem.kinetics
Taught: rate of reaction and units; initial rate; ways to measure rates (gas syringe, mass loss, colorimeter, titration of samples, clock/cross experiment with rate proportional to 1/time); average rate and rate at a point from the gradient of a tangent; effective collision, orientation, collision theory; activation energy as the minimum energy for an effective collision; effect of concentration, pressure and surface area; Boltzmann distribution and its features; why temperature has a large effect (proportion with E at least Ea, not collision frequency); catalysts (alternative route, lower Ea, same ΔH and equilibrium); heterogeneous and homogeneous catalyst as labels; the summary table of factors.
Assumed: nothing. (It does not explain why the endothermic direction of a reversible reaction is sped up more by a rise in temperature, which chem.equilibria says it does.)

## chem.periodicity
Taught: periodicity; the structures of Na to Ar; atomic and ionic radius trends (positive ions smaller, negative larger, jump between); melting point trend explained by structure (metallic charge, electrons and size; giant covalent Si; id-id forces by electron count); conductivity including the semiconductor; ionisation energy and electronegativity across the period with the electronegativity-difference tables; reactions of the elements with oxygen, chlorine and water; oxidation numbers from outer electrons; oxide structure, bonding, reaction with water and pH, basic/amphoteric/acidic oxides and their equations; the hydroxides of Na, Mg, Al; the chlorides (ionic versus hydrolysed, pH values); the explanation from electronegativity; deducing bonding and identity from properties and from successive ionisation energies; predicting Ge and Se by group; moles problems on the Period 3 reactions.
Assumed: "sublimes" and "volatile"; the hydrated ion [Al(H2O)6]3+ and the complex ion [Al(OH)4]- (what a complex ion is, how water binds to Al3+); log and Kw in the two A Level link examples (marked optional).

## chem.group2
Taught: Group 2 (Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) outer electrons and +2 ions, why +2 from successive ionisation energies, reactivity increasing down the group; reactions with oxygen, water (Mg slow, steam) and dilute acids, including the sulfate coating; oxides, hydroxides and carbonates as bases and their reactions; thermal decomposition of carbonates and nitrates, stability increasing down the group, calculations; hydroxide solubility rising and sulfate solubility falling; uses (antacid, soil, barium meal), the sulfate test; predicting radium; (A Level) polarisation, charge density, polarising power, thermal stability explanation; ΔHsol, ΔHhyd, lattice energy cycle and the solubility explanation.
Assumed: "qualitative analysis notes" (the NaOH test results for cations, with a forward link to chem.practical); lattice energy and entropy (forward links, A Level section only; lattice energy is explained briefly in place).

## chem.group17
Taught: halogen, halide ion (halogen versus halide), the colours and states of Cl2, Br2, I2 and their vapours, sublime, volatility and its id-id explanation, X-X bond energies (and F-F as an anomaly); halogens as oxidising agents, displacement reactions with colours (also in cyclohexane, starch test for I2) and the order Cl2 > Br2 > I2 explained by size and shielding; reaction with hydrogen, bond-energy dH of H2 + X2 and the thermal stability of HX; halide ions as reducing agents I- > Br- > Cl-; solid halide + concentrated H2SO4 (steamy HX; Br- gives Br2 and SO2; I- gives I2, SO2, S, H2S; sulfur oxidation numbers); why H3PO4 is used for pure HBr/HI; the silver nitrate then ammonia test for Cl-, Br-, I- (white/cream/pale yellow; soluble/partly/insoluble) with nitric acid first; disproportionation of Cl2 with cold and hot NaOH (chlorate(I) bleach, chlorate(V)), naming ClO- and ClO3-; Cl2 in water (HCl + HOCl, HOCl as a weak acid, water purification, Le Chatelier on the equilibrium); mass-of-chlorine and mass-of-AgCl calculations; iodine-thiosulfate titration of bleach (stretch).
Assumed: "complex ion" ([Ag(NH3)2]+, mentioned as optional); "standard electrode potential" and volts (the note box, forward link); what the chem-trend widget shows (not explained in the text).

## chem.nitrogen
Taught: why N2 is unreactive (N≡N 944 kJ mol-1, non-polar) and the bond-energy values N-N, N=N; ammonia (pyramidal, lone pair, weak base, reactions with acids to ammonium salts); the ammonium ion, its tetrahedral shape and the dative bond; displacing NH3 from an ammonium salt with alkali and the damp red litmus test; Haber process ΔH from bond energies; fertilisers, percentage of nitrogen by mass, eutrophication; NOx (NO, NO2) from lightning and hot engines; the three engine pollutants; catalytic converters (Pt, Pd, Rh; equations; heterogeneous catalyst; adsorbed); photochemical smog, PAN, primary and secondary pollutants; acid rain (SO2 to SO3 to H2SO4, NO2 to HNO3/HNO2, NO2 as a homogeneous catalyst for SO2 oxidation); the Contact process in brief (V2O5, oleum); mass of sulfuric acid from sulfur.
Assumed: the pressure unit atm (200 atm, 1-2 atm); "nucleophile" and "electrophile" (the stretch question, forward link to chem.org.halogen).

## chem.org.intro
Taught: why carbon forms chains; straight-chained, branched, cyclic; hydrocarbon, alkane, saturated/unsaturated, homologous series, functional group; stems meth- to hex-; molecular, empirical, general, structural, displayed and skeletal formulas and how to read a skeletal formula; the nine AS functional groups with suffixes and general formulas; carbonyl, hydroxyl, carboxyl; primary/secondary/tertiary alcohols and halogenoalkanes; the naming rules (longest chain, lowest number, alphabetical prefixes, di-/tri-, esters as alkyl alkanoate, nitrile carbon in the chain); R, R' and X; homolytic and heterolytic fission, free radicals, initiation/propagation/termination in outline; curly arrows (start at a bond or lone pair); nucleophile and electrophile; the reaction types addition, substitution, elimination, hydrolysis, condensation, oxidation, reduction with [O] and [H]; the four mechanism names; shape and bond angle at sp3, sp2, sp carbon, counting sigma and pi bonds, why ethene is planar.
Assumed: how amines are named (the table gives "propylamine" but the naming steps list -amine as an ending); how a reaction that fits two labels (hydrolysis and substitution) is classified; "-enal" and "-enenitrile" name patterns (but-2-enal, but-3-enenitrile) used in examples.

## chem.org.isomerism
Taught: isomers, structural versus stereoisomers; chain, positional and functional-group isomers and the systematic method for listing them (C5H12, C3H8O, C4H8, C4H9Br); cis-trans isomerism from restricted rotation (two conditions, in rings with wedge/hash, E/Z noted); chiral centre, enantiomers, non-superimposable mirror images, finding chiral centres including in rings, 2^n stereoisomers; deducing isomers from a formula using the H count; (A Level) plane-polarised light, optical activity, racemic mixtures, same properties apart from rotation and biological activity, why planar intermediates give racemic products, chirality in drug synthesis and chiral catalysts.
Assumed: how ethers are named (methoxymethane, methoxyethane appear with no rule); "racemic mixture", "plane-polarised light" and "optically inactive" in the three A Level exam-practice questions placed before the A Level section that defines them.

## chem.org.hydrocarbons
Taught: alkane boiling points (chain length, branching) and why alkanes are unreactive; making alkanes (hydrogenation, cracking); complete and incomplete combustion, gas-volume and mass calculations; free-radical substitution with initiation, propagation, termination and why a mixture forms; propagation-step ΔH from bond energies; cracking (Al2O3, heat); engine pollutants and the converter; alkenes, sigma and pi bonds in ethene, electrophile and electrophilic addition; making alkenes; addition of H2, steam, HX, X2, cold KMnO4 with conditions; the bromine water test; the mechanisms for Br2 + ethene and HBr + propene (induced dipole, carbocation); the inductive effect and carbocation stability, major and minor product; hot concentrated KMnO4 splitting C=C and finding the double bond; addition polymerisation, repeat unit, monomer from polymer; disposal problems and burning PVC.
Assumed: what "slow" and "fast" mean for the two mechanism steps (rate-determining step is not taught before this); the widget chem-orgmap (one sentence only).

## chem.org.halogen
Taught: halogenoalkane naming and primary/secondary/tertiary classification, polar C-X bond; making them from alkanes, alkenes and alcohols (HX, KCl with acid, PCl3, PCl5, SOCl2); the substitution reactions with NaOH(aq), KCN in ethanol, NH3 in ethanol, AgNO3 in ethanol; reflux apparatus; aqueous versus ethanolic NaOH; the SN2 mechanism (one step, transition state, curly arrows, back-side attack); SN1 (carbocation, two steps); why tertiary prefers SN1; elimination with NaOH in ethanol and naming all alkenes; rates of hydrolysis I > Br > Cl by C-X bond energy and the silver nitrate precipitate test; synthesis routes; percentage yield.
Assumed: "rate-determining step" (used to explain the 2 in SN2 and in a quiz question); "optically active" and "racemic" in the SN1 stretch question (defined only in the A Level section of chem.org.isomerism); the ether name methoxyethane.

## chem.org.alcohols
Taught: alcohol definition and naming (including diols and triols); classifying every OH group; hydrogen bonding, boiling points and solubility of alcohols; six routes to alcohols (hydration, cold KMnO4, NaOH(aq), NaBH4/LiAlH4 reduction, ester hydrolysis); fermentation versus hydration; combustion; alcohol to halogenoalkane reagents; reaction with sodium; oxidation with acidified K2Cr2O7/KMnO4 (1° to aldehyde then acid, 2° to ketone, 3° none), distillation versus reflux, balanced ionic equations; dehydration and naming the alkenes; esterification and yield; the dichromate test and the tri-iodomethane test with which alcohols pass; why alcohols are weaker acids than water; routes using several steps.
Assumed: how to write the organic half-equation (the examples give it ready-made); the reduction of aldehydes/ketones and Tollens'/Fehling's tests (forward links to chem.org.carbonyl); phenol (forward link).
