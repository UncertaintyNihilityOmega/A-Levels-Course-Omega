# READER: what each chapter taught

## phys.overview
Taught: the five 9702 papers (times, marks, what each tests), AS and A Level routes, AO1/AO2/AO3, the course map (25 topics, chapter ids), the sample study plan, how Cambridge compares with AQA, the habits for an A* (definitions, derivations, units and s.f., past papers, data sheet, practical work, command words).
Assumed: what SI units are (only named); what the "study guide" (#road.technique, #road.commands) is; terms used only as labels in the map tables (p.d., e.m.f., r.m.s., uncertainty).

## phys.f.basics
Taught: standard form and the calculator EXP key, significant figures (rules for answers), SI base units and prefixes (G to p), squared and cubed units, converting to base units first, rearranging formulae (reverse-order method, unit check), axes labelled quantity / unit, y = mx + c, gradient with unit, intercept, direct and inverse proportion (and plotting y against 1/x), area under a speed-time graph (distance) and gradient (acceleration), hypotenuse/opposite/adjacent, Pythagoras, SOH CAH TOA, inverse trig, components of a force, circle/sphere/cylinder/block areas and volumes, radius against diameter; school definitions: speed, velocity, scalar, vector, acceleration, force, weight (W = mg), mass, resultant force, F = ma, energy, E_k, mgΔh, work = Fs, power, efficiency, density, pressure, current, conventional current, p.d., resistance, ammeter/voltmeter placement, P = VI, wavelength, frequency, period, amplitude, v = fλ, transverse/longitudinal, the EM spectrum order, atom structure, nucleon/proton number, isotopes, alpha/beta/gamma, half-life, decay equations.
Assumed: the meaning of the symbol Δ ("change in"); the area of a trapezium; why the angle between a weight and the normal to a slope equals the slope angle; bequerel (Bq) and "activity"; "ionising"; "in phase".

## phys.units
Taught: physical quantity = number x unit, table and axis headings as quantity / unit, SI base quantities and units (kg, m, s, A, K; mol and cd named), derived units found from a defining equation (N, Pa, J, W, C, V, ohm, Hz), writing units with negative powers, homogeneous equations (and what the test cannot prove), finding an unknown power or constant by units, prefixes p to T (squared and cubed with the unit), estimates and typical values, random and systematic error, zero error, precision and accuracy, uncertainty (absolute, fractional, percentage), half the range, one reading on analogue and digital instruments, adding uncertainties for sums, products, quotients and powers, writing a final answer (1 s.f. uncertainty, same decimal places), scalars and vectors, adding vectors tip to tail, cosine rule, subtracting vectors (change in velocity), components F cos and F sin, the micrometer lab.
Assumed: the sine rule (used, never stated); bearings ("bearing 010 degrees"); torque (named in the homogeneity box); coplanar; tension, g and the functions sin, ln, e^x (used in homogeneity examples); the Young modulus (in one question, formula given).

## phys.kinematics
Taught: distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, average and instantaneous values, sign convention and "deceleration", s-t, v-t and a-t graphs (gradient and area, tangent), the four constant-acceleration equations with a derivation, the solving recipe (positive direction, list s u v a t), thinking, braking and stopping distance, acceleration of free fall g, rejecting a root, the falling-ball experiment for g (h against t squared; t against root h for a release delay), projectiles (horizontal, angled), range and greatest height, the top of the path.
Assumed: the quadratic formula (used in the first free-fall example, from the maths course); the area of a trapezium (proof of s = (u+v)t/2); "suvat" is not used here; relative motion (stretch question, hints explain); light gate (named in the picket-fence alternative).

## phys.dynamics
Taught: mass (resists changes of motion), weight W = mg, g as field strength and as free-fall acceleration, Newton's three laws and third-law pairs, momentum p = mv, force as rate of change of momentum, F = ma as the constant-mass case, impulse and the area under a force-time graph, crumple zones, rebounds (momenta add), F = (dm/dt) v for a flow of mass, free-body diagram recipe, connected bodies, lifts and apparent weight, slopes (mg sin and mg cos), friction and drag, terminal velocity and the skydiver v-t graph, conservation of momentum (1-D and 2-D), elastic and inelastic collisions, relative speed of approach = separation, explosions and recoil.
Assumed: "suvat" (Before you start); what "normal" contact force means (used from the free-body figure, explained only in phys.forces); upthrust (used in the terminal-velocity paragraph); "light" string and "smooth" pulley or surface; inertia (quiz option); the energy formulae and P = Fv (hints in two stretch questions).

## phys.forces
Taught: centre of gravity and its lab, stability and toppling, moment of a force (perpendicular distance), couple and torque of a couple, principle of moments, the two conditions for equilibrium, choosing the pivot, beam on two supports, hinged beam with a cable, three forces in equilibrium (closed triangle), density, pressure (normal force per area), hydrostatic pressure (derived), total pressure, manometer, upthrust (derived) and Archimedes' principle, floating and fraction submerged.
Assumed: the sine rule (stated only in a hint); "smooth" meaning no friction (explained inside one example); "rough" ground; mercury density (given in the question).

## phys.energy
Taught: stores of energy, work W = Fs cos theta, the joule, work as area under a force-displacement graph, conservation of energy, dissipation, GPE (derived), kinetic energy (derived), E_K = p squared over 2m, the energy-problem recipe, v = root(2gh), energy graphs, efficiency, Sankey diagram, motor-efficiency lab, power, P = Fv (derived), top speed and power, kW h, the common mistakes.
Assumed: "closed system" (used in the first section, never defined); the quadratic formula (bungee stretch question); the elastic energy (1/2)kx squared (given in the bungee question, taught next chapter); circular orbit (satellite example); the rate mgv (in the energy-graph paragraph, before P = Fv).

## phys.solids
Taught: tension, compression, load, extension, Hooke's law, limit of proportionality, spring constant, springs in series and parallel, stress, strain, Young modulus, stress-strain graphs, the long thin wire experiment, elastic and plastic deformation, elastic limit, unloading line parallel to OP, energy dissipated past the elastic limit, brittle, ductile and polymeric materials, elastic potential energy (1/2)Fx = (1/2)kx squared, estimating the area under a curve by trapezia.
Assumed: resistors in series and parallel (a comparison in the springs section); Searle's apparatus (named, not explained); "light" spring (one exam question); the quadratic formula (two exam questions).
