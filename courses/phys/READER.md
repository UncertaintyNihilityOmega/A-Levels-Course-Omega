# READER: what each chapter taught

## phys.overview
Taught: the five 9702 papers (times, marks, what each tests), AS and A Level routes, AO1/AO2/AO3, the course map (25 topics, chapter ids), the sample study plan, how Cambridge compares with AQA, the habits for an A* (definitions, derivations, units and s.f., past papers, data sheet, practical work, command words).
Assumed: what SI units are (only named); what the "study guide" (#road.technique, #road.commands) is; terms used only as labels in the map tables (p.d., e.m.f., r.m.s., uncertainty).

## phys.f.basics
Taught: standard form and the calculator EXP key, significant figures (rules for answers), SI base units and prefixes (G to p), squared and cubed units, converting to base units first, rearranging formulae (reverse-order method, unit check), axes labelled quantity / unit, y = mx + c, gradient with unit, intercept, direct and inverse proportion (and plotting y against 1/x), area under a speed-time graph (distance) and gradient (acceleration), hypotenuse/opposite/adjacent, Pythagoras, SOH CAH TOA, inverse trig, components of a force, circle/sphere/cylinder/block areas and volumes, radius against diameter; school definitions: speed, velocity, scalar, vector, acceleration, force, weight (W = mg), mass, resultant force, F = ma, energy, E_k, mgΔh, work = Fs, power, efficiency, density, pressure, current, conventional current, p.d., resistance, ammeter/voltmeter placement, P = VI, wavelength, frequency, period, amplitude, v = fλ, transverse/longitudinal, the EM spectrum order, atom structure, nucleon/proton number, isotopes, alpha/beta/gamma, half-life, decay equations.
Assumed: the meaning of the symbol Δ ("change in"); the area of a trapezium; why the angle between a weight and the normal to a slope equals the slope angle; bequerel (Bq) and "activity"; "ionising"; "in phase".

## phys.units
Taught: physical quantity = number x unit, table and axis headings as quantity / unit, SI base quantities and units (kg, m, s, A, K; mol and cd named), derived units found from a defining equation (N, Pa, J, W, C, V, ohm, Hz), writing units with negative powers, homogeneous equations (and what the test cannot prove), finding an unknown power or constant by units, prefixes p to T (squared and cubed with the unit), estimates and typical values, random and systematic error, zero error, precision and accuracy, uncertainty (absolute, fractional, percentage), half the range, one reading on analogue and digital instruments, adding uncertainties for sums, products, quotients and powers, writing a final answer (1 s.f. uncertainty, same decimal places), scalars and vectors, adding vectors tip to tail, cosine rule, subtracting vectors (change in velocity), components F cos and F sin, the micrometer lab.
Assumed: the sine rule (used, never stated); bearings ("bearing 010 degrees"); torque (named in the homogeneity box); coplanar; tension, g and the functions sin, ln, e^x (used in homogeneity examples); the Young modulus (in one question, formula given).

## phys.kinematics
Taught: distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, average and instantaneous values, sign convention and "deceleration", s-t, v-t and a-t graphs (gradient and area, tangent), the four constant-acceleration equations with a derivation, the solving recipe (positive direction, list s u v a t), thinking, braking and stopping distance, acceleration of free fall g, rejecting a root, the falling-ball experiment for g (h against t squared; t against root h for a release delay), projectiles (horizontal, angled), range and greatest height, the top of the path.
Assumed: the quadratic formula (used in the first free-fall example, from the maths course); the area of a trapezium (proof of s = (u+v)t/2); "suvat" is not used here; relative motion (stretch question, hints explain); light gate (named in the picket-fence alternative).

## phys.dynamics
Taught: mass (resists changes of motion), weight W = mg, g as field strength and as free-fall acceleration, Newton's three laws and third-law pairs, momentum p = mv, force as rate of change of momentum, F = ma as the constant-mass case, impulse and the area under a force-time graph, crumple zones, rebounds (momenta add), F = (dm/dt) v for a flow of mass, free-body diagram recipe, connected bodies, lifts and apparent weight, slopes (mg sin and mg cos), friction and drag, terminal velocity and the skydiver v-t graph, conservation of momentum (1-D and 2-D), elastic and inelastic collisions, relative speed of approach = separation, explosions and recoil.
Assumed: "suvat" (Before you start); what "normal" contact force means (used from the free-body figure, explained only in phys.forces); upthrust (used in the terminal-velocity paragraph); "light" string and "smooth" pulley or surface; inertia (quiz option); the energy formulae and P = Fv (hints in two stretch questions).

## phys.forces
Taught: centre of gravity and its lab, stability and toppling, moment of a force (perpendicular distance), couple and torque of a couple, principle of moments, the two conditions for equilibrium, choosing the pivot, beam on two supports, hinged beam with a cable, three forces in equilibrium (closed triangle), density, pressure (normal force per area), hydrostatic pressure (derived), total pressure, manometer, upthrust (derived) and Archimedes' principle, floating and fraction submerged.
Assumed: the sine rule (stated only in a hint); "smooth" meaning no friction (explained inside one example); "rough" ground; mercury density (given in the question).

## phys.energy
Taught: stores of energy, work W = Fs cos theta, the joule, work as area under a force-displacement graph, conservation of energy, dissipation, GPE (derived), kinetic energy (derived), E_K = p squared over 2m, the energy-problem recipe, v = root(2gh), energy graphs, efficiency, Sankey diagram, motor-efficiency lab, power, P = Fv (derived), top speed and power, kW h, the common mistakes.
Assumed: "closed system" (used in the first section, never defined); the quadratic formula (bungee stretch question); the elastic energy (1/2)kx squared (given in the bungee question, taught next chapter); circular orbit (satellite example); the rate mgv (in the energy-graph paragraph, before P = Fv).

## phys.solids
Taught: tension, compression, load, extension, Hooke's law, limit of proportionality, spring constant, springs in series and parallel, stress, strain, Young modulus, stress-strain graphs, the long thin wire experiment, elastic and plastic deformation, elastic limit, unloading line parallel to OP, energy dissipated past the elastic limit, brittle, ductile and polymeric materials, elastic potential energy (1/2)Fx = (1/2)kx squared, estimating the area under a curve by trapezia.
Assumed: resistors in series and parallel (a comparison in the springs section); Searle's apparatus (named, not explained); "light" spring (one exam question); the quadratic formula (two exam questions).

## phys.waves
Taught: progressive wave (energy moves, matter does not), source, medium, wavefronts and rays, ripple tank, displacement, amplitude (not peak-to-peak), wavelength, period, frequency, wave speed, phase difference (x/lambda x 360 degrees or 2 pi x/lambda, in phase and antiphase), displacement-distance against displacement-time graphs, which way a particle moves on a snapshot, v = f lambda (derived), source fixes f and medium fixes v, c.r.o. (time-base, y-gain, period and amplitude from the trace), intensity I = P/A, I proportional to amplitude squared, point-source inverse square law, transverse and longitudinal waves (compressions, rarefactions, the displacement graph of a longitudinal wave), the Doppler effect for a moving source of sound (formula and derivation), the EM spectrum (regions, wavelengths, same speed c in free space), polarisation (plane polarised, polarising filter, transmission axis), Malus's law and series of filters.
Assumed: "in phase" in the wavelength definition (explained a section later); "crossed" filters (LCD bullet, explained only in a figure caption); why the energy of an oscillation goes as amplitude squared (stated); electric and magnetic fields (named in the EM paragraph).

## phys.superposition
Taught: principle of superposition, constructive and destructive superposition, path difference (n lambda, (n + 1/2) lambda), stationary wave, node, antinode, graphical method, stationary against progressive wave table, resonant frequencies, string fixed at both ends (L = n lambda/2), string experiment, microwave stationary wave (nodes lambda/2 apart), pipes closed at one end and open at both ends, resonance-tube method for the speed of sound (difference of lengths removes the end correction), diffraction at a gap (gap against wavelength), interference, coherence and the conditions for fringes, interference of water, sound, microwaves and light, Young's double slit (lambda = ax/D, derivation, white-light fringes, laser lab), the diffraction grating (d sin theta = n lambda, d = 1/N, highest order, number of maxima, overlap of spectra, lab).
Assumed: "harmonic" and "first harmonic" (the formula box says only "harmonic number"); "end correction" (used before the lab explains it); "octave"; that the water surface in the resonance tube is a closed end; why the plate is a node for microwaves (electric field in a conductor); "monochromatic" and "falls normally" (grating example); the angle in the small triangle of the double-slit proof (not drawn).

## phys.electricity
Taught: free electrons, ions, charge carriers, current I = dQ/dt, conventional current, Q = It, the coulomb, area under an I-t graph, elementary charge and quantisation (Q = ne), drift velocity and I = Anvq (derivation), number density, p.d. V = W/Q, the volt, electron through a p.d., P = VI = I squared R = V squared/R, kW h, resistance R = V/I and the ohm, Ohm's law, ohmic and non-ohmic, I-V characteristics of a wire, filament lamp and diode (with the temperature explanation), the I-V lab, resistivity (R = rho L/A, rho = RA/L), resistivity lab with a graph of R against L, LDR and NTC thermistor.
Assumed: "non-relativistic" (electron example); "rectification of alternating current" (diode paragraph); "potential divider" (I-V lab intro; taught in D.C. circuits); the unit lux (LDR graph); semiconductor (used in the Tip box with only the carrier-density explanation); e.m.f. (pointed to D.C. circuits).

## phys.particles
Taught: the alpha-scattering experiment (apparatus, three observations and conclusions), nuclear atom, nucleon, proton number Z, nucleon number A, nuclide notation, isotopes, size of atom and nucleus, alpha, beta minus, beta plus and gamma (nature, charge, penetration, deflection), antiparticle, positron, electron neutrino and antineutrino, energy spectra of alpha and beta particles, decay equations (conservation of nucleon number and charge), gamma emission from an excited nucleus, the unified atomic mass unit, quarks (six flavours, charges, antiquarks), hadron, baryon, meson, lepton, proton uud and neutron udd, quark changes in beta decay.
Assumed: "ionising" (table); "annihilates" and "photon" (table); "daughter" and "parent" nucleus; charge-to-mass ratio of the electron (the text says it is found in a later example, but it is not); the electronvolt MeV (stretch question); mass-energy (why a free proton does not decay).

## phys.circular
Taught: radian (definition, 2 pi rad = 360 degrees), s = r theta, angular displacement, angular speed omega = 2 pi/T = 2 pi f, v = r omega (derived), converting rpm, why circular motion at constant speed means acceleration, centripetal acceleration a = v squared/r = r omega squared (derived), centripetal force as the resultant of real forces, no centrifugal force, flat bend, string on a smooth table, the whirling-bung lab (T squared against r), conical pendulum, vertical circle (tensions at top and bottom, minimum speed), hump-back bridge, banked track (stretch), rotor.
Assumed: "inertia" (centrifugal warning); magnetic flux density B, the force Bqv and the milli prefix in context (the charged-particle paragraph, taught in Magnetic fields).

## phys.gravity
Taught: field (a region where a force acts), gravitational field strength g = F/m, field lines (radial and uniform), Newton's law of gravitation (r from the centre, sphere as point mass, inverse square), g = GM/r squared (derived), free fall in orbit (weightlessness), g nearly constant near the surface, circular orbit (v = root of GM/r), Kepler's third law T squared = 4 pi squared r cubed/GM (derived), mass of a planet from a moon, geostationary orbit (four conditions and the radius), gravitational potential phi = -GM/r, equipotentials, delta E_P = m delta phi, E_P = -GMm/r, energies of a satellite (E_K = GMm/2r, total = -GMm/2r), escape speed (beyond the syllabus).
Assumed: the area under a 1/x squared graph and d(phi)/dr = +GM/r squared (stated without working); the energy and graph read-outs of the orbit widget (energies are taught after the widget).

## phys.temperature
Taught: temperature against thermal energy, direction of energy transfer, thermal equilibrium, thermometric properties (liquid length, gas volume, metal resistance, thermocouple, thermistor), fixed points and why scales differ between fixed points, thermodynamic scale, absolute zero, K and degrees Celsius (a temperature change is the same number), specific heat capacity E = mc delta theta, the electrical lab (gradient P/mc, c too large), method of mixtures, specific latent heat of fusion and vaporisation (E = mL), why temperature is constant during a change of state, heating curve, the funnel lab for ice, the two-power method for L_v (derived).
Assumed: "internal energy" (absolute zero definition); "thermal capacity" (exam box); "joulemeter" and "immersion heater"; e.m.f. of a thermocouple (not explained).

## phys.gases
Taught: the mole, the Avogadro constant, molar mass, the four symbols n, N, M, m, Boyle's law, Charles's law, the pressure law, isotherm, extrapolating to absolute zero, the ideal gas (pV = nRT and pV = NkT, k = R/N_A), p1V1/T1 = p2V2/T2, absolute against gauge pressure, changing amount of gas, gas density pM/RT, kinetic-theory assumptions, how molecules make pressure, pV = (1/3) N m mean-square c (derived), mean-square and r.m.s. speed, mean translational kinetic energy (3/2) kT (derived), c_rms = root(3kT/m).
Assumed: nRT in the Boyle lab (before the equation of state); "absolute pressure" (tyre example, before the warning box); "monatomic", "translational" and "internal energy" (kinetic-theory paragraphs); the heading "Changes of state of a fixed mass" (the term meant melting and boiling in Temperature).
