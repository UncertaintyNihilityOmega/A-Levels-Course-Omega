## Exam structure (9702)
| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Content | AS weight | A Level weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multiple Choice | 1 h 15 min | 40 | 40 four-option questions on AS content | 31% | 15.5% |
| 2 | AS Level Structured Questions | 1 h 15 min | 60 | structured questions on AS content | 46% | 23% |
| 3 | Advanced Practical Skills | 2 h | 40 | lab practical: two questions, each 1 hour and 20 marks | 23% | 11.5% |
| 4 | A Level Structured Questions | 2 h | 100 | A Level content; AS knowledge required | – | 38.5% |
| 5 | Planning, Analysis and Evaluation | 1 h 15 min | 30 | written; two compulsory 15-mark questions | – | 11.5% |

- Topics 1–11 are AS; topics 12–25 are A Level only. AS content is assumed knowledge for Papers 4 and 5.
- Routes: AS only (Papers 1, 2, 3 in one series); staged A Level (AS in year 1, then Papers 4 and 5); A Level in one
  series (all five papers). AS grades a–e; A Level grades A*–E. Series: June and November (March in India only).
- Assessment objectives: AO1 knowledge and understanding 40%, AO2 handling, applying and evaluating information 40%,
  AO3 experimental skills and investigations 20% (AS and A Level). Papers 1, 2, 4: AO1 50%, AO2 50%. Papers 3, 5: AO3 100%.
- Guided learning hours: about 180 for AS, about 360 for A Level. Recommended prior study: IGCSE/O Level Physics or
  Co-ordinated Science.
- Data and formulae: page 2 of Papers 1 and 2; pages 2 and 3 of Paper 4. Decimal marker: a single dot on the line.
  SI units (or BIPM-approved units such as the minute); no imperial units.
- Paper 3, Question 1: collect data, plot a graph, draw conclusions (min. 7 marks MMO, 6 presentation, 4 analysis,
  3 floating). Question 2: collect data and draw conclusions, may or may not include a graph; the method is
  inaccurate and candidates evaluate it and suggest improvements (min. 5 MMO, 2 recording, 10 analysis/evaluation:
  conclusions, estimating uncertainties, limitations, improvements; 3 floating). The two questions are in different
  areas of physics; no prior knowledge of the theory is required.
- Paper 5, Question 1: planning (15 marks: defining the problem, methods of data collection, method of analysis,
  additional detail including safety). Question 2: analysis, conclusions and evaluation (15 marks: data analysis,
  table of results, graph, conclusion, treatment of uncertainties); candidates are given an equation and data, find a
  constant and estimate its uncertainty. Contexts may be outside the syllabus; needed information is given.
- Command words (syllabus list): calculate, comment, compare, define, describe, determine, explain, give, identify,
  justify, predict, show (that), sketch, state, suggest.

## Syllabus learning outcomes
Compact form: `- 5.1 Title: 1 … · 2 …` (numbers are the syllabus outcome numbers, e.g. 5.1.3).

### AS Level
- 1.1 Physical quantities: 1 every physical quantity is a numerical magnitude and a unit · 2 make reasonable estimates of physical quantities in the syllabus
- 1.2 SI units: 1 recall base quantities and units: mass (kg), length (m), time (s), current (A), temperature (K) · 2 express derived units as products or quotients of base units; use derived units for syllabus quantities · 3 use base units to check the homogeneity of equations · 4 recall and use prefixes p, n, μ, m, c, d, k, M, G, T
- 1.3 Errors and uncertainties: 1 effects of systematic errors (including zero errors) and random errors · 2 distinction between precision and accuracy · 3 uncertainty in a derived quantity by simple addition of absolute or percentage uncertainties
- 1.4 Scalars and vectors: 1 difference between scalars and vectors, with syllabus examples · 2 add and subtract coplanar vectors · 3 represent a vector as two perpendicular components
- 2.1 Equations of motion: 1 define and use distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration · 2 graphical methods to represent them · 3 displacement = area under v–t graph · 4 velocity = gradient of s–t graph · 5 acceleration = gradient of v–t graph · 6 derive the uniform-acceleration equations from the definitions of velocity and acceleration · 7 solve problems with them, including free fall without air resistance · 8 describe an experiment to determine g using a falling object · 9 describe and explain motion with uniform velocity in one direction and uniform acceleration in a perpendicular direction (projectiles)
- 3.1 Momentum and Newton's laws (IGCSE forces assumed): 1 mass is the property that resists change in motion · 2 recall F = ma; acceleration and resultant force always in the same direction · 3 define and use linear momentum p = mv · 4 define and use force as rate of change of momentum · 5 state and apply each of Newton's laws · 6 weight is the effect of a gravitational field on a mass; W = mg
- 3.2 Non-uniform motion: 1 qualitative friction and viscous/drag forces including air resistance (no coefficients; drag increases with speed) · 2 describe and explain qualitatively motion in a uniform field with air resistance · 3 objects moving against a resistive force may reach terminal (constant) velocity
- 3.3 Linear momentum and its conservation: 1 state the principle of conservation of momentum · 2 apply it to elastic and inelastic interactions in one and two dimensions (no coefficient of restitution) · 3 elastic collision: total KE conserved and relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation · 4 momentum always conserved but KE may change
- 4.1 Turning effects: 1 weight acts at the centre of gravity · 2 define and apply the moment of a force · 3 a couple is a pair of forces producing rotation only · 4 define and apply the torque of a couple
- 4.2 Equilibrium of forces: 1 state and apply the principle of moments · 2 no resultant force and no resultant torque means equilibrium · 3 vector triangle for coplanar forces in equilibrium
- 4.3 Density and pressure: 1 define and use density · 2 define and use pressure · 3 derive Δp = ρgΔh from the definitions of pressure and density · 4 use Δp = ρgΔh · 5 upthrust is due to a difference in hydrostatic pressure · 6 calculate upthrust with F = ρgV (Archimedes' principle)
- 5.1 Energy conservation (IGCSE energy assumed): 1 work done = force × displacement in the direction of the force · 2 principle of conservation of energy · 3 efficiency = useful energy output / total energy input · 4 solve efficiency problems · 5 define power as work done per unit time · 6 use P = W/t · 7 derive P = Fv and use it
- 5.2 GPE and KE: 1 derive ΔE_P = mgΔh using W = Fs · 2 recall and use ΔE_P = mgΔh · 3 derive E_K = ½mv² from the equations of motion · 4 recall and use E_K = ½mv²
- 6.1 Stress and strain: 1 deformation from tensile or compressive forces (one dimension) · 2 load, extension, compression, limit of proportionality · 3 recall and use Hooke's law · 4 spring constant k = F/x · 5 define and use stress, strain and the Young modulus · 6 describe an experiment to determine the Young modulus of a metal wire
- 6.2 Elastic and plastic behaviour: 1 elastic deformation, plastic deformation, elastic limit · 2 area under force–extension graph = work done · 3 elastic potential energy from that area (within the limit of proportionality) · 4 recall and use E_P = ½Fx = ½kx²
- 7.1 Progressive waves (IGCSE colour assumed): 1 wave motion in ropes, springs and ripple tanks · 2 displacement, amplitude, phase difference, period, frequency, wavelength, speed · 3 use the CRO time-base and y-gain to find frequency and amplitude · 4 derive v = fλ from the definitions · 5 recall and use v = fλ · 6 progressive waves transfer energy · 7 intensity = power/area; intensity ∝ amplitude²
- 7.2 Transverse and longitudinal: 1 compare them · 2 analyse and interpret graphs of both
- 7.3 Doppler effect (sound): 1 a source moving relative to a stationary observer gives a different observed frequency (moving observer not required) · 2 use f_o = f_s v/(v ± v_s)
- 7.4 EM spectrum: 1 all EM waves are transverse and travel at c in free space · 2 approximate wavelength ranges from radio to γ-rays · 3 visible light is 400–700 nm in free space
- 7.5 Polarisation: 1 polarisation is associated with transverse waves · 2 Malus's law I = I₀cos²θ through one or more polarising filters (effect of a filter on unpolarised light not required)
- 8.1 Stationary waves: 1 explain and use the principle of superposition · 2 experiments with microwaves, stretched strings and air columns (end corrections negligible, not required) · 3 formation by a graphical method; nodes and antinodes · 4 wavelength from positions of nodes or antinodes
- 8.2 Diffraction: 1 meaning of diffraction · 2 experiments, including the qualitative effect of gap width relative to wavelength (e.g. ripple tank)
- 8.3 Interference: 1 interference and coherence · 2 two-source interference with water waves, sound, light and microwaves · 3 conditions needed to observe two-source fringes · 4 recall and use λ = ax/D (double slit, light)
- 8.4 Diffraction grating: 1 recall and use d sin θ = nλ · 2 describe using a grating to find the wavelength of light (no spectrometer)
- 9.1 Electric current: 1 current is a flow of charge carriers · 2 charge on carriers is quantised · 3 recall and use Q = It · 4 use I = Anvq (n = number density of carriers)
- 9.2 P.d. and power: 1 define p.d. as energy transferred per unit charge · 2 recall and use V = W/Q · 3 recall and use P = VI, P = I²R, P = V²/R
- 9.3 Resistance and resistivity: 1 define resistance · 2 recall and use V = IR · 3 sketch I–V characteristics of a metallic conductor at constant temperature, a semiconductor diode, a filament lamp · 4 filament lamp resistance rises with current because temperature rises · 5 state Ohm's law · 6 recall and use R = ρL/A · 7 LDR resistance decreases as light intensity increases · 8 thermistor (NTC) resistance decreases as temperature increases
- 10.1 Practical circuits: 1 recall and use the section 6 circuit symbols · 2 draw and interpret circuit diagrams · 3 define and use e.m.f. as energy transferred per unit charge in driving charge round a complete circuit · 4 distinguish e.m.f. and p.d. by energy · 5 effect of internal resistance on terminal p.d.
- 10.2 Kirchhoff's laws: 1 first law is a consequence of conservation of charge · 2 second law is a consequence of conservation of energy · 3 derive series resistance formula · 4 use it · 5 derive parallel resistance formula · 6 use it · 7 use Kirchhoff's laws to solve simple circuit problems
- 10.3 Potential dividers: 1 principle of a potential divider · 2 recall and use the potentiometer to compare p.d.s · 3 galvanometer in null methods · 4 thermistors and LDRs in potential dividers giving p.d.s that depend on temperature and light
- 11.1 Atoms, nuclei and radiation: 1 infer from α-scattering the existence and small size of the nucleus · 2 simple nuclear atom model: protons, neutrons, orbital electrons · 3 nucleon number vs proton number · 4 isotopes · 5 notation ᴬ_Z X · 6 nucleon number and charge conserved in nuclear processes · 7 composition, mass and charge of α, β (β⁻ electrons and β⁺ positrons) and γ · 8 antiparticle: same mass, opposite charge; positron is the antiparticle of the electron · 9 electron antineutrinos in β⁻ decay, electron neutrinos in β⁺ decay · 10 α discrete energies, β continuous range because (anti)neutrinos are emitted · 11 decay equations like ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂α · 12 use the unified atomic mass unit u
- 11.2 Fundamental particles: 1 quarks are fundamental; six flavours: up, down, strange, charm, top, bottom · 2 recall and use quark charges; antiquarks have opposite charge (nothing else about quarks required) · 3 protons and neutrons are not fundamental; quark compositions (uud, udd) · 4 hadrons: baryons (three quarks) or mesons (quark + antiquark) · 5 quark changes in β⁻ and β⁺ decay · 6 electrons and neutrinos are fundamental particles called leptons

### Practical skills (Papers 3 and 5)
- P3 MMO: set up apparatus from written instructions and diagrams; collect enough data; repeat readings; use mm scales, protractors, top-pan balances, newton meters, analogue/digital meters, measuring cylinders, calipers (vernier or digital), micrometer screw gauges, thermometers; time an appropriate number of consecutive oscillations; span the largest possible range
- P3 presentation: one table with raw and calculated columns; headings quantity / unit (e.g. I / mA; "I (mA)" and "I in mA" allowed, "I mA" not); raw readings to the same precision as the instrument; calculated values to n or n+1 s.f. where n is the least s.f. in the data
- P3 graphs: axes labelled quantity / unit; points occupy at least half the grid both ways; false origin where appropriate; scales 1, 2 or 5 units per 2 cm; labels at least every 2 cm; points plotted to better than 1 mm, diameter under 1 mm; best-fit line or curve with points evenly either side; anomalous point circled; tangents to curves
- P3 analysis: relate straight lines to y = mx + c; read coordinates on the line; gradient using points more than half the line apart; y-intercept, with a false origin by substituting a point into y = mx + c
- P3 uncertainties: absolute uncertainty of a measurement; absolute ↔ percentage; repeated readings: half the range
- P3 conclusions: find constants; test a hypothesis by comparing the percentage difference between two values of a constant with a given percentage uncertainty; predictions
- P3 evaluation: identify limitations and the most significant sources of uncertainty (state the quantity and the reason); suggest realistic improvements (other apparatus or procedures, not a different experiment)
- P5 planning: independent, dependent and controlled variables; how to vary and measure them; labelled diagram of a workable arrangement; method of analysis (what to plot and how the constant follows from gradient/intercept); additional detail and safety (risks and precautions); oscilloscope to measure voltage, current, time and frequency; light gates and data logger for time, velocity and acceleration; other sensors (e.g. motion sensor)
- P5 analysis: rearrange into y = mx + c, y = axⁿ (plot lg y against lg x) and y = ae^(kx) (plot ln y against x); choose derived quantities to plot; logarithm columns: unit inside, e.g. ln (d / cm), decimal places of the log = s.f. of the value (L = 76.5 cm → lg(L / cm) = 1.884 or 1.8837)
- P5 graph: error bars (both directions where appropriate); best-fit line and worst acceptable line (steepest or shallowest through all error bars, dashed or labelled)
- P5 uncertainties: absolute ↔ fractional/percentage; uncertainty beside every table value; uncertainty in derived quantities; uncertainty in gradient = |gradient of best fit − gradient of worst acceptable line| (same for intercept); express a result as value ± uncertainty with unit

### Mathematical requirements (section 6)
