# P17: Mock papers: AS (Papers 1, 2 and 3)

Chapters, in this order: **phys.mock.p1**, **phys.mock.p2**, **phys.mock.p3**. No widget to build.
Read first: COURSE.md, kit/STYLE.md ("Mock papers" and "Syntax"), chapters/phys.dc.md (the gold chapter), then this
file. Nothing else, except the greps this file asks for.
These are mock papers (`@level mock`): a full timed paper with the real paper's time, total marks, number and style of
questions. They are not topic chapters: no `:::why`, warm-up, `:::def`, `:::quiz`, `:::checklist` or `:::recap`.

## Rules for all three mocks

### Originality
Every question is **original**, written in Cambridge 9702 style and mark-scheme language. Never copy or paraphrase a
real past-paper question, and **never repeat the course's own worked examples or practice questions**. Before you
write a question, grep the chapter it revises for its contexts and numbers and choose a different context or
different numbers:
`grep -n ":::eg\|^Q:" chapters/<id>.md | cut -c1-140`. For phys.mock.p1, also grep the ten quiz questions and the
technique demonstrations in phys.exam.p1 (`grep -n "^?\|^### Technique" chapters/phys.exam.p1.md`) and do not reuse them.
Exam facts (times, marks, weightings) come only from the facts below.

### File shape
```
@chapter phys.mock.p1
@title Mock paper: Paper 1 multiple choice
@level mock
@paper CAIE 9702 Paper 1
@time 1 h 15 min
@summary An original 40-question multiple-choice paper on the AS content (topics 1–11), timed like the real Paper 1: 1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks.

:::exam Instructions
…
:::

:::try
Q: [1 mark] (#phys.units) …
A: …
:::

… one bare :::try per question …

Next: [Mock paper: Paper 2 AS structured questions](#phys.mock.p2)
```
- The title in `@title` must match course.json exactly.
- One **bare** `:::try` per question (no title): the page numbers them Question 1, 2, … Each starts
  `Q: [n marks] (#chapter-to-revise)`, where `(#id)` is the one chapter a reader who drops marks should revise (for a
  synoptic question, the chapter of the hardest part). No ★ ratings, no `H:` hints (it is an exam).
- Parts: (a), (b), (c)(i), … each start a new line **with a blank line before it**, in the `Q:` and in the `A:`. End
  each part of the question with its marks in round brackets, `(2 marks)`; the `[n marks]` at the start is the total.
- `A:` is the full mark scheme, mark tokens at the end of the line that earns them (COURSE.md "Mark-scheme style"):
  C1 on the formula/substitution line of every calculation, A1 on the answer with unit and sensible s.f., B1 for each
  independent statement, reading or reason, M1 only in "show that" and derivations. After the marks, add a short note
  in round brackets where examiners accept alternatives: `[A1] (allow 0.49 to 0.51)`. `check` adds the marks in each
  answer and fails when they differ from `[n marks]`.
- Figures: hand-drawn SVG in `:::html` following COURSE.md's figure rules and the gold chapter's patterns (classes,
  arrowheads, dark mode); graphs of functions with `:::plot`; data as Markdown tables with units in the headings
  (`| m / kg | t / s |`). Keep every figure and table within 375 px (`phone`).
- End with the `Next:` line given for each mock.

### Checks file (required)
`checks/<id>.py` recomputes **every number** in the paper: each given value that is derived from others, every
option of every multiple-choice question (the key and each distractor), every answer in every mark scheme, and every
accepted range. It must also assert the marks: a list of marks per question whose sum equals the paper total
(Paper 1: 40 questions, each 1 mark, total 40; Paper 2: total 60; Paper 3: two questions of 20, total 40). For
multiple-choice questions, assert that the computed answer equals the keyed option and differs from every distractor.

### Exam facts you may use (FACTS.md)
- Paper 1: Multiple Choice, 1 h 15 min, 40 marks, 40 four-option questions on AS content; 31% of AS, 15.5% of A Level.
- Paper 2: AS Level Structured Questions, 1 h 15 min, 60 marks, structured questions on AS content; 46% of AS, 23% of A Level.
- Paper 3: Advanced Practical Skills, 2 h, 40 marks: two questions, each 1 hour and 20 marks, in different areas of
  physics; no prior knowledge of the theory is required. Question 1: data, a graph, conclusions (7 marks MMO =
  manipulation, measurement and observation, 6 presentation, 4 analysis, 3 floating). Question 2: data and conclusions
  from an inaccurate method that candidates evaluate and improve (5 MMO, 2 recording, 10 analysis and evaluation:
  conclusions, estimating uncertainties, limitations, improvements; 3 floating).
- Data and formulae: page 2 of Papers 1 and 2 (COURSE.md "Data values"). Decimal marker: a dot. SI units only.
- AO1 and AO2 are 50% each in Papers 1 and 2: mix recall questions with application to unfamiliar situations.
- The real paper's number of structured questions in Paper 2 is not in FACTS.md: this mock uses **7 questions**, a
  typical number, and the instructions box does not claim that the real paper always has 7.

## phys.mock.p1: Mock paper: Paper 1 multiple choice
`@level mock` · `@paper CAIE 9702 Paper 1` · `@time 1 h 15 min` · 40 questions, 40 marks ·
`@summary An original 40-question multiple-choice paper on the AS content (topics 1–11), timed like the real Paper 1: 1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks.` ·
`Next: [Mock paper: Paper 2 AS structured questions](#phys.mock.p2)`

### `:::exam Instructions` box
1 hour 15 minutes · 40 questions · 40 marks (one mark each) · AS content (topics 1–11) · answer all questions; each
has four options A, B, C and D and exactly one is correct; write your letter for each question on paper, then press
Finish and mark yourself (no marks are lost for a wrong answer, so give an answer to every question) · calculator
allowed · the data the paper needs: g, e, c and any other constant used (list them, values from COURSE.md) · plan:
about 1.5 minutes per question on a first pass, then 15 minutes for the questions you flagged (link
[Paper 1 survival guide](#phys.exam.p1)).

### Multiple-choice format (every question)
```
:::try
Q: [1 mark] (#phys.kinematics) A ball is thrown … What is the time taken to reach the ground?

**A** \(0.45\,\mathrm{s}\)

**B** \(0.64\,\mathrm{s}\)

**C** \(0.90\,\mathrm{s}\)

**D** \(1.3\,\mathrm{s}\)
A: **B**. \(s = \tfrac12 gt^2\) with \(s = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}\) gives \(t = \sqrt{2 \times 2.0/9.81} = 0.64\,\mathrm{s}\). [B1]
:::
```
- Options A–D are part of the question, each on its own line with a blank line before it, bold letter first.
  Numerical options in ascending order; statement options as short parallel sentences. Where options are graphs,
  draw one small SVG with four labelled panels A–D (2 × 2 grid, fits 375 px).
- `A:` gives the letter in bold and a **one-line reason** (the key step, or why the tempting distractor is wrong),
  then `[B1]`. Nothing longer.
- Every distractor is a real slip: a forgotten square, degrees for radians, mm² not converted, the wrong component,
  a missing factor of 2 or ½, adding percentage uncertainties for a sum, area vs gradient, mass vs weight.
- Mix AO1 (recall: a unit, a definition, a graph shape) and AO2 (apply to a new situation); about 15 questions need
  no calculation.

## phys.mock.p3: Mock paper: Paper 3 practical skills (written)
`@level mock` · `@paper CAIE 9702 Paper 3` · `@time 2 h` · 2 questions, 40 marks (20 each) ·
`@summary An original written version of the practical paper: you analyse supplied readings from two experiments, draw a graph, find a gradient and intercept, estimate uncertainties and evaluate a method. 2 hours, 40 marks, like the real Paper 3.` ·
`Next: [Mock paper: Paper 4 A Level structured questions](#phys.mock.p4)`

The real Paper 3 is a hands-on lab paper. This mock is a **written** paper with **supplied readings**: the reader
cannot touch apparatus, so each question describes the experiment (with an SVG of the set-up), gives the raw readings
a careful candidate took (including repeat readings and one or two instrument drawings to read), and asks exactly
the kinds of task the real paper marks. Say this in the instructions box, and say that the best practice for the
real paper is doing experiments in a lab.

### `:::exam Instructions` box
2 hours · 40 marks · 2 questions of 20 marks, about 1 hour each · a written version of Paper 3 with supplied readings
· you need a pencil, a ruler and graph paper (or squared paper) for Question 1 · no theory beyond what each question
gives is needed · give raw readings and calculated values to a sensible precision, with units in table headings ·
press Finish to see the mark scheme; for the graph, compare your gradient and intercept with the accepted ranges ·
revise with [Practical skills](#phys.practical).

### Question 1 (20 marks): graph experiment, current in a resistance wire (revise phys.practical)
Context (electricity): a cell of e.m.f. E and a fixed resistor are connected in series with a length L of
resistance wire and an ammeter; the current I is read for six lengths (0.200 m to 0.700 m in 0.100 m steps), twice
each (the crocodile clip is moved off and back). It is suggested that \(\dfrac{1}{I} = PL + Q\), where P and Q are
constants.
Supply: an SVG of the circuit (gold-chapter circuit symbols); a table of raw readings (L / m, I₁ / A, I₂ / A; currents
to 0.01 A, one pair that differs a little more than the others); an SVG analogue ammeter scale and an SVG micrometer
reading of the wire's diameter for part (a). Generate the readings from chosen values (e.g. E = 1.50 V, fixed
resistance plus internal resistance about 2 Ω, wire about 4 Ω m⁻¹) with realistic scatter (about ±1%), and record the
exact generator values in the checks file.
| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) MMO | read the ammeter drawing (1) and the micrometer drawing (1); state the precision the current should be recorded to and why (1); why repeat each reading and how to treat the slightly odd pair (1); suggest one further length that extends the range sensibly (1); one reason the wire's temperature could change the readings and how to reduce it (switch off between readings) (1); state the uncertainty in one current reading (1) | 7 (B1 each) |
| (b) presentation | table: column headings with units for mean I and 1/I (`1/I / A⁻¹`) (1), raw currents to a consistent precision (1), 1/I to the s.f. of the raw data or one more (1), mean I and 1/I values correct (1); graph: axes labelled with units and scales using more than half the grid (1), all six points plotted to half a small square (1) | 6 (B1 each) |
| (c) analysis | line of best fit with balanced points (1); gradient from a triangle using more than half the line (1); y-intercept read or calculated (1); P and Q stated with their units (1) | 4 (B1 each) |
| (d) floating | given \(P = \rho/(EA)\) and E, deduce the resistivity ρ from P and the measured diameter (C1) with unit (A1); predict I for L = 1.000 m to a sensible s.f. (B1) | 3 |
The answer gives the expected mean-I and 1/I columns, the expected gradient and intercept, and accepted ranges (e.g.
gradient within ±5% of the least-squares value, intercept within a stated band), all computed in the checks file.
The reader plots on their own graph paper; the answer describes what a full-mark graph looks like.

### Question 2 (20 marks): evaluation experiment, a falling paper cone (revise phys.practical)
Context (mechanics, a different area from Question 1, as on the real paper): a paper cone is dropped from height h
and the fall time t is measured with a hand-held stopwatch, three times at h = 0.400 m and three times at
h = 0.800 m. It is suggested that \(t = kh\), where k is a constant (the cone reaches terminal velocity quickly).
Supply: an SVG of the set-up (cone, vertical metre rule, release point); the raw readings table (times to 0.01 s as
displayed, with a spread typical of reaction time); an SVG stopwatch display for one reading and an SVG vernier
caliper reading of the cone's diameter. Choose the data so that the conclusion is clear-cut either way, and make the
conclusion follow the course's criterion in [Practical skills](#phys.practical): compare the percentage difference
between the two k values with the percentage uncertainty.
| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) MMO | read the stopwatch drawing (1); read the caliper drawing (1); mean t for each height (1); absolute uncertainty in t as half the range, and its percentage (1); estimate the uncertainty in h with a reason (judging the release point by eye) (1) | 5 (B1 each) |
| (b) recording | mean t values recorded with units (1) and to the precision the readings justify (1) | 2 (B1 each) |
| (c) analysis and evaluation | k for both heights (1); conclusion: do the results support \(t = kh\)? percentage difference vs percentage uncertainty, with the criterion stated (1); four limitations, each a specific source of error (4 × B1); four matched improvements, each a specific, practical change (4 × B1) | 10 |
| (d) floating | justify the number of s.f. in k (1); state which measurement contributes the largest percentage uncertainty (1); explain what a graph of t against h would show if the suggestion were true (straight line through the origin) (1) | 3 (B1 each) |
For (c), the answer lists about six limitation–improvement pairs and says "any four limitations, any four
improvements": e.g. reaction time at start and stop → video with a timer in view, or light gates; only two heights →
at least five heights and a graph of t against h; the cone drifts sideways and hits things → drop it in still air or
inside a wide tube; hard to judge the release height → fix a clamp and a pointer at the release point; the cone may
not reach terminal velocity at once → measure the time over a lower section only, with two light gates; cones vary
→ use the same cone, check it is undamaged. Generic answers ("human error", "repeat more") score 0: say so in a note.
Q1: 7 + 6 + 4 + 3 = 20. Q2: 5 + 2 + 10 + 3 = 20. Paper total 40; the checks file asserts all three.
Before writing, `grep -n -i "cone\|resistance wire\|1/I" chapters/phys.practical.md chapters/phys.planning.md | cut -c1-120`
and change the context or numbers if the course already uses them (the practical chapter uses a pendulum, T² against l).
