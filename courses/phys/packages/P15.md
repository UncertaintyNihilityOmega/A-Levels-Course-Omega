### Exam facts you may use (FACTS.md)
- Paper 1: Multiple Choice, 1 h 15 min, 40 marks, 40 four-option questions on AS content; 31% of AS, 15.5% of A Level;
  tests AO1 and AO2 (50% each); the data and formulae sheet is page 2.
- Paper 2: AS Level Structured Questions, 1 h 15 min, 60 marks, AS content; 46% of AS, 23% of A Level; AO1/AO2 50/50;
  data sheet page 2.
- Paper 4: A Level Structured Questions, 2 h, 100 marks, A Level content with AS knowledge required; 38.5% of A Level;
  AO1/AO2 50/50; data and formulae on pages 2 and 3.
- Command words and their syllabus meanings: Calculate: work out from given facts, figures or information.
  Comment: give an informed opinion. Compare: identify/comment on similarities and/or differences. Define: give
  precise meaning. Describe: state the points of a topic / give characteristics and main features. Determine:
  establish an answer using the information available. Explain: set out purposes or reasons / make the relationships
  between things evident / provide why and/or how and support with relevant evidence. Give: produce an answer from a
  given source or recall/memory. Identify: name/select/recognise. Justify: support a case with evidence/argument.
  Predict: suggest what may happen based on available information. Show (that): provide structured evidence that
  leads to a given result. Sketch: make a simple freehand drawing showing the key features. State: express in clear
  terms. Suggest: apply knowledge and understanding to situations where there are a range of valid responses in order
  to make proposals.

  φ = −GM/r, E_P = −GMm/r, p = ⅓(Nm/V)⟨c²⟩, a = −ω²x, v = v₀ cos ωt, v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²), V = Q/(4πε₀r),

  s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as, Δp = ρgΔh, F = ρgV, f_o = f_s v/(v ± v_s), I = Anvq, resistors in series and parallel.

## phys.exam.p1: Paper 1 survival guide: multiple choice
`@level guide` · `@paper CAIE 9702 Paper 1` · `@time 2–3 hours` · `Next: [Papers 2 and 4 survival guide: structured questions](#phys.exam.p24)`

### What to cover
1. The paper in numbers (table from the facts): 40 questions in 75 minutes is under 2 minutes each; no penalty for
   wrong answers in a four-option paper, so never leave a blank (say only "there is no reason to leave a blank", as the
   mark scheme is one mark per correct answer; do not invent rules).
2. A working method: read the stem fully; predict before looking at options; work on paper (you can write on the
   question paper); use the data sheet; eliminate with units, limiting cases and sense checks; flag and return.
3. Techniques, each with one original `:::eg` MCQ demonstration (question, options A–D, and the reasoning):
   unit/homogeneity elimination (base units of an unknown constant); estimation (order of magnitude of the kinetic
   energy of a running student or the power of a lamp); ratio and proportion without full calculation (change r to 2r
   in F ∝ 1/r²; resistance of a wire with double length and half diameter); reading graphs (gradient vs area); vector
   options (resultant of two forces, components); "which statement is correct" questions on definitions (choosing
   between near-identical wordings; e.g. e.m.f. vs p.d.); combining uncertainties; circuit questions by symmetry
   (potential at points in a bridge); wave graph reading (displacement–distance vs displacement–time); particle physics
   charge counting (quark compositions).
4. Common traps table (trap → why → how to avoid): radians vs degrees (rarely in P1 but check), mm²→m², confusing
   wavelength and period on graphs, adding percentage uncertainties for a sum, area vs gradient, weight vs mass,
   internal resistance ignored, Newton's third-law pairs, components with sin/cos swapped.
5. Calculator and time management; a 2-week Paper 1 practice plan using topic links (each technique links its topic
   chapter).
6. A `:::quiz` of 10 original Paper 1-style questions across the AS topics, each wrong option a real misconception,
   with `>` explanations linking to the chapter (e.g. "> Revise [Kinematics](#phys.kinematics)").
7. `:::recap`, `:::links` (Paper 1 past papers on Physics & Maths Tutor, [unofficial]), Next line.

## phys.exam.p24: Papers 2 and 4 survival guide: structured questions
`@level guide` · `@paper CAIE 9702 Papers 2 and 4` · `@time 3–4 hours` · `Next: [Mixed practice: AS Level](#phys.mixed.as)`

### What to cover
1. The papers in numbers (Paper 2: 60 marks in 75 min ≈ 1.25 min per mark; Paper 4: 100 marks in 120 min ≈ 1.2 min
   per mark) and what "AS knowledge required" means for Paper 4 (synoptic questions: e.g. circular motion with
   energy, fields with kinematics).
2. Command words table (all 15, with the syllabus meaning and what a full answer looks like in physics, with a
   short example each: "State Newton's first law" vs "Explain why the car reaches a terminal velocity" vs
   "Show that the speed is about 3 m s⁻¹").
3. Definitions: why they are worth easy marks; a **master table of the key definitions** in the course, grouped by
   topic, each linking its chapter. If a chapter is already written, you may grep its `:::def` lines
   (`grep -n -A2 ":::def" chapters/<id>.md`) so the wording matches; otherwise write standard Cambridge wording. Key
   definitions: displacement, velocity, acceleration, momentum, force (rate of change
   of momentum), Newton's laws, principle of conservation of momentum, moment, couple, torque of a couple, principle
   of moments, density, pressure, work done, power, efficiency, Hooke's law, stress, strain, Young modulus, elastic
   limit, wavelength, frequency, phase difference, intensity, coherence, diffraction, principle of superposition,
   current, coulomb, potential difference, volt, resistance, ohm, Ohm's law, resistivity, e.m.f., Kirchhoff's two
   laws, isotope, antiparticle, radian, angular speed, gravitational field strength, gravitational potential,
   specific heat capacity, specific latent heat, ideal gas, internal energy, first law, SHM, damping, resonance,
   electric field strength, electric potential, capacitance, time constant, magnetic flux density, magnetic flux,
   flux linkage, Faraday's law, Lenz's law, r.m.s. current, photon, work function, threshold frequency, de Broglie
   wavelength, mass defect, binding energy, activity, decay constant, half-life, acoustic impedance, luminosity,
   standard candle, Hubble's law. Use the gold chapter's wording for e.m.f., p.d. and internal resistance, and keep
   each definition to one line.
4. "Show that" questions: show every step, give the answer to more s.f. than the given value; a demonstration.
5. Calculations: the C1/A1 pattern (formula and substitution, then answer with unit and sensible s.f.); carrying
   unrounded values; s.f. rules; common unit slips (table).
6. Explanations: chain-of-reasoning answers (one mark per link), with two demonstrations (terminal velocity; why the
   maximum KE of photoelectrons does not depend on intensity), each showing a weak and a full answer.
7. Graphs and sketches: what "sketch" demands (shape, key points, asymptotes, labelled axes; examples: I–V of a diode,
   binding energy per nucleon, damping, discharge of a capacitor).
8. Derivations the syllabus can ask for (list with chapter links: suvat, ΔE_P = mgΔh, E_K = ½mv², P = Fv, Δp = ρgΔh,
   v = fλ, series and parallel resistors, g = GM/r², pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ and ½m⟨c²⟩ = 3/2 kT, series and parallel
   capacitors, V_H = BI/(ntq)).
9. Paper 4 synoptic demonstrations: one worked multi-part question that crosses topics (e.g. a satellite: circular
   motion + gravitational potential energy + energy needed to change orbit), done as an `:::eg` with marks.
10. A `:::quiz` of 6–8 questions on exam technique (which answer would score full marks?), `:::recap`, `:::links`
    (Paper 2 and Paper 4 past papers on Physics & Maths Tutor, [unofficial]), Next line.
