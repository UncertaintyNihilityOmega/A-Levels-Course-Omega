## phys.dynamics: Dynamics and momentum
`@level as` · `@paper CAIE P1 · P2 (AS)` · `@time 6–8 hours` · `Next: [Forces, density and pressure](#phys.forces)`
Before you start: [Kinematics](#phys.kinematics), vectors in [Physical quantities, units and uncertainty](#phys.units).
The syllabus assumes IGCSE forces: re-teach in two sentences what a force, a resultant force and weight are.
Warm-up: from Kinematics (suvat: find a; gradient of v–t) and Units (resultant of two perpendicular forces).

### Syllabus outcomes (cover every one)
- 3.1.1 understand that mass is the property of an object that resists change in motion
- 3.1.2 recall F = ma and solve problems using it, understanding that acceleration and resultant force are always in the same direction
- 3.1.3 define and use linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity
- 3.1.4 define and use force as rate of change of momentum
- 3.1.5 state and apply each of Newton's laws of motion
- 3.1.6 describe and use the concept of weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass and recall that the weight of an object is equal to the product of its mass and the acceleration of free fall
- 3.2.1 show a qualitative understanding of frictional forces and viscous/drag forces including air resistance (no treatment of the coefficients of friction and viscosity is required; a simple model of drag force increasing as speed increases is sufficient)
- 3.2.2 describe and explain qualitatively the motion of objects in a uniform gravitational field with air resistance
- 3.2.3 understand that objects moving against a resistive force may reach a terminal (constant) velocity
- 3.3.1 state the principle of conservation of momentum
- 3.3.2 apply the principle of conservation of momentum to solve simple problems, including elastic and inelastic interactions between objects in both one and two dimensions (knowledge of the concept of coefficient of restitution is not required)
- 3.3.3 recall that, for an elastic collision, total kinetic energy is conserved and the relative speed of approach is equal to the relative speed of separation
- 3.3.4 understand that, while momentum of a system is always conserved in interactions between objects, some change in kinetic energy may take place

### Sections (suggested)
1. Mass and inertia; weight W = mg (weight is a force, in N; mass in kg; on the Moon g is smaller, the mass is
   the same).
2. Newton's laws, one subsection each, with the exam statements, an everyday example and a common
   misconception (first law: no force is needed to keep moving; third law pairs act on **different** bodies, are
   the **same type** of force, equal and opposite; weight and normal contact force on a book are NOT a third-law
   pair).
3. Momentum p = mv (kg m s⁻¹ = N s); force as rate of change of momentum F = Δp/Δt; F = ma as the special case of
   constant mass (`:::proof` plain: Δ(mv)/Δt = m Δv/Δt). Impulse FΔt = Δp and the area under a force–time graph
   (useful, though only "force = rate of change of momentum" is in the syllabus). Contexts: crumple zones, airbags,
   catching a ball, a rocket or a hose (mass flow: F = (Δm/Δt)v).
4. F = ma problems: resultant force first (free-body diagram), connected bodies (car and trailer, lift with a
   person: reading of scales in an accelerating lift), objects on slopes (component mg sin θ). Acceleration is
   always in the direction of the resultant force.
5. Resistive forces: friction (opposes relative motion of surfaces) and drag/viscous forces (increase with speed;
   simple model only). Falling with air resistance: at the start a = g, then drag grows, a falls, terminal
   velocity when drag = weight. Skydiver v–t graph through parachute opening (sudden deceleration, new lower
   terminal velocity). Terminal velocity also for a car at full power (driving force = drag) and for a ball
   bearing in oil.
6. Conservation of momentum: statement; 1D collisions (sign convention!), explosions and recoil (gun, two
   trolleys pushed apart by a spring: total momentum zero before and after), 2D problems (glancing collision of
   two pucks or snooker balls; resolve into two perpendicular directions and conserve each; or a vector
   triangle). Elastic vs inelastic (perfectly inelastic: they stick together). Relative speed of approach =
   relative speed of separation for elastic collisions (use it to avoid the quadratic). Where the "lost" kinetic
   energy goes (thermal, sound, deformation). Embed **phys-collide** here.

### Definitions (`:::def`, exam wording)
- Mass: "the property of an object that resists changes in its motion".
- Weight: "the force on a mass due to a gravitational field" (W = mg).
- Linear momentum: "the product of mass and velocity".
- Force: "the rate of change of momentum".
- Newton's first law: "a body remains at rest or continues to move with constant velocity unless acted on by a
  resultant force". Second law: "the rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the resultant force
  acting on it and takes place in the direction of the force". Third law: "if body A exerts a force on body B,
  then body B exerts a force on body A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction" (add: same type of
  force).
- Principle of conservation of momentum: "the total momentum of a system of interacting bodies remains constant,
  provided no resultant external force acts on the system".
- Elastic collision: "a collision in which the total kinetic energy is conserved". Inelastic: "total kinetic
  energy is not conserved" (momentum still is).
- Terminal velocity: "the constant velocity reached when the resistive force equals the driving force (e.g.
  weight), so the resultant force is zero".

### Worked examples and question ideas
- Car of 1200 kg, driving force 3.0 kN, drag 1.2 kN: acceleration; later at terminal speed.
- Lift: 60 kg person on scales, lift accelerating up at 1.5 m s⁻²: scale reading 679 N (60 × 11.31).
- Ball of 0.15 kg hits a wall at 20 m s⁻¹, rebounds at 15 m s⁻¹, contact 0.010 s: Δp = 5.25 N s, F = 525 N
  (sign trap: Δp is not 0.75 N s).
- Water from a hose: 2.0 kg s⁻¹ at 12 m s⁻¹ hitting a wall and stopping: force 24 N.
- Trolley collisions (stick together; KE lost and its percentage); elastic collision of equal masses (they
  exchange velocities), checked with relative speeds.
- Explosion/recoil: 4.0 kg rifle fires 10 g bullet at 400 m s⁻¹: recoil 1.0 m s⁻¹.
- 2D: a 0.17 kg ball at 2.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary equal ball; afterwards they move at 30° and 60° to the
  original line: find both speeds (1.73 and 1.00 m s⁻¹; show the collision is elastic).
- Exam practice ★→★★★ (6–8): state Newton's third law and a pair [2]; F = Δp/Δt with rebound [3]; skydiver
  graph description and explanation [4]; connected bodies [4]; 1D collision with KE check (elastic or not?) [5];
  ★★★ 2D collision by components [6]; ★★★ synoptic: bullet into a block on a string (momentum then energy:
  ballistic pendulum, link [Work, energy and power](#phys.energy)) [7].
- Stretch: a rocket or a conveyor belt with mass flow (force needed to keep a belt moving as sand falls on it,
  power = Fv and why only half of it becomes kinetic energy); or show that in a 1D elastic collision, relative
  speed of approach equals relative speed of separation (from both conservation laws).
- Spot the error: momentum of a rebounding ball calculated without the change of sign.

### Pitfalls
Third-law pairs on the same body; forgetting momentum is a vector (signs!); claiming kinetic energy is
conserved in every collision; thinking a heavier object falls faster without air resistance; "terminal
velocity means zero acceleration and zero force" (zero resultant force, not zero force); using mass in N.

### Figures
Free-body diagrams (hand SVG; COURSE.md conventions): book on a table, car with driving force and drag,
skydiver at three stages. A skydiver v–t graph (hand SVG, curve rising to terminal velocity, sharp drop at the
parachute, second plateau; label the stages). A 2D collision diagram with velocity arrows and angles.

### Board box
AQA and OCR use the term "impulse" explicitly and examine force–time graph areas; Cambridge states force as rate
of change of momentum. All boards: same Newton's laws and conservation of momentum.

### Links (verified)
explore: PhET `collision-lab`, `forces-and-motion-basics`.

## phys.forces: Forces, density and pressure
`@level as` · `@paper CAIE P1 · P2 (AS)` · `@time 5–7 hours` · `Next: [Work, energy and power](#phys.energy)`
Before you start: [Dynamics and momentum](#phys.dynamics), components in [Physical quantities, units and uncertainty](#phys.units).
Warm-up: weight of a 2.5 kg mass; component of a force; F = ma (name the chapters).

### Syllabus outcomes (cover every one)
- 4.1.1 understand that the weight of an object may be taken as acting at a single point known as its centre of gravity
- 4.1.2 define and apply the moment of a force
- 4.1.3 understand that a couple is a pair of forces that acts to produce rotation only
- 4.1.4 define and apply the torque of a couple
- 4.2.1 state and apply the principle of moments
- 4.2.2 understand that, when there is no resultant force and no resultant torque, a system is in equilibrium
- 4.2.3 use a vector triangle to represent coplanar forces in equilibrium
- 4.3.1 define and use density
- 4.3.2 define and use pressure
- 4.3.3 derive, from the definitions of pressure and density, the equation for hydrostatic pressure Δp = ρgΔh
- 4.3.4 use the equation Δp = ρgΔh
- 4.3.5 understand that the upthrust acting on an object in a fluid is due to a difference in hydrostatic pressure
- 4.3.6 calculate the upthrust acting on an object in a fluid using the equation F = ρgV (Archimedes' principle)

### Sections (suggested)
1. Centre of gravity (finding it with a plumb line for a lamina; stability: tipping when the line of action of the
   weight passes outside the base).
2. Moment of a force (perpendicular distance from the pivot to the **line of action**; force at an angle:
   F × d sin θ or the component method). Units N m.
3. Couples and torque (steering wheel, tap, screwdriver; a couple has zero resultant force, so it rotates
   without translating). Torque of a couple = one force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action.
4. Principle of moments and equilibrium: both conditions (no resultant force, no resultant torque). Problems:
   seesaw, uniform beam on two supports (find both support forces: take moments about one support), crane or
   boom, a hinged sign held by a cable at an angle (vertical and horizontal hinge forces), forearm and biceps.
5. Three coplanar forces in equilibrium: the vector triangle (closed triangle, arrows head to tail); solve by
   scale drawing or by sine/cosine rule or resolving. Picture hanging on two strings; a ball on a slope held by
   a string. The three lines of action meet at one point (state it).
6. Density ρ = m/V (kg m⁻³; g cm⁻³ conversion 1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³). Pressure p = F/A (Pa = N m⁻²);
   examples: snowshoes, knife edge, stiletto heel.
7. Hydrostatic pressure: `:::proof examinable` Δp = ρgΔh from p = F/A and ρ = m/V (a column of liquid of area A
   and height Δh: weight ρAΔh g over area A). Total pressure at depth = atmospheric + ρgh. Pressure independent of
   the container's shape. Divers, dams (thicker at the base), blood pressure, manometer.
8. Upthrust: from the pressure difference between the bottom and top of a submerged block
   (`:::proof examinable`-style derivation is optional; the syllabus asks to "understand" it: show
   (p_bottom − p_top)A = ρgΔh A = ρgV). Archimedes F = ρgV (ρ of the **fluid**, V the **submerged** volume).
   Floating: upthrust = weight; fraction submerged = ρ_object/ρ_fluid; ship, hot-air balloon, hydrometer.

### Definitions (`:::def`, exam wording)
- Centre of gravity: "the point at which the whole weight of an object may be considered to act".
- Moment of a force: "the product of the force and the perpendicular distance of its line of action from the pivot".
- Couple: "a pair of equal and opposite forces whose lines of action are parallel but do not coincide; it produces rotation only".
- Torque of a couple: "the product of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the two forces".
- Principle of moments: "for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point".
- Equilibrium: "no resultant force and no resultant torque".
- Density: "mass per unit volume". Pressure: "normal force per unit area".
- Upthrust: "the upward force on an object in a fluid, caused by the difference in hydrostatic pressure between its lower and upper surfaces".

### Worked examples and question ideas
- Uniform 4.0 m beam of weight 200 N on supports at its ends, a 500 N person 1.0 m from one end: support forces
  (475 N and 225 N).
- Hinged uniform shelf or sign held by a cable at 30° to the horizontal: cable tension and hinge force (★★★).
- Picture of weight 12 N on two strings each at 40° to the horizontal: tensions by resolving and by triangle.
- Pressure 30 m under the sea (ρ = 1030 kg m⁻³, p_atm = 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa): total pressure, force on a 0.20 m²
  window.
- Block of wood 0.10 m cube floating in water: depth submerged for ρ_wood = 700 kg m⁻³; upthrust on a 2.0 kg
  steel block submerged (ρ_steel = 7800 kg m⁻³): apparent weight.
- Exam practice ★→★★★: define moment [1]; torque of a couple [2]; beam on two supports [4]; vector triangle [4];
  derive Δp = ρgΔh [3]; floating object [4]; ★★★ hinged boom with cable [7]; ★★★ object hanging from a spring
  balance lowered into water, link pressure difference and upthrust [6].
- Stretch: a ladder leaning on a smooth wall (find the friction needed at the floor); or a U-tube with two
  immiscible liquids (find the density of one from the heights).
- Spot the error: using the distance along the beam instead of the perpendicular distance for a slanted force.

### Pitfalls
Taking moments about a point and forgetting a force; perpendicular distance; using the density of the object
instead of the fluid for upthrust; forgetting atmospheric pressure (or adding it when the question asks for the
pressure "due to the water"); cm³ to m³ conversions (×10⁻⁶); a couple's torque is not 2 × F × d/2 confusion
(it is F × d either way — show both views agree).

### Figures
Beam with forces (hand SVG, pivot triangle `fm2`), couple on a steering wheel, vector triangle for three forces
(`c1` `c2` `c3`), submerged block with pressure arrows on top and bottom faces (longer at the bottom), floating
block.

### Board box
AQA and OCR use "moment" and "couple" the same way; hydrostatic pressure and upthrust appear on Edexcel IAL and
OCR, less on AQA. Content-only and hedged.

### Links (verified)
explore: PhET `balancing-act`, `density`, `buoyancy`.

## Widget to build: `phys-collide` (widgets/phys-collide.js)
A one-dimensional collision lab. Embed in phys.dynamics:
`<div class="widget" data-w="phys-collide" data-m1="2" data-m2="1" data-u1="3" data-u2="0" data-type="elastic"></div>`
- Attributes: `data-m1`, `data-m2` (kg, 0.1–10), `data-u1`, `data-u2` (m s⁻¹, −10 to 10, positive = to the right),
  `data-type="elastic|stick|explode"` (default elastic). Clamp and validate; friendly `.w-err`.
- Tabs: **Elastic**, **Stick together**, **Explosion** (explosion: both start at rest together; a slider sets the
  energy released, 0–50 J, and the trolleys fly apart with equal and opposite momenta).
- Sliders (`W.sliderNum`) for m1, m2, u1, u2.
- Physics: elastic: v1 = ((m1 − m2)u1 + 2m2u2)/(m1 + m2), v2 = ((m2 − m1)u2 + 2m1u1)/(m1 + m2); stick:
  v = (m1u1 + m2u2)/(m1 + m2); explosion: v1 = −√(2E m2/(m1(m1 + m2))), v2 = +√(2E m1/(m2(m1 + m2))).
  If the trolleys would never meet (u1 ≤ u2 with trolley 1 on the left), say so in the note and show no collision.
- Canvas (height ~200): a track with two labelled trolleys (rectangles sized by mass, colours `sc` and `sc2`),
  velocity arrows above each; a **Play** button runs the collision animation (`W.animate`; under reduced motion
  draw before and after states side by side). A **Reset** button.
- Readout (`W.readout`): total momentum before and after (kg m s⁻¹), total KE before and after (J), KE change (J
  and %), relative speed of approach and of separation. Values to 3 s.f.
- `.w-note`: "Momentum is always the same before and after. Kinetic energy is only conserved in the elastic case:
  then the relative speeds match too."
- Probe: `widget phys-collide --probe`; also `data-type="stick" data-u1="-2" data-u2="4"` (no collision) and
  `data-m1="abc"`.
