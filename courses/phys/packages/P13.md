# P13: Practical skills

Chapters, in this order: **phys.practical**, **phys.planning**. Builds widget: **phys-bestfit** (widgets/phys-bestfit.js).
Read first: COURSE.md, kit/STYLE.md, chapters/phys.dc.md (the gold chapter), then this file. Nothing else.
Papers 3 and 5 together are 23% of the AS (Paper 3) and 23% of the A Level (Papers 3 and 5, 11.5% each): these
chapters matter as much as any topic. They are skill chapters but use the full topic-chapter shape (`:::why`,
warm-up, `:::def`, worked examples with marks, try, exam practice, stretch, quiz, checklist, recap), with
realistic data tables. Every number in every table is recomputed in the check script.

### Exam facts to use (from FACTS.md; state nothing else about the papers)
- Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills: 2 hours, 40 marks, two questions each of 1 hour and 20 marks, set in different
  areas of physics, no prior knowledge of the theory needed; 23% of the AS, 11.5% of the A Level.
  Question 1: collect data, plot a graph, draw conclusions (at least 7 marks for manipulation, measurement and
  observation; 6 for presentation; 4 for analysis; 3 more spread across these). Question 2: collect data and draw
  conclusions, perhaps without a graph; the method is deliberately inaccurate and you evaluate it and suggest
  improvements (at least 5 for collecting data; 2 for recording; 10 for analysis, conclusions and evaluation; 3
  more spread across these).
- Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation: 1 hour 15 minutes, 30 marks, two compulsory 15-mark questions; 11.5% of
  the A Level. Question 1: planning (defining the problem; methods of data collection; method of analysis; additional
  detail including safety), answered with a diagram and extended writing. Question 2: analysis, conclusions and
  evaluation: given an equation and data, find a constant and estimate its uncertainty (data analysis, table of
  results, graph, conclusion, treatment of uncertainties). Contexts may be outside the syllabus.

## phys.practical: Practical skills: measurement, tables, graphs and uncertainty
`@level as` · `@paper CAIE P3 (AS) · P5` · `@time 6–8 hours` · `Next: [Planning, analysis and evaluation](#phys.planning)`
Before you start: [Physical quantities, units and uncertainty](#phys.units) (errors, uncertainty rules),
[Graphs and straight lines](#math.f.graphs). Warm-up: from Units (percentage uncertainty of 2.40 ± 0.05 cm),
Kinematics (gradient of a v–t graph), Electricity (R from V and I).

### Syllabus skills (cover every one; numbers refer to FACTS.md "Practical skills")
- P3 MMO: set up apparatus from written instructions and diagrams (including circuit diagrams); collect an appropriate quantity of data; repeat readings where appropriate; use mm scales, protractors, top-pan balances, newton meters, analogue and digital meters, measuring cylinders, calipers (vernier or digital), micrometer screw gauges, thermometers; time an appropriate number of consecutive oscillations with a stopwatch; use analogue scales and digital displays; make measurements spanning the largest possible range.
- P3 presentation: a single table with raw and calculated columns; headings "quantity / unit" (I / mA; "I (mA)" or "I in mA" also accepted; "I mA" or "mA" alone not accepted); raw readings recorded to the same precision, matching the instrument (not "2 cm" or "2.00 cm" on a mm rule); calculated values to n or n + 1 significant figures where n is the least number of s.f. in the raw data used (e.g. V to 2 s.f. and I to 4 s.f. → R to 2 or 3 s.f.).
- P3 graph: axes labelled quantity / unit; data occupy at least half the grid in both directions; false origin where appropriate; scales of 1, 2 or 5 units per 2 cm; numbered at least every 2 cm; points plotted to better than 1 mm, finely drawn (diameter under 1 mm); a best-fit straight line or smooth curve with points balanced either side along its length; one anomalous point may be ignored if circled/labelled; tangents to curves.
- P3 analysis: relate a straight line to y = mx + c and say what the gradient and intercept equal; read coordinates on the line; gradient from two points more than half the line apart; y-intercept, by substituting a point and the gradient into y = mx + c when there is a false origin.
- P3 uncertainties: estimate the absolute uncertainty of a measurement; convert absolute ↔ percentage; for repeated readings use half the range.
- P3 conclusions: find constants; decide whether data support a hypothesis (e.g. "k is constant") by calculating the percentage difference between two values of the constant and comparing it with a stated percentage uncertainty (the syllabus method); make predictions.
- P3 evaluation: identify limitations of the procedure and the most significant sources of uncertainty (state the quantity and the reason); suggest realistic improvements (other apparatus or procedures, not a different experiment), described clearly in words or a diagram.

### Sections (suggested)
1. What Paper 3 tests (the facts above, in a table) and how to work in the exam room (read the whole question, set up
   quickly, draw the table before measuring, take readings across the whole range).
2. Instruments and their resolution (table: mm rule 1 mm; vernier calipers 0.1 mm (often 0.02 mm); digital calipers
   0.01 mm; micrometer 0.01 mm; protractor 1°; stopwatch 0.01 s but human reaction ≈ 0.1–0.2 s; top-pan balance
   0.1 g or 0.01 g; thermometer 1 °C; digital meters: last digit). Reading a micrometer and vernier calipers
   (figures with a worked reading; zero error and how to correct it). Parallax; reading the bottom of a meniscus.
3. Timing oscillations: time 10 or 20 oscillations, repeat, use a fiducial (reference) marker at the centre of the
   swing; why this reduces the percentage uncertainty.
4. Tables of results: the rules above, with a correct table and a table with five errors to spot.
5. Graphs: the rules above; choosing scales (worked: data from 0.32 to 0.78 → a false origin), plotting, best-fit
   lines, anomalous points; a worked gradient and intercept with a false origin.
6. Uncertainties: absolute/percentage, half-range, combining by simple addition (from phys.units), and a full worked
   example taking a 20-oscillation timing and a length to a value of g with its uncertainty.
7. Testing a relationship: the "is k constant?" procedure with two readings: calculate k for each, the percentage
   difference ((k₁ − k₂)/average × 100%), compare with the stated percentage uncertainty (or one you estimate), and
   conclude. Also proportionality from a graph (straight line through the origin, within uncertainty).
8. Evaluation (Paper 3 Question 2): a table of classic limitations and matching improvements, each specific to the
   quantity measured (e.g. "two readings are not enough to draw a valid conclusion" → "take many readings and plot a
   graph"; "difficult to judge when the ball first hits the floor / reaction time significant compared with a 0.4 s
   fall" → "video with a timer or a light gate connected to a timer"; "parallax in reading the rule" → "fix the rule
   vertically next to the object, use a set square"; "the wire is not straight/kinked" → "use a heavier load/clamp";
   "temperature of the resistor rises" → "switch off between readings"; "drafts move the paper" → "do the experiment
   in a closed room/use a draught shield"). Stress: vague answers ("human error", "be more careful", "repeat
   readings" without saying for what) score nothing.
9. A full Paper 3 Question 1-style walk-through as a long `:::eg` (a pendulum, a spring, or a ball rolling down a
   ramp): setup, table, graph, gradient, conclusion. And a Question 2-style evaluation `:::eg`.

### Definitions (`:::def`)
Resolution (smallest change an instrument can show), zero error, anomalous result, independent / dependent / control
variable, line of best fit, false origin, percentage difference.

### Worked examples and question ideas
- Micrometer and vernier readings with zero errors.
- Period from 20 oscillations: 31.6 s, 31.2 s → T = 1.57 s ± 0.01 s (half-range 0.2 s ÷ 20).
- Is R constant? Two wires: V and I readings give R₁ and R₂; percentage difference vs a stated 5% uncertainty.
- A table with log columns is for phys.planning; here keep to raw and simple calculated columns (e.g. T² from T).
- Exam practice ★→★★★ (6–8): correct a badly headed table [2]; s.f. of a calculated column [1]; choose a scale [2];
  gradient from a given graph with false origin [3]; percentage uncertainty of a timing [2]; "do the results support
  k = constant?" [3]; ★★★ four limitations and four improvements for a described experiment [8]; ★★★ a full
  analysis of a given data set to a constant with units [6].
- Stretch: a hypothesis test where the percentage difference is close to the uncertainty (and why more readings
  help); or show that timing 20 swings instead of 1 cuts the percentage uncertainty in T by a factor 20.
- Spot the error: a gradient calculated from two data points that are not on the line (or too close together).

### Figures
A correct results table (Markdown table) and a faulty one; a micrometer and vernier scale reading (hand SVG, careful:
check every tick with Python); a graph with false origin, best-fit line, gradient triangle (large, more than half the
line), and an anomalous point circled (hand SVG or `:::plot` with `--pt` points).

AQA, OCR and Edexcel UK assess practical skills partly through a separate practical endorsement and written questions;
Edexcel IAL has written practical papers. Keep it short and factual; no paper details beyond this.

## phys.planning: Planning, analysis and evaluation
`@level a2` · `@paper CAIE P5 (A Level)` · `@time 6–8 hours` · `Next: [Paper 1 survival guide: multiple choice](#phys.exam.p1)`
Before you start: [Practical skills: measurement, tables, graphs and uncertainty](#phys.practical),
[Logarithms and exponentials](#math.p3.logs). Warm-up: from Practical (percentage uncertainty; gradient), Logarithms
(lg of 76.5; ln of e^(3x)), Capacitance or Nuclear (an exponential decay equation).

### Syllabus skills (cover every one)
- Planning: identify the independent variable, the dependent variable and the variables to be kept constant; describe how to vary the independent variable, how to measure both variables, and how to keep the others constant; a clear labelled diagram of a workable arrangement with instruments fit for purpose; describe how the data will be used to reach a conclusion, including derived quantities and what to plot; additional detail including safety (assess risks and describe precautions); describe the use of an oscilloscope (storage oscilloscope) to measure voltage, current, time and frequency; describe light gates connected to a data logger to determine time, velocity and acceleration; describe other sensors with a data logger (e.g. motion sensor).
- Data analysis: rearrange expressions into the forms y = mx + c, y = axⁿ and y = ae^(kx); use a graph of y against x to find m and c; a graph of lg y against lg x to find a and n; a graph of ln y against x to find a and k; decide what derived quantities to calculate to plot an appropriate graph.
- Table of results: Paper 3 conventions; calculated quantities; correct s.f.; logarithm columns show the unit inside the log, e.g. ln (d / cm), and the logarithm has no unit; the number of decimal places of a log equals the number of s.f. of the quantity (L / cm = 76.5 (3 s.f.) → lg (L / cm) = 1.884 (3 d.p.) or 1.8837 (4 d.p.)).
- Graph: Paper 3 conventions; error bars in both directions where appropriate; a best-fit straight line and a worst acceptable straight line (the steepest or shallowest line through all the error bars), drawn dashed or labelled.
- Conclusion: gradient and y-intercept; derive the expressions they equal; state the constant with correct units and s.f.
- Uncertainties: convert absolute ↔ fractional/percentage; absolute uncertainty beside every table value; uncertainties in derived quantities (including logs: Δ(lg x) ≈ lg(x + Δx) − lg x is acceptable as max − value); uncertainty in the gradient = gradient of best-fit line − gradient of worst acceptable line (same for the intercept); express a result as value ± uncertainty with unit.

### Sections (suggested)
1. What Paper 5 tests (facts above) and a planning checklist students can memorise (variables; method of changing
   the independent variable; measuring each variable with a named instrument; keeping each control constant and how;
   diagram; analysis: "plot … against …, the relationship is valid if the graph is a straight line through the
   origin / with gradient … , then k = …"; safety specific to the experiment; extra detail: repeat readings, initial
   trials, calibration, method to reduce a specific error).
2. Instruments for planning: oscilloscope (time-base for period/frequency, y-gain for voltage; current via the p.d.
   across a known resistor), light gates and data logger (one gate: speed from card length / time; two gates:
   acceleration), motion sensors, temperature probes, Hall probe, G-M tube and counter, calipers/micrometer.
3. A full planning `:::eg` with a model answer built up mark point by mark point (contexts outside the syllabus, e.g.
   "how the frequency of a vibrating ruler depends on its overhanging length", "how the resistance of a strip of
   conducting putty depends on its length and width", "how the terminal speed of a sphere in oil depends on its
   radius"). Then a second shorter planning question in `:::try`.
4. Linearising: y = mx + c directly; power laws y = axⁿ → lg y = n lg x + lg a (gradient n, intercept lg a); exponentials
   y = ae^(kx) → ln y = kx + ln a (gradient k, intercept ln a); worked conversions for physics equations
   (T = 2π√(l/g) → T² against l; V = V₀e^(−t/RC) → ln V against t; I = kV^n for a lamp → lg I against lg V).
5. Log tables: the unit and decimal-place rules with examples; uncertainties in log values.
6. Error bars and the worst acceptable line: drawing them, finding the gradient uncertainty; intercept uncertainty
   (including using a point and the gradient when there is a false origin). Embed **phys-bestfit**.
7. A full Question 2-style `:::eg`: given an equation (e.g. a capacitor discharge or a cantilever oscillation) and a
   table of raw data with uncertainties, complete the table, plot, find gradient ± uncertainty, derive the constant,
   find its percentage uncertainty, and state it to sensible s.f. Every number checked.

### Definitions (`:::def`)
Independent variable, dependent variable, control variable, error bar, worst acceptable line, fractional uncertainty.

### Worked examples and question ideas
- The full planning and analysis examples above.
- Linearise: P = kv³ (lg P against lg v, gradient 3); A = A₀e^(−λt); f = (1/2L)√(T/μ) (f against √T).
- Gradient uncertainty: best fit 2.48, worst 2.61 → ± 0.13 (5.2%).
- Exam practice ★→★★★ (6–8): identify variables for a stated plan [2]; choose what to plot for y = axⁿ [2]; lg column
  to correct d.p. [2]; uncertainty in a log value [2]; gradient and its uncertainty from a given graph [4]; derive a
  constant from a gradient with its percentage uncertainty [4]; ★★★ a full planning question with a mark-point answer
  [15]; ★★★ a full analysis question [15].
- Stretch: a data set where the intercept, not the gradient, gives the constant, with a false origin and its
  uncertainty; or design a test of y = axⁿ that distinguishes n = 2 from n = 1.5 given the uncertainties.
- Spot the error: a worst acceptable line that misses one error bar, or lg values with a unit.

### Figures
Graph with error bars, best-fit line (solid `c1`) and worst acceptable line (dashed `c2 dash`), gradient triangles
(hand SVG with coordinates checked in Python). Planning diagrams: e.g. a light-gate setup and an oscilloscope screen.

but the Cambridge Paper 5 format (one planning, one analysis question) is distinctive. Keep it short.

## Widget to build: `phys-bestfit` (widgets/phys-bestfit.js)
Embed in phys.planning (and optionally phys.practical): `<div class="widget" data-w="phys-bestfit" data-preset="pendulum"></div>`.
- An editable table (5–8 rows) of x, y and Δy (and optional Δx); presets: `pendulum` (l / m and T / s data → plot T²
  against l), `discharge` (t / s and V / V → ln (V / V) against t), `power` (x and y with y = axⁿ → lg y against lg x),
  `blank`. Data in `data-x`, `data-y`, `data-dy` as comma lists override presets.
- Transform tabs: **y against x**, **lg y against lg x**, **ln y against x** (and **y² against x** for the pendulum
  preset); transformed error bars from the max/min values (y ± Δy transformed).
- Canvas: points with error bars; least-squares best-fit line (`sc`), worst acceptable line (`sc2`, dashed) = the
  steeper or shallower of the two lines through the extreme ends of the first and last error bars that still passes
  through every error bar (if neither does, say so); axis labels follow the quantity / unit convention.
- Readout: gradient ± uncertainty (best − worst), intercept ± uncertainty, percentage uncertainties, and for the
  presets the derived constant (g = 4π²/gradient for the pendulum; RC = −1/gradient; n and a for the power law).
- Friendly errors for non-numbers, too few points (< 3), and logs of non-positive values.
