# P18: Mock papers: A Level (Papers 4 and 5)

Chapters, in this order: **phys.mock.p4**, **phys.mock.p5**. No widget to build.
Read first: COURSE.md, kit/STYLE.md ("Mock papers" and "Syntax"), chapters/phys.dc.md (the gold chapter), then this
file. Nothing else, except the greps this file asks for.
These are mock papers (`@level mock`): a full timed paper with the real paper's time, total marks, number and style of
questions. They are not topic chapters: no `:::why`, warm-up, `:::def`, `:::quiz`, `:::checklist` or `:::recap`.

## Rules for both mocks

### Originality
Every question is **original**, written in Cambridge 9702 style and mark-scheme language. Never copy or paraphrase a
real past-paper question, and **never repeat the course's own worked examples or practice questions**. Before you
write a question, grep the chapter it revises for its contexts and numbers and choose a different context or
different numbers:
`grep -n ":::eg\|^Q:" chapters/<id>.md | cut -c1-140`. For phys.mock.p4 also grep the synoptic demonstrations in
chapters/phys.exam.p24.md and chapters/phys.mixed.a2.md; for phys.mock.p5 grep chapters/phys.planning.md (it already
uses: steel sphere in oil, conducting putty, filament lamp \(I = kV^n\), capacitor discharge, pendulum, wind turbine,
flow rate \(Q = kr^n\), stretched string, bouncing ball). Exam facts only from the facts below.

### File shape
```
@chapter phys.mock.p4

@level mock
@paper CAIE 9702 Paper 4

:::exam Instructions

(b) … (2 marks)
A: (a) … [B1]

(b) … [C1] … [A1]
:::

… one bare :::try per question …

Next: …
```
- The title in `@title` must match course.json exactly.
- One **bare** `:::try` per question (no title): the page numbers them. Each starts `Q: [n marks] (#chapter-to-revise)`
  with the one chapter a reader who drops marks should revise (for a synoptic question, the chapter of its main
  topic; name the AS chapter in a note after the marks: `(also revise [Kinematics](#phys.kinematics))`). No ★
  ratings, no `H:` hints.
- Parts (a), (b), (c)(i), … each start a new line **with a blank line before it**, in the `Q:` and in the `A:`. End each
  part of the question with its marks in round brackets, `(2 marks)`; `[n marks]` at the start is the total.
- `A:` is the full mark scheme (COURSE.md "Mark-scheme style"): C1 on the formula/substitution line of each
  calculation, A1 on the answer with unit and sensible s.f., B1 for each independent statement, reading or reason,
  M1 only in "show that" and derivations. Accepted alternatives in a note after the marks: `[A1] (allow 2.0 to 2.1)`.
  `check` adds the marks in each answer and fails when they differ from `[n marks]`.
- Definitions use the wording of the course's `:::def` cards (grep the chapter: `grep -n -A2 ":::def <Term>" chapters/<id>.md`).
- Figures: hand-drawn SVG in `:::html` following COURSE.md's figure rules and the gold chapter's patterns; graphs of
  functions with `:::plot`; data as Markdown tables with units in the headings. Everything fits 375 px (`phone`).

### Checks file (required)
`checks/<id>.py` recomputes **every number** in the paper: each given value derived from others, every answer in
every mark scheme, every accepted range (for Paper 5, the least-squares gradient, the best-fit and worst acceptable
gradients from the stated points, the uncertainties). It must also assert the marks: a list of marks per question,
and per part, whose sum equals the paper total (Paper 4: 12 questions, total 100; Paper 5: 2 questions of 15, total 30).

### Exam facts you may use (FACTS.md)
- Paper 4: A Level Structured Questions, 2 h, 100 marks, A Level content (topics 12–25); AS knowledge required;
  38.5% of A Level; AO1 and AO2 50% each; data and formulae on pages 2 and 3. Constants: COURSE.md "Data values and
  conventions". Page 3 formulae (given to candidates, so questions need not ask for them to be recalled): φ = −GM/r,
  E_P = −GMm/r, p = ⅓(Nm/V)⟨c²⟩, a = −ω²x, v = v₀ cos ωt, v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²), V = Q/(4πε₀r), E_P = Qq/(4πε₀r),
  capacitors in series and parallel, x = x₀e^(−t/RC), V_H = BI/(ntq), x = x₀ sin ωt, x = x₀e^(−λt), λ = 0.693/t½,
  I_R/I₀ = (Z₁ − Z₂)²/(Z₁ + Z₂)², L = 4πσr²T⁴, Δλ/λ ≈ Δf/f ≈ v/c.
- Paper 5: Planning, Analysis and Evaluation, 1 h 15 min, 30 marks, written; two compulsory 15-mark questions; 11.5%
  of A Level; AO3 100%. Question 1: planning (defining the problem, methods of data collection, method of analysis,
  additional detail including safety). Question 2: analysis, conclusions and evaluation (data analysis, table of
  results, graph, conclusion, treatment of uncertainties): given an equation and data, find a constant and estimate
  its uncertainty. Contexts may be outside the syllabus; all needed information is given.
- The number of structured questions in the real Paper 4 is not in FACTS.md: this mock uses **12 questions**, one per
  main A Level topic area, and the instructions box does not claim the real paper always has 12.

### `:::exam Instructions` box

### Question 1 (15 marks): planning (revise phys.planning)
Default context (new to the course): a hacksaw blade is clamped horizontally at one end with a small mass fixed at the
free end; it is suggested that the frequency f of its vertical oscillations depends on the free length L as
\(f = kL^n\), where k and n are constants. "Plan a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between f and L.
Draw a diagram showing the arrangement of your equipment. Explain how the results could be used to determine k and n."
Give an SVG of the clamped blade (unlabelled, as a starting picture) only if it does not give the plan away; the
reader draws their own labelled diagram.
Mark scheme, matching the headings of the planning chapter (one B1 per point, the answer lists more points than marks
where Cambridge does, "any n from"):
| Heading | Marks | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Defining the problem | 3 | independent L (1); dependent f (1); keep constant: the mass at the end, the same blade, clamp tightness (1) |
| Methods of data collection | 6 | labelled diagram with clamp, blade, bench and rule (1); vary L by moving the blade in the clamp, measure L with a metre rule from clamp edge to mass (1); measure f: time 20 oscillations with a stopwatch, T = t/20, f = 1/T, or a motion sensor / video with frame count (1); at least five lengths over a stated range (1); how to start the oscillation with a small, repeatable displacement (1); check the oscillations are vertical and the clamp does not slip (1) |
| Method of analysis | 3 | plot lg f against lg L (1); relationship valid if a straight line (1); n = gradient, k = 10^(y-intercept) (1) |
| Additional detail including safety | 3 | specific safety: the blade's teeth or a snapping blade: tape the teeth / wear eye protection / clamp the bench (1); repeat timings and average (1); one further detail from: fiducial marker at the centre of the oscillation, trial run, small amplitudes, avoid a draught (1) |
Total 3 + 6 + 3 + 3 = 15. Model the answer's layout on the planning chapter's worked plan (bold headings, one point per
bullet with its mark), but in a different context and with different wording.

### Question 2 (15 marks): analysis, conclusions and evaluation (revise phys.planning)
Default context (new to the course): a ball rolls from rest down a ramp of fixed length s at angle θ; the time t is
measured. It is suggested that \(t^2 = \dfrac{14s}{5g}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\). s is given with its uncertainty
(e.g. \(s = 1.200 \pm 0.005\,\mathrm{m}\)). Supply a table of six angles (about 5° to 30°) with t and its absolute
uncertainty (e.g. ±0.05 s), and an SVG of the ramp. Generate t from a chosen g with small realistic scatter; record
the generator values in the checks file.
| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | a graph of t² against 1/sin θ is plotted: express the gradient in terms of s and g | 1 (B1) |
| (b) | complete the table: 1/sin θ and t² with the absolute uncertainty in t² (doubled percentage) | 2 (B1 values, B1 uncertainties) |
| (c)(i) | plot the points with error bars for t² | 2 (B1 points, B1 error bars) |
| (c)(ii) | draw the best-fit line and the worst acceptable line, both labelled | 2 (B1 B1) |
| (c)(iii) | gradient of the best-fit line with its absolute uncertainty (difference from the worst acceptable gradient) | 2 (C1 A1) |
| (d)(i) | value of g from the gradient, with unit | 2 (C1 A1) |
| (d)(ii) | percentage uncertainty in g, combining gradient and s | 2 (C1 A1) |
| (e) | t predicted for θ = a new angle, with its absolute uncertainty | 2 (C1 A1) |
Total 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 15. Paper total 30. The answer states accepted ranges for every graph-derived
value (computed in the checks file from the best-fit and steepest/shallowest worst lines through the error bars).
