(a) Define gravitational field strength. (1 mark)

(b) \(\dagger\) Show that for a circular orbit \(T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}\,r^3\). (2 marks)

(c) The moon has \(r = 4.20 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}\) and \(T = 6.30\) days. Calculate \(M\). (2 marks)

(d) A satellite is to orbit with a period equal to the rotation period of the planet. Calculate the radius of its orbit. (2 marks)

(e) The satellite of mass 640 kg is moved from the orbit in (d) to a higher circular orbit of radius \(1.20 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}\). Calculate the change in its gravitational potential energy. (2 marks)
A: (a) The gravitational force per unit mass acting on a small test mass placed at that point. [B1] (allow \(g = F/m\) with symbols defined)

(b) The gravitational force provides the centripetal force: \(\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = mr\omega^2\) [M1]

With \(\omega = 2\pi/T\): \(\dfrac{GM}{r^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}\), so \(T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}\,r^3\). [A1]

(c) \(T = 6.30 \times 24 \times 3600 = 5.44 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{s}\), and \(M = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GT^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 (4.20 \times 10^{8})^3}{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times (5.44 \times 10^{5})^2}\) [C1]

\(M = 1.48 \times 10^{26}\,\mathrm{kg}\) [A1]

(d) \(T = 14.0 \times 3600 = 5.04 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s}\), and \(r^3 = \dfrac{GMT^2}{4\pi^2}\) [C1]

\(r = 8.60 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}\) [A1] (allow \(8.5\) to \(8.7 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}\))

(e) \(\Delta E_P = GMm\left(\dfrac{1}{r_1} - \dfrac{1}{r_2}\right) = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 1.48 \times 10^{26} \times 640 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{8.60 \times 10^{7}} - \dfrac{1}{1.20 \times 10^{8}}\right)\) [C1]

\(\Delta E_P = +2.1 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}\) (an increase) [A1] (allow error carried forward from (c) and (d))

Q: [7 marks] (#phys.temperature) A student uses an electric heater to find thermal properties of water. The heater is in an insulated beaker.

(a) Define specific latent heat of vaporisation. (1 mark)

(b) A 60 W heater warms 0.250 kg of water from \(20.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) to \(34.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) in 245 s. Assume that all the energy goes to the water. Calculate the specific heat capacity of water. (2 marks)

(c) The water is now heated to boiling. The student records the mass of water that boils away in 300 s at each of two powers.

| Power of heater / W | Time / s | Mass boiled away / g |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 300 | 6.4 |
| 90 | 300 | 10.3 |

Use the table to calculate the specific latent heat of vaporisation of water. (2 marks)

(d) Explain why using two powers removes the effect of energy lost to the surroundings. (2 marks)
A: (a) The energy required per unit mass to change a substance from liquid to gas without any change in temperature. [B1]

(b) \(c = \dfrac{Pt}{m\Delta\theta} = \dfrac{60 \times 245}{0.250 \times 14.0}\) [C1]

\(c = 4200\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) [A1]

(c) \((P_2 - P_1)\,t = (m_2 - m_1)\,L\), so \(L = \dfrac{(90 - 60) \times 300}{(10.3 - 6.4) \times 10^{-3}}\) [C1]

\(L = 2.3 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}\) [A1] (allow \(2.2\) to \(2.4 \times 10^{6}\))

(d) The water is at the same temperature (\(100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\)) in both runs, so energy is lost to the surroundings at the same rate. [B1]

Over the same time the same energy is lost, so it cancels when the energy supplied at one power is subtracted from the energy supplied at the other. [B1]

Q: [8 marks] (#phys.astro) A star has a luminosity of \(4.0 \times 10^{27}\,\mathrm{W}\). The wavelength at which its spectrum has maximum intensity is 450 nm. Wien's law is \(\lambda_{max}T = 2.90 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,K}\).

(a) Define luminosity. (1 mark)

(b) Calculate the surface temperature of the star. (2 marks)

(c) Calculate the radius of the star. (2 marks)

(d) In the light from a galaxy the calcium K line has a wavelength of 393.4 nm in the laboratory but is observed at 398.6 nm. Calculate the speed at which the galaxy is receding, and its distance from us, taking the Hubble constant to be \(70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\). (2 marks)

(e) State what Hubble's law suggests about the universe. (1 mark)
A: (a) The total power of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a star. [B1]

(b) \(T = \dfrac{2.90 \times 10^{-3}}{450 \times 10^{-9}}\) [C1]

\(T = 6.44 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{K}\) [A1]

(c) \(L = 4\pi\sigma r^2 T^4\), so \(r = \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{4\pi\sigma T^4}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{4.0 \times 10^{27}}{4\pi \times 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times (6.44 \times 10^{3})^4}}\) [C1]
