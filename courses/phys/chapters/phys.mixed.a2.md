Paper 4 does not come with the topic named on the front. A question on a satellite needs circular motion, gravitation and energy, and one on a generator needs induction and a.c. together with the resistor it drives. This page gives you that mix: five sets of structured questions in the style of Paper 4, covering every A Level topic and calling on AS knowledge as the real paper does. They are practice, not a mock paper: there is no fixed total and no clock until you choose to use one.

:::tip How to use this page
Do each set in one sitting, without notes, at about 1.2 minutes per mark (a 9-mark question gets about 11 minutes). Then mark yourself honestly against the scheme: a mark needs the idea or the working shown, not just the right final number. For every mark you lose, open the chapter named under the answer and read that section again. Questions that cross two or more chapters end their mark scheme with an "Also revise" line. Questions give the values that are not on the data sheet. For the rest use \(G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{2}\,kg^{-2}}\), \(h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\), \(c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\), \(k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\), \(R = 8.31\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\), \(N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\), \(1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\) and \(g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Carry the unrounded value from one part to the next and give each answer to the number of significant figures of the data.

Q: [8 marks] (#phys.circular) ★★ A car of mass 1100 kg goes round a flat circular bend of radius 45 m at a constant speed of \(12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). (a) Calculate the angular speed of the car about the centre of the bend. (b) Calculate the resultant force on the car. (c) State what provides this force and give its direction. (d) The friction between the tyres and the road can provide a force of at most 0.60 times the weight of the car. Calculate the greatest speed at which the car can take the bend without skidding.
A: (a) \(\omega = \dfrac{v}{r} = \dfrac{12}{45}\) [C1] \(= 0.27\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(b) \(F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{1100 \times 12^2}{45}\) [C1] \(= 3.5 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}\). [A1]
(c) The force is the friction between the tyres and the road. [B1] It acts horizontally, towards the centre of the circle. [B1]
(d) At the limit, \(0.60\,mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\) [C1] so \(v = \sqrt{0.60 \times 9.81 \times 45} = 16\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1] The mass cancels, so the limit does not depend on how heavily the car is loaded.
Also revise: [Dynamics and momentum](#phys.dynamics).
Q: [11 marks] (#phys.gravity) ★★★ A satellite of mass 850 kg is in a circular orbit 400 km above the surface of the Earth. Take the mass of the Earth to be \(5.97 \times 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}\) and its radius to be \(6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}\). (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the satellite. (b) Calculate the period of the orbit. (c) Calculate the kinetic energy of the satellite and its gravitational potential energy. (d) The satellite is moved to a geostationary orbit of radius \(4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}\). Calculate the minimum energy that must be supplied to the satellite for this change.
H: The gravitational force provides the centripetal force. The mass of the satellite cancels in part (a).
H: Potential energy is negative, with zero at infinity: \(E_P = -GMm/r\).
H: For any circular orbit the total energy is \(E_K + E_P\). The energy supplied is the difference between the total energies of the two orbits.
A: (a) The gravitational force provides the centripetal force: \(\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\). [B1] The orbit radius is \(r = 6.37 \times 10^{6} + 4.0 \times 10^{5} = 6.77 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}\). Then \(v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.97 \times 10^{24}}{6.77 \times 10^{6}}}\) [C1] \(= 7.7 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(b) \(T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 6.77 \times 10^{6}}{7.67 \times 10^{3}}\) [C1] \(= 5.5 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}\) (92 minutes). [A1]
(c) \(E_K = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \tfrac12 \times 850 \times (7.67 \times 10^{3})^2\) [C1] \(= 2.5 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1] \(E_P = -\dfrac{GMm}{r} = -5.0 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(d) The total energy of a circular orbit is \(E = -\dfrac{GMm}{2r}\), which is half the potential energy. So the new total energy is \(-\dfrac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.97 \times 10^{24} \times 850}{2 \times 4.22 \times 10^{7}} = -4.0 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}\). [C1] The original total energy is \(-2.5 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}\). [C1] The energy to be supplied is \(-4.0 \times 10^{9} - (-2.5 \times 10^{10}) = 2.1 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
Also revise: [Circular motion](#phys.circular) and [Work, energy and power](#phys.energy).
Q: [9 marks] (#phys.oscillations) ★★★ A mass of 0.40 kg hangs from a spring of spring constant \(64\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\). It is pulled down and released, and then oscillates vertically with simple harmonic motion of amplitude 5.0 cm. (a) Show that the period of the oscillation is 0.50 s. (b) Calculate the maximum speed of the mass. (c) Calculate the total energy of the oscillation. (d) The top end of the spring is now driven up and down with a small amplitude, and the driving frequency is slowly increased from 0.5 Hz to 4.0 Hz. Describe how the amplitude of the oscillation of the mass changes, and explain what happens to the amplitude at resonance if a light card is added to the mass so that air resistance increases.
H: The angular frequency of a mass on a spring is \(\omega = \sqrt{k/m}\), and the maximum speed is \(\omega x_0\).
H: The total energy is \(\tfrac12 m\omega^2 x_0^2\), which also equals the elastic energy \(\tfrac12 kx_0^2\) at maximum displacement.
A: (a) \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{0.40}{64}}\) [C1] \(= 0.497\,\mathrm{s} = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}\). [A1]
(b) \(\omega = \sqrt{k/m} = 12.6\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\) and \(v_0 = \omega x_0 = 12.6 \times 0.050\) [C1] \(= 0.63\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(c) \(E = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 x_0^2 = \tfrac12 \times 0.40 \times 12.6^2 \times 0.050^2\) [C1] \(= 0.080\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(d) The amplitude increases as the driving frequency approaches the natural frequency of 2.0 Hz, is a maximum at about 2.0 Hz (resonance), and then decreases again. [B1] At resonance the card causes more damping, so the peak amplitude is smaller. [B1] The resonance peak is also broader and less sharp. [B1]
Also revise: [Dynamics and momentum](#phys.dynamics) and [Deformation of solids](#phys.solids).

Q: [8 marks] (#phys.temperature) ★★ A kettle contains 1.5 kg of water at \(20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\). The kettle has a power rating of 2.2 kW and 90% of this power heats the water. The specific heat capacity of water is \(4200\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\). (a) Calculate the energy needed to bring the water to \(100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\). (b) Calculate the time taken. (c) The kettle fails to switch off and boils for a further 120 s. The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is \(2.26 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}\). Calculate the mass of water that boils away. (d) Explain why the temperature of the water stays at \(100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) while it boils.
A: (a) \(Q = mc\Delta\theta = 1.5 \times 4200 \times 80\) [C1] \(= 5.0 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(b) The useful power is \(0.90 \times 2200 = 1980\,\mathrm{W}\), so \(t = \dfrac{5.04 \times 10^{5}}{1980}\) [C1] \(= 2.5 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{s}\). [A1]
(c) The useful energy supplied is \(1980 \times 120 = 2.38 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}\) and \(m = \dfrac{E}{L} = \dfrac{2.376 \times 10^{5}}{2.26 \times 10^{6}}\) [C1] \(= 0.11\,\mathrm{kg}\). [A1]
(d) The energy supplied is used to separate the molecules against the attraction between them, so it increases their potential energy. [B1] The mean kinetic energy of the molecules, which sets the temperature, does not change. [B1]
Also revise: [Electricity](#phys.electricity).
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.gases) ★★ A sealed cylinder holds helium gas, which you can treat as an ideal gas, at a pressure of \(1.5 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}\) and a thermodynamic temperature of 300 K. The volume of the gas is \(2.4 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{3}}\). A helium atom has a mass of 4.0 u. (a) Calculate the amount of gas in the cylinder. (b) Calculate the number of atoms. (c) Calculate the mean kinetic energy of one atom. (d) Calculate the root-mean-square speed of the atoms.
A: (a) \(n = \dfrac{pV}{RT} = \dfrac{1.5 \times 10^{5} \times 2.4 \times 10^{-3}}{8.31 \times 300}\) [C1] \(= 0.14\,\mathrm{mol}\). [A1]
(b) \(N = nN_A = 0.1444 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}\) [C1] \(= 8.7 \times 10^{22}\). [A1]
(c) \(\bar{E}_K = \tfrac32 kT = \tfrac32 \times 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 300\) [C1] \(= 6.2 \times 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(d) \(\tfrac12 m\langle c^2\rangle = \tfrac32 kT\), so \(c_{\mathrm{r.m.s.}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3kT}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3 \times 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 300}{4.0 \times 1.66 \times 10^{-27}}}\) [C1] \(= 1.4 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.thermo) ★★★ An ideal monatomic gas is at a pressure of \(1.0 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}\), a volume of \(2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{3}}\) and a temperature of 300 K. It is heated at constant pressure and expands to a volume of \(3.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{3}}\). During this change 250 J of energy is transferred to the gas by heating. (a) Calculate the work done by the gas. (b) Calculate the increase in the internal energy of the gas. (c) Calculate the final thermodynamic temperature of the gas. (d) Show that your answer to (b) is consistent with the gas being monatomic, using \(U = \tfrac32 nRT\) for the internal energy.
H: Use the first law \(\Delta U = q + W\), where \(W\) is the work done on the gas. A gas that expands does work on its surroundings, so \(W\) is negative.
H: At constant pressure \(V/T\) is constant. For part (d) first find the amount of gas from \(pV = nRT\).
A: (a) \(W = p\Delta V = 1.0 \times 10^{5} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\) [C1] \(= 100\,\mathrm{J}\) of work done by the gas. [A1]
(b) The work done on the gas is \(-100\,\mathrm{J}\), so \(\Delta U = q + W = 250 - 100\) [C1] \(= 150\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(c) \(\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}\), so \(T_2 = 300 \times \dfrac{3.0}{2.0}\) [C1] \(= 450\,\mathrm{K}\). [A1]
(d) \(n = \dfrac{pV_1}{RT_1} = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{5} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-3}}{8.31 \times 300} = 0.0802\,\mathrm{mol}\) and the temperature rise is 150 K, so \(\Delta U = \tfrac32 nR\Delta T = \tfrac32 \times 0.0802 \times 8.31 \times 150\) [C1] \(= 150\,\mathrm{J}\), the same as part (b). [A1]
Also revise: [Ideal gases](#phys.gases).

Q: [11 marks] (#phys.magnetic) ★★★ A proton is released from rest and accelerated through a potential difference of 1500 V in a vacuum. It then enters a region of uniform magnetic field of flux density 0.080 T, travelling at right angles to the field. The field is directed into the page and the proton is moving from left to right across the page. The mass of a proton is \(1.67 \times 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}\). (a) Calculate the speed of the proton as it enters the magnetic field. (b) State the direction in which the proton is first deflected. (c) Calculate the radius of the circular path in the magnetic field. (d) Calculate the time taken for one complete revolution. (e) An electron is accelerated through the same potential difference and enters the same field in the same way. State and explain how the radius of its path compares with that of the proton.
H: The electric force does work \(eV\) on the proton, and this becomes kinetic energy.
H: In the magnetic field the magnetic force provides the centripetal force: \(Bev = mv^2/r\).
H: For part (e) write \(r\) in terms of \(m\), \(V\), \(B\) and \(e\) only, so that the speed has gone.
A: (a) \(eV = \tfrac12 mv^2\) [C1] so \(v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 1500}{1.67 \times 10^{-27}}} = 5.4 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(b) By Fleming's left-hand rule (the current is the same way as the motion of the proton) the force is upwards, so the proton is deflected towards the top of the page. [B1]
(c) The magnetic force is the centripetal force: \(Bev = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\) [C1] so \(r = \dfrac{mv}{Be}\) [C1] \(= \dfrac{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 5.36 \times 10^{5}}{0.080 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 7.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1]
(d) \(T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v} = \dfrac{2\pi m}{Be}\) [C1] \(= \dfrac{2\pi \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27}}{0.080 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 8.2 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}\). [A1]
(e) Since \(eV = \tfrac12 mv^2\), the momentum is \(mv = \sqrt{2meV}\), so \(r = \dfrac{\sqrt{2meV}}{Be}\) and \(r \propto \sqrt{m}\) for the same \(V\), \(B\) and \(e\). [B1] The electron has a much smaller mass, so its radius is smaller, by a factor of \(\sqrt{9.11 \times 10^{-31}/1.67 \times 10^{-27}} = 0.023\). [B1] It is negatively charged, so it is deflected the opposite way and goes round the circle in the opposite sense. [B1]
Also revise: [Electric fields](#phys.efields) and [Motion in a circle](#phys.circular).
Q: [9 marks] (#phys.capacitance) ★★★ A supercapacitor of capacitance 1.0 F is charged to 12 V. It is then discharged through a heating coil of resistance \(10\,\Omega\) that is immersed in 25 g of water. The specific heat capacity of water is \(4200\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\). (a) Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor. (b) Assume that all this energy heats the water. Calculate the rise in temperature of the water. (c) Calculate the time constant of the discharge and the initial current in the coil. (d) Calculate the time taken for the p.d. across the capacitor to fall to 3.0 V.
H: The energy stored is \(\tfrac12 CV^2\), and the thermal energy gained by the water is \(mc\Delta\theta\).
H: A discharge follows \(V = V_0 e^{-t/RC}\). Rearrange it with a natural logarithm.
A: (a) \(W = \tfrac12 CV^2 = \tfrac12 \times 1.0 \times 12^2\) [C1] \(= 72\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(b) \(\Delta\theta = \dfrac{E}{mc} = \dfrac{72}{0.025 \times 4200}\) [C1] \(= 0.69\,\mathrm{K}\). [A1]
(c) \(\tau = RC = 10 \times 1.0 = 10\,\mathrm{s}\) [A1] and \(I_0 = \dfrac{V_0}{R} = \dfrac{12}{10} = 1.2\,\mathrm{A}\). [A1]
(d) \(3.0 = 12\,e^{-t/10}\) [C1] so \(t = 10\ln\dfrac{12}{3.0}\) [C1] \(= 14\,\mathrm{s}\). [A1]
Also revise: [Temperature](#phys.temperature).
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.magnetic) ★★ A Hall probe contains a thin slice of semiconductor of thickness \(2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}\) and width \(4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}\). The number density of charge carriers is \(2.0 \times 10^{22}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}\). A current of 5.0 mA passes along the slice, which is in a uniform magnetic field of flux density 0.15 T at right angles to the slice. (a) Calculate the Hall voltage. (b) A strip of copper of the same thickness, with \(8.5 \times 10^{28}\) free electrons per cubic metre, carries the same current in the same field. Calculate its Hall voltage and explain why a semiconductor is used in a Hall probe. (c) Calculate the drift speed of the charge carriers in the semiconductor. (d) Show that, in the steady state, the magnetic force on a charge carrier equals the force from the Hall electric field.
A: (a) \(V_H = \dfrac{BI}{ntq} = \dfrac{0.15 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-3}}{2.0 \times 10^{22} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-4} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}}\) [C1] \(= 1.2 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1]
(b) \(V_H = 2.8 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{V}\) for copper. [A1] The semiconductor has far fewer charge carriers per unit volume, so its Hall voltage is far larger and easy to measure. [B1]
(c) The cross-sectional area is \(A = 4.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-4} = 8.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^{2}}\) and \(v = \dfrac{I}{nAq}\) [C1]
\[v = \dfrac{5.0 \times 10^{-3}}{2.0 \times 10^{22} \times 8.0 \times 10^{-7} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] [A1]
(d) The magnetic force is \(Bqv = 0.15 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 1.95 = 4.7 \times 10^{-20}\,\mathrm{N}\) and the electric force is \(qE_H = \dfrac{qV_H}{w} = \dfrac{1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 1.17 \times 10^{-3}}{4.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 4.7 \times 10^{-20}\,\mathrm{N}\). [C1] The two forces are equal and opposite, so the carriers are no longer deflected and the Hall voltage stays steady. [B1]
Q: [10 marks] (#phys.induction) ★★★ A simple generator has a coil of 250 turns, each of area \(4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{2}}\). The coil rotates at 25 revolutions per second in a uniform magnetic field of flux density 0.20 T. The coil is connected to a resistor of resistance \(15\,\Omega\). (a) Calculate the maximum magnetic flux linkage of the coil. (b) Calculate the peak e.m.f. induced. (c) Calculate the r.m.s. e.m.f., and the mean power dissipated in the resistor. (d) The coil is now rotated at half the frequency. State the new peak e.m.f. and the new mean power.
H: The maximum flux linkage is \(NBA\), when the plane of the coil is at right angles to the field.
H: The e.m.f. is the rate of change of flux linkage, so \(E_0 = NBA\omega\).
H: The mean power in a resistor is \(V_{\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^2/R\), or half of \(V_0^2/R\).
A: (a) \(N\Phi = NBA = 250 \times 0.20 \times 4.0 \times 10^{-3}\) [C1] \(= 0.20\,\mathrm{Wb}\). [A1]
(b) \(\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 25 = 157\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\) and \(E_0 = NBA\omega = 0.20 \times 157\) [C1] \(= 31\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1]
(c) \(E_{\mathrm{r.m.s.}} = \dfrac{E_0}{\sqrt2} = \dfrac{31.4}{\sqrt2}\) [C1] \(= 22\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1] \(P = \dfrac{E_{\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^2}{R} = \dfrac{22.2^2}{15}\) [C1] \(= 33\,\mathrm{W}\). [A1]
(d) The peak e.m.f. is proportional to \(\omega\), so it halves to 16 V. [B1] The mean power is proportional to the square of the e.m.f., so it falls to a quarter, 8.2 W. [B1]
Also revise: [Alternating currents](#phys.ac).
Q: [9 marks] (#phys.ac) ★★ A 230 V r.m.s. mains supply feeds the primary coil of a transformer, which has 920 turns. The secondary coil delivers 12 V r.m.s. The secondary output goes to a bridge rectifier and then to a smoothing capacitor of capacitance \(2200\,\mu\mathrm{F}\) in parallel with a load of resistance \(24\,\Omega\). The mains frequency is 50 Hz. (a) Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. (b) Calculate the peak p.d. across the secondary coil. (c) Explain why the output of the bridge rectifier has a frequency of 100 Hz. (d) The capacitor discharges through the load for 0.010 s between successive peaks. Calculate the ripple, the fall in p.d. across the load in this time. (e) State one change that reduces the ripple.
A: (a) \(\dfrac{N_s}{N_p} = \dfrac{V_s}{V_p}\) [C1] so \(N_s = 920 \times \dfrac{12}{230} = 48\) turns. [A1]
(b) \(V_0 = \sqrt2 \times V_{\mathrm{r.m.s.}} = \sqrt2 \times 12\) [C1] \(= 17\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1]
(c) A bridge rectifier gives full-wave rectification: both the positive and the negative half-cycles produce current in the same direction through the load. [B1] There are two peaks in each cycle of the 50 Hz supply, so the frequency is 100 Hz. [B1]
(d) \(RC = 24 \times 2200 \times 10^{-6} = 0.0528\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(V = V_0 e^{-t/RC} = 17.0\,e^{-0.010/0.0528}\) [C1] \(= 14.0\,\mathrm{V}\), so the ripple is \(16.97 - 14.04 = 2.9\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1]
(e) Increase the capacitance of the smoothing capacitor (or use a larger load resistance), which increases the time constant \(RC\). [B1]
Also revise: [Electromagnetic induction](#phys.induction).

Q: [9 marks] (#phys.quantum) ★★ Ultraviolet radiation of wavelength 250 nm shines on a clean metal surface. The work function of the metal is 4.2 eV. (a) Calculate the energy of one photon of the radiation. (b) Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons and the stopping potential. (c) Calculate the threshold wavelength of the metal. (d) The intensity of the radiation is doubled without changing its wavelength. State what happens to the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons and to the photoelectric current, giving a reason for each.
A: (a) \(E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.00 \times 10^{8}}{250 \times 10^{-9}}\) [C1] \(= 8.0 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1]
(b) \(\Phi = 4.2 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} = 6.72 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}\) and \(E_{K(\max)} = hf - \Phi = 7.956 \times 10^{-19} - 6.72 \times 10^{-19}\) [C1] \(= 1.2 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}\). [A1] The stopping potential is \(\dfrac{1.236 \times 10^{-19}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 0.77\,\mathrm{V}\). [A1]
(c) At the threshold \(hf_0 = \Phi\), so \(\lambda_0 = \dfrac{hc}{\Phi} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.00 \times 10^{8}}{6.72 \times 10^{-19}}\) [C1] \(= 3.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1]
(d) The maximum kinetic energy is unchanged, because it depends on the energy of each photon (the frequency), not on the number of photons. [B1] The current doubles, because twice as many photons arrive each second and each photon releases at most one electron. [B1]
Q: [10 marks] (#phys.nuclear) ★★★ Fluorine-18 is a \(\beta^+\) emitter with a half-life of 110 minutes, used as a tracer in PET scanning. A patient is injected with a sample of activity \(3.7 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Bq}\). (a) Write the decay equation, with the correct nucleon and proton numbers. (b) Calculate the decay constant and the number of fluorine-18 nuclei in the sample. (c) Calculate the activity of the fluorine-18 in the patient 3.0 hours later, if none is removed from the body. (d) The positrons annihilate with electrons in the tissue. Calculate the energy of each of the photons produced, and explain why two photons are emitted in opposite directions.
H: Use \(\lambda = 0.693/t_{1/2}\) with the half-life in seconds, and \(A = \lambda N\).
H: \(A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}\).
H: The rest energy of a positron or electron is \(mc^2\). Think about the momentum before and after the annihilation.
A: (a) \(\;^{18}_{9}\mathrm{F} \to \;^{18}_{8}\mathrm{O} + \;^{0}_{+1}\mathrm{e} + \nu_e\). [B1] The nucleon number (18) and the charge (+9 before and \(8 + 1\) after) balance, and a neutrino is emitted with the positron. [B1]
(b) \(\lambda = \dfrac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} = \dfrac{0.693}{110 \times 60}\) [C1] \(= 1.05 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\). [A1] \(N = \dfrac{A}{\lambda} = \dfrac{3.7 \times 10^{8}}{1.05 \times 10^{-4}} = 3.5 \times 10^{12}\). [A1]
(c) \(t = 3.0 \times 3600 = 10\,800\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t} = 3.7 \times 10^{8} \times e^{-1.05 \times 10^{-4} \times 10\,800}\) [C1] \(= 1.2 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Bq}\). [A1]
(d) The two particles have the same rest energy, so each photon has \(E = m_e c^2 = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times (3.00 \times 10^{8})^2\) [C1] \(= 8.2 \times 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J}\) (0.51 MeV). [A1] The total momentum before is almost zero, so the photons must carry equal and opposite momentum to conserve momentum. [B1]
Also revise: [Medical physics](#phys.medical).
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.nuclear) ★★ In a nuclear power station each fission of a uranium-235 nucleus releases about 200 MeV. The reactor produces thermal energy at a rate of 3.0 GW. The mass of a uranium-235 nucleus is 235 u. (a) Calculate the number of fissions each second. (b) Calculate the mass of uranium-235 that undergoes fission each day. (c) Calculate the mass defect, in kilograms and in unified atomic mass units, for one fission. (d) The station converts 40% of the thermal power into electrical power. Calculate the electrical power.
A: (a) The energy per fission is \(200 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} = 3.2 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{J}\), so the rate is \(\dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{9}}{3.2 \times 10^{-11}}\) [C1] \(= 9.4 \times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(b) The number of fissions in one day is \(9.375 \times 10^{19} \times 86\,400\) [C1] and the mass of each nucleus is \(235 \times 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}\), [C1] so the mass is \(3.2\,\mathrm{kg}\). [A1]
(c) \(\Delta m = \dfrac{E}{c^2} = \dfrac{3.2 \times 10^{-11}}{(3.00 \times 10^{8})^2}\) [C1] \(= 3.6 \times 10^{-28}\,\mathrm{kg}\), which is 0.21 u. [A1]
(d) \(P = 0.40 \times 3.0 \times 10^{9} = 1.2 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{W}\). [A1]
Also revise: [Work, energy and power](#phys.energy).
Q: [10 marks] (#phys.medical) ★★ A parallel beam of X-rays passes through a patient. The linear attenuation coefficient is \(0.18\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) for soft tissue and \(0.60\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) for bone. (a) Calculate the fraction of the intensity transmitted through 6.0 cm of soft tissue. (b) A second path through the patient has 2.0 cm of bone and 4.0 cm of soft tissue. Calculate the fraction transmitted along this path, and the ratio of the intensity along this path to that along the path in (a). State what this ratio shows about the image. (c) Calculate the thickness of soft tissue that reduces the intensity to half. (d) Ultrasound is reflected at a boundary between soft tissue of acoustic impedance \(1.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\) and bone of acoustic impedance \(7.8 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\). Calculate the fraction of the intensity that is reflected.
A: (a) \(\dfrac{I}{I_0} = e^{-\mu x} = e^{-0.18 \times 6.0}\) [C1] \(= 0.34\). [A1]
(b) \(\dfrac{I}{I_0} = e^{-(0.60 \times 2.0 + 0.18 \times 4.0)} = e^{-1.92}\) [C1] \(= 0.15\). [A1] The ratio is \(\dfrac{0.1466}{0.3396} = 0.43\), [A1] so the bone gives a much weaker transmitted beam and a visible contrast in the image. [B1]
(c) \(x_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\mu} = \dfrac{0.693}{0.18}\) [C1] \(= 3.9\,\mathrm{cm}\). [A1]
(d) \(\dfrac{I_R}{I_0} = \dfrac{(Z_2 - Z_1)^2}{(Z_2 + Z_1)^2} = \dfrac{(7.8 - 1.6)^2}{(7.8 + 1.6)^2}\) [C1] \(= 0.44\). [A1]
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.astro) ★★★ A star has a peak wavelength in its spectrum of 460 nm. Its luminosity is \(1.9 \times 10^{27}\,\mathrm{W}\), known because it is a standard candle, and the radiant flux intensity measured at the Earth is \(2.0 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\). Wien's law constant is \(2.898 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,K}\), the Stefan-Boltzmann constant is \(\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}}\) and one light-year is \(9.46 \times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{m}\). The radius of the Sun is \(6.96 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}\). (a) Calculate the surface temperature of the star. (b) Calculate the distance to the star, in metres and in light-years. (c) Calculate the radius of the star and compare it with the radius of the Sun.
H: Wien's law is \(\lambda_{\max} T = \text{constant}\).
H: The flux obeys an inverse square law: \(F = L/(4\pi d^2)\).
H: The luminosity of a star is \(L = 4\pi\sigma r^2 T^4\), and you have already found \(T\).
A: (a) \(T = \dfrac{2.898 \times 10^{-3}}{460 \times 10^{-9}}\) [C1] \(= 6.3 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{K}\). [A1]
(b) \(d = \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{4\pi F}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1.9 \times 10^{27}}{4\pi \times 2.0 \times 10^{-9}}}\) [C1] \(= 2.7 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1] Dividing by \(9.46 \times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{m}\) gives 29 light-years. [A1]
(c) \(r = \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{4\pi\sigma T^4}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1.9 \times 10^{27}}{4\pi \times 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 6300^4}}\) [C1] \(= 1.3 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1] This is \(\dfrac{1.30 \times 10^{9}}{6.96 \times 10^{8}} = 1.9\) times the radius of the Sun. [A1]

Q: [8 marks] (#phys.efields) ★★★ In an experiment to measure charge, a tiny oil drop of radius \(1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\) is held at rest between two horizontal parallel plates 12 mm apart. The p.d. between the plates is 925 V. The density of the oil is \(900\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\). The drop is negatively charged. (a) Calculate the mass of the drop and its weight. (b) Calculate the electric field strength between the plates. (c) Calculate the charge on the drop. (d) Calculate the charge as a multiple of the elementary charge, and state what many such results would show. (e) State which plate is positive.
H: A drop at rest has zero resultant force on it: the weight is balanced by the electric force \(qE\).
H: The volume of a sphere is \(\tfrac43\pi r^3\), and \(E = V/d\) between plates.
A: (a) \(m = \rho V = 900 \times \tfrac43\pi \times (1.0 \times 10^{-6})^3\) [C1] \(= 3.8 \times 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{kg}\) and the weight is \(mg = 3.7 \times 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}\). [A1]
(b) \(E = \dfrac{V}{d} = \dfrac{925}{12 \times 10^{-3}} = 7.7 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). [A1]
(c) The drop is in equilibrium, so \(qE = mg\) [C1] and \(q = \dfrac{3.698 \times 10^{-14}}{7.708 \times 10^{4}} = 4.8 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\). [A1]
(d) \(\dfrac{4.8 \times 10^{-19}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 3.0\), so the drop carries three elementary charges. [A1] Many results that are all whole-number multiples of \(1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\) show that charge is quantised. [B1]
(e) The electric force on the negative drop must be upwards, against the weight, so the field points downwards and the top plate is positive. [B1]
Also revise: [Forces, density and pressure](#phys.forces) and [Dynamics and momentum](#phys.dynamics).
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.quantum) ★★★ Electrons are accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 120 V and then pass through a thin crystal, where the spacing between layers of atoms is \(2.5 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\). The mass of an electron is \(9.11 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\). (a) Calculate the momentum of an electron after the acceleration. (b) Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons. (c) Use the condition \(d\sin\theta = \lambda\) to calculate the angle at which the first-order diffraction maximum is seen. (d) Calculate the energy of an X-ray photon of the same wavelength, and comment on how it compares with the energy of the electron.
H: The kinetic energy of the electron is \(eV\), and \(p^2 = 2mE_K\).
H: De Broglie wavelength: \(\lambda = h/p\).
A: (a) \(eV = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}\) [C1] so
\[p = \sqrt{2 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 120} = 5.9 \times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}\] [A1]
(b) \(\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{5.915 \times 10^{-24}}\) [C1] \(= 1.1 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1]
(c) \(\sin\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{d} = \dfrac{1.121 \times 10^{-10}}{2.5 \times 10^{-10}}\) [C1] so \(\theta = 27^\circ\). [A1]
(d) \(E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.00 \times 10^{8}}{1.121 \times 10^{-10}} = 1.8 \times 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{J}\) (about 11 keV). [A1] The electron has only 120 eV, so for the same wavelength a photon carries about 90 times more energy: \(E = pc\) for a photon, but \(E = p^2/2m\) for a slow electron. [B1]
Also revise: [Superposition](#phys.superposition) and [Electric fields](#phys.efields).
Q: [8 marks] (#phys.astro) ★★ In the spectrum from a distant galaxy, a spectral line that has a wavelength of 656 nm in the laboratory is seen at 680 nm. Take the Hubble constant to be \(2.2 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), and one year to be \(3.16 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{s}\). (a) Calculate the speed at which the galaxy is receding. (b) Calculate the distance to the galaxy. (c) Estimate the age of the Universe from the Hubble constant, in years. (d) Explain how observations of redshift support the idea that the Universe began with a Big Bang.
A: (a) \(\dfrac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda} = \dfrac{680 - 656}{656} = 0.0366\) and \(v = c\,\dfrac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda} = 3.00 \times 10^{8} \times 0.0366\) [C1] \(= 1.1 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
(b) \(v = H_0 d\) so \(d = \dfrac{v}{H_0} = \dfrac{1.098 \times 10^{7}}{2.2 \times 10^{-18}}\) [C1] \(= 5.0 \times 10^{24}\,\mathrm{m}\). [A1]
(c) \(t = \dfrac{1}{H_0}\) [C1] \(= 4.5 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.4 \times 10^{10}\) years. [A1]
(d) The light from distant galaxies is redshifted, so the galaxies are moving away from us, and the further away they are the faster they recede. [B1] This is an expanding Universe. Running the expansion backwards, everything was once at the same point, in a hot dense state, which then expanded. [B1]
Also revise: [Waves](#phys.waves).
