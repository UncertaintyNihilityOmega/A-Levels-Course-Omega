Q: [6 marks] (#phys.units) This question is about units and uncertainties.

(a) The spring constant \(k\) of a spring is defined by \(F = kx\), where \(F\) is the force applied to the spring and \(x\) is its extension. Show that the SI base units of \(k\) are \(\mathrm{kg\,s^{-2}}\). (2 marks)

(b) A solid metal cylinder is measured in order to find the density of the metal.

| quantity | value | absolute uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| diameter \(d\) / mm | 12.00 | 0.02 |
| length \(L\) / mm | 85.0 | 0.5 |
| mass \(m\) / g | 80.6 | 0.1 |

(i) Calculate the density of the metal. (2 marks)

(ii) Calculate the absolute uncertainty in the density. (2 marks)
A: (a) The force \(F = ma\) has base units \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\). [B1]

\(k = F/x\), so the units of \(k\) are \(\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}\) divided by m, which is \(\mathrm{kg\,s^{-2}}\). [B1]

(b)(i) \(\rho = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{4m}{\pi d^2 L}\) [C1]

\(\rho = \dfrac{4 \times 80.6 \times 10^{-3}}{\pi \times (12.00 \times 10^{-3})^2 \times 85.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 8.38 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\) [A1]

(b)(ii) Percentage uncertainty in \(\rho\) = \(0.12\% + 2 \times 0.17\% + 0.59\% = 1.05\%\) (the percentage for \(d\) is doubled because \(d\) is squared). [C1]

Absolute uncertainty = \(1.05\% \times 8.38 \times 10^{3} = 0.09 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), so \(\rho = (8.38 \pm 0.09) \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\). [A1] (allow \(\pm 88\) or \(\pm 90\); the uncertainty must have 1 or 2 s.f. and match the decimal place of the value)
:::

(a) Define acceleration. (1 mark)

(b) Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. (2 marks)

(c) Calculate

(i) the maximum height reached by the ball, (2 marks)

(ii) the horizontal distance from the kick to the point where the ball lands. (2 marks)

(d) In practice air resistance acts on the ball. State two ways in which the path of the ball differs from the path in the figure. (2 marks)
A: (a) The rate of change of velocity. [B1]

(b) \(v_x = 22\cos 50^\circ\) and \(v_y = 22\sin 50^\circ\) [C1]

\(v_x = 14\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) and \(v_y = 17\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) [A1]

(c)(i) \(0 = v_y^2 - 2gH\), so \(H = \dfrac{v_y^2}{2g} = \dfrac{(22\sin 50^\circ)^2}{2 \times 9.81}\) [C1]

\(H = 14\,\mathrm{m}\) [A1] (allow 14.5 m)

(c)(ii) Time of flight \(t = \dfrac{2v_y}{g} = \dfrac{2 \times 22\sin 50^\circ}{9.81} = 3.4\,\mathrm{s}\) and range \(= v_x t\) [C1]

range \(= 22\cos 50^\circ \times 3.44 = 49\,\mathrm{m}\) [A1] (allow 48 to 49 m)

(d) The maximum height is lower (and the range is shorter). [B1]

The path is not symmetrical: the ball falls more steeply than it rose, because the horizontal velocity decreases throughout the flight. [B1] (allow "the time of flight is shorter" for one of the two points, but not the same point twice)

(a) State the principle of conservation of momentum. (1 mark)

(b) Calculate the common velocity \(v\). (2 marks)

(c) Use the graph and your knowledge of impulse to calculate the maximum force \(F_\text{max}\) on trolley B. (2 marks)

(d) Show that the collision is inelastic. (2 marks)

(e) Compare the force that A exerts on B with the force that B exerts on A, and give a reason for your answer. (2 marks)
A: (a) The total momentum of a system of interacting bodies is constant provided no resultant external force acts on the system. [B1] (the mark needs both parts: "constant" and "no external force")

(b) \(0.50 \times 2.4 = (0.50 + 0.30)\,v\) [C1]

\(v = 1.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) [A1]

(c) Impulse on B = change in momentum of B = \(0.30 \times 1.5 = 0.45\,\mathrm{N\,s}\), and the impulse is the area under the graph, \(\tfrac12 \times 0.060 \times F_\text{max}\) [C1]

\(F_\text{max} = \dfrac{2 \times 0.45}{0.060} = 15\,\mathrm{N}\) [A1]

The kinetic energy decreases (by 0.54 J), so kinetic energy is not conserved and the collision is inelastic. [A1]

(e) The forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. [B1]

They are a Newton's third law pair: the two forces act on different bodies, are of the same type and act for the same time. [B1]

Q: [9 marks] (#phys.forces) A uniform horizontal beam of length \(2.0\,\mathrm{m}\) and weight \(120\,\mathrm{N}\) is hinged to a vertical wall at one end. It is held in equilibrium by a steel wire joined to the free end of the beam and to the wall. The wire makes an angle of \(35^\circ\) with the beam. A load of weight \(90\,\mathrm{N}\) hangs from the beam at a point \(1.5\,\mathrm{m}\) from the hinge.

(a) Define the moment of a force. (1 mark)

(b) By taking moments about the hinge, calculate the tension in the wire. (3 marks)

(c) The wire has a circular cross-section of diameter \(1.2\,\mathrm{mm}\). Calculate the stress in the wire. (2 marks)

(d) The wire has an unstretched length of \(2.44\,\mathrm{m}\). The Young modulus of the steel is \(2.0 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{Pa}\).

(i) Calculate the extension of the wire. (2 marks)

(ii) State the condition that the wire must satisfy for your answer to (i) to be valid. (1 mark)
A: (a) The product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot (point) to the line of action of the force. [B1]

(b) Clockwise moment about the hinge \(= 120 \times 1.0 + 90 \times 1.5 = 255\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) [C1]

\(x = 2.4 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}\) [A1] (allow 2.4 mm)

(ii) State the phase difference between the vibrations of a point in one loop and a point in the next loop. (1 mark)

(c) Light of wavelength \(640\,\mathrm{nm}\) is incident normally on a diffraction grating that has 420 lines per millimetre.

(i) Calculate the angle between the second-order maximum and the straight-through direction. (2 marks)

(ii) Determine the highest order of maximum that can be seen, and the total number of maxima that can be seen. (3 marks)

- a stationary wave has nodes and antinodes, a progressive wave does not
- the amplitude of a stationary wave varies from zero at a node to a maximum at an antinode, but all points on a progressive wave have the same amplitude
- a stationary wave transfers no energy along the wave, a progressive wave does
- the phase of a stationary wave is the same for all points in one loop (and opposite in the next loop), but the phase of a progressive wave changes continuously along the wave

(c)(ii) The largest possible \(\sin\theta\) is 1, so \(n_\text{max} = \dfrac{d}{\lambda} = \dfrac{2.38 \times 10^{-6}}{640 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.72\) [C1]

The highest order seen is 3. [A1]

Total number of maxima \(= 2 \times 3 + 1 = 7\) (three each side of the central maximum). [B1]

Q: [6 marks] (#phys.particles) This question is about the nucleus.

(a) In the α-particle scattering experiment, most α-particles passed straight through a thin gold foil, but a very small number were scattered through angles greater than \(90^\circ\). State what each of these two observations shows about the structure of the atom. (2 marks)

(b) Sodium-22, \(^{22}_{11}\mathrm{Na}\), decays by β⁺ emission to a nucleus of neon, Ne. Complete the equation for this decay.

\[ ^{22}_{11}\mathrm{Na} \rightarrow \;^{\,\cdots}_{\,\cdots}\mathrm{Ne} + \ldots + \ldots \]

(2 marks)

(c) Describe the change in the quark structure of the nucleus in this decay, and use charge to check your answer. (2 marks)
A: (a) Most α-particles pass straight through, so the atom is mostly empty space. [B1]

A few are deflected through large angles, so the atom has a small, dense nucleus that is positively charged (and contains most of the mass). [B1]

(b) The neon nucleus is \(^{22}_{10}\mathrm{Ne}\). [B1]

The other products are a positron \(^{0}_{+1}\mathrm{e}\) and an electron neutrino \(\nu_\mathrm{e}\): \(^{22}_{11}\mathrm{Na} \rightarrow \,^{22}_{10}\mathrm{Ne} + \,^{0}_{+1}\mathrm{e} + \nu_\mathrm{e}\). [B1]

(c) An up quark changes to a down quark (a proton, uud, becomes a neutron, udd). [B1]

Charge check: \(+\tfrac23 \to -\tfrac13 + 1 + 0\), so the charge before and after is \(+\tfrac23\). [B1]
