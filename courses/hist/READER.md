# READER: what each chapter teaches, in course order

## hist.overview
Taught: the four papers (1 Historical Sources, 2 Outline Study, 3 Historical Interpretations, 4 Depth Study) with times, marks and weights; AS, staged and all-at-once routes; the three AS options and their topics, the 2027-29 Paper 1 rotation; the Paper 3 and Paper 4 options; the four assessment objectives (AO1-AO4); marking by levels, not points; the ten habits of a top student; the chapter map.
Assumed: nothing (a first chapter). It uses "provenance", "extract", "command words" and "weighing" before they are taught; the next chapters teach them.

## hist.f.basics
Taught: history, evidence, source, primary and secondary source, source types, provenance (nature, origin, purpose, context, then content against the question), bias is not enough, reliable versus useful, corroboration, fact, opinion, interpretation, inference, cause, long-term cause, short-term cause, trigger, necessary and sufficient, six ways to weigh causes, consequence, change and continuity, significance, centuries, BC/AD, c., Old Style and New Style dates, timeline, key-people table, factor grid, reading a historian's argument (five steps), analysis against narrative, PEEL, the definitions of ideology, liberalism, conservatism, nationalism, socialism, communism, fascism, imperialism, absolute monarchy, autocracy, constitutional monarchy, republic, democracy, dictatorship, constitution, sovereignty, federalism.
Assumed: French terms and events used in examples before France is studied (livres, taille, Notables, Parlement, Estates-General, Third Estate, Ancien Regime); the American words Union, secede, border states; Marxist.

## hist.f.eras
Taught: why the syllabus is split by topic; eras (Hobsbawm's long nineteenth and short twentieth century); phenomena that cross borders; the world before 1789 (absolutism, empires, slave trade, Enlightenment writers, Seven Years' War, American Revolution, early industrialisation); five eras from 1774 to 1991 with the main events; the connections between topics (loans circle, tariffs, freedom without land, Japan copying Prussia); revisionist; three reading paths; how to add international context to a one-country answer.
Assumed: Ancien Regime, Terror, Directory, First Consul (named, not explained); Red Two Years, Spartacist rising, gold standard, IRI, Credit-Anstalt (named in passing).

## hist.f.choose
Taught: what each AS option, Paper 3 topic and Paper 4 option contains; what must be studied (all three topics of one AS option, one Paper 3 topic, at least two topics of one Paper 4 option); how to choose; which topic is on Paper 1 in 2027, 2028 and 2029 and which two are on Paper 2; routes and the June and November series; sample study plans in weeks and hours.
Assumed: Ancien Regime (named in the France summary).

## hist.skills.sources
Taught: Paper 1 format (four sources, 600 words, (a) [15] compare, (b) [25] how far), the (a) and (b) level tables; reading a source (says, suggests, leaves out), inference, tone and language, silences; nature, origin, purpose and audience; "as evidence of what"; replacing "biased" with an evaluation; reliable, useful, corroboration; using own knowledge to place, test and fill a gap; reading cartoons, photographs and posters; part (a) method (themes, similarities and differences, explain, verdict); part (b) five steps (decode the claim, one line per source, weigh, knowledge, group by argument and judge); time plan.
Assumed: knowledge of the Russian 1905 and Munich 1938 topics in the practice questions; the words paternalist, satire.

## hist.skills.essays
Taught: command words (Explain, How far, To what extent, Assess, Evaluate); unpacking a question (command, topic, focus, limits); judgement and criterion; planning in 3-5 minutes; Paper 2 (a) [10] levels (describe, explain, connect), four kinds of link; Paper 2 (b) [20] levels (one perspective, alternative perspectives, comparative judgement); criteria (necessary, decisive, widest in effect, most lasting, root cause, success against aims); Paper 4 [30] two tables and what is different; introductions, argumentative paragraphs and conclusions; time plans.
Assumed: Prussia, Austria, Bismarck, Moltke, the League of Nations (Covenant, sanctions, unanimity), Nazi terms (plebiscite, Enabling Act, Night of the Long Knives) in the worked answers; knowledge of Russia 1905, the Crash, 1848, Munich, Reconstruction, Abyssinia and Montgomery in the practice questions.

## hist.eu.france.1
Taught: Ancien Regime; absolute monarchy and its limits (ministers, intendants, parlements, lettres de cachet); the three Estates and the grievances of the Third Estate; taille, corvee, tithe, seigneurial dues; the American war debt and the Enlightenment; Turgot, Necker, Calonne and why their reforms failed; Assembly of Notables, Brienne, May Edicts and the aristocratic revolt; the 1788 budget; the Estates-General (1614 ruling, doubling, Sieyes, cahiers); the hunger of 1788-89; the National Assembly, Tennis Court Oath, royal session, the Bastille, Great Fear, August Decrees, Declaration of the Rights of Man, the October Days; weighing the causes (necessary but not sufficient); Lefebvre, Cobban, Doyle, Furet in brief; model Paper 1 and 2 answers.
Assumed: livre and sou (money units); cockade; the Invalides; "voting by head" (used before it is defined).

## hist.eu.france.2
Taught: the Constituent Assembly's aim of a constitutional monarchy; Jacobins, Cordeliers, Feuillants, Girondins, the Plain; emigres, refractory priests and why the counter-revolution failed; Church lands, assignats, new taxes, d'Allarde and Le Chapelier laws; 83 departments; active and passive citizens; the new justice system; the Civil Constitution of the Clergy and the oath; the 1791 constitution; Varennes and its consequences; the Champ de Mars massacre; the Legislative Assembly, the war of April 1792, Brunswick Manifesto, 10 August, September Massacres, Convention and the Republic; weighing the causes; model answers.
Assumed: left and right as political positions; the Festival of the Federation (listed but not explained in the text); the revolutionary calendar (Year I).

## hist.eu.france.3
Taught: the crises of 1793 (execution of Louis XVI, First Coalition, levy of 300,000, Dumouriez, Vendée, federalist revolts, assignat collapse); Girondins, Montagnards, the Plain; the purge of 2 June; sans-culottes, Enragés, journées; Constitution of 1793; Committee of Public Safety and its members, Committee of General Security, Revolutionary Tribunal, representatives on mission, Law of Revolutionary Government, armées révolutionnaires; levée en masse; Robespierre; the Terror (Law of Suspects, Law of 22 Prairial, Great Terror) and its scale (Greer, about 16,600); dechristianisation, Supreme Being; Hébertists and Indulgents; General Maximum; 9 Thermidor, Thermidorian reaction, White Terror, Germinal and Prairial risings; Constitution of the Year III (Five Hundred, Ancients, five Directors, property franchise); two-thirds decree, Vendémiaire; Directory's finance (mandats territoriaux, bankruptcy of the two-thirds), war, coups (Fructidor, Floréal, Prairial) and corruption; Palmer, Soboul, Furet, McPhee, Lyons; model answers.
Assumed: the Paris Commune, the sections and the National Guard as institutions (used, not explained); "Dantonists" (= Indulgents, never equated); "Great Book" (Source D), "sister republics", "faubourg", plebiscite; the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.france.4
Taught: Bonaparte's rise (Toulon, Vendémiaire, Italy, Egypt) and propaganda; Brumaire; Constitution of the Year VIII (First Consul, Council of State, Tribunate, Legislative Body, Senate, lists of notables), plebiscite and the figures; Consul for life, Emperor; the six means of control (ministries, prefects, police and judiciary, censorship and propaganda, elections, Empire); Civil Code, lycées and Imperial University, Bank of France, franc germinal, taxes, bread prices, Concordat and Organic Articles; force against consent; the extent of the Empire in 1812; conscription and economic decline; Trafalgar and the Continental System; Peninsular War; nationalism; Russia 1812, Leipzig, allies in Paris, abdication; Geyl, Ellis, Roberts, Broers; model answers.
Assumed: gabelle; the Grande Armée (used in the dates table and propaganda section before it is defined), the Confederation of the Rhine, Hanseatic ports, Illyrian Provinces (in the Empire table); Hannibal and Charlemagne (Source C of the Paper 1 (b) example); the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.germany.1
Taught: Holy Roman Empire, Congress of Vienna, Metternich and his system, the German Confederation (39 states, Diet at Frankfurt, unanimity, Article 13), the Third Germany, Burschenschaft, Wartburg, Kotzebue and the Carlsbad Decrees, liberalism and nationalism (as different aims), the middle class, the 1830 revolutions, Hambach, Six and Ten Articles, Göttingen Seven, Prussian provincial diets and the United Diet, Rhine crisis, Prussian Customs Union and Zollverein, railways, population growth, pauperism, artisans, the Silesian weavers, the crisis of 1846-47, Offenburg and Heppenheim; Nipperdey, Sperber, Carr; model answers.
Assumed: Habsburg, divine right, the "monarchical principle", what a "constitution of estates" is, navvies; the Frankfurt Parliament (named before Germany (2)); the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.germany.2
Taught: the March 1848 movement and demands, the fall of Metternich, Berlin barricades and Frederick William IV's role, Pre-Parliament, Frankfurt Parliament (Paulskirche, Regent, Provisional Central Power, no army), Schleswig-Holstein and Malmo, Basic Rights, grossdeutsch and kleindeutsch, the constitution of 1849 and the refused crown, the May risings and Forty-Eighters, Realpolitik, why Austria recovered (army, Schwarzenberg, Russia), the three-class franchise, the Erfurt Union, Olmutz and Dresden, the Zollverein quarrel, growth in Prussia and Austria's finances, the Crimean War, New Era, Nationalverein, Bismarck to 1859; Namier, Taylor, Sperber, Clark; model answers.
Assumed: Reich, suspensive veto, Landtag (defined only in Germany (3)), Hohenzollern, Archduke, Elector, Magyars, the Weimar constitution and Basic Law (named); the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.germany.3
Taught: Wilhelm I and the New Era, Roon, Moltke, Landwehr and the army reform, the German Progressive Party, the constitutional conflict, Bismarck's background and appointment (Babelsberg), Minister-President, "iron and blood", gap theory, the Alvensleben Convention, the Congress of Princes, the Schleswig-Holstein question and London Protocol, the Danish war and Treaty of Vienna, condominium, Gastein, isolating Austria (Biarritz, Italy), the proposal to the Diet, the Seven Weeks' War and Königgrätz, Nikolsburg and Prague, the North German Confederation (Bundesrat, Reichstag), the Indemnity Bill, National Liberals, plan or opportunism (Gall, Taylor, Pflanze, Steinberg); model answers.
Assumed: ministerial responsibility, Napoleon III, Palmerston, Venetia, "federal execution"; House of Representatives (used before its definition); the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.germany.4
Taught: the unfinished unification of 1866, the southern states' reasons for holding back, the secret alliances, the Zollparlament, Napoleon III's weakness, the Luxembourg crisis, the Hohenzollern candidature, the Ems telegram (and why it did not alone cause the war), Bismarck's and Moltke's advantages in 1870, Sedan and its impact, the Treaty of Frankfurt, bringing in the south, the imperial title, the proclamation at Versailles, the 1871 constitution (Kaiser, Chancellor, Bundesrat, Reichstag), weighing the causes of unification; Taylor, Pflanze, Steinberg, Howard, Wawro, Bohme; model answers.

## hist.eu.russia.1
Taught: Old Style dates, the empire's size and peoples, autocracy, the assassination of Alexander II and the People's Will, the Manifesto of 1881, how Russia was ruled (State Council, ministers, Senate, Okhrana, army, Church, governors, zemstvos, commune), counter-reform (State Order Statute, press, universities, land captains, zemstvo statute), Russification and the Jews (Pale, pogroms, May Laws), Bunge, Vyshnegradsky and Witte's start, the famine of 1891-92, nobility, peasants, workers, middle class and intelligentsia, Morozov strike, Marxism and Populism, Plekhanov; Seton-Watson, Pipes, Figes, Hosking; model answers.
Assumed: Bolshevik and Socialist Revolutionaries (named), Bloody Sunday and 1905 (named ahead), gymnasium; the numbering of the practice questions.

## hist.eu.russia.2
