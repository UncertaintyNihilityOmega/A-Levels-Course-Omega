# READER: what each chapter teaches, in course order

## hist.overview
Taught: the four papers (1 Historical Sources, 2 Outline Study, 3 Historical Interpretations, 4 Depth Study) with times, marks and weights; AS, staged and all-at-once routes; the three AS options and their topics, the 2027-29 Paper 1 rotation; the Paper 3 and Paper 4 options; the four assessment objectives (AO1-AO4); marking by levels, not points; the ten habits of a top student; the chapter map.
Assumed: nothing (a first chapter). It uses "provenance", "extract", "command words" and "weighing" before they are taught; the next chapters teach them.

## hist.f.basics
Taught: history, evidence, source, primary and secondary source, source types, provenance (nature, origin, purpose, context, then content against the question), bias is not enough, reliable versus useful, corroboration, fact, opinion, interpretation, inference, cause, long-term cause, short-term cause, trigger, necessary and sufficient, six ways to weigh causes, consequence, change and continuity, significance, centuries, BC/AD, c., Old Style and New Style dates, timeline, key-people table, factor grid, reading a historian's argument (five steps), analysis against narrative, PEEL, the definitions of ideology, liberalism, conservatism, nationalism, socialism, communism, fascism, imperialism, absolute monarchy, autocracy, constitutional monarchy, republic, democracy, dictatorship, constitution, sovereignty, federalism.
Assumed: French terms and events used in examples before France is studied (livres, taille, Notables, Parlement, Estates-General, Third Estate, Ancien Regime); the American words Union, secede, border states; Marxist.

## hist.f.eras
Taught: why the syllabus is split by topic; eras (Hobsbawm's long nineteenth and short twentieth century); phenomena that cross borders; the world before 1789 (absolutism, empires, slave trade, Enlightenment writers, Seven Years' War, American Revolution, early industrialisation); five eras from 1774 to 1991 with the main events; the connections between topics (loans circle, tariffs, freedom without land, Japan copying Prussia); revisionist; three reading paths; how to add international context to a one-country answer.
Assumed: Ancien Regime, Terror, Directory, First Consul (named, not explained); Red Two Years, Spartacist rising, gold standard, IRI, Credit-Anstalt (named in passing).

## hist.f.choose
Taught: what each AS option, Paper 3 topic and Paper 4 option contains; what must be studied (all three topics of one AS option, one Paper 3 topic, at least two topics of one Paper 4 option); how to choose; which topic is on Paper 1 in 2027, 2028 and 2029 and which two are on Paper 2; routes and the June and November series; sample study plans in weeks and hours.
Assumed: Ancien Regime (named in the France summary).

## hist.skills.sources
Taught: Paper 1 format (four sources, 600 words, (a) [15] compare, (b) [25] how far), the (a) and (b) level tables; reading a source (says, suggests, leaves out), inference, tone and language, silences; nature, origin, purpose and audience; "as evidence of what"; replacing "biased" with an evaluation; reliable, useful, corroboration; using own knowledge to place, test and fill a gap; reading cartoons, photographs and posters; part (a) method (themes, similarities and differences, explain, verdict); part (b) five steps (decode the claim, one line per source, weigh, knowledge, group by argument and judge); time plan.
Assumed: knowledge of the Russian 1905 and Munich 1938 topics in the practice questions; the words paternalist, satire.

## hist.skills.essays
Taught: command words (Explain, How far, To what extent, Assess, Evaluate); unpacking a question (command, topic, focus, limits); judgement and criterion; planning in 3-5 minutes; Paper 2 (a) [10] levels (describe, explain, connect), four kinds of link; Paper 2 (b) [20] levels (one perspective, alternative perspectives, comparative judgement); criteria (necessary, decisive, widest in effect, most lasting, root cause, success against aims); Paper 4 [30] two tables and what is different; introductions, argumentative paragraphs and conclusions; time plans.
Assumed: Prussia, Austria, Bismarck, Moltke, the League of Nations (Covenant, sanctions, unanimity), Nazi terms (plebiscite, Enabling Act, Night of the Long Knives) in the worked answers; knowledge of Russia 1905, the Crash, 1848, Munich, Reconstruction, Abyssinia and Montgomery in the practice questions.

## hist.eu.france.1
Taught: Ancien Regime; absolute monarchy and its limits (ministers, intendants, parlements, lettres de cachet); the three Estates and the grievances of the Third Estate; taille, corvee, tithe, seigneurial dues; the American war debt and the Enlightenment; Turgot, Necker, Calonne and why their reforms failed; Assembly of Notables, Brienne, May Edicts and the aristocratic revolt; the 1788 budget; the Estates-General (1614 ruling, doubling, Sieyes, cahiers); the hunger of 1788-89; the National Assembly, Tennis Court Oath, royal session, the Bastille, Great Fear, August Decrees, Declaration of the Rights of Man, the October Days; weighing the causes (necessary but not sufficient); Lefebvre, Cobban, Doyle, Furet in brief; model Paper 1 and 2 answers.
Assumed: livre and sou (money units); cockade; the Invalides; "voting by head" (used before it is defined).

## hist.eu.france.2
Taught: the Constituent Assembly's aim of a constitutional monarchy; Jacobins, Cordeliers, Feuillants, Girondins, the Plain; emigres, refractory priests and why the counter-revolution failed; Church lands, assignats, new taxes, d'Allarde and Le Chapelier laws; 83 departments; active and passive citizens; the new justice system; the Civil Constitution of the Clergy and the oath; the 1791 constitution; Varennes and its consequences; the Champ de Mars massacre; the Legislative Assembly, the war of April 1792, Brunswick Manifesto, 10 August, September Massacres, Convention and the Republic; weighing the causes; model answers.
Assumed: left and right as political positions; the Festival of the Federation (listed but not explained in the text); the revolutionary calendar (Year I).
