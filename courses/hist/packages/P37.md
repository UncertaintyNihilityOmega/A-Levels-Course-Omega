### hist.d.safrica.4 · End of minority rule (4): the end of white minority rule in Zimbabwe, 1965–80 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 8 End of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe, 1948–94
- Key question 8.4 How was the ending of white minority rule in Zimbabwe achieved in the period 1965–80?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The Declaration of Independence, 1965: Demands by the Rhodesian government for independence; Talks between Wilson and Smith and their disagreements; The Unilateral Declaration of Independence (UDI) and the reactions of the Rhodesian people and Britain
  - The Civil War, 1964–79: The aims and methods of the Rhodesian government; Zimbabwe African People’s Union (ZAPU) , the Zimbabwe People’s Revolutionary Army (ZIPRA) and Nkomo; Zimbabwe African National Union (ZANU), the Zimbabwe African National Liberation Army (ZANLA) and Mugabe; The nature of the fighting
  - The Internal Settlement and the ending of white minority rule: Reasons why Smith agreed to the Internal Settlement; The multiracial elections and the new government under Muzorewa; Rejection by the British government and ZAPU and ZANU
  - The Lancaster House Agreement, 1979 and a final settlement: Reasons why the fighting ended; Negotiations in London, 1979; The Lancaster House Agreement; The 1980 elections; Formation of a new government headed by Mugabe
- **What to cover and stress.** Southern Rhodesia demands independence (after Federation's end 1963; NIBMAR); Wilson–Smith talks (1964–66; Tiger December 1966 and Fearless October 1968 later); UDI (11 November 1965) and reactions (Rhodesians; Britain: sanctions, no force; UN mandatory sanctions 1966; Beira patrol). Civil war (the syllabus dates it 1964–79): Rhodesian government aims and methods (Rhodesian Front; protected villages; Selous Scouts; cross-border raids), ZAPU, ZIPRA and Nkomo (Soviet-backed, Zambia), ZANU, ZANLA and Mugabe (Chinese-backed, Mozambique after 1975), nature of the fighting (guerrilla war, Chinhoyi 1966, intensification from 1972); South Africa's and the frontline states' pressure; Kissinger (1976). Internal Settlement (3 March 1978; Smith with Muzorewa, Sithole, Chirau): reasons; April 1979 elections; Zimbabwe-Rhodesia under Muzorewa; rejection by Britain, ZANU and ZAPU. Lancaster House (September–December 1979): why fighting ended (war weariness, pressure from Mozambique and Zambia, South Africa, Thatcher and Carrington, Commonwealth Lusaka 1979), the Agreement (constitution, 20 reserved white seats, land 'willing seller, willing buyer', ceasefire), elections February 1980 (ZANU-PF 57 of 80 common-roll seats; check), Mugabe PM, independence 18 April 1980.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Britain's policies were the main reason white minority rule in Rhodesia lasted until 1980.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons why the Lancaster House talks succeeded. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Martin Meredith, The Past is Another Country (1979) and Mugabe (2002); David Martin and Phyllis Johnson, The Struggle for Zimbabwe (1981); Robert Blake, A History of Rhodesia (1977).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: war casualties (about 20,000–30,000 deaths; give range). Use Rhodesia/Zimbabwe names correctly by date.
