### hist.d.malaysia.1 · The route to independence (1): nationalism and colonial rule in Malaya to 1945 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 9 The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909–67
- Key question 9.1 How widespread was the growth of nationalism and hostility to colonial rule in Malaya in the period up to 1945?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The nature of colonial rule, including: Reasons why Britain gained protection of the Unfederated Malay States, including: The Bangkok Treaty, 1909 and its importance; Divide and rule; Immigration and the polarisation of society; Sir Cecil Clementi and the failure of Malayanisation
  - The growth of nationalism, including: Kesatuan Melayu Muda (KMM); Opposition from English-educated Malays; Kaum Muda; Malayan associations and clubs
  - Impact of Japanese government on Malaya: Treatment of the different races; Malayan Military Administration (MMA); The growth of resistance movements, including: Malayan People’s Anti-Japanese Army (MPAJA)
  - Increasing hostility during the Japanese occupation: Contribution of the Japanese to the growth of nationalism; The ambitions of the MPAJA; The role of Kesatuan Melayu Muda (KMM); Pembela Tanah Air (PETA); Kesatuan Rakyat Indonesia Semenanjung (KRIS)
- **What to cover and stress.** British rule: Straits Settlements, Federated Malay States (1895) and Unfederated Malay States; Bangkok (Anglo-Siamese) Treaty (10 March 1909: Kedah, Perlis, Kelantan, Terengganu transferred to British protection) and its importance; Johor (1914); divide and rule; immigration (Chinese for tin, Indians for rubber) and polarisation; Sir Cecil Clementi (High Commissioner 1930–34) and decentralisation, failure of Malayanisation. Nationalism: Kaum Muda (reformist Islam, al-Imam 1906), English-educated Malays, Malay associations (Kesatuan Melayu Singapura 1926; state associations late 1930s; congresses 1939–40), KMM (1938; Ibrahim Yaacob; left-wing, pan-Malay). Japanese occupation (December 1941–September 1945; Singapore falls 15 February 1942): treatment of races (sook ching massacre of Chinese, February–March 1942; Malays favoured; Indians and the INA), Thai control of the northern states (1943), MPAJA (MCP-led; Lai Teck; Chin Peng), British Military Administration (BMA, September 1945–April 1946; the syllabus says MMA), growth of hostility and nationalism: Japanese contribution, MPAJA ambitions, KMM's role, PETA (Malayan volunteer army Giyugun), KRIS (Kesatuan Rakyat Indonesia Semenanjung, 1945, plan for union with Indonesia).
- **Worked examples and practice.** Specimen Q9 asked about nationalism under Japanese occupation: write different questions, e.g. 'British policies before 1941 did more to divide than to rule Malaya.' Evaluate this view. Assess the strength of Malay nationalism before 1941. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Barbara Watson Andaya and Leonard Andaya, A History of Malaysia (1982; later editions); William Roff, The Origins of Malay Nationalism (1967); Cheah Boon Kheng, Red Star over Malaya (1983).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: the sook ching massacres; ethnic relations: neutral, careful terms.
- **Figures and widgets.** Schematic SVG of the three groupings of Malay states before 1941 (labelled), or a table.
- **Next line.** `Next: [The route to independence (2): the formation of Malaysia](#hist.d.malaysia.2)`

### hist.d.malaysia.2 · The route to independence (2): the formation of Malaysia (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 9 The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909–67
- Key question 9.2 What factors led to the formation of Malaysia?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Re-establishing British rule after the defeat of Japan, including: Failure to form the Malayan Union; The role of Data Onn bin Jaafar and the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO); The formation of the Federation of Malaya, 1948; Non-Malay opposition to the Federation, including: The Pan-Malayan Council of Joint Action (PMCJA), Pusat Tenaga Rakyat (PUTERA)
  - The Emergency, including: The role of Lai Teck and Chin Peng; The communist terror, 1948–49; Britain’s reaction to the Emergency and reasons for its success, including: The Briggs Plan, The role of General Sir Gerald Templar
  - The growth of political parties and the election of 1955, including: UMNO; The Malayan Chinese Association (MCA); The Malayan Indian Congress (MIC); The UMNO-MCA Alliance
  - The formation of Malaysia, including: Britain’s role in granting independence to Malaya, including: The Reid Commission, The Merdeka Constitution; The support of Tunku Abdul Rahman; Political, economic and cultural reasons for Singapore’s merger with Malaya; The Constitution of the Federation of Malaysia, including: Political and economic differences that led to Singapore separating from Malaya
- **What to cover and stress.** Malayan Union (April 1946; MacMichael treaties; equal citizenship; sultans' powers reduced), Malay opposition (Dato Onn bin Jaafar; UMNO founded May 1946), Federation of Malaya (1 February 1948), non-Malay opposition (PMCJA and PUTERA; AMCJA–PUTERA People's Constitutional Proposals 1947; hartal October 1947). Emergency (June 1948–1960): Lai Teck (exposed 1947), Chin Peng, MCP's armed struggle (planters killed at Sungai Siput 16 June 1948), terror 1948–49, Gurney killed (October 1951), Briggs Plan (1950; New Villages, about 500,000 resettled; check), Templer (1952–54; 'hearts and minds'), reasons for success. Parties: UMNO (Tunku Abdul Rahman from 1951), MCA (1949; Tan Cheng Lock), MIC (1946), Alliance (1952–55), 1955 election (Alliance 51 of 52 seats). Independence: London talks (1956), Reid Commission (1956–57), Merdeka Constitution (special position of Malays, citizenship), independence 31 August 1957; Malaysia: Tunku's proposal (27 May 1961), reasons for Singapore's merger (political: fear of communism; economic: common market; cultural), Sabah and Sarawak (Cobbold Commission 1962), Malaysia formed 16 September 1963; constitution; differences (PAP vs UMNO, 'Malaysian Malaysia', race riots 1964, economic disputes) and Singapore's separation (9 August 1965).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Emergency was won mainly by military methods.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for Singapore's separation from Malaysia in 1965. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Andaya and Andaya; Richard Stubbs, Hearts and Minds in Guerrilla Warfare (1989); Karl Hack; Tan Tai Yong, Creating 'Greater Malaysia' (2008).
- **Pitfalls.** Malaya (to 1963) vs Malaysia (from 1963): use the right name for the date.
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline table 1946–65.
