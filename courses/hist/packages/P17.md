### hist.int.1929.1 · International history, 1929–39 (1): the rise of extremism and international relations (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS International option (Section C). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 8 International relations 1919–29: conflict and cooperation
- Key question 9.1 How did the rise of extremism affect international relations?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Impact of the Great Depression on political ideologies and intentions, including: The collapse of support for democracy and rise of Nazism in Germany; The collapse of democratic government and rise of militarism in Japan; Pressure on Mussolini for a change of policy in Italy; Isolationist pressures on the USA
  - Failure of the World Disarmament Conference, 1932–34, including: Reasons for the Conference; Effect of international events; Attitude of major powers especially France and Germany
  - Changing relationship between the powers, including: London Naval Conference, 1930; Stresa Front, 1935: membership and purpose; Rome–Berlin Axis, 1936; Anti-Comintern Pact, 1936–37
  - Changing nature of relations with the USSR and impact on foreign policy, including: Soviet response to the rise of Nazism; Comintern and fear of Communism
- **What to cover and stress.** Great Depression and ideology: Germany (unemployment about 6 million by early 1932; Nazi vote 2.6 per cent (1928) to 37.3 per cent (July 1932); check), Japan (collapse of party government, militarism, the army in Manchuria), Italy (pressure on Mussolini to act abroad), USA (isolation, Neutrality Acts 1935–37). World Disarmament Conference (February 1932–1934): reasons, events (Manchuria, Hitler's arrival), French security vs German equality; Germany leaves (October 1933). London Naval Conference (1930); Stresa Front (April 1935: Britain, France, Italy; purpose; collapsed with Abyssinia and the Anglo-German Naval Agreement); Rome–Berlin Axis (October–November 1936); Anti-Comintern Pact (Germany–Japan November 1936; Italy November 1937). USSR: Soviet response to Nazism (Litvinov, collective security, League entry 1934, Franco-Soviet and Czech pacts 1935, Popular Fronts 1935), fear of communism in the West (Comintern).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Hitler's speeches on disarmament (e.g. 17 May 1933 'peace speech'); the Stresa declaration (1935); Mussolini's Milan speech on the 'axis' (1 November 1936); the Anti-Comintern Pact; Litvinov's speeches at the League; David Low's cartoons in the Evening Standard (visual).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why the World Disarmament Conference failed. P2(b): 'The Great Depression was the main reason for the rise of extremism in the 1930s.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far was the Stresa Front a serious attempt to contain Germany? P1 on Soviet foreign policy.
- **Historians you may name.** Zara Steiner, The Triumph of the Dark (2011); Richard Overy, The Origins of the Second World War (1987); A.J.P. Taylor, The Origins of the Second World War (1961).
- **Pitfalls.** Keep election percentages exact and cite (Reichstag elections 1928–33). This KQ is international relations, not domestic history of each state.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): Nazi vote share and German unemployment 1928–33; or a table.
- **Next line.** `Next: [International history, 1929–39 (2): why the League failed to keep the peace in the 1930s](#hist.int.1929.2)`

### hist.int.1929.2 · International history, 1929–39 (2): why the League failed to keep the peace in the 1930s (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS International option (Section C). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 8 International relations 1919–29: conflict and cooperation
- Key question 9.2 Why did the League of Nations fail to keep the peace in the 1930s?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Response of the League to major crises, including: Manchuria, 1931–33; Abyssinia, 1935–36
  - Changing attitudes of major powers towards the League of Nations, including: Withdrawal from membership of Japan, March 1933; Germany, October 1933; and Italy, 1936; Admission of the USSR, 1934; Negotiations taking place outside the League of Nations
  - Responses to the Spanish Civil War, including: Why the League of Nations did not respond; The creation of the Non-intervention Agreement, 1936; Reason for failure of the agreement, including: Intervention of Germany, Italy and the USSR, Lack of commitment of France and Britain, The International Brigades and other volunteers
  - Causes of the failure of the League of Nations, including: Unable to achieve widespread disarmament; Absence of major powers; Reliance on Britain and France; Effects of Great Depression; Increasing nationalism
- **What to cover and stress.** Manchuria (Mukden incident 18 September 1931; Manchukuo March 1932; Lytton Report October 1932; Japan leaves March 1933); Abyssinia (Walwal December 1934; invasion October 1935; limited sanctions, no oil sanctions, Suez left open; Hoare–Laval Pact December 1935; Haile Selassie's speech 30 June 1936; sanctions lifted July 1936; Italy leaves 1937 (the syllabus says 1936: note the formal notice was December 1937; say 'announced its withdrawal in December 1937')). Membership changes: Japan (notice March 1933), Germany (October 1933), Italy, USSR admitted September 1934; negotiations outside the League (Stresa, Munich). Spanish Civil War (July 1936–April 1939): why the League did not act, Non-Intervention Agreement and Committee (August–September 1936), why it failed (German and Italian intervention: Condor Legion, Guernica 26 April 1937; Soviet aid; British and French lack of commitment; International Brigades). Causes of failure: disarmament, absence of great powers, reliance on Britain and France, Depression, nationalism.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** The Lytton Report (1932); Haile Selassie's address to the League Assembly (30 June 1936); the Hoare–Laval proposals (1935); the Non-Intervention Agreement (1936); Picasso's Guernica (1937; visual); David Low's cartoons on the League (visual).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a) (the 2024 paper asked why the USSR was a League member only 1934–39: write another): Explain why the League failed to stop Italy invading Abyssinia. P2(b): 'The League failed in the 1930s because of the absence of the great powers.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far did the Spanish Civil War show the weakness of the League? P1 on Manchuria.
- **Historians you may name.** Christopher Thorne, The Limits of Foreign Policy (1972); Steiner; Ruth Henig.
- **Pitfalls.** Check the exact dates of withdrawals: notice is not departure (League membership ended two years after notice). The syllabus lists 'Italy, 1936': explain what happened and when.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of crises (date, aggressor, League response, outcome).
- **Next line.** `Next: [International history, 1929–39 (3): appeasement: why, and with what effects](#hist.int.1929.3)`
