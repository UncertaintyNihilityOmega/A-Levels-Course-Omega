### hist.us.depression.1 · Crash, Depression and New Deal (1): the causes of the Great Crash and the Great Depression (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 6 The Great Crash, the Great Depression and the New Deal, 1920–41
- Key question 6.1 What were the causes of the Great Crash and the Great Depression?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The Republican administrations of 1921–29, including: Economic policy under the Harding and Coolidge administrations; The post-war economic boom; Unequal distribution of wealth; Urban/rural divide
  - The causes of the Great Crash, including: A decline in confidence in the American economy; The stock market boom and lack of regulation; Speculation and the overvaluation of stocks
  - The causes of the Great Depression, including: The impact of the First World War on the US and international trade; Structural weaknesses in the US economy and the banking system, and the role of the Federal Reserve; Agriculture, the old and new industries, mass production and oversupply
- **What to cover and stress.** Harding (1921–23) and Coolidge (1923–29): Mellon's tax cuts, high tariffs (Fordney–McCumber 1922), laissez-faire, 'normalcy'; the boom (cars: Ford, mass production, credit, electrification, consumer goods, advertising); unequal distribution of wealth (the top 5 per cent's share of income; give a source); urban–rural divide and the farm depression from 1920–21; causes of the Crash: confidence, the bull market, buying on the margin, investment trusts, little regulation, overvaluation; the Crash (Black Thursday 24 October, Black Tuesday 29 October 1929); causes of the Depression: First World War debts and reparations, tariffs and trade (Smoot–Hawley 1930), structural weaknesses (old industries: coal, textiles, railroads), overproduction and underconsumption, banking (thousands of small unit banks), the Federal Reserve (1928–29 policy, failure to stop bank collapses), agriculture.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Coolidge's address to the American Society of Newspaper Editors (1925: 'the chief business of the American people is business'); Hoover's 'rugged individualism' campaign speech (22 October 1928); newspaper headlines of October 1929; Federal Reserve statements; cartoons of the speculation boom (visual); J.K. Galbraith, The Great Crash 1929 (1954) as a historian, not a source.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why there was a stock market boom in the late 1920s. P2(b) (specimen asked whether government policies were responsible: write another): 'The Wall Street Crash was the main cause of the Great Depression.' How far do you agree? P1 on the causes of the Crash.
- **Historians you may name.** J.K. Galbraith, The Great Crash 1929 (1954); Milton Friedman and Anna Schwartz, A Monetary History of the United States (1963: the Fed's failure); Charles Kindleberger, The World in Depression 1929–1939 (1973: no international leader); Peter Temin.
- **Pitfalls.** The Crash and the Depression are different events with different causes: keep them apart. Statistics: Dow Jones peak 381.17 (3 September 1929), low 41.22 (8 July 1932); verify any figure you use.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (from P5, if built): Dow Jones Industrial Average 1925–32, and unemployment 1929–33, with sources.

### hist.us.depression.2 · Crash, Depression and New Deal (2): the impact of the Crash and the Depression, and Hoover's response (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 6 The Great Crash, the Great Depression and the New Deal, 1920–41
- Key question 6.2 What was the impact of the Great Crash and the Great Depression in the United States?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The impact of the Great Crash, including: The drop in the value of shares and the crisis of confidence; Bankruptcy for banks, insurance companies and individuals; The demand for greater regulation
  - The impact of the Great Depression, including: Mass unemployment, agricultural depression and the collapse of industry; Deflation; The lack of welfare, Hoovervilles, employment discrimination and the impact on families and minorities
  - The response of the Hoover Administration to the Great Depression, including: The conflicting views over solutions to the Great Depression; The support for agriculture, home loans, the Reconstruction Finance Corporation (RFC) and the Relief and Reconstruction Act; The treatment of the Bonus Marchers and degree of support for welfare
  - The election of 1932 and creation of the New Deal coalition
- **What to cover and stress.** Impact of the Crash: share values, confidence, bank runs and failures (about 9,000 banks failed 1930–33; check), insurance companies and individuals, demand for regulation. Depression: unemployment (about 3 per cent in 1929 to about 25 per cent in 1933; check), farm prices and foreclosures, collapse of industrial output, deflation, no federal welfare, Hoovervilles, discrimination in employment, impact on families, African Americans, Mexican Americans (repatriation), women. Hoover: voluntarism, conferences with business, Agricultural Marketing Act (1929), Federal Farm Board, Smoot–Hawley, public works, Federal Home Loan Bank Act (1932), Reconstruction Finance Corporation (January 1932), Emergency Relief and Construction Act (July 1932), moratorium on war debts (1931); the Bonus Army (July 1932; MacArthur). Election of 1932 (Roosevelt about 57 per cent of the popular vote, 472 to 59 electoral votes) and the New Deal coalition (urban workers, ethnic groups, Southern whites, farmers, later African Americans).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Hoover's speeches defending voluntarism; Roosevelt's acceptance speech (2 July 1932: 'a new deal for the American people'); photographs of Hoovervilles and the Bonus Army (visual); letters to the Roosevelts and to Hoover from ordinary Americans; newspaper reports of bank runs.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Roosevelt won the 1932 presidential election. P2(b): 'Hoover did nothing to tackle the Depression.' How far do you agree? P2(b): Which groups suffered most in the Depression? (as 'How far were minorities the main victims…?'). P1 on the Bonus Army.
- **Historians you may name.** David Kennedy, Freedom from Fear (1999); Joan Hoff Wilson, Herbert Hoover: Forgotten Progressive (1975); Robert McElvaine, The Great Depression (1984).
- **Pitfalls.** Hoover was not inactive: weigh what he did against its scale and his principles. Check every statistic against a cited source.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart: unemployment and bank failures 1929–33.
