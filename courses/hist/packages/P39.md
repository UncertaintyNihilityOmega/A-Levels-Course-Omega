### hist.d.malaysia.3 · The route to independence (3): the Indonesian nationalist movement to 1945 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 9 The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909–67
- Key question 9.3 In what ways, and for what reasons, did the nationalist movement develop in Indonesia up to 1945?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The nature of colonial rule and effects of foreign influence: Impacts of the Ethical Policy and the Volksraad; Domination of trade by Chinese merchants; Impacts of language and religion; Impact of westernised education
  - The growth of nationalism, including: Formation and aims of different nationalist parties, including: Partai Komunis Indonesia (PKI), Partai Nasional Indonesia (PNI), Partai Indonesia (Partindo) and Gerindo, Club Pendidikan Nasional Indonesia; Leadership of Sukarno, Hatta and Sartono; The Soetardjo Petition, 1936; Formation and aims of Gabungun Politik Indonesia (Gapi), 1939
  - Economic and social effects of Japanese occupation, including: Impact of joining the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere; Impact of conscription; Changes to the administration; Formation of the Triple A movement
  - Reasons for and methods used by Japan to encourage nationalism, including: Anti-western sentiment and support of Indonesians for the war effort; Promises of self-government; Formation and importance of, Pusat Tenaga Rakyat (Putera), Sukarela Tentera Pembela Tanah Air (Peta); Formation and importance of underground resistance
- **What to cover and stress.** Dutch colonial rule: the Ethical Policy (1901), Volksraad (1918), Chinese domination of trade, language (Malay as a lingua franca; Youth Pledge 28 October 1928: one nation, one people, one language) and religion (Sarekat Islam 1912, Muhammadiyah 1912), Western education and a new elite (Budi Utomo 1908). Nationalist parties: PKI (1920; risings 1926–27 crushed), PNI (1927; Sukarno; arrested 1929, trial and 'Indonesia Accuses'), Partindo (1931) and Gerindo (1937), Club Pendidikan Nasional Indonesia (PNI-Baru, 1931; Hatta and Sjahrir), leadership of Sukarno, Hatta and Sartono, the Soetardjo Petition (1936; rejected 1938), GAPI (1939; 'Indonesia Berparlemen'). Japanese occupation (March 1942–August 1945): Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, romusha forced labour (estimates vary widely; say so), conscription, administration changes (Indonesians in posts), the Triple A movement (1942), why Japan encouraged nationalism (anti-Western feeling, war effort, promises of self-government: Koiso declaration September 1944), Putera (1943), PETA (October 1943), underground resistance (Sjahrir).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Japanese occupation was the most important factor in the growth of Indonesian nationalism.' Evaluate this view. Assess the importance of Sukarno's leadership before 1945. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** M.C. Ricklefs, A History of Modern Indonesia since c.1200 (4th edition 2008); George Kahin, Nationalism and Revolution in Indonesia (1952); Benedict Anderson, Java in a Time of Revolution (1972).
- **Pitfalls.** Spellings: Indonesian spelling changed in 1972 (Soekarno to Sukarno): use modern spelling, mention the old once. Many Indonesians have one name.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of nationalist organisations (year, leader, aims, fate).
- **Next line.** `Next: [The route to independence (4): Indonesia: independence and challenges to the mid-1960s](#hist.d.malaysia.4)`

### hist.d.malaysia.4 · The route to independence (4): Indonesia: independence and challenges to the mid-1960s (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 9 The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909–67
- Key question 9.4 What factors led to the formation of Indonesia and what challenges did it face up to by the mid-1960s?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Attempts to secure Indonesian independence by the Japanese: 1944 Preparatory Committee for Indonesian Independence; Role of nationalist leaders including Sukarno and Hatta; Student protest; Sukarno’s five principles (Pancasila)
  - The Indonesian Revolution and reasons for the failure of the Dutch to re-establish control: Reactions to the declaration of independence, August 1945; Re-occupation, peace talks and the attempted coup, 1946; Reasons for the failure of the Linggadjati Agreement; Impact of the involvement of the United Nations and the USA
  - Challenges facing the newly independent Republic of Indonesia in the 1950s, including: Political challenges: debates about the Constitution and form of government; Leadership of Sukarno; Economic problems; Social issues
  - Key events and reasons for the transition to the New Order under Suharto: Impact of the Konfrontasi with Malaysia in 1963; 30 September Movement; Purge of communists and KAMI demonstration; Order of Eleventh March (Supersemar)
- **What to cover and stress.** BPUPK (Investigating Committee, 1945) and PPKI (Preparatory Committee, August 1945; the syllabus says 1944 Preparatory Committee: explain the committees accurately), Sukarno and Hatta, student pressure (the Rengasdengklok kidnapping 16 August), Pancasila (Sukarno's speech 1 June 1945), proclamation (17 August 1945). Revolution (1945–49): reactions, British landing and Surabaya (November 1945), Dutch re-occupation, peace talks, the attempted coup (3 July 1946 affair), Linggadjati Agreement (November 1946, signed March 1947) and its failure, first 'police action' (July 1947), Renville (January 1948), Madiun (September 1948), second 'police action' (December 1948), UN and US pressure (Marshall Aid threat), Round Table Conference (1949), sovereignty transferred 27 December 1949. 1950s: unitary state (1950), constitution debates, parliamentary democracy and the 1955 election, regional rebellions (PRRI/Permesta 1958), Sukarno's Guided Democracy (1957–59; 1945 constitution restored 5 July 1959), economic problems (inflation, nationalisation of Dutch firms 1957), social issues. Transition to the New Order: Konfrontasi with Malaysia (1963–66), the 30 September Movement (1 October 1965; generals killed), the purge of communists (killings 1965–66: estimates from about 500,000 to 1 million; say so), KAMI demonstrations (1966), Supersemar (11 March 1966), Suharto acting president (1967).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Dutch failed to re-establish control mainly because of international pressure.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the transition to Suharto's New Order. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Ricklefs; Kahin; Robert Cribb (ed.), The Indonesian Killings 1965–1966 (1990); John Roosa, Pretext for Mass Murder (2006).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: the 1965–66 killings; responsibility debated: present it factually with sources.
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline 1945–67; table of agreements (Linggadjati, Renville, Round Table).
- **Next line.** `Next: [Paper 1: Historical sources](#hist.exam.p1)`
