### hist.us.civilwar.3 · Civil War and Reconstruction (3): why the Civil War lasted four years (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 4 The Civil War and Reconstruction, 1820–77
- Key question 4.3 Why did the Civil War last four years?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The military strategies and military leadership of the two sides, including: The Anaconda Plan of the North; Defensive and offensive strategy of the South; The strengths and weaknesses of the military leadership of both sides
  - The resources available to the two sides, including: Industry; Population; Infrastructure
  - The political leadership of the two sides, including: The roles of Lincoln and Davis; Conscription, taxation and the management of resources; Retaining the support of the Border States; Reasons for, and consequences of, the Emancipation Proclamation; Limitations on civil liberties
  - The role of foreign powers, including: Great Britain; France; Russia
- **What to cover and stress.** Strategy: Anaconda Plan (Scott); Southern defensive war and offensives (Antietam September 1862, Gettysburg July 1863); leadership (Lee, Jackson; McClellan, Grant, Sherman; Grant as general-in-chief 1864); resources (population about 22 million vs 9 million including about 3.5 million enslaved; industry; railways; check figures), infrastructure; political leadership: Lincoln vs Davis; conscription (Confederacy April 1862, Union 1863; New York draft riots July 1863), taxation (income tax), paper money (greenbacks; Confederate inflation), border states (Kentucky, Maryland, Missouri, Delaware), Emancipation Proclamation (preliminary 22 September 1862; 1 January 1863) and its reasons and effects (Black soldiers, about 180,000), civil liberties (habeas corpus suspended; Copperheads; Vallandigham). Foreign powers: Britain (Trent affair 1861, cotton, Alabama), France (Mexico), Russia (fleet visits 1863). Why four years: Southern defensive advantages, size of territory, early Union leadership failures, Confederate will, the 1864 election; end: Atlanta (September 1864), Sherman's march, Appomattox (9 April 1865).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** The Emancipation Proclamation (1 January 1863); Lincoln's letter to Horace Greeley (22 August 1862); the Gettysburg Address (19 November 1863); Jefferson Davis's messages to the Confederate Congress; Sherman's letter to the mayor of Atlanta (September 1864); Union and Confederate recruiting posters; Punch cartoons on the war (British opinion).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation. P2(b): 'The North won the Civil War because of its greater resources.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far was poor Northern military leadership the reason the war lasted four years? P1 on British attitudes.
- **Historians you may name.** McPherson (contingency); David Donald (ed.), Why the North Won the Civil War (1960); Gary Gallagher, The Confederate War (1997).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: casualties (about 620,000–750,000 deaths; give the range and the source of the higher estimate, J. David Hacker 2011). The Emancipation Proclamation did not free enslaved people in the loyal border states.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built) or a table comparing Union and Confederate resources in 1860 with sources.
- **Next line.** `Next: [Civil War and Reconstruction (4): the aims and success of Reconstruction](#hist.us.civilwar.4)`

### hist.us.civilwar.4 · Civil War and Reconstruction (4): the aims and success of Reconstruction (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 4 The Civil War and Reconstruction, 1820–77
- Key question 4.4 What were the aims of Reconstruction and how successful was it?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The problems facing the United States at the end of the Civil War, including: The conflicting views of the Radicals, Conservatives and Copperheads; Economic recovery and the management and reintegration of the South
  - The roles of Lincoln, Johnson and Congress, including: Lincoln, the Wade-Davis Bill and his legacy; The policies of Johnson between 1865 and 1869; Congress and Radical Reconstruction; The reasons for, and impact of, the 13th, 14th and 15th Constitutional Amendments
  - The changing position of former slaves and responses of the White South, including: The Freedmen’s Bureau and sharecropping; Carpetbaggers, the Black Codes and the rise of the Ku Klux Klan; The rise in violence; The growing dominance of whites in the governments and legislatures of the South
  - Grant’s Reconstruction policies, the Compromise of 1877 and the end of Reconstruction, including: The Force Acts, the Amnesty Act, the Civil Rights Act and Grant’s support for them; The reasons for, and the results of, the Compromise of 1877; The extent to which Reconstruction should be seen as a success or a failure
- **What to cover and stress.** Problems in 1865: devastation, 4 million freed people, readmitting the South; Radicals (Stevens, Sumner), Conservatives, Copperheads. Lincoln's Ten Percent Plan (1863), the Wade–Davis Bill (1864) and pocket veto; assassination (14–15 April 1865). Johnson's Presidential Reconstruction (1865–66): pardons, Black Codes, vetoes (Freedmen's Bureau, Civil Rights Act 1866), 1866 elections; Radical (Congressional) Reconstruction: Reconstruction Acts (1867; military districts), Tenure of Office Act, impeachment (1868; acquitted by one vote). 13th (1865), 14th (1868), 15th (1870) Amendments. Freedmen's Bureau (1865); sharecropping; schools and churches; Black officeholders (e.g. Hiram Revels, first Black senator, 1870); carpetbaggers and scalawags; Ku Klux Klan (1865–66 founding; violence; Colfax massacre 1873); 'Redeemers'. Grant (1869–77): Enforcement/Force Acts (1870–71), Ku Klux Klan Act (1871), Amnesty Act (1872), Civil Rights Act (1875); Panic of 1873; Northern retreat; disputed election of 1876 and the Compromise of 1877 (troops withdrawn). Success or failure.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** The Wade–Davis Manifesto (1864); Mississippi's Black Code (1865); Johnson's veto message on the Civil Rights Act (1866); the 14th Amendment; Thomas Nast's cartoons in Harper's Weekly (e.g. on the Klan and the White League, 1874; visual); testimony to the congressional Klan hearings (1871); Frederick Douglass's speeches on Reconstruction.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Congress impeached Johnson in 1868. P2(b) (the specimen used 'Grant made Reconstruction a success': write another): 'Reconstruction failed because of Southern white resistance.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far did the position of former slaves improve between 1865 and 1877? P1 on the Black Codes.
- **Historians you may name.** Eric Foner, Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution, 1863–1877 (1988); W.E.B. Du Bois, Black Reconstruction in America (1935); the discredited Dunning School (explain why it was discredited).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: racial violence; factual, respectful, name victims' communities. Avoid Dunning-School language ('Black misrule', 'tragic era') except as historiography.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of the three amendments (date, what each did, how it was later undermined).
- **Next line.** `Next: [Gilded Age and Progressive Era (1): why there was rapid industrialisation after 1870](#hist.us.gilded.1)`
