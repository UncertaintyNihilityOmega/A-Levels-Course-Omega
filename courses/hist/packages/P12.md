### hist.us.depression.3 · Crash, Depression and New Deal (3): how successful Roosevelt's first New Deal was (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 6 The Great Crash, the Great Depression and the New Deal, 1920–41
- Key question 6.3 How successful was Roosevelt’s first New Deal?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The situation in March 1933 and the new administration: The growing crisis between January and March 1933; The new administration and the Brains Trust; The Democratic majority in Congress
  - The First 100 Days, including: Congressional legislation; Executive actions; The new Administrations
  - The Impact of the New Deal and the Alphabet Administrations, including: The support for agriculture, business and industry; The regulation of banking, finance, the stock market, agriculture and industry; The creation of jobs, the support for families and the destitute
  - Critics and opponents of the New Deal and the impact of the Supreme Court decisions: Critics from the Left, including socialists and communists, Townsend, Coughlin and Long; Critics from the Right, including Republicans, Southern Democrats, industry, business and the press; The Supreme Court’s rulings on the New Deal; Roosevelt’s Court packing plan and his new appointments to the Court
- **What to cover and stress.** Interregnum crisis (January–March 1933: bank runs, state bank holidays); inauguration 4 March 1933; Brains Trust (Moley, Tugwell, Berle), Frances Perkins, Hopkins, Ickes; Democratic majorities. First 100 Days (9 March–16 June 1933): bank holiday and Emergency Banking Act, fireside chats, Economy Act, Beer and Wine Revenue Act, CCC, FERA, AAA, TVA, Securities Act, HOLC, Glass–Steagall Banking Act (FDIC), NIRA (NRA and PWA); gold standard left; executive actions. Impact: agriculture (prices and incomes up, sharecroppers evicted), business and industry (NRA codes, Blue Eagle), regulation (SEC 1934), jobs and relief (CWA winter 1933–34). Critics: left (socialists, communists, Townsend Plan, Coughlin, Huey Long's Share Our Wealth 1934); right (Republicans, Southern Democrats, business, American Liberty League 1934, press). Supreme Court: Schechter (May 1935, NIRA), Butler (January 1936, AAA); court-packing plan (February 1937) and new appointments (here or in KQ 6.4; coordinate: introduce in 6.3, analyse in 6.4).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Roosevelt's First Inaugural (4 March 1933: 'the only thing we have to fear is fear itself'); the first fireside chat (12 March 1933); Huey Long's 'Every Man a King' broadcast (1934); Father Coughlin's broadcasts; Liberty League pamphlets; cartoons on the NRA and on the Court plan (visual).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a) (specimen asked about the First 100 Days: write another): Explain why the Supreme Court struck down parts of the New Deal. P2(b): How successful was the First New Deal in reviving the economy? P2(b): 'Critics of the New Deal were more of a threat from the left than from the right.' How far do you agree? P1 on the NRA.
- **Historians you may name.** William Leuchtenburg, Franklin D. Roosevelt and the New Deal (1963); Anthony Badger, The New Deal (1989); Kennedy.
- **Pitfalls.** 'First' and 'Second' New Deal are historians' labels with blurred dates (1933–34; 1935–38): say so. Do not list alphabet agencies without saying what each did and how well.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of the main agencies (letters, full name, year, aim, success, fate in court).

### hist.us.depression.4 · Crash, Depression and New Deal (4): the Second New Deal and its impact by 1941 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS American option (Section B). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 6 The Great Crash, the Great Depression and the New Deal, 1920–41
- Key question 6.4 Why was there a second New Deal and how successful was it?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The election of 1936 and Roosevelt’s political skills, including: Maintaining the New Deal coalition; The popularity of the New Deal and the nature of Democratic support; Roosevelt’s communication skills and use of patronage
  - The reasons for a second New Deal and its development, including: Roosevelt’s motives for the legislation of 1935–37; The principal legislation of 1935–37; The impact and importance of the Second New Deal
  - The recession of 1937–38, including: The causes of the recession; The impact on employment, business and society; The reasons for its decline
  - The impact of the Second New Deal by 1941: Groups that benefitted from the New Deal; Groups who did not benefit from the New Deal; Changes to the role of the American government; Economic recovery
- **What to cover and stress.** 1936 election (Roosevelt about 61 per cent, 523 to 8 electoral votes; check) and the coalition; FDR's communication (fireside chats, press conferences) and patronage; motives for 1935–37 laws (pressure from left critics, Court decisions, 1934 midterms, the economy); Second New Deal: WPA (1935), Social Security Act (August 1935), Wagner Act (July 1935; NLRB), Revenue Act (1935, 'soak the rich'), Resettlement Administration, Rural Electrification Administration, Banking Act 1935, Fair Labor Standards Act (1938); court-packing and the 'switch in time' (West Coast Hotel 1937); recession 1937–38 (budget cuts, Fed tightening, Social Security taxes; unemployment back up to about 19 per cent; check), recovery with rearmament. By 1941: who benefited (organised labour, the elderly, farmers with land, some unemployed), who did not (sharecroppers and tenants, African Americans in the South, domestic and agricultural workers excluded from Social Security, women), changes to government (bigger federal role, welfare state, regulation), economic recovery (unemployment still about 15 per cent in 1940; GNP; check).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Roosevelt's Second Inaugural (20 January 1937: 'one-third of a nation ill-housed, ill-clad, ill-nourished'); his Madison Square Garden speech (31 October 1936); the Social Security Act; WPA posters (visual); letters from African Americans to the NAACP or to Eleanor Roosevelt; statistics on unemployment.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Roosevelt introduced a Second New Deal in 1935. P2(b): 'The New Deal achieved economic recovery by 1941.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far did the New Deal benefit African Americans? P1 on the 1937 court plan.
- **Historians you may name.** Leuchtenburg; Badger; Ira Katznelson, When Affirmative Action Was White (2005); Barton Bernstein (New Left critique, 1968).
- **Pitfalls.** Recovery came with war spending after 1940: use the figures to judge. The New Deal's record on race was mixed: give both the exclusions and the gains.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart: unemployment 1929–41 with the 1937–38 recession visible.
