### hist.d.usa61.1 · A time of challenges, 1961–74 (1): the presidencies of Kennedy, Johnson and Nixon (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 5 A time of challenges: the USA, 1961–74
- Key question 5.1 How significant were the presidencies of Kennedy, Johnson and Nixon for the USA?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The reasons for Kennedy’s election victory in 1960, including: Kennedy’s New Frontier; Kennedy’s youth; Kennedy’s campaigning tactics and skills; The importance of Johnson; Nixon’s campaign and the importance of the African American vote
  - Kennedy’s domestic policies and the New Frontier: Measures to stimulate the economy; Support for the poor; Housing and welfare reforms; Healthcare; Education; Equal rights for women; Support for farmers; Civil rights
  - Johnson and the policies of the Great Society: Johnson’s vision of a Great Society; The war on poverty; Medical care; Civil rights; Education reform; Urban renewal, rural development, environmental initiatives; Reasons for opposition to the reforms; The impact of the reforms
  - The domestic policies of Nixon and his fall from power: Reasons for Nixon’s election victory in 1968, the New Economic Policy and New Federalism; Environmental reforms; Civil Rights; Watergate and Nixon’s resignation in 1974
- **What to cover and stress.** 1960 election (New Frontier, youth, TV debates 26 September 1960, campaigning, Johnson and Texas, Nixon's campaign, African American vote after Kennedy's call to Coretta Scott King; result 303–219 electoral votes and about 0.2 per cent of the popular vote; check). Kennedy domestic: economy (tax-cut proposal, minimum wage, Area Redevelopment Act 1961), poor, housing (Housing Act 1961), healthcare (Medicare failed), education (aid failed), women (President's Commission 1961, Equal Pay Act 1963), farmers, civil rights (cautious; Executive Order 10925; civil rights bill June 1963). Johnson: Great Society (May 1964), War on Poverty (Economic Opportunity Act 1964), Medicare and Medicaid (1965), Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965, Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965), Higher Education Act, HUD (1965), urban renewal, environment (Wilderness Act 1964), opposition (cost, Vietnam, conservatives, riots), impact (poverty rate from about 19 per cent in 1964 to about 11–12 per cent in 1973; check). Nixon: 1968 election (law and order, Southern strategy, Wallace; Humphrey), New Economic Policy (August 1971: wage and price controls, end of gold convertibility), New Federalism (revenue sharing), environment (EPA 1970, Clean Air Act 1970), civil rights (Philadelphia Plan, school desegregation progress), Watergate (17 June 1972 break-in; tapes; resignation 9 August 1974).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Johnson's Great Society was more successful than Kennedy's New Frontier.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for Nixon's resignation in 1974. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Robert Dallek (Kennedy, Johnson biographies); Joan Hoff, Nixon Reconsidered (1994); Stanley Kutler, The Wars of Watergate (1990).
- **Pitfalls.** Nixon's domestic record was more liberal than his image: weigh it.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of major laws by president.

### hist.d.usa61.2 · A time of challenges, 1961–74 (2): Cuba and Vietnam (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 5 A time of challenges: the USA, 1961–74
- Key question 5.2 How well did US governments deal with Cuba and Vietnam?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Kennedy and the Cuban Missile Crisis: Kennedy’s view of the Castro regime; the importance of the Bay of Pigs; Discovery of Soviet missiles in Cuba and Kennedy’s options; The exchanges between Kennedy and Khrushchev; The outcomes for the US, relations with the USSR
  - Reasons for US involvement in Vietnam: The Geneva Accords 1954 and the situation in Vietnam; Fears of Communism – Eisenhower and the Domino Theory; US support for Ngo Dinh Diem’s regime and its fall; Reasons for increased US military involvement under Kennedy
  - Escalation under Johnson and US strategy in Vietnam: The Gulf of Tonkin Incident, 1964; Escalation in 1965: increase in troops numbers; The effectiveness of US tactics in South Vietnam 1965–68; Bombing and Operation Rolling Thunder
  - US withdrawal and the ending of the war: The role of the US media, My Lai, different attitudes in the US towards the war; The importance of the Tet Offensive; Nixon’s policy and promise of peace with honour; Increase in bombing, invasion of Cambodia; Vietnamisation and the withdrawal of US troops; The Paris peace talks; The fall of Saigon
- **What to cover and stress.** Cuba: Castro (1959), Bay of Pigs (17–19 April 1961), Operation Mongoose, Soviet missiles discovered (U-2 photos 14 October 1962), ExComm options (air strike, invasion, blockade 'quarantine' 22 October), Kennedy–Khrushchev letters (26 and 27 October), Jupiter missiles deal, resolution (28 October), outcomes (hotline 1963, Test Ban Treaty August 1963). Vietnam: Geneva Accords (1954), Diem, domino theory, US support, Strategic Hamlets, Buddhist crisis, coup (1 November 1963), Kennedy's advisers (about 16,000 by 1963; check), Gulf of Tonkin (2 and 4 August 1964; resolution 7 August), escalation (Marines at Da Nang March 1965; troops to 184,000 by end of 1965, peak about 543,000 in 1969; check), tactics (search and destroy, Agent Orange, napalm), Rolling Thunder (1965–68), media, My Lai (March 1968; revealed 1969), Tet Offensive (January 1968), Johnson's withdrawal from the election (31 March 1968), Nixon's 'peace with honour', Vietnamisation, bombing, Cambodia (1970), Paris Peace Accords (27 January 1973), fall of Saigon (30 April 1975).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Kennedy's handling of the Cuban Missile Crisis was a success.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for US withdrawal from Vietnam. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Graham Allison, Essence of Decision (1971); Sheldon Stern; George Herring, America's Longest War (1979); Fredrik Logevall, Choosing War (1999).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: civilian deaths (My Lai): factual. Casualty and troop figures: cite.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): US troop levels in Vietnam 1960–73.
- **Next line.** `Next: [A time of challenges, 1961–74 (3): civil rights for African Americans](#hist.d.usa61.3)`
