### hist.eu.germany.1 · Germany (1): the causes of the revolutions of 1848 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 2 Liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815–71
- Key question 2.1 What were the causes of the Revolutions of 1848?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The Congress of Vienna and the formation of the Confederation: Strengths and weaknesses of the Confederation
  - The impact of Metternich’s system on the States of Germany, including: The Carlsbad Decrees
  - The influence of Liberal ideas: The aims of the Liberals; The emergence of the middle class; The extent of support for liberalism in Prussia
  - The growth of nationalist ideas: The impact of the 1830 revolutions on German states; Support for nationalist ideas in the universities; Reactions to the growth of nationalist ideas, The Six Articles and Ten Articles, 1832; Reasons for the growth of nationalism in the 1840s
  - The economic and political impact of: Prussian Customs Union and the Zollverein; Railway development
  - Problems facing the German States in the 1840s: Economic and social problems in the 1840s, including: The economic crisis of 1846–47, Impact of urbanisation, Impact of industrialisation on skilled workers
- **What to cover and stress.** Congress of Vienna 1815 and the German Confederation (39 states; Diet at Frankfurt under Austrian presidency; strengths: peace, defence; weaknesses: no common army, economy or law, Austria's veto). Metternich's system: Carlsbad Decrees (1819, after the murder of Kotzebue by the student Sand), censorship, university supervision; Wartburg Festival (1817). Liberalism: constitutions, rights, the middle class (Bildungsbürgertum), southern constitutions (Baden, Bavaria, Württemberg); Prussia's limited liberalism (no constitution until 1848 despite 1815 promise; United Diet 1847). Nationalism: 1830 revolutions (Brunswick, Saxony, Hanover, Hesse-Cassel constitutions); Hambach Festival (May 1832); the Six Articles (June 1832) and Ten Articles (July 1832); Burschenschaften; Rhine crisis 1840 and national songs; reasons for growth in the 1840s. Zollverein (Prussian customs union 1818; Zollverein 1 January 1834; Austria outside) and railways (Nuremberg–Fürth 1835; about 6,000 km by 1850, check figure). 1840s problems: population growth, the Silesian weavers' revolt (1844), potato blight and harvest failure 1845–47, crisis of 1846–47 (food prices, unemployment, credit), urbanisation, industrialisation and artisans.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** The Carlsbad Decrees (1819); speeches at the Hambach Festival (1832); the Six Articles (1832); Heinrich Heine's poem 'The Silesian Weavers' (1844); Hoffmann von Fallersleben's 'Das Lied der Deutschen' (1841); Friedrich List's writings on railways and a national economy; the Prussian United Diet debates (1847).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Metternich issued the Carlsbad Decrees. P2(b): 'Economic distress was the main cause of the 1848 revolutions in Germany.' How far do you agree? P2(b) (specimen style): 'The Zollverein's role was economic not political.' How far do you agree? (Specimen used this: write a different question.) P1(a) compare Hambach speeches and the Six Articles as evidence about nationalism; P1(b) how far the sources show liberalism had wide support before 1848.
- **Historians you may name.** William Carr, A History of Germany 1815–1990; Thomas Nipperdey, Germany from Napoleon to Bismarck (1983, English 1996); Jonathan Sperber, The European Revolutions 1848–1851 (1994).
- **Pitfalls.** Germany did not exist as a state: say 'the German states' or 'the Confederation'. The Zollverein excluded Austria; it was an economic union, its political effect is the debate. Liberalism and nationalism are different (liberals wanted rights and constitutions; nationalists unity).
- **Figures and widgets.** Schematic SVG of the German Confederation (Austria, Prussia, the 'Third Germany' middle states) with the Zollverein marked, or a table. hist-chart widget (only if built) for railway kilometres 1835–50 from a cited source.

### hist.eu.germany.2 · Germany (2): the 1848–49 revolutions and their consequences (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 2 Liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815–71
- Key question 2.2 What happened during the 1848–49 revolutions and what were their consequences?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The spread of revolution in the German states: The fall of Metternich; Revolution in Prussia; Role of Friedrich Wilhelm IV of Prussia
  - The Frankfurt Parliament: Formation and actions of the Parliament; Reasons for its collapse; Impact of the 1848 revolutions
  - Reasons for the reassertion of Austrian control: The strength of the conservatives; Divisions between the revolutionaries
  - Changing relations between Austria and Prussia after 1848: The humiliation of Olmütz; Economic issues, including: Disagreements over the Zollverein, Economic development and growth in Prussia, Economic and financial problems facing Austria; Political issues, including: The growth of liberalism, Prussia’s international position, Bismarck’s role up to 1859, including his attitude towards Austria
- **What to cover and stress.** March 1848: fall of Metternich (13 March) and Vienna; Berlin barricades (18 March), Frederick William IV's 'To my dear Berliners' and his ride in the black-red-gold colours (21 March); Pre-Parliament; Frankfurt Parliament (opened 18 May 1848; St Paul's Church; professors and officials; Heinrich von Gagern; Archduke John as Regent); actions: Basic Rights (December 1848), Schleswig-Holstein crisis and the Malmö armistice (August 1848), Grossdeutsch vs Kleindeutsch, constitution of March 1849, the crown offered to Frederick William IV (refused 3 April 1849); collapse (no army, no money, divisions, refusal of the crown, rump parliament dispersed June 1849). Reassertion of Austrian control: Windischgrätz (Prague June, Vienna October 1848), Schwarzenberg, Russian help in Hungary (1849); Prussian counter-revolution (November–December 1848, imposed constitution, three-class franchise 1849). Divisions: liberals vs radicals, workers vs middle class, nationalities. Erfurt Union (1849–50) and the humiliation of Olmütz (29 November 1850). 1850s: Austria's attempts to join or break the Zollverein; Prussian economic growth (coal, iron, railways); Austrian financial problems and the Crimean War isolation; growth of liberalism (Nationalverein 1859); Prussia's position; Bismarck at the Frankfurt Diet (1851–59) and his hostility to Austria.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Frederick William IV's proclamations of 19 and 21 March 1848; the Basic Rights of the German People (December 1848); Frederick William IV's refusal of the imperial crown (April 1849) and his private letters on it; the Punctation of Olmütz (1850); Bismarck's dispatches from Frankfurt in the 1850s on Austria.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why the Frankfurt Parliament failed. P2(b): To what extent was the humiliation of Olmütz a victory for Austria? P2(b): How far was the strength of the conservatives the main reason for the failure of the 1848 revolutions? P1 sources on Frederick William IV's attitude to the revolution.
- **Historians you may name.** Lewis Namier, 1848: The Revolution of the Intellectuals (1944); A.J.P. Taylor ('the turning point at which German history failed to turn'); Christopher Clark, Iron Kingdom (2006).
- **Pitfalls.** Taylor's phrase is often misquoted: use it only with care, as a view. Austria was not part of the Zollverein. Olmütz was a Prussian humiliation but the Confederation restored was no Austrian triumph either: weigh it.
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline table March 1848–November 1850; a two-column SVG or table comparing Austria and Prussia in the 1850s (economy, army, diplomacy).
- **Next line.** `Next: [Germany (3): Bismarck's appointment and his aims to 1866](#hist.eu.germany.3)`
