### hist.d.safrica.1 · End of minority rule (1): the apartheid state, 1948–59 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 8 End of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe, 1948–94
- Key question 8.1 How was the apartheid state established in South Africa and what was its impact from 1948 to 1959?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Life in South Africa, c.1948: Economic development: mining, manufacturing and agriculture; The lives and work of Black South Africans: segregation, discrimination, life in townships, and in urban and rural areas
  - The reasons for the election victory of the National Party in 1948, including: The impact of the Second World War; The National Party’s appeal to Afrikaner nationalism; The campaign of Smuts and the United Party; The electoral system
  - The introduction and impact of apartheid measures: The apartheid legislation; The pass system; The creation of Bantustans; Changes to education for Black South Africans; The impact of these measures on people’s everyday lives 1948 to 1959? continued
  - Opposition to, and enforcement of, apartheid: The African National Congress (ANC) and the Pan Africanist Congress (PAC); The Defiance Campaign, 1952, resistance to the pass laws, including women and the anti-pass law march of 1956; The Freedom Charter of 1955; Government suppression, and the Treason Trials of the late 1950s
- **What to cover and stress.** South Africa c.1948: mining (gold), manufacturing growth in the war, agriculture; Black South Africans' lives (migrant labour, reserves (1913 and 1936 Land Acts), townships, urban and rural poverty, existing segregation). 1948 election: war's impact (urbanisation, Black worker militancy, 1946 mineworkers' strike), Afrikaner nationalism (Broederbond, economic grievances), the Sauer Report, Smuts and the United Party (Fagan Report), the electoral system (rural weighting: the National Party–Afrikaner Party won more seats with fewer votes; check figures). Apartheid laws: Prohibition of Mixed Marriages (1949), Immorality Amendment (1950), Population Registration (1950), Group Areas (1950), Suppression of Communism (1950), Bantu Authorities (1951), Abolition of Passes and Coordination of Documents (1952, reference books), Separate Amenities (1953), Bantu Education (1953), Native Resettlement (1954; Sophiatown removals 1955), Separate Representation of Voters (1951–56; Coloured voters removed); impact on daily lives. Opposition: ANC (Youth League; Programme of Action 1949), PAC (1959), Defiance Campaign (1952), women's anti-pass march (9 August 1956; about 20,000; Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph), Freedom Charter (Kliptown, 26 June 1955), Treason Trial (1956–61; all acquitted).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The National Party won the 1948 election because of Afrikaner nationalism.' Evaluate this view. Assess the impact of apartheid legislation on Black South Africans by 1959. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Nigel Worden, The Making of Modern South Africa (1994; later editions); Leonard Thompson, A History of South Africa (1990); Hermann Giliomee, The Afrikaners (2003); Saul Dubow.
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: racial classification terms. Use the period's official terms only with explanation and in quotation marks where offensive; prefer 'Black South Africans', 'Coloured', 'Indian' as in current historical usage.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of apartheid laws (year, name, what it did).

### hist.d.safrica.2 · End of minority rule (2): resistance and repression in South Africa, 1960–77 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 8 End of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe, 1948–94
- Key question 8.2 How did resistance grow in South Africa and how did the authorities respond in the period 1960–77?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The development of resistance inside South Africa: The ANC, the PAC; The Year of the Pass, 1960; Sharpeville and Langa; Mandela and Umkhonto we Sizwe; The Rivonia Trial; Biko, the South African Student Organisation (SASO) and Black Consciousness Movement (BCM); The Soweto uprising
  - The impact of international opposition: International reaction to Sharpeville; The activities of the Anti-Apartheid Movement; Divisions in the United Nations (UN) and the international community over sanctions; UN embargo on arms, 1977; Political changes in southern Africa; The Organisation of African Unity’s (OAU) support for the ANC and the PAC, including military bases
  - The National Party in power: separate development and expansion of the Bantustans: Verwoerd and Vorster and separate development: the Bantu Self-Government Act 1959, the strengthening of the Bantustans; Bantustans and independence; Life in the Bantustans
  - The National Party in power: its reaction to resistance: The government’s reaction to Sharpeville: the state of emergency, banning of the ANC and the PAC; Vorster’s use of police powers and defence forces; The persecution of Biko and leaders of the BCM; The government reaction to Soweto
- **What to cover and stress.** Resistance: ANC and PAC, the pass campaign (1960), Sharpeville (21 March 1960; 69 killed) and Langa, state of emergency, ANC and PAC banned (April 1960), Mandela and Umkhonto we Sizwe (16 December 1961), arrest (1962), Rivonia Trial (1963–64; life sentences June 1964; Mandela's statement 20 April 1964), Biko, SASO (1968–69), Black Consciousness Movement, Soweto uprising (16 June 1976; Hector Pieterson; Afrikaans medium decree). International opposition: reaction to Sharpeville, Anti-Apartheid Movement (1959–60), UN divisions over sanctions (Resolution 1761, 1962), UN arms embargo (Resolution 418, 4 November 1977), sporting boycotts (Olympics 1964; D'Oliveira affair 1968), changes in southern Africa (Mozambique and Angola independence 1975), OAU support (bases in Tanzania, Zambia). Separate development: Verwoerd (PM 1958–66; assassinated 1966) and Vorster (1966–78), Promotion of Bantu Self-Government Act (1959), Transkei self-government (1963) and 'independence' (1976), life in the Bantustans, forced removals (about 3.5 million people 1960–83; check, Surplus People Project). Reactions to resistance: emergency, bannings, Vorster's police powers (BOSS 1969; Terrorism Act 1967; detention without trial), Biko's death in detention (12 September 1977), reaction to Soweto.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Sharpeville was a turning point in the struggle against apartheid.' Evaluate this view. Assess the significance of the Black Consciousness Movement. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Tom Lodge, Black Politics in South Africa since 1945 (1983); Worden; Thompson; Mandela, Long Walk to Freedom (1994) as a memoir to evaluate.
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: deaths at Sharpeville and Soweto (give official and other estimates), Biko's death.
- **Figures and widgets.** Schematic SVG map of the Bantustans (labelled, schematic) or a table.
