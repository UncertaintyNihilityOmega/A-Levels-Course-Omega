### hist.d.italy.3 · Mussolini's Italy (3): how successful Mussolini's economic policies were (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 1 Mussolini’s Italy, 1919–41
- Key question 1.3 How successful were Mussolini’s economic policies?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Attempts to modernise Italian economy, including: Aims and development of economic policy; Di Stefani and orthodox policies; Economic ‘battles’: lira, grain and marshes; Autarky; Outcomes and extent of success
  - Responses to the Great Depression, including: The impact of the Great Depression on Italy’s economy; Government intervention: IMI and IRI, roles and impact; Public works
  - Corporate State, including: Aims: the ‘Third Way’; Creation through Vidoni Pact, Rocco Law, Ministry of Corporations, Charter of Labour and National Council of Corporations; Outcomes
  - Living standards, including: Welfare system: health, pensions, employment levels and childcare; Levels of pay, working hours and consumption
- **What to cover and stress.** Aims; De Stefani (finance minister 1922–25: laissez-faire, budget balanced, growth); Volpi; battle for the lira (Pesaro speech 1926; Quota 90, 1927; deflation), battle for grain (1925; wheat output up, imports down, but other crops and livestock suffered), battle for the marshes (Pontine Marshes, new towns such as Littoria 1932), battle for land; autarky (from 1935–36 sanctions); outcomes. Depression: impact (unemployment over 1 million by 1932; check), IMI (1931), IRI (1933; by 1939 the state controlled a large share of industry and banking: check), public works (autostrade, electrification). Corporate State: Third Way between capitalism and socialism; Vidoni Palace Pact (October 1925), Rocco Law (April 1926), Ministry of Corporations (1926), Charter of Labour (1927), National Council of Corporations (1930), 22 corporations (1934), Chamber of Fasces and Corporations (1939); outcomes (employers dominant). Living standards: welfare (pensions, insurance, family allowances 1934, ONMI 1925 maternity and childcare), employment, real wages (fell in the 1920s–30s; check), working hours (40-hour week 1934 with pay cut), consumption (less meat), Dopolavoro (OND, in KQ 1.4).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Statistics from cited sources (e.g. wheat production, real wages) in a table with checks.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Corporate State existed more in propaganda than in practice.' Evaluate this view. Assess the success of Mussolini's economic 'battles'. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Martin Clark, Modern Italy 1871–1995; Vera Zamagni, The Economic History of Italy 1860–1990 (1993); Morgan.
- **Pitfalls.** Statistics must be cited and checked; prefer clear trends to precise but unsupported numbers.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): wheat output and real wages; table of the corporative laws.

### hist.d.italy.4 · Mussolini's Italy (4): how far Mussolini transformed Italian society (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 1 Mussolini’s Italy, 1919–41
- Key question 1.4 How far did Mussolini transform Italian society?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Impact on women and young people of fascist policies, including: Fascist attitudes towards women; Aims to increase birth rate and population; Attempts to limit employment of women; Battle for Births: propaganda, incentives, abortion, contraception and divorce, and outcomes; Indoctrination of youth through education and youth movements; Gentile’s reforms; Curriculum and textbook changes; Control over teachers; Fascism and university education; ONB: aims, organisation, activities and outcomes
  - Religious policies, including: Mussolini’s early anti-clericalism and changing policies; Lateran Treaties, impact and significance; Examples of church support for the regime; Growing tensions between church and state in 1930s over Catholic Action and antisemitism
  - Impact of fascist organisations, including: OND: structure, aims, organisation and outcomes; Impact of Reform of Customs
  - Extent of opposition to regime, including: Strikes and demonstrations; Communist Party; Justice and liberty; Non-conformity; Assassination attempts
- **What to cover and stress.** Women: fascist attitudes; birth-rate aims (Ascension Day speech 26 May 1927: target 60 million by 1950), Battle for Births (bachelor tax 1927, marriage loans, prizes, bans on contraception and abortion, divorce illegal), outcomes (birth rate kept falling; check), limits on women's employment (1933 and 1938 quotas: 10 per cent in public jobs). Youth: Gentile reform (1923), textbook control (Libro unico 1929), teachers' oath (1929 schools, 1931 universities; only about a dozen professors refused), university GUF, ONB (1926; Balilla, Avanguardisti, Piccole Italiane), GIL (1937), Saturday fascista (1935), outcomes (membership patchy, especially in the south: the specimen mark scheme cites an estimated 30–40 per cent not joining). Religion: early anti-clericalism; Lateran Treaties (11 February 1929: Vatican City, Concordat, compensation) and significance; Church support (1929 plebiscite, Abyssinia); tensions (Catholic Action 1931, the encyclical Non abbiamo bisogno, racial laws 1938). OND/Dopolavoro (1925; structure, leisure, outcomes), Reform of Customs (1938: Lei banned, the Voi, Roman step, no handshakes). Opposition: strikes, PCI (underground; Gramsci imprisoned 1926–37), Giustizia e Libertà (1929; the Rosselli brothers, murdered 1937), non-conformity, assassination attempts (Zaniboni 1925, Violet Gibson April 1926, Zamboni October 1926).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Use precise evidence; specimen Paper 4 Question 1 ('Italian fascist policies towards the young failed to gain their support') is on this KQ: never copy it, write different questions.
- **Worked examples and practice.** Evaluate the view that the Lateran Treaties were Mussolini's greatest domestic success. Assess the success of the Battle for Births. (A youth question must be worded differently from the specimen.) Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Victoria de Grazia, How Fascism Ruled Women (1992) and The Culture of Consent (1981, on Dopolavoro); Tracy Koon, Believe, Obey, Fight (1985, youth); Bosworth.
- **Pitfalls.** 'Totalitarian' was Mussolini's word: test it, do not assume it.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of youth organisations by age group; hist-chart (if built) of the birth rate 1922–40 from a cited source.
