### hist.interp.holocaust.1 · The Holocaust (1): from antisemitism to genocide (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 3** (one extract, one [40] question; FACTS.md has the levels).
- **What to cover and stress.** Context (AO1): European and German antisemitism (religious, racial antisemitism from the 1870s; Wilhelm Marr; Houston Stewart Chamberlain; Social Darwinism and eugenics); Nazi antisemitism (Mein Kampf; 25-point programme 1920); persecution 1933–41 (boycott 1 April 1933; Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service April 1933; Nuremberg Laws 15 September 1935; Aryanisation; Kristallnacht 9–10 November 1938; emigration and Évian 1938; Madagascar plan 1940); the impact of war (Poland 1939; ghettos: Łódź 1940, Warsaw November 1940; Einsatzgruppen in the USSR from June 1941; Babi Yar 29–30 September 1941); T4 'euthanasia' programme (1939–41) and its staff; deportations of German Jews (October 1941); Chełmno (December 1941); Wannsee Conference (20 January 1942); Operation Reinhard camps (Bełżec, Sobibór, Treblinka); Auschwitz-Birkenau; about six million Jews murdered (cite USHMM); Roma and Sinti, disabled people, Soviet POWs, Poles; Jewish responses (emigration, Judenräte, smuggling, cultural resistance, armed resistance: Warsaw ghetto uprising April–May 1943; Sobibór revolt October 1943; partisans); contemporary reactions (Riegner telegram August 1942; Allied declaration 17 December 1942; Bermuda Conference April 1943; War Refugee Board January 1944; Auschwitz bombing debate).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Use real, carefully chosen documents only from reliable collections (USHMM, Fordham Holocaust sourcebook): the Nuremberg Laws; the Wannsee Protocol; Himmler's Posen speech (October 1943). Handle with care and context.
- **Worked examples and practice.** Knowledge-application examples: how knowledge of Kristallnacht, the Einsatzgruppen or Wannsee is used to test an interpretation; a model 'context' paragraph.
- **Historians you may name.** For context only (debate chapter has them): Raul Hilberg; Saul Friedländer.
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: the most important rule of the course. Factual, respectful, no graphic detail beyond the need, victims as people (use names and testimonies from USHMM or Yad Vashem resources), numbers with sources (USHMM), never 'debate' about whether it happened; denial is not an interpretation (link HDOT). Use 'murdered', not euphemisms, except when quoting Nazi language with explanation.

### hist.interp.holocaust.2 · The Holocaust (2): the historians' debate (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 3** (one extract, one [40] question; FACTS.md has the levels).
- **What to cover and stress.** All syllabus bullets: pre-Nazi racist ideas (Daniel Goldhagen, Hitler's Willing Executioners 1996: 'eliminationist antisemitism'; critics); intentionalism (Lucy Dawidowicz, The War Against the Jews 1975; Gerald Fleming, Hitler and the Final Solution 1982; Eberhard Jäckel); structuralism (Hans Mommsen: 'cumulative radicalisation', polycratic state; Martin Broszat's 1977 article on the genesis of the Final Solution); functionalism (Broszat; Karl Schleunes, The Twisted Road to Auschwitz 1970; Götz Aly on population planning); synthesis (Christopher Browning, The Origins of the Final Solution 2004: decision in autumn 1941 'euphoria of victory'; Ian Kershaw: 'working towards the Führer'; Saul Friedländer, Nazi Germany and the Jews 1997 and 2007: 'redemptive antisemitism'; Peter Longerich, Holocaust 2010); perpetrators (Browning, Ordinary Men 1992: Reserve Police Battalion 101; Goldhagen's opposite reading; Jan T.
