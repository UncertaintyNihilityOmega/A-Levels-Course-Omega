### hist.eu.russia.3 · Russia (3): how and why the Bolsheviks seized power in 1917 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 3 Russia from autocracy to revolution, 1881–1924
- Key question 3.3 How and why did the Bolsheviks seize power in October 1917?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The impact of the First World War on Tsarist rule: The impact of defeats during the First World War on the Tsar’s position; Weaknesses in the government during the war
  - The causes and effects of the February Revolution
  - Economic and social problems on the home front, including: Inflation; Food shortages; Land seizures
  - Events of February 1917 leading to the abdication of the Tsar
  - Formation and aims of the Provisional Government
  - Reasons for the failure of the Provisional Government: Challenges facing the Provisional Government leading to its failure, including: Failure to end the war, Need for land reform, The July Days, Kornilov Revolt; Methods used by the Bolsheviks to seize power in October 1917, including: Bolshevik promises, Lenin’s leadership, The role of the Petrograd Soviet and Trotsky, Military Revolutionary Committee
  - The events of the October Revolution
- **What to cover and stress.** War 1914–17: Tannenberg (August 1914), Great Retreat (1915), Brusilov offensive (1916); Nicholas takes command (August 1915), the Tsarina and Rasputin (murdered December 1916), 'ministerial leapfrog'; Progressive Bloc (1915). Home front: inflation, transport breakdown, food shortages in Petrograd, land seizures (1917). February Revolution (23 February–2 March 1917, Old Style: International Women's Day, strikes, garrison mutiny), Duma committee and the Petrograd Soviet, Order No. 1, abdication (2 March 1917). Provisional Government: aims (Constituent Assembly, civil liberties, continue the war); dual power; failures: war (June offensive), land, July Days (3–4 July), Kerensky, Kornilov revolt (August), arming the Red Guards. Bolsheviks: Lenin's return (3 April; April Theses), 'Peace, Land, Bread', 'All power to the Soviets', German money (debated), Trotsky (chair of the Petrograd Soviet from September), the Military Revolutionary Committee, October Revolution (24–26 October 1917; Winter Palace; Second Congress of Soviets).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Rodzianko's telegrams to Nicholas II (February 1917); the abdication manifesto (2 March 1917); Order No. 1; Lenin's April Theses; the Provisional Government's declarations; Sukhanov's memoir of 1917; John Reed, Ten Days that Shook the World (1919), a sympathetic eyewitness; Kornilov's statements.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a) (specimen used Trotsky: choose another): Explain why the Provisional Government continued the war. P2(b): 'Lenin's leadership was the main reason for the Bolshevik seizure of power.' How far do you agree? P2(b): To what extent was the First World War responsible for the fall of the Tsar? P1(a) compare two eyewitness accounts of the October Revolution.
- **Historians you may name.** Figes; Richard Pipes, The Russian Revolution (1990: a conspiratorial coup); Alexander Rabinowitch, The Bolsheviks Come to Power (1976: popular support in Petrograd); Sheila Fitzpatrick; Robert Service, Lenin (2000).
- **Pitfalls.** Two calendars (the Bolsheviks switched on 1 February 1918, Old Style). 'February' was largely spontaneous; 'October' was planned. Do not overstate Bolshevik numbers.
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline widget (if built) February–October 1917 with lanes Government, Soviet, Bolsheviks.

### hist.eu.russia.4 · Russia (4): how the Bolsheviks consolidated power to 1924 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2029**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2028**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 3 Russia from autocracy to revolution, 1881–1924
- Key question 3.4 How were the Bolsheviks able to consolidate their power up to 1924?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Bolshevik policies, including: Establishment of Sovnarkom; Decrees on rights, workers’ control, peace and land; Treaty of Brest-Litovsk
  - The establishment of a dictatorship: The Cheka; The closure of the Constituent Assembly
  - Reasons for the Bolshevik victory in the Civil War: Strengths of the Bolsheviks, including: Leadership, Popular support, Geographical factors, Unity and organisation, The introduction and impact of War Communism; Weaknesses of the Whites, including: Leadership, Use of conscription, Issues of supply, Foreign intervention
  - The importance of the Kronstadt Mutiny: The Kronstadt Munity causes, events and impact; The introduction and impact of the New Economic Policy
- **What to cover and stress.** Sovnarkom; decrees on peace, land, workers' control, rights of the peoples; press decree; Cheka (December 1917; Dzerzhinsky); Constituent Assembly (elections November 1917: SRs largest party; met and dispersed 5–6 January 1918); Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918; losses of territory, population and industry: give figures with a source); Civil War (1918–21): Whites (Denikin, Kolchak, Yudenich, Wrangel), Czech Legion, Greens, foreign intervention; Red strengths (Trotsky and the Red Army, central position and railways, unity, propaganda, War Communism, Red Terror after August 1918), White weaknesses (divided aims, leadership, conscription, supply, foreign help half-hearted, association with landlords); murder of the royal family (July 1918). War Communism (grain requisitioning, nationalisation) and its effects (famine 1921–22; estimates up to about 5 million deaths, give range). Kronstadt mutiny (March 1921): causes, demands, suppression; Tenth Party Congress; NEP (from March 1921) and ban on factions; position by Lenin's death (21 January 1924) and the USSR (1922).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Decree on Land (26 October 1917); Lenin's 'Theses on the Constituent Assembly' (December 1917); the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk; the Petropavlovsk resolution of the Kronstadt sailors (28 February 1921); Lenin's speeches to the Tenth Party Congress (March 1921); Civil War posters (visual); Trotsky's My Life (1930), to evaluate.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why the Bolsheviks closed the Constituent Assembly. P2(b) (specimen asked about NEP's popularity: choose another): 'The weakness of the Whites was the main reason for the Bolshevik victory in the Civil War.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far did the Kronstadt mutiny force Lenin to change policy? P1 on Kronstadt.
- **Historians you may name.** Evan Mawdsley, The Russian Civil War (1987); Figes; Pipes; Israel Getzler, Kronstadt 1917–1921 (1983).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: Red and White Terror, famine; factual and with ranges. NEP was a retreat in economics not politics: the ban on factions came at the same congress.
- **Figures and widgets.** Schematic SVG map of the Civil War fronts (Reds in the centre; Kolchak east, Denikin south, Yudenich north-west), clearly labelled 'schematic'.
