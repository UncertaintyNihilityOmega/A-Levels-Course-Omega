### hist.eu.germany.3 · Germany (3): Bismarck's appointment and his aims to 1866 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 2 Liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815–71
- Key question 2.3 Why was Bismarck appointed as Minister-President and what were his aims in the period up to 1866?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The Constitutional Crisis: The accession of Wilhelm I; Proposals for army reforms and the reaction of the Liberals; The formation of the German Progressive Party; Bismarck’s appointment and how he resolved the crisis
  - The Congress of Princes, August 1863 and relations with Austria
  - War with Denmark, 1864: Causes of the war; Reasons for Bismarck’s involvement; The outcome, including: Increased tensions between Germany and Austria, The Convention of Gastein
  - Preparation for war with Austria: Meeting with Napoleon III at Biarritz; Secret alliance with Italy; Reasons why war broke out, including: Bismarck’s proposal to the Federal Diet for a new constitution, Austria’s violation of the Convention of Gastein; The outcome of the Seven Weeks’ War, including: The Treaty of Prague, The formation of the North German Confederation, The Liberals change in attitude toward Bismarck, The Indemnity Bill, 1866
- **What to cover and stress.** Wilhelm I (regent 1858, king 1861); Roon's army reforms (larger army, three years' service, Landwehr reduced); Liberal opposition; the German Progressive Party (1861) and its election victories (1861, 1862); constitutional crisis; Bismarck appointed Minister-President (September 1862); 'iron and blood' speech (30 September 1862); the gap theory and collecting taxes without a budget; Congress of Princes at Frankfurt (August 1863) and Bismarck's persuading Wilhelm not to attend; Polish revolt 1863 and the Alvensleben Convention; Schleswig-Holstein (Danish November constitution 1863), war with Denmark (1864; Düppel), Convention of Gastein (14 August 1865); Biarritz meeting with Napoleon III (October 1865); secret alliance with Italy (April 1866); Bismarck's federal reform proposal (universal male suffrage parliament, April 1866); Austria's appeal to the Diet over the duchies (June 1866) as violation of Gastein; Seven Weeks' War (Königgrätz/Sadowa, 3 July 1866); Treaty of Prague (23 August 1866); annexations; North German Confederation (1867); Indemnity Bill (September 1866) and the split of liberals (National Liberals). Aims debate: master plan or opportunism.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Bismarck's 'iron and blood' speech to the Budget Committee (30 September 1862); Wilhelm I's correspondence; the Convention of Gastein (1865); the Treaty of Prague (1866); Liberal Progressive Party statements; Bismarck's memoirs (Thoughts and Recollections, 1898), a source to evaluate with great care.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Bismarck was appointed Minister-President in 1862. P2(b): 'Bismarck had a clear plan to unify Germany by 1866.' How far do you agree? P2(b): To what extent was the war with Denmark in 1864 caused by Bismarck? P1 on the constitutional crisis.
- **Historians you may name.** A.J.P. Taylor, Bismarck: The Man and the Statesman (1955: an opportunist); Otto Pflanze, Bismarck and the Development of Germany (1963, revised 1990); Jonathan Steinberg, Bismarck: A Life (2011); Lothar Gall, Bismarck: The White Revolutionary (1980).
- **Pitfalls.** Bismarck's memoirs are self-serving: evaluate them, do not use them as fact. Quote 'iron and blood' accurately (the order in German is Eisen und Blut).
- **Figures and widgets.** Schematic SVG map of Prussia, Austria, the duchies and the North German Confederation after 1866; a table of the stages 1862–66.
- **Next line.** `Next: [Germany (4): how and why unification was achieved by 1871](#hist.eu.germany.4)`

### hist.eu.germany.4 · Germany (4): how and why unification was achieved by 1871 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2028**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2027** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 2 Liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815–71
- Key question 2.4 How and why was German unification achieved by 1871?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Pressure from nationalists to complete the unification process
  - Problem with the southern states, including: The Zollparlament; Lack of political unity
  - Napoleon III’s ambitions: The Luxemburg Crisis, including: Causes, Why Bismarck encouraged it, Outcome and effects on relations with France; The Hohenzollern candidature, including: Spanish crown accepted by Prince Leopold, Reaction of Napoleon III, The Ems Telegram, Declaration of war by France
  - The Franco–Prussian War, 1870–71: Why Bismarck was in a strong position, including: Lack of international support for France and the weakness of the French army, The strength of the Prussian Army under General Moltke, Impact of the German victory at Sedan, Armistice agreed, 28 January 1871, Treaty of Frankfurt, 1871
  - Creation of the German Empire, 1871: Concessions to the southern states; Wilhelm proclaimed Kaiser; Constitution of the new Reich
- **What to cover and stress.** Nationalist pressure (Nationalverein, National Liberals) to complete unification; the southern states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden, Hesse-Darmstadt south of the Main): Catholic, particularist, anti-Prussian; secret defensive alliances 1866; Zollparlament (1868 elections returned anti-Prussian majorities in the south); Napoleon III's ambitions after Sadowa ('compensation'); Luxemburg crisis (1867; London conference, neutralisation); Hohenzollern candidature (Leopold accepts June 1870; withdrawn 12 July); Benedetti at Ems (13 July); the Ems telegram as edited by Bismarck; France declares war (19 July 1870); why Bismarck was strong (no allies for France; Moltke, railways, conscripts, breech-loaders and Krupp artillery; southern states join); Sedan (1–2 September 1870; Napoleon III captured); siege of Paris; armistice 28 January 1871; Treaty of Frankfurt (10 May 1871; Alsace-Lorraine, 5 billion francs). German Empire proclaimed at Versailles (18 January 1871); concessions to the south (Bavaria's reserved rights); the 1871 constitution (Kaiser, Chancellor, Bundesrat, Reichstag by universal male suffrage; Prussian dominance).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Abeken's telegram from Ems and Bismarck's edited version (13 July 1870), with Bismarck's own later account in his memoirs; the French declaration of war; Anton von Werner's painting of the proclamation at Versailles (visual; later versions altered); the 1871 constitution; southern newspapers on the Zollparlament.
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why France declared war on Prussia in July 1870 (the specimen asked why the war 'broke out': choose another angle). P2(b): How far was Prussian military strength the main reason for German unification by 1871? P2(b): 'Unification was achieved by Bismarck's diplomacy.' How far do you agree? P1(a): the two versions of the Ems telegram as evidence about Bismarck's aims.
- **Historians you may name.** Taylor and Steinberg (as above); Michael Howard, The Franco-Prussian War (1961); Geoffrey Wawro, The Franco-Prussian War (2003); Helmut Böhme on economic foundations.
- **Pitfalls.** The Ems telegram did not 'cause' the war alone: French public opinion and Gramont's demand for guarantees matter. 1871 Germany excluded Austria (kleindeutsch).
- **Figures and widgets.** SVG or table of the 1871 constitution (Kaiser, Chancellor, Bundesrat, Reichstag) with who controls what.
- **Next line.** `Next: [Russia (1): the challenges facing Tsarism, 1881–94](#hist.eu.russia.1)`
