### hist.int.empire.3 · Imperialism and world powers (3): why Japan emerged as a world power (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS International option (Section C). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2027**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2028** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 7 Imperialism and the emergence of world powers, c.1870–1918
- Key question 7.3 Why did Japan emerge as a world power and what was the impact on international relations?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Japanese relations with western powers in the nineteenth century, including: Commodore Perry’s mission; Unequal treaties
  - Reasons for rapid modernisation and military development, including: Meiji Restoration; Industrialisation; Adoption of western technology; Education
  - International recognition of Japan as a world power, including: Japanese foreign policy objectives: relations with Korea; Anglo Japanese Treaty, 1902: causes and consequences; Russo-Japanese War, 1904–05 and reason for Japan’s victory
  - Japan’s role in the First World War and global position by 1918, including: Contribution to global war; Role in the Pacific, including seizure of German possessions; Intervention in Russian Civil War
- **What to cover and stress.** Tokugawa isolation; Commodore Perry (July 1853, return 1854; Treaty of Kanagawa); unequal treaties (Harris Treaty 1858; extraterritoriality, tariffs); Meiji Restoration (1868); Charter Oath (1868); modernisation: abolition of domains (1871), samurai abolished (Satsuma Rebellion 1877), conscription (1873), Meiji Constitution (1889), industrialisation (state then zaibatsu), railways, Western technology and advisers (Iwakura Mission 1871–73), education (1872 system; Imperial Rescript on Education 1890). Recognition: Korea (Treaty of Ganghwa 1876; Sino-Japanese War 1894–95; Treaty of Shimonoseki; Triple Intervention), revision of unequal treaties (1894, 1911), Anglo-Japanese Alliance (30 January 1902): causes and consequences, Russo-Japanese War (1904–05; Port Arthur, Mukden, Tsushima; Treaty of Portsmouth 5 September 1905) and reasons for victory, annexation of Korea (1910). First World War: joins Allies (August 1914), takes Tsingtao and German Pacific islands, Twenty-One Demands (1915), navy in the Mediterranean, Paris 1919 (racial equality proposal rejected; Shantung), intervention in Siberia (1918–22).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** President Fillmore's letter carried by Perry (1853); the Charter Oath (1868); the Imperial Rescript on Education (1890); the Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902); Japanese woodblock prints of the Russo-Japanese War (visual); the Twenty-One Demands (1915).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Britain signed an alliance with Japan in 1902. P2(b): 'Victory over Russia in 1905 was the main reason Japan was recognised as a world power.' How far do you agree? P2(b): To what extent was Japan's modernisation driven by fear of the West? P1 on the Meiji reforms.
- **Historians you may name.** Marius Jansen, The Making of Modern Japan (2000); W.G. Beasley, The Meiji Restoration (1972).
- **Pitfalls.** Japanese names: family name first (Itō Hirobumi). The Meiji period is 1868–1912.
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline widget or table 1853–1918.
- **Next line.** `Next: [Imperialism and world powers (4): why the USA emerged as a world power](#hist.int.empire.4)`

### hist.int.empire.4 · Imperialism and world powers (4): why the USA emerged as a world power (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS International option (Section C). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2027**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2028** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 7 Imperialism and the emergence of world powers, c.1870–1918
- Key question 7.4 Why did the USA emerge as a world power and what was the impact on international relations?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Changing attitudes to overseas expansion, including: Impact of the closing of the frontier on US foreign policy; Development of naval power: Mahan’s ‘The Influence of Sea Power upon History’, building of the Great White Fleet; Rapid economic growth; Economic cycle and the Panic of 1893; Building of the Panama Canal relations? continued
  - Reasons for, and impact of, the Spanish–American War, 1898, including: Increasing US interest in Central and Latin America; Yellow journalism; Sinking of the Maine; Taft and Dollar Diplomacy
  - Reasons for, and impact of, the Philippine–American War, 1899–1902, including: US expansion into the Pacific; Controversy over US motives and actions; Filipino resistance
  - Reasons for, and impact of, the USA’s entry into the First World War, including: US relations with Germany, Britain and France; Wilson’s policy and anti-war pressure groups; Actions of German U-boats and the sinking of the Lusitania; Zimmerman telegram; Impact of US intervention
- **What to cover and stress.** Closing of the frontier (1890 census; Turner 1893); Mahan, The Influence of Sea Power upon History (1890); naval building; Great White Fleet (1907–09); economic growth and the search for markets; Panic of 1893; Spanish–American War (1898): Cuba (Weyler's reconcentration), yellow journalism (Hearst, Pulitzer), de Lôme letter, USS Maine (15 February 1898), Treaty of Paris (December 1898: Philippines, Puerto Rico, Guam; Hawaii annexed July 1898); Teller and Platt Amendments; Panama Canal (1903–14; Roosevelt Corollary 1904; 'big stick'); Taft's Dollar Diplomacy; Philippine–American War (1899–1902; Aguinaldo; deaths of Filipino civilians: estimates range widely, say so; Anti-Imperialist League; water cure hearings 1902); entry into the First World War: neutrality, trade and loans to the Allies, U-boats (Lusitania 7 May 1915, Sussex pledge 1916, unrestricted submarine warfare February 1917), Zimmermann telegram (January, published 1 March 1917), Wilson's war message (2 April 1917; war declared 6 April), anti-war groups; impact of US intervention (troops, money, Fourteen Points).
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Albert Beveridge, 'The March of the Flag' (16 September 1898); McKinley's war message (11 April 1898); the platform of the American Anti-Imperialist League (1899); the Roosevelt Corollary (December 1904); the Zimmermann telegram (1917); Wilson's war message (2 April 1917); cartoons on imperialism (visual).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a) (the 2024 paper asked why US foreign policy became more imperialistic after 1898: write another): Explain why the USA went to war with Spain in 1898. P2(b): 'The USA entered the First World War because of German submarine warfare.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far was US expansion after 1898 motivated by economic interests? P1 on the Philippines.
- **Historians you may name.** William Appleman Williams, The Tragedy of American Diplomacy (1959: economic expansion); Walter LaFeber, The New Empire (1963); Paul Kramer, The Blood of Government (2006).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: the Philippine–American War. The Maine's cause was probably an internal explosion (1976 Rickover inquiry): say how historians know.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of US acquisitions 1898–1917.
