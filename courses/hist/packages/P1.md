### hist.eu.france.2 · France (2): how and why France became a republic by 1792 (`@level as`)

- Examined: AS European option (Section A). **Paper 1** (sources) in **2027**; **Paper 2** (essays) in **2028** and **2029**. Teach both styles (COURSE.md). Opening `:::exam Where this is examined` box says this.
- Syllabus topic: 1 France, 1774–1814
- Key question 1.2 How and why did France become a republic by 1792?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The revolutionary and counter-revolutionary groups: Aims of the revolutionary groups, including: The Jacobins, the Feuillants and the Girondins; Counter-revolutionary groups: reasons for failure
  - Reforms: Financial, including: Assignats, Taxation reform; Local government; Justice; Church reforms
  - Political instability: Disagreement on the terms of the new constitution; The behaviour of the King and the flight to Varennes; The Champ de Mars
  - From constitutional monarchy to republic: Legislative Assembly; Declaration of war on Austria, April 1792; September Massacres; The National Convention and abolition of the monarchy
- **What to cover and stress.** October 1789 to 21–22 September 1792. Clubs: Jacobins (from the Breton Club; Robespierre), Feuillants (split July 1791 after Varennes; Lafayette, Barnave), Girondins (Brissot; war party 1791–92); Cordeliers (Danton, Marat) as context. Counter-revolution: émigrés (Artois), refractory priests, Pillnitz (27 August 1791), why it failed (divided, abroad, foreign help self-interested). Reforms of the Constituent Assembly: Church lands nationalised (2 November 1789), assignats (December 1789, paper money from 1790, inflation), new direct taxes on land and property (1790–91) replacing the old taxes, 83 departments (1790), elected officials, active/passive citizens (the marc d'argent), justice (elected judges, juries in criminal cases, abolition of parlements and lettres de cachet), Civil Constitution of the Clergy (12 July 1790), the oath (November 1790) and the Pope's condemnation (1791), splitting France. Constitution of 1791: single chamber, suspensive veto, property qualification. Varennes (20–21 June 1791) and the 'kidnap' fiction; Champ de Mars massacre (17 July 1791). Legislative Assembly (from 1 October 1791); war declared on Austria 20 April 1792 (why the Girondins and the king both wanted it); defeats; vetoes; 20 June 1792; Brunswick Manifesto (25 July 1792); 10 August 1792 (Tuileries); September Massacres (2–6 September 1792; about 1,100–1,400 prisoners killed, estimates vary); Valmy (20 September 1792) briefly; Convention meets 20 September and abolishes the monarchy 21 September; Year I of the Republic from 22 September.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Civil Constitution of the Clergy (12 July 1790); Louis XVI's declaration left in Paris when he fled (20 June 1791); the Declaration of Pillnitz (27 August 1791); Robespierre's speeches against war (January 1792) and Brissot's for war (December 1791); the Brunswick Manifesto (25 July 1792); Marat's newspaper L'Ami du peuple; Edmund Burke, Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790); Olympe de Gouges, Declaration of the Rights of Woman (1791); prints of the return from Varennes (1791).
- **Worked examples and practice.** P2(a): Explain why Louis XVI tried to flee in June 1791. P2(b): 'The flight to Varennes was the main reason France became a republic.' How far do you agree? P2(b): How far was the Civil Constitution of the Clergy the most divisive reform of 1789–91? P1(a) compare Pillnitz and the Brunswick Manifesto as evidence about foreign attitudes to the Revolution; P1(b) how far the sources agree that war caused the fall of the monarchy.
- **Historians you may name.** Timothy Tackett, When the King Took Flight (2003); William Doyle, The Oxford History of the French Revolution (1989); Peter McPhee, The French Revolution 1789–1799 (2002); John McManners, The French Revolution and the Church (1969).
- **Pitfalls.** Do not call the Feuillants royalists or the Girondins moderates in 1792 (they led the war party). Distinguish the Constituent Assembly (1789–91), the Legislative Assembly (1791–92) and the Convention (from September 1792). The Republic was not declared by a single decree on 22 September: monarchy abolished 21 September, documents dated Year I from 22 September.
- **Figures and widgets.** A timeline table; a hist-timeline widget of 1789–92 with lanes Politics, Religion, War. A small SVG of the political groupings (left to right) in 1791–92.

## Widgets (this package builds them; one file each in widgets/<name>.js; kit/WIDGETS.md)

- `hist-timeline`: Generic interactive timeline (COURSE.md gives the attribute format). Attributes: data-title, data-events (events separated by ' ;; ', fields by ' | ': date (YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD) | short label | one- or two-sentence detail | optional lane), optional data-lanes (comma list). Shows events in date order on a horizontal (desktop) or vertical (phone) axis with year ticks; Previous/Next buttons and ←/→ keys step through events; the selected event's date, label and detail show in a readout (aria-live); lane filter buttons (.w-tabs, aria-pressed) when lanes are given; dates spaced proportionally but with a minimum gap so labels never overlap (or a list mode toggle). Bad or missing attributes: a friendly .w-err message. Probe with 3, 12 and 30 events and with long labels.
