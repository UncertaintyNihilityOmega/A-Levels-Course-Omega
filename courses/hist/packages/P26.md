### hist.d.hitler.1 · Hitler's Germany (1): how Hitler gained control (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 3 Hitler’s Germany, 1929–41
- Key question 3.1 How did Hitler gain control of Germany?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Problems facing Weimar governments, including: Impact of the Great Depression on Germany; Unpopularity of Müller and Bruning’s Weimar governments; Rise of communism
  - Reasons for the growth of support for the Nazis, 1929–32, including: The appeal of Hitler; The role of the SA; Nazi propaganda and promises; Elite support
  - Hitler’s appointment as Chancellor, 1933, including: The elections of 1932; Von Schleicher’s government; Hindenburg and von Papen’s actions in 1933
  - Hitler’s consolidation of power and creation of a dictatorship, 1933–34, including: The Reichstag Fire and subsequent repression; March 1933 election; Enabling Act; Night of the Long Knives; Hindenburg’s death and the army oath of loyalty
- **What to cover and stress.** Weimar problems: Depression (unemployment about 6 million early 1932; check), Müller's grand coalition collapse (March 1930), Brüning's presidential government (Article 48, deflation, 'hunger chancellor'), KPD growth. Nazi growth 1929–32: Young Plan campaign (1929), Hitler's appeal, the SA (about 400,000 by 1932; check), propaganda and promises (Goebbels, tailored messages), elite support (Hugenberg, some industrialists), elections: 1930 (18.3 per cent), presidential election March–April 1932 (Hitler 36.8 per cent in second round; check), July 1932 (37.3 per cent, 230 seats), November 1932 (33.1 per cent, 196). Papen and Schleicher governments, Schleicher's Querfront, Papen and Hindenburg's deal: Hitler appointed 30 January 1933. Consolidation 1933–34: Reichstag fire (27 February 1933) and decree, March 1933 election (43.9 per cent), Enabling Act (23 March 1933; 444 to 94), Gleichschaltung (Länder, unions 2 May 1933, parties banned by July 1933), Night of the Long Knives (30 June–2 July 1934), Hindenburg's death (2 August 1934) and the army oath; plebiscite (August 1934).
- **Worked examples and practice.** Specimen Q3 asked about a 'legal revolution': write different questions, e.g. 'The Depression was the main reason Hitler became Chancellor in January 1933.' Evaluate this view. Assess the importance of the Night of the Long Knives in Hitler's consolidation of power. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Ian Kershaw, Hitler (1998, 2000); Richard Evans, The Coming of the Third Reich (2003); Thomas Childers, The Nazi Voter (1983); Henry Turner, German Big Business and the Rise of Hitler (1985).
- **Pitfalls.** Election percentages: check every figure against one cited source. Hitler was appointed, not elected, Chancellor.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): Reichstag election results 1928–33 for NSDAP, SPD, KPD, Centre, DNVP; unemployment.

### hist.d.hitler.2 · Hitler's Germany (2): how effectively the Nazis governed Germany (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 3 Hitler’s Germany, 1929–41
- Key question 3.2 How effectively did the Nazis govern Germany?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The terror state, including: Examples of repression such as the Gestapo; Block wardens; Police and SD; Judiciary and courts; SS; Concentration camps
  - Propaganda, including: Goebbels and Ministry of Propaganda and Enlightenment; Control of the media: radio, newspapers, film, art and culture; Nuremberg Rallies; 1936 Olympics
  - Extent of opposition to regime, including: Opposition from youth, including Swing movement and Edelweiss Pirates; Church, including the Confessing Church, the Pastors Emergency League and Galen; Political opposition from the left-wing; Passive resistance
  - Nazi foreign policy, including: Aims: reversal of Treaty of Versailles, pan-Germanism, Lebensraum and defeating communism; Methods: leaving the League of Nations; The remilitarisation of the Rhineland, Anti-Comintern Pact, Anschluss, Sudentenland and Czechoslovakia, Nazi–Soviet Pact, invasion of Poland and Operation Barbarossa; Extent of planning and consistency in policies
- **What to cover and stress.** Terror state: Gestapo (about 15,000–20,000 officers for the whole Reich, relying on denunciations; check), block wardens (Blockleiter), police and SD (Heydrich), judiciary and People's Court (1934), SS (Himmler), concentration camps (Dachau March 1933). Propaganda: Goebbels and the Ministry (March 1933), media (radio: People's Receiver; newspapers; film: Riefenstahl's Triumph of the Will 1935), art ('degenerate art' exhibition 1937), Nuremberg rallies, 1936 Olympics. Opposition: youth (Swing Youth, Edelweiss Pirates), churches (Pastors' Emergency League 1933, Confessing Church 1934, Niemöller, Galen's sermons 1941 against 'euthanasia'), left (SPD and KPD underground, Red Orchestra), passive resistance, conservative and military (outline only). Foreign policy: aims (Versailles, pan-Germanism, Lebensraum, anti-communism), methods: leaving the League (October 1933), Rhineland (1936), Anti-Comintern Pact, Anschluss, Sudetenland, Czechoslovakia, Nazi–Soviet Pact, Poland (1 September 1939), Barbarossa (22 June 1941); planning and consistency debate.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Nazi regime relied on terror rather than popular support.' Evaluate this view. Assess the consistency of Hitler's foreign policy aims, 1933–41. (2024 asked about the churches: reword any church question.) Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Robert Gellately, Backing Hitler (2001) and The Gestapo and German Society (1990); Eric Johnson, Nazi Terror (1999); Kershaw, The 'Hitler Myth' (1987); Detlev Peukert, Inside Nazi Germany (1982); A.J.P. Taylor vs Trevor-Roper on foreign policy.
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: camps and repression. The 'terror vs consent' debate: present both, with evidence.
- **Figures and widgets.** SVG of the police and terror apparatus (SS, Gestapo, SD, courts, camps) as a labelled structure.
