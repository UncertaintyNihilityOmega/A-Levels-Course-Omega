### hist.d.usa45.1 · Post-war USA, 1945–61 (1): Truman and the internal issues of post-war America (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 4 Truman, Eisenhower and post-war USA, 1945–61
- Key question 4.1 How well did Truman deal with the internal issues facing post-war US?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The impact of Truman’s Fair Deal policies, including: Welfare and social reforms: healthcare, education, housing; Economic policies; Employment policy: minimum wage, price controls, support for farmers; Infrastructure: land reclamation, hydro electric power, slum clearance
  - The challenges of McCarthyism and the Red Scare: Reasons for fear of Communism: concerns about infiltration and spying, external factors such as Soviet expansion; Party politics and the support for McCarthy, House Un-American Activities committee hearings and their effect; Reasons for decline of McCarthy’s influence
  - The reasons for Truman’s election victory in 1948, including: Truman’s personality and campaigning; Winning over key elements in New Deal coalition by measures to help African Americans and farmers; Conservative approval for Cold War policies and anti-Communism; Dewey’s personality and nature of Republican campaign
  - Truman’s promises and actions on Civil Rights: Problems facing African Americans in post-war period; Opposition among Southern Democrats; Truman’s measure to end discrimination; Truman’s relationship with Civil Rights groups
- **What to cover and stress.** Fair Deal (1949; State of the Union): healthcare (national health insurance defeated), education (federal aid failed), Housing Act 1949, minimum wage raised (40 to 75 cents, 1949), Social Security expanded (1950), price controls ended (1946) and inflation, strikes (1946), Taft–Hartley (1947, over veto), farm support (Brannan Plan defeated), infrastructure (reclamation, hydroelectric, slum clearance); 80th Congress. Red Scare: Soviet expansion, spies (Hiss 1948–50, Fuchs, the Rosenbergs 1951), Loyalty Program (1947), HUAC (Hollywood Ten 1947), McCarran Act (1950), McCarthy (Wheeling speech 9 February 1950), party politics, decline (Army–McCarthy hearings 1954, censure December 1954). 1948 election: Truman's whistle-stop campaign, coalition (African Americans after Executive Order 9981 and the 1948 platform, farmers, labour), Dixiecrats (Thurmond) and Progressives (Wallace), Dewey's complacent campaign; result (Truman 303 electoral votes; check). Civil rights: problems (segregation, lynchings, veterans attacked), To Secure These Rights (1947), Executive Orders 9980 and 9981 (July 1948), Southern Democrats' opposition, relations with NAACP.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 2024 asked 'Truman's victory in 1948 was a totally unexpected result.' Discuss: write different questions, e.g. 'Assess the reasons for Truman's victory in 1948' must be reworded; 'McCarthyism was mainly the product of party politics.' Evaluate this view. 'Truman's Fair Deal achieved little.' Assess this view.
- **Historians you may name.** Alonzo Hamby, Man of the People (1995); David McCullough, Truman (1992); Ellen Schrecker, Many Are the Crimes (1998).
- **Pitfalls.** Distinguish HUAC (House) from McCarthy (Senate).
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of the 1948 election (four candidates).

### hist.d.usa45.2 · Post-war USA, 1945–61 (2): how effective Eisenhower was as President (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 4 Truman, Eisenhower and post-war USA, 1945–61
- Key question 4.2 How effective was Eisenhower as President?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The reasons for Eisenhower’s election victories: Presidential election of 1952: personal appeal, use of TV, anti-Communism, promise to resolve Korean War, limitations of Democrats; Presidential election 1956: impact of handling of Suez and Hungary, support from African Americans, personal appeal, prosperity of 1950s, Democrat opposition
  - Eisenhower’s domestic policies: ‘Modern Republicanism’: increases in social security; low-cost housing; minimum wage; Department of Health, Education and Welfare; Infrastructure: Interstate Highway program; Balanced budget and fiscal policy; Concerns about the military-industrial complex
  - The developments of Civil Rights movement and the response of Federal Government: Impact of civil rights organisations and individuals including NAACP, Rosa Parks and the Montgomery Bus Boycott, Sit-ins and SNCC; Martin Luther King Jr and SCLC; Supreme Court Decisions including Brown versus Topeka Board of Education; Federal Government responses to attempts to block desegregation, Little Rock; Civil Rights legislation, 1957
- **What to cover and stress.** 1952 election (personal appeal 'I like Ike', TV ads, Nixon's Checkers speech, anti-communism, 'I shall go to Korea', Democrats' weaknesses; Stevenson); 1956 election (Suez and Hungary during the campaign, African American support, prosperity, Democrat opposition). Domestic: Modern Republicanism, Social Security extended (1954, 1956), housing, minimum wage to $1 (1955), Department of Health, Education and Welfare (1953), Interstate Highway Act (1956; 41,000 miles), balanced budgets (three), fiscal policy and recessions (1953–54, 1957–58, 1960), farewell address on the military-industrial complex (17 January 1961). Civil rights: NAACP (Thurgood Marshall), Brown v. Board of Education (17 May 1954; Brown II 1955), Emmett Till (1955), Rosa Parks and the Montgomery bus boycott (December 1955–December 1956; Browder v. Gayle), King and the SCLC (1957), Little Rock (September 1957; 101st Airborne), Civil Rights Act 1957 (weak), 1960 Act, sit-ins (Greensboro 1 February 1960), SNCC (April 1960); Eisenhower's reluctance.
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Eisenhower was a successful president in domestic affairs.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the progress of civil rights in the 1950s. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Fred Greenstein, The Hidden-Hand Presidency (1982); Stephen Ambrose, Eisenhower (1983–84); Taylor Branch, Parting the Waters (1988); Jim Newton, Eisenhower: The White House Years (2011).
- **Pitfalls.** Eisenhower's role on civil rights: weigh his private views against Little Rock and judicial appointments (Earl Warren).
- **Figures and widgets.** Timeline table of civil rights 1954–60.
