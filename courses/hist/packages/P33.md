### hist.d.usa74.4 · The USA, 1974–93 (4): changing relations with the USSR (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 6 The USA, 1974–93
- Key question 6.4 Why did relations between the US and the USSR change?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Attempts at détente, 1972–79: US motives for détente; Origins and nature of SALT I, 1972; Nature and importance of Helsinki Accords, 1975; Extent and importance of technical and economic cooperation, 1975–79
  - Reasons for the failure of détente: Importance of ideology in the Second Cold War; Impact of Afghanistan invasion; Change of leadership; New defence policy; Failure of ratification of SALT II
  - The Reagan Doctrine and rollback: Reasons for and aims of Reagan Doctrine and rollback interventions; US policy in Nicaragua – causes, nature and consequences; US intervention in Afghanistan – causes, nature and consequences; US intervention in Angola – causes, nature and consequences
  - Reagan and Gorbachev: Significance of Gorbachev’s policies for relations with US; Influence of Reagan’s policies in bringing about change in Soviet regime; Changing policy of US towards the Soviet regime
- **What to cover and stress.** Détente 1972–79: US motives (Vietnam, economy, Sino-Soviet split, nuclear parity), SALT I (May 1972; ABM Treaty, Interim Agreement), Helsinki Accords (1 August 1975; three 'baskets'; human rights), technical and economic cooperation (grain deals, Apollo–Soyuz 1975), limits (Jackson–Vanik 1974). Failure of détente: ideology (Second Cold War), Afghanistan (December 1979; Carter Doctrine, grain embargo, Olympic boycott), leadership change (Reagan), new defence policy (build-up; SDI March 1983; 'evil empire' March 1983), SALT II (signed June 1979, not ratified). Reagan Doctrine and rollback: aims; Nicaragua (Contras; Boland Amendments; Iran–Contra 1986), Afghanistan (mujahideen; Stinger missiles 1986), Angola (UNITA; Clark Amendment repeal 1985). Reagan and Gorbachev: Gorbachev's policies (new thinking), summits (Geneva 1985, Reykjavik 1986, Washington 1987 INF Treaty, Moscow 1988), Reagan's influence on Soviet change (debated), US policy shift (Bush 1989–91; Malta 1989; START 1991).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Reagan's policies were the main reason for the end of the Cold War.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the failure of détente. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Raymond Garthoff, Détente and Confrontation (1985) and The Great Transition (1994); John Lewis Gaddis; Archie Brown, The Gorbachev Factor (1996); James Mann, The Rebellion of Ronald Reagan (2009).
- **Pitfalls.** Overlap with the Soviet Empire topic (hist.d.soviet): keep the US perspective here.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of arms control agreements (date, parties, terms, ratified?).
