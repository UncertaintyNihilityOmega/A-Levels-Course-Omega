### hist.d.soviet.4 · The Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe (4): the collapse of Soviet control, 1980–91 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, International option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 7 The Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe, 1953–91
- Key question 7.4 How and why did Soviet control of Eastern Europe collapse in 1991?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Reasons for the development and growth of Solidarity in Poland
  - Economic problems facing Poland
  - Events of August 1980 in Gdansk
  - 21 Demands
  - Reactions to Solidarity: From the Polish government; From the USSR; Imposition of Martial Law, 1981
  - Impact of Gorbachev’s policies on Eastern Europe: Nationalist movements in Hungary, Czechoslovakia and Romania; Rise in dissent and Soviet reactions to uprisings in Kazakhstan and the Baltic States; Sinatra Doctrine; Re-legalising Solidarity in 1989; Fall of the Berlin Wall
  - Reasons for the collapse of the Soviet Union: Opposition to Gorbachev’s policies; Causes and impact of the coup of August, 1991; Gorbachev’s resignation; Collapse of Soviet control in the Republics
- **What to cover and stress.** Poland: economic problems (debt, price rises July 1980), Gdańsk Lenin Shipyard strike (14 August 1980; Wałęsa, Anna Walentynowicz), the 21 Demands, Gdańsk Agreement (31 August 1980), Solidarity (about 10 million members; check), reactions of the Polish government and the USSR, martial law (13 December 1981; Jaruzelski). Gorbachev's impact: nationalist movements in Hungary (reburial of Nagy June 1989; border opened), Czechoslovakia (Velvet Revolution November 1989), Romania (Ceaușescu executed 25 December 1989); dissent and Soviet responses in Kazakhstan (Alma-Ata December 1986), the Baltic States (Baltic Way 23 August 1989; Vilnius January 1991); Sinatra Doctrine (October 1989); Solidarity re-legalised (April 1989; Round Table; June 1989 elections); fall of the Berlin Wall (9 November 1989); German reunification (3 October 1990). Collapse of the USSR: opposition to Gorbachev (hardliners and radicals; Yeltsin), coup of 19–21 August 1991 (causes, failure, impact), republics' independence, Belovezha Accords (8 December 1991), Gorbachev's resignation (25 December 1991).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Solidarity was the most important cause of the collapse of Soviet control in Eastern Europe.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the failure of the August 1991 coup. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Timothy Garton Ash, The Polish Revolution: Solidarity (1983) and We the People (1990); Stephen Kotkin, Uncivil Society (2009); Serhii Plokhy, The Last Empire (2014); Mary Elise Sarotte, 1989 (2009).
- **Pitfalls.** The syllabus title says 'collapse in 1991' but Eastern European regimes fell in 1989: distinguish the satellites (1989) from the USSR (1991).
