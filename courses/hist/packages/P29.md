### hist.d.usa45.3 · Post-war USA, 1945–61 (3): economic and social change, 1945–61 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 4 Truman, Eisenhower and post-war USA, 1945–61
- Key question 4.3 How far were the years 1945–61 ones of economic and social change?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The impact of consumerism and urbanisation: Economic impact of growth in demand for consumer products; growth of domestic market and services; Technological developments; Impact on credit and debt; Social impacts of consumer culture; Urban growth: rural urban divide, inner city problems, suburban growth
  - Changing role and status of women: Impact of economic growth and demand for labour; Impact of more education; Impact of suburban culture and desire for return to pre-war ‘normal’; Role of feminism
  - The development of a youth culture: Role of wartime impact on family life and more parents working in 1950s; Role of development of consumerism and products and marketing directed at the young; Role of media – TV and movies; Role of popular music
  - Prosperity and poverty in the 1950s: Reasons for greater prosperity in 1950s – GI Bill, defence spending, rising demand, technology, infrastructure spending; Reasons for continuing poverty: differential between unionised industries, rural poverty in south and southwest, inner city decline as suburban growth developed, unskilled workers and discrimination in pay for women and ethnic minorities
- **What to cover and stress.** Consumerism and urbanisation: demand for cars, TVs (TV households from about 9 per cent in 1950 to about 87 per cent in 1960; check), appliances; services; technology; credit (Diners Club 1950; consumer debt) and debt; consumer culture; suburbs (Levittown 1947), the rural–urban divide, inner-city problems, Sun Belt. Women: wartime work and post-war return, demand for labour, education, suburban culture and the 'feminine mystique', the roots of feminism. Youth culture: wartime family change, working parents, consumer products for teenagers, TV and films (Rebel Without a Cause 1955), rock and roll (Elvis). Prosperity: GI Bill (1944), defence spending, demand, technology, infrastructure; poverty: non-unionised industries, rural South and Southwest, inner cities, unskilled, discrimination; Michael Harrington, The Other America (1962) as later evidence; poverty rate about 22 per cent in 1959 (check, census).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 2024/specimen asked about consumers driving growth: write differently, e.g. 'The 1950s were a decade of conformity rather than change.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the development of a youth culture. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** James Patterson, Grand Expectations (1996); Lizabeth Cohen, A Consumers' Republic (2003); Elaine Tyler May, Homeward Bound (1988).
- **Pitfalls.** Specimen Paper 4 Question 4 is on this KQ: never copy it.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): GNP and TV ownership 1945–61 from cited sources.

### hist.d.usa45.4 · Post-war USA, 1945–61 (4): the foreign policies of Truman and Eisenhower (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 4 Truman, Eisenhower and post-war USA, 1945–61
- Key question 4.4 How different were the foreign policies of Truman and Eisenhower?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Truman and the policy of containment: Truman’s concerns about Soviet expansion; Potsdam Conference; State of post war Western Europe and Marshall Aid; Greece and Truman Doctrine
  - The issue of Germany: Post-war division of Germany and tensions between victor powers; Berlin Blockade causes, course and consequences; Creation of two Germanies; Berlin Crisis, 1958–59
  - The significance of US involvement in Korea: Division of Korea 1945 and US policy towards South Korea; US reaction to invasion of South Korea, 1950; US participation in Korean War and decision to invade North Korea; Impact of Korean War on US policy in Asia
  - Eisenhower’s foreign policy – a change in direction?: Policy in South Asia – Korea, Vietnam; Policy towards USSR – defence policy and dependence on nuclear deterrent, relations after 1953, peaceful co-existence, Geneva Summit, meeting with Khrushchev, U2 affair, Hungary; Policy towards Middle East – Iran and Suez; Latin America – Guatemala and Cuba
- **What to cover and stress.** Truman and containment: Potsdam; Long Telegram and Kennan; Western Europe's state; Truman Doctrine (March 1947: Greece and Turkey), Marshall Aid (about $13 billion 1948–52; check); Germany: zones, tensions, Berlin Blockade (June 1948–May 1949) and Airlift, two Germanies (1949), Berlin crisis 1958–59 (Khrushchev's ultimatum November 1958). Korea: division (1945, 38th parallel), US policy (Acheson's perimeter speech January 1950), invasion (25 June 1950), UN response, Inchon (September 1950), crossing the 38th parallel, Chinese intervention (October–November 1950), MacArthur dismissed (April 1951), armistice (27 July 1953), impact on Asia policy (NSC-68, Japan, Taiwan, rearmament). Eisenhower: New Look, massive retaliation, brinkmanship (Dulles), Korea armistice, Vietnam (Dien Bien Phu 1954; Geneva; SEATO 1954; domino theory April 1954), USSR after Stalin's death (1953), Geneva Summit (1955), peaceful coexistence, Khrushchev visit (Camp David 1959), U-2 (May 1960), Hungary (1956: no intervention), Middle East (Iran coup 1953; Suez 1956; Eisenhower Doctrine 1957), Latin America (Guatemala 1954; Cuba 1959–60).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Eisenhower's foreign policy was a continuation of Truman's.' Evaluate this view. Assess the significance of the Korean War for US foreign policy. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** John Lewis Gaddis, Strategies of Containment (1982); Melvyn Leffler, A Preponderance of Power (1992); Bruce Cumings on Korea.
- **Pitfalls.** Overlap with Paper 3 Cold War: here it is US policy and the comparison of the two presidents.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table comparing Truman and Eisenhower by region and method.
