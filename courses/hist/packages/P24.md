### hist.d.stalin.1 · Stalin's Russia (1): how Stalin gained control (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 2 Stalin’s Russia, 1924–41
- Key question 2.1 How did Stalin gain control of the Soviet Union?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The power vacuum following Lenin’s death, including: The nature of leadership of the party: collective leadership; Lenin’s Testament and funeral
  - The strengths and weaknesses of the candidates, including: Respective personalities and appeal of Stalin, Trotsky, Bukharin, Kamenev, Rykov, Tomsky and Zinoviev; Stalin’s use of roles within the party such as General Secretary
  - Ideological differences and the power struggle, including: Arguments relating to economic development: New Economic Policy (NEP) versus industrialisation; Socialism in One Country versus Permanent Revolution; Changing alliances
  - Repression and propaganda to 1929, including: The beginning of the cult of personality: ‘Lenin’s disciple’; Machinery of repression
- **What to cover and stress.** Lenin's strokes (1922–23), death (21 January 1924), the Testament (December 1922–January 1923: 'Stalin is too rude'), its suppression (May 1924), Lenin's funeral (Trotsky absent). Collective leadership: Politburo members Stalin, Trotsky, Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin, Rykov, Tomsky: personalities and appeal. Stalin's posts: General Secretary (1922), Orgburo, Rabkrin, control of appointments (nomenklatura), the Lenin enrolment (1924). Ideology: NEP vs rapid industrialisation (left vs right), Socialism in One Country (1924) vs Permanent Revolution. Alliances: triumvirate vs Trotsky (1923–25); Trotsky dismissed as war commissar (January 1925); United Opposition (1926); expulsions (1927), Trotsky exiled (1928, deported 1929); defeat of the Right (Bukharin, Rykov, Tomsky; 1928–29). Repression and propaganda to 1929: 'Lenin's disciple' cult, Leninism (Foundations of Leninism 1924), OGPU, Shakhty trial (1928), ban on factions (1921) used against rivals.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Quotations only if certain: Lenin's Testament ('Stalin is too rude') is well documented.
- **Worked examples and practice.** Assess the reasons for Stalin's victory in the power struggle (1924–29). 'Trotsky's mistakes were the main reason for Stalin's rise.' Evaluate this view. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Robert Service, Stalin (2004); Stephen Kotkin, Stalin: Paradoxes of Power (2014); Isaac Deutscher, Stalin (1949); E.H. Carr.
- **Pitfalls.** Avoid hindsight: in 1924 Stalin looked the least dangerous to his colleagues.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of the power struggle stages (year, alliance, who was defeated, issue).

### hist.d.stalin.2 · Stalin's Russia (2): how effectively Stalin governed the Soviet Union (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, European option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 2 Stalin’s Russia, 1924–41
- Key question 2.2 How effectively did Stalin govern the Soviet Union?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Terror and the Purges, including: Reasons for the Terror and Purges; Opposition to Stalin and Kirov’s murder; Role of NKVD; Show trials; Yezhovshchina: forced labour and gulags, and purge of the armed forces
  - The Cult of Personality, including: The development of the Stalin cult; Propaganda methods: aims and impact
  - Stalin’s leadership, including: The nature of Stalin’s leadership and the Stalin Constitution
  - Aims and impact of foreign policy, including: Relations with Germany; Search for collective security: Comintern, League of Nations, France and Czechoslovakia; Intervention in Spanish Civil War; Policies towards China and Japan; Nazi–Soviet Pact
- **What to cover and stress.** Terror: reasons (security, scapegoats for economic failure, Stalin's personality and paranoia, the system), opposition (Ryutin platform 1932; 17th Congress 1934 'Congress of Victors'), Kirov's murder (1 December 1934) and its use, NKVD (Yagoda, Yezhov, Beria), show trials (Zinoviev and Kamenev August 1936; Pyatakov 1937; Bukharin March 1938), Yezhovshchina (1937–38; Order 00447; about 680,000 executed in 1937–38 according to NKVD records; check and cite), Gulag (camps population over 1.5 million by 1939; check), military purge (Tukhachevsky June 1937; most senior commanders). Cult of personality: development (50th birthday 1929), methods (posters, renamed cities, Short Course 1938, art, history rewritten), aims and impact. Leadership: nature (Politburo meetings fewer, personal rule), Stalin Constitution (1936: rights on paper). Foreign policy: relations with Germany (Rapallo legacy; 1933 change), collective security (Comintern Popular Fronts 1935, League 1934, pacts with France and Czechoslovakia 1935), Spain (aid 1936–38), China (Sino-Soviet non-aggression 1937) and Japan (Khalkhin Gol 1939; neutrality pact April 1941), Nazi–Soviet Pact (23 August 1939), Finland (Winter War 1939–40) for context, to Barbarossa (22 June 1941).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The Purges were mainly the result of Stalin's personality.' Evaluate this view. Assess the effectiveness of Stalin's foreign policy, 1929–41. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Robert Conquest, The Great Terror (1968); J. Arch Getty, Origins of the Great Purges (1985: revisionist, centre–periphery); Oleg Khlevniuk; Sheila Fitzpatrick; Kotkin; Jonathan Haslam on foreign policy.
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: numbers from archival research (cite, give ranges); victims named where possible.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of the show trials; hist-chart (if built) of Gulag population from cited archival figures.
