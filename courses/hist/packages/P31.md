### hist.d.usa61.3 · A time of challenges, 1961–74 (3): civil rights for African Americans (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 5 A time of challenges: the USA, 1961–74
- Key question 5.3 How much progress was made in achieving civil rights for African Americans?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The methods and achievements of civil rights organisations: The ideas and methods of Martin Luther King and civil rights organisations; The Birmingham Campaign 1963 and its consequences; The Washington March; The Freedom Riders; The Mississippi Freedom Summer Project and the reaction to it; Bloody Sunday and the Selma to Montgomery, March 1965
  - Opposition to civil rights: Attempts by state officials in the South to stop African Americans from registering to vote; The actions of local authorities against civil rights including Bull Connor and Governor Wallace in Alabama; Opposition of Southern Congressmen; Local citizens groups and reasons for popular opposition; The activities of the Ku Klux Klan in the 1960s
  - The role of federal institutions in improving civil rights: The importance of actions by the executive before 1964; Political change: the Civil Rights Acts, 1964 and 1968, the Voting Rights Act, 1965; Social policies: the Fair Housing Act, 1968, the Equal Employment Opportunity Act 1972; Rulings of the Supreme Court including that against laws banning interracial marriage, 1967
- **What to cover and stress.** King's ideas and methods (nonviolence, direct action, SCLC), Birmingham campaign (April–May 1963; 'Letter from Birmingham Jail' 16 April; Bull Connor's dogs and hoses; children's crusade), March on Washington (28 August 1963), Freedom Rides (May 1961; CORE), Freedom Summer (1964; Goodman, Chaney and Schwerner murdered June 1964; MFDP), Selma (Bloody Sunday 7 March 1965; march to Montgomery 21–25 March). Opposition: voter registration barriers (literacy tests, poll taxes; 24th Amendment 1964), Bull Connor and Governor Wallace ('segregation forever', January 1963; stand in the schoolhouse door June 1963), Southern congressmen (filibuster 1964), White Citizens' Councils, the Klan (16th Street Baptist Church bombing 15 September 1963). Federal role: executive actions before 1964 (Kennedy's interventions at Ole Miss 1962 and Alabama 1963), Civil Rights Acts 1964 and 1968, Voting Rights Act 1965 (registration figures in Mississippi from about 7 per cent to about 60 per cent; check), Fair Housing Act 1968, Equal Employment Opportunity Act 1972, Loving v. Virginia (12 June 1967).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'The federal government was the most important factor in the progress of civil rights in the 1960s.' Evaluate this view. Assess the importance of King's leadership. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Taylor Branch (trilogy 1988–2006); Clayborne Carson, In Struggle (1981); Adam Fairclough, To Redeem the Soul of America (1987); Charles Payne (grassroots organising).
- **Pitfalls.** Avoid a King-only story: local organisers, SNCC, women (Ella Baker, Fannie Lou Hamer).
- **Figures and widgets.** Table of laws and what each changed; hist-timeline (if built) 1960–68.

### hist.d.usa61.4 · A time of challenges, 1961–74 (4): challenges to authority (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 5 A time of challenges: the USA, 1961–74
- Key question 5.4 Why were there challenges to authority?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - The aims, methods and achievements of the Feminist Movement: The influence of leading campaigners including Betty Friedan, the formation and aims of NOW; The activities and impact of the women’s liberation movement; The Roe versus Wade judgement, 1973; Opposition to the women’s movement
  - Alternative approaches by African Americans: Malcom X and the Nation of Islam; Stokely Carmichael and the growth and impact of the Black Power movement; Bobby Seale and Huey Newton and the Black Panther Movement: its political activities and social programmes; Opposition to Black Power
  - The emergence of identity politics: Discrimination and the struggle for lesbian, gay and transgender rights, Stonewall 1969, the formation and impact of the Gay Liberation Front and the Gay Activist Alliance; Problems facing Native Americans; The American Indian Movement
  - Popular protests and the reaction of the authorities: The anti-Vietnam demonstrations including the Democratic National Convention, 1968 and the reaction of Mayor Daley; The impact of the Kent State killings; Inner city riots including the Watts riots and the 1968 riots following Martin Luther King’s assassination
- **What to cover and stress.** Feminism: Betty Friedan, The Feminine Mystique (1963), NOW (1966; aims), women's liberation (Miss America protest 1968, consciousness-raising, Ms. magazine 1972), Title IX (1972), ERA passed by Congress (1972) and the ratification fight, Roe v. Wade (22 January 1973), opposition (Phyllis Schlafly's STOP ERA 1972). Alternative African American approaches: Malcolm X and the Nation of Islam (assassinated 21 February 1965), Stokely Carmichael and Black Power (June 1966), Bobby Seale and Huey Newton, Black Panther Party (October 1966; Ten-Point Program; free breakfast programmes; armed patrols; FBI COINTELPRO), opposition to Black Power. Identity politics: Stonewall (28 June 1969), Gay Liberation Front (1969), Gay Activists Alliance (1969); Native Americans (poverty, termination policy), the American Indian Movement (1968; Alcatraz occupation 1969–71; Wounded Knee 1973). Protests: anti-war (SDS, teach-ins, draft resistance), Democratic National Convention Chicago (August 1968; Mayor Daley; the Walker Report's 'police riot'), Kent State (4 May 1970; four students killed), riots: Watts (August 1965; 34 deaths), 1967 Detroit and Newark, April 1968 after King's assassination (4 April 1968); Kerner Commission (1968).
- **Worked examples and practice.** Specimen Q5 asked about reasons for the growth of feminism (and 2024 about opposition to feminism): write different questions, e.g. 'The Black Power movement achieved more than it is given credit for.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons why protest movements grew in the late 1960s. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Ruth Rosen, The World Split Open (2000); Peniel Joseph, Waiting 'til the Midnight Hour (2006); Todd Gitlin, The Sixties (1987).
- **Pitfalls.** Sensitive: deaths at Kent State, in riots: factual. Use respectful, current terms for groups.
- **Figures and widgets.** Table: movement, aims, methods, achievements, opposition.
- **Next line.** `Next: [The USA, 1974–93 (1): the problems of the late 1970s](#hist.d.usa74.1)`
