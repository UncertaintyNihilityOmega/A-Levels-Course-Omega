### hist.skills.sources · Skills: reading and comparing sources (Paper 1) (`@level as`)

- **What to cover and stress.** Paper 1 skills in depth (AS, AO3 75 per cent): reading a source (content, tone, language, omissions); provenance (nature, origin, purpose) and context; cross-referencing; 'as evidence' meaning; comparing two sources: similarities AND differences, explained by context and provenance (Table A levels); using four sources for 'how far' (Table B levels): grouping, support and challenge, evaluating each source's weight in context (not generic 'bias'), knowledge to interpret and test sources, a judgement on 'how far'; visual sources (cartoons: symbols, labels, the artist and publication; photographs: staging, selection; posters); timing (about 25 minutes for (a), 45 for (b), reading time). Use real sources from several AS topics, labelled.
- **Real sources for Paper 1 practice and quotation.** Real documents from across the AS options (e.g. a Punch or Low cartoon described in words; the Fourteen Points; a Russian decree), labelled and adapted correctly.
- **Worked examples and practice.** Worked examples: one (a) at Levels 2/3/4; one (b) at Levels 3/4/5; spot the weak paragraph (generic bias comment). Exam practice on three different topics, one per option.
- **Pitfalls.** 'Bias' is not evaluation unless explained from context. Never fabricate a source.

### hist.skills.essays · Skills: writing the analytical essay (Papers 2 and 4) (`@level as`)

- **What to cover and stress.** Essay skills for Paper 2 (AS) and Paper 4 (A Level): command words (Explain why; To what extent; How far do you agree; How far was … the main reason; How successful/important; Assess; Evaluate) from FACTS.md; Paper 2 (a): factors, explanation, connections, conclusion (Table A); Paper 2 (b): given vs alternative perspectives, comparative judgement (Table B); Paper 4: criteria, balance, sustained argument, judgement, precise knowledge (Tables A and B, 15 + 15); planning in 3–5 minutes; introductions that set out the argument; paragraphs that argue (point, evidence, explanation, link to question); criteria for judgement ('most important' = necessary, widest impact, most lasting); conclusions that weigh; timing (Paper 2 about 15 minutes for (a), 35 for (b); Paper 4 about 50 minutes per essay).

## Widgets (this package builds them; one file each in widgets/<name>.js; kit/WIDGETS.md)

- `hist-provenance`: Source-evaluation trainer. Attributes: data-question (the 'as evidence about…' issue), data-sources ('label | provenance line | short content ;; …'), data-answers ('label | stronger or weaker | reason ;; …'). For each source the reader chooses nature, origin and purpose tags from given options and judges 'stronger' or 'weaker' as evidence for the question, then compares with a model judgement and reason. Keyboard and screen-reader friendly. First use in hist.skills.sources with real labelled sources.
- `hist-essayplan`: Essay-plan builder. Attributes: data-question, data-factors ('factor | side (support or challenge) | evidence hint ;; …'). The reader assigns each factor to support or challenge (buttons), ranks factors with up/down buttons, chooses a criterion and a judgement from options, then 'Build plan' prints an ordered paragraph outline (intro line, paragraphs, conclusion) as text they can copy. No dragging. First use in hist.skills.essays.
- Probe each (`node kit/zta.js widget <name> '<attrs>' --probe`) until clean; look once; use it with the keyboard only; test its logic with a small Node script.
