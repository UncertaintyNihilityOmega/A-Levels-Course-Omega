### hist.d.usa74.1 · The USA, 1974–93 (1): the problems of the late 1970s (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 6 The USA, 1974–93
- Key question 6.1 How significant were the problems facing the US in the late 1970s?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Ford’s response to the problems facing the US: Responses to problems of stagflation, unemployment, fiscal policy, policies towards energy; Responses to social divisions such as opposition to busing; Reponses to post-Watergate loss of trust in politics
  - Reasons for Carter’s victory in 1976 and his domestic policies, including: Carter’s personal appeal and disillusion with Republicans; Presidential style, fiscal policy, energy problems and responses including Energy Security Act; Relations with Congress, decline of reputation
  - The rise of the New Right: Influence of religious groups including evangelical Christians; Reaction against social change including abortion, gay rights; Opposition to the counter culture; Ending of consensus on role of state in economic policy
  - Reasons for Reagan’s election victory in 1980, including: Reagan’s personal appeal; Economic issues and spreads of alternative supply side economic doctrines; Perceived failures of Carter at home and abroad; Social change and impact of the rise of New Right
- **What to cover and stress.** Ford (August 1974–January 1977): pardon of Nixon (8 September 1974), stagflation (inflation about 11 per cent in 1974; unemployment about 8.5 per cent in 1975; check), WIN campaign, tax cut 1975, energy (Energy Policy and Conservation Act 1975), New York City fiscal crisis, busing (Boston 1974–76), post-Watergate distrust (Church Committee 1975), vetoes. 1976 election (Carter's outsider appeal, honesty, born-again faith; result 297–240). Carter: style, fiscal policy, energy crisis ('moral equivalent of war' 1977; Department of Energy 1977; 'malaise' speech 15 July 1979; Energy Security Act 1980), inflation (about 13 per cent in 1979; check), Volcker at the Fed (1979), relations with a Democratic Congress, decline (hostage crisis, Kennedy challenge). New Right: evangelicals (Moral Majority 1979; Falwell), reaction to abortion, gay rights, ERA, counter-culture; end of consensus on the economy (supply-side, Laffer; Proposition 13 1978). 1980 election (Reagan's appeal, economic issues and supply-side, Carter's failures at home and abroad, New Right; result 489–49 electoral votes, about 50.7 per cent; check).
- **Worked examples and practice.** Specimen Q6 asked whether Reagan owed his 1980 success to his personality: write a different question, e.g. 'Carter's presidency was a failure in domestic affairs.' Evaluate this view. Assess the significance of the rise of the New Right by 1980. Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** James Patterson, Restless Giant (2005); Bruce Schulman, The Seventies (2001); Dominic Sandbrook, Mad as Hell (2011).
- **Pitfalls.** Statistics: cite the source (BLS) and year for every figure.

### hist.d.usa74.2 · The USA, 1974–93 (2): the New Conservatism, 1981–92 (`@level a2`)

- Examined: A Level **Paper 4**, American option: [30] essays, 'Assess' and 'Evaluate' (FACTS.md).
- Syllabus topic: 6 The USA, 1974–93
- Key question 6.2 How great was the impact of the New Conservatism, 1981–92?
- Content (cover all of it; 'including' items in full):
  - Reaganomics in theory and practice: Key features of Reagonomics and neo liberal economic theory; Reasons for the end of Keynesian consensus; impact of tax cuts and deregulation; Extent of reduction of federal spending
  - The impact of Reagan’s domestic policies on the American people: Impact of tax policies and budget cuts; Policy to Trade Unions and Air Traffic Controllers’ Strike; Social issues – policy towards Civil Rights and AIDs; Deregulation and role and scope of the Federal Government
  - Bush’s domestic policies and the 1992 election: Fiscal policies and reaction to high levels of federal debt; Social reforms including Disabilities Act, 1990 and Clean Air Act, 1990; Relations with Congress; Election of 1992 – including: recession, impact of candidacy of Ross Perot, Dan Quayle, Clinton’s appeal
  - The failure of the Democrats in the 1980s: ‘Civil War’ between Carter and Kennedy; Divisions in the 1980s between radicals and more conservative elements; Problems faced by Mondale in modernising the party; Ongoing electoral weaknesses in the South
- **What to cover and stress.** Reaganomics: supply-side theory, Economic Recovery Tax Act 1981 (top rate 70 to 50 per cent; 1986 Tax Reform Act to 28 per cent), deregulation, monetarism (Volcker), end of Keynesian consensus, federal spending (domestic cuts but defence growth; deficits and debt roughly tripled 1981–89; check); impact on people (recession 1981–82; boom 1983–89; inequality; welfare cuts), PATCO strike (August 1981: over 11,000 controllers fired), civil rights (opposition to busing and affirmative action; Martin Luther King Day signed 1983; Civil Rights Restoration Act veto overridden 1988), AIDS response (slow), role of federal government. Bush (1989–93): 'read my lips' and the 1990 budget deal, Americans with Disabilities Act (1990), Clean Air Act amendments (1990), Congress, recession (1990–91). 1992 election: recession, Ross Perot (about 19 per cent of the popular vote), Dan Quayle, Clinton's appeal ('It's the economy'). Democrats' failure in the 1980s: Carter vs Kennedy (1980 primaries), radicals vs moderates (Democratic Leadership Council 1985), Mondale (1984), weakness in the South (Reagan Democrats).
- **Worked examples and practice.** 'Reaganomics benefited only the wealthy.' Evaluate this view. Assess the reasons for the Democrats' electoral failures in the 1980s. (2024 asked about living standards in the 1980s: reword.) Full Level 5 answer.
- **Historians you may name.** Lou Cannon, President Reagan (1991); Sean Wilentz, The Age of Reagan (2008); Patterson.
- **Pitfalls.** Neutral on political judgements: present the case for and against Reaganomics with data.
- **Figures and widgets.** hist-chart (if built): federal deficit and debt 1980–92 from cited data.
