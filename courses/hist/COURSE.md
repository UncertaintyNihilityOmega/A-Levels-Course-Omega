## Facts checked by the Organizer (2026-11-07, from the 2027–2029 syllabus)
- Paper 1 Historical sources, 1 h 15 min, 40 marks, AS 40% / A Level 20%: one two-part question on the option's
  Paper 1 topic: (a) 15 marks compare two sources, (b) 25 marks use all four sources and own knowledge.
- Paper 2 Outline study, 1 h 45 min, 60 marks, AS 60% / A Level 30%: two two-part questions on the option's other two
  topics: (a) 10 marks causation, (b) 20 marks given vs alternative factor with a judgement.
- Paper 3 Historical interpretations, 1 h 15 min, 40 marks, A Level 20%: one question on one topic, command phrase
  "What can you learn …" about an extract (AO1 50%, AO4 50%).
- Paper 4 Depth study, 1 h 45 min, 60 marks, A Level 30%: two questions from a choice of three on one option, 30 marks
  each, command words "Assess" and "Evaluate".
- **Topic rotation:** each AS option's Paper 1 topic changes every year (2027 / 2028 / 2029: European France /
  Germany / Russia; American Gilded Age / Great Crash / Civil War; International Imperialism / 1919–29 / 1929–39);
  the other two topics go to Paper 2. Every topic chapter states which papers it can appear on in each year.
- Routes: AS only (P1+P2), staged A Level (P1+P2 then P3+P4), A Level in one series (all four).

## Notes for this course
- **Dates, names, figures and historians' views must be exact.** Take key-question content from the syllabus; take
  historians (Paper 3) from well-known published works and name them only when you are sure of the work and its
  argument. Never invent a quotation: Paper 3 extracts in this course are written as clearly labelled paraphrased
  summaries of a historian's view, or short quotes you are certain of.
- Sources in Paper 1 practice: real documents described or briefly quoted with provenance (who, when, why), or clearly
  labelled adapted extracts; never a fabricated "primary source" passed off as real.
- `checks/*.py` are optional (essay course): use one where a chapter has numbers (statistics tables, dates
  arithmetic, mark totals).
- Sensitive topics (the Holocaust, apartheid, colonialism, the Terror, the Gulag): factual, respectful, no graphic
  detail beyond what the history needs.
- Widgets that earn their place: an interactive timeline, a source-provenance trainer, an essay-plan builder, a map
  stepper. Each belongs to one package.
