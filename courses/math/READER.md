# READER: what each chapter taught

## math.overview
Taught: the three strands; AS and A2; the six 9709 papers (times, marks, weights) and the AS and A Level routes; MF19 formula booklet; Edexcel IAL and UK board differences; AO1/AO2; M and A marks in words; error log; study pace; the chapter map.
Assumed: nothing (GCSE only).

## math.f.numbers
Taught: integer, rational, irrational, real, natural numbers and the symbols N, Z, Q, R and "x in R"; negatives; BIDMAS; fractions and mixed numbers; recurring decimals and their fractions; percentages, percentage change, multipliers, reverse and repeated change; ratio, direct and inverse proportion; decimal places, significant figures, estimation; primes, prime factors, HCF, LCM.
Assumed: the constant e (named as irrational, never explained); the mark tokens [M1] [A1] [B1] and the star levels (not explained here); "compound interest" as a term; a recurring decimal that starts with a non-repeating digit (Try Q3); the calculator Ans key.

## math.f.algebra
Taught: term, expression, equation, formula, coefficient, like terms; expanding up to three brackets; (a+b)^2 and difference of two squares; factorising (common factor, difference of two squares, x^2+bx+c); algebraic fractions (simplify, add, multiply, divide); linear equations with fractions; inequalities and the sign reversal; simultaneous equations by elimination and substitution; substitution; changing the subject (including a repeated subject); forming equations from words.
Assumed: why x cannot equal a value that makes a denominator 0; that a pair of simultaneous equations can have no solution; cubing a bracket.

## math.f.powers
Taught: base, index, indices; the index laws; zero, negative and fractional indices; equations with an unknown index; surds, simplifying, like surds, expanding with surds; rationalising (one surd, conjugate); proof by contradiction (root 2); standard form and its calculator entry.
Assumed: matching rational and surd parts of two equal expressions (used in Exam practice and Stretch, never stated).

## math.f.graphs
Taught: coordinates, axes, origin, intercepts, roots; sketch; gradient and y = mx + c; parallel lines; equation of a line from two points or a point and gradient (by substituting for c); sketching a line from its intercepts; the shapes of x^2, x^3, 1/x, 1/x^2, a^x, a^-x; asymptote; intersections of a line and a curve.
Assumed: numbering of the quadrants; the form y - y1 = m(x - x1) (used, not taught); turning point.

## math.f.geometry
Taught: angle facts and reasons; polygon angle sums; Pythagoras and its proof; sine, cosine, tangent; angles of elevation and depression; exact values for 30, 45, 60 degrees; sine rule, cosine rule, area 1/2 ab sin C with proofs; ambiguous case; circle theorems; arc length and sector area in degrees; prism, cylinder, cone, sphere.
Assumed: the exterior angle of a polygon (not defined); sin^2 theta notation; sin(180 - x) = sin x; where the sqrt 3 in the half-equilateral triangle comes from; applying the cosine rule to a named angle such as B.

## math.p1.quadratics
Taught: quadratic, linear, parabola, coefficient, root, vertex, line of symmetry; shape from the sign of a; solving by factorising (a = 1 and the ac method), by completing the square and by the formula (with proof); discriminant and number of roots; line meeting, touching or missing a curve; quadratic inequalities; one linear and one quadratic simultaneous equation; disguised quadratics by substitution.
Assumed: f(x) notation and "range" (used before the Functions chapter); alpha and beta as names for roots and that a root gives a factor; solving k^2 > 16 before inequalities are taught.

## math.p1.functions
Taught: function, f(x) and f : x to ..., domain, range, largest domain, one-one and many-one, horizontal line test; composite functions and order; inverse functions, their domain, the graph as a reflection in y = x; restricting a domain; transformations of graphs (translation, stretches, reflections), inside/outside rule, combined transformations.
Assumed: column vector notation for translations; "stretch parallel to an axis" and "scale factor"; midpoint and perpendicular-gradient rules (used in the inverse-graph proof); "increasing function".

## math.p1.coord
Taught: distance, midpoint and gradient of two points (and finding an end point from the midpoint); the three line forms y = mx + c, y - y1 = m(x - x1), ax + by + c = 0 (gradient -a/b); parallel and perpendicular gradients (m1 m2 = -1); perpendicular bisector; intersection of lines; equation of a circle (centre, radius), expanded form with g and f, completing the square; tangent and normal to a circle; line meeting a circle by the discriminant; circle through three points; angle in a semicircle; a locus that is a circle (Stretch).
Assumed: the modulus bars |a| (used in the distance proof, taught only in math.p3.algebra); the arrow for "so" (used in the Exam style example, never explained); the converse of Pythagoras (proof of perpendicular gradients).

## math.p1.circular
Taught: radian, 2 pi rad = 360 degrees, conversions both ways, RAD mode; sector (minor, major), arc length s = r theta, sector area, perimeter; chord, segment, chord length 2r sin(theta/2); segment area; sector-and-triangle problems.
Assumed: "subtends" (used in the radian definition and the segment section); a diagram the last Try question says is shown but the page does not draw.

## math.p1.trig
Taught: unit circle, sin, cos, tan of any angle, CAST, related angles, exact values in degrees and radians; graphs of sin, cos, tan and their transformations (amplitude, period, shifts); asymptote; principal values of the inverse functions; solving trig equations in a given interval (multiple and shifted angles, negative intervals, radians); identity and the symbol for it; tan = sin/cos; sin^2 + cos^2 = 1; proving identities; equations needing an identity; quadratics in sin, cos, tan.
Assumed: why sin(bx + c) shifts by -c/b (stated, not shown); sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x (used in the first Stretch answer, taught later in math.p3.trig); "odd" and "even" function in one bullet.

## math.p1.series
Taught: factorial, nCr, Pascal's triangle; binomial theorem for positive integer n, general term, constant term, products of brackets; sequence and series; AP (nth term, sum, in AP: 2b = a + c); GP (nth term, sum, in GP: b^2 = ac); convergence and sum to infinity; recurring decimal as a GP; AP and GP in real contexts; problems with two progressions.
Assumed: "ascending powers of x"; the arrow meaning "tends to" (r^n -> 0); "if and only if".

## math.p1.diff
Taught: tangent, chord, gradient of a curve as the limit of chord gradients; derivative dy/dx and f'(x); power rule for any rational power; roots and fractions as powers; chain rule; second derivative; tangent and normal; increasing and decreasing; stationary points and the second-derivative test; connected rates of change; maximum and minimum problems.
Assumed: "nature" of a stationary point; units written with negative powers (m s^-1); how dy/dx may be multiplied like a fraction in the chain rule after being called "not a fraction"; how the signs in the gradient table are found.

## math.p1.integ
Taught: integration as reversing differentiation, constant of integration, power rule, (ax + b)^n, finding c from a point, definite integrals, area under a curve (and below the axis, and by integrating in y), area between two graphs, improper integrals, volumes of revolution about either axis, with a hole.
Assumed: limit notation lim, t -> 0+ and "integrand"; "even function" (washer example); the volume formula for a frustum (Spot the error check); a figure the last Start-easy Try question says is shown.

## math.p3.algebra
Taught: modulus, V-shaped graph of |ax + b|, equations |ax + b| = c and with a variable on both sides (squaring and checking), modulus inequalities, polynomial and degree, long division by a linear or quadratic, remainder theorem, factor theorem, solving cubics, unknown coefficients.
Assumed: the "if and only if" double arrow; the a_n, a_(n-1) ... a_0 subscripts of a polynomial; how to divide a cubic by (x - a) by comparing coefficients (exam box says it, no example shows it).

## math.p3.logs
Taught: logarithm as the inverse of a power, log_a b = c, laws of logs, ln and e^x with graphs, growth and decay, solving a^x = b, equations that are quadratics in e^x or a^x, equations and inequalities with logs (reject invalid arguments), exponential models and half-life, linear form of y = kx^n and y = ka^x.
Assumed: e^(2x) = (e^x)^2 (used in the first disguised-quadratic example, shown only in a Try answer); the capital letters Y and X in "Y = mX + c".

## math.p3.trig
Taught: sec, cosec, cot and their graphs and ranges; sec^2 = 1 + tan^2 and cosec^2 = 1 + cot^2; compound angle formulae for sin, cos, tan (with proof) and exact values such as sin 75; double angle formulae (three forms of cos 2A); solving equations by factorising (never dividing by a trig function); proving identities and "hence"; a sin t + b cos t as R sin(t +- a) or R cos(t +- a), greatest and least values, solving equations with it.
Assumed: the symbol for "opposite sign" in tan(A +- B) = ... over 1 -+ tan A tan B; why R^2 = a^2 + b^2 (needs sin^2 + cos^2 = 1, not said); how the second and later solutions are found when the angle is t + alpha (390 degrees appears with no method); the end-of-proof symbol.

## math.p3.diff
Taught: derivatives of e^x, ln x, sin x, cos x, tan x and the ax + b versions (radians only); chain rule for composites; product rule; quotient rule; tangents, normals and stationary points of these functions; parametric differentiation; implicit differentiation; derivative of tan^-1 x; rates of change with exponentials.
Assumed: that dy/dx = 1/(dx/dy) and dt/dx = 1/(dx/dt) ("turn upside down"); the prime notation u', v' for du/dx, dv/dx; what the triangles are in the sin h < h < tan h argument and why cos h - 1 = -sin^2 h/(1 + cos h); the units of t in the population example.

## math.p3.integ
Taught: integrals of e^(ax+b), 1/(ax+b), sin, cos, sec^2 with ax + b; ln|x|; expanding or dividing before integrating; definite integrals with ln and e; sin^2, cos^2, tan^2 by identities; areas and volumes; rate of change integrated to a total; trapezium rule (ordinates, n strips) and over/underestimate by convex or concave; the integral of 1/(x^2 + a^2).
Assumed: where the "true value" 3 ln 3 - 2 of the integral of ln x comes from (by parts, taught later); the second derivative of e^(x^2) and the simplification 1/2 x sqrt(2/3) = 1/sqrt 6 (steps not shown).

## math.p3.numerical
Taught: root of f(x) = 0; sign change rule and its three failures; locating a root with two graphs; showing a root to n d.p. with the half-unit interval; iteration x(n+1) = F(x(n)) with the ANS key; convergence, divergence and oscillation; choosing a rearrangement; finding the equation from an iteration.
Assumed: interval notation [a, b] and (a, b); the meaning of "continuous" at its first use (explained only in the proof after the key box); whether "oscillate" means convergent or not.

## math.p3.partial
Taught: partial fractions for distinct linear, repeated linear and irreducible quadratic factors (substitution, comparing coefficients, cover-up proof); binomial series for any rational n with |x| < 1; (a + bx)^n by factoring out a^n; range of validity; expanding a fraction by partial fractions and the binomial series.
Assumed: why a value such as x = 1 may be substituted when it makes the original denominator zero; which terms give the x^2 and constant terms when comparing coefficients; the expansion of (1 + x)^-2 (used, not derived); that c is the speed of light; how "divide first" is done (called outside the syllabus here, used in the next chapter).

## math.p3.inttech
Taught: integrand; the f'/f rule; integration with partial fractions (dividing first); integration by parts (choice of u, ln x, repeated application, definite form); integration by substitution with a given substitution and changed limits; choosing a method.
Assumed: how dx is replaced from du/dx in the substitution routine (rearranging is not stated); why ln(x - 1) has no modulus bars in the divide-first example.

## math.p3.diffeq
Taught: differential equation; general and particular solution; initial condition; forming a DE from "proportional to"; separating the variables; growth, decay and cooling; draining tank; logistic growth by partial fractions; slope field; interpreting the long-term behaviour.
Assumed: the practical step of separating (divide by g(y), multiply by dx) once dy/dx is called "not a fraction"; e^(12k) = 2^(12/5); the sign in the integral of 1/(10 - P) and the algebra for P; what the slope-field widget shows below P = 0.

## math.p3.vectors
Taught: vector and scalar; column and i, j, k forms; magnitude and unit vector; parallel vectors; position vector; AB = b - a; midpoint; vector equation of a line; line through two points; intersecting, parallel and skew lines; scalar product and the angle between vectors or lines; foot of the perpendicular and shortest distance; axes on a cuboid or pyramid.
Assumed: that r in r = a + tb is the position vector of a general point; that the direction vector of a moving point is its velocity and its magnitude the speed.

## math.p3.complex
Taught: i, complex number, real and imaginary part, Re and Im; adding, subtracting, multiplying; conjugate z*, dividing by multiplying by the conjugate; equating real and imaginary parts; quadratics with negative discriminant, conjugate pairs of roots of a real polynomial; Argand diagram, modulus, argument (principal value, quadrant adjustment); modulus-argument and exponential form; products and quotients in polar form (with proof); square roots of a complex number; loci |z - a| = k, |z - a| = |z - b|, arg(z - a) = alpha, shaded regions.
Assumed: sqrt(-36) = 6i; what e^(i theta) means (Euler's formula, given as a fact); that conjugates respect sums, products, powers and quotients; how the third factor of a cubic is found by comparing coefficients; |z + 2i| as |z - (-2i)| and |x + iy - 1| written out in x and y; the power rule (re^(i theta))^n; why the furthest point of a circle from the origin is distance to centre plus radius.

## math.s1.data
Taught: discrete, continuous, grouped data; stem-and-leaf and back-to-back diagrams with a key; median, quartiles (median of each half), IQR, range, mode; five-number summary, box plot, outlier rule 1.5 x IQR; mean, variance and standard deviation by the formula with sum x and sum x^2 (and its proof), frequency tables, grouped data by midpoints, coded data, combining two data sets; histograms with frequency density; cumulative frequency graphs, median, quartiles, percentiles by interpolation; comparing data sets in context.
Assumed: the sigma sign for "add up" and x bar; sigma as the symbol for standard deviation; the median rule for an even number of values; why the linear interpolation formula works.

## math.s1.perms
Taught: multiplication principle (and adding alternatives); factorial, 0! = 1; permutations nPr and combinations nCr (with proofs); arrangements with repeats n!/(p! q!); together, apart, at the ends; numbers from digits (even, odd, greater than); selections with "exactly", "at least", "must include" by cases or complement; seating in rows; counting probabilities.
Assumed: nothing beyond GCSE and the factorial from math.p1.series (the 9 x 9 x 8 x 7 count of four-digit numbers with no repeated digit does not say why the second digit has 9 choices).

## math.s1.prob
Taught: event, sample space, equally likely outcomes, complement; union and intersection, P(A or B), Venn diagrams; mutually exclusive and independent events and the product test; tree diagrams with and without replacement; conditional probability P(A | B) and the multiplication rule (with proof); probabilities by counting with nCr.
Assumed: the symbols n(A) and n(S) (recap only); that D stands for defective in the factory example.

## math.s1.drv
Taught: random variable, discrete, probability distribution table, sum of probabilities = 1; E(X), E(X^2), Var(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2; finding unknowns in a table; binomial B(n, p) conditions, P(X = r), mean np, variance npq (mean proof); "at least" by complement; smallest n using logarithms; geometric Geo(p), P(X = r), P(X > r), mean 1/p (proof); choosing between the models; commenting on the conditions.
Assumed: the sigma sign (with limits in the proof); what the tilde in X ~ B(n, p) means; how cumulative binomial totals (P(X <= 6) etc.) are found on the calculator; logarithms from math.p3.logs (a student who has done only P1 has not met them).

## math.s1.normal
Taught: continuous random variable, density curve, P(X = a) = 0; N(mu, sigma^2) (second number is the variance); standardising Z = (X - mu)/sigma (with proof); Phi(z), Phi(-z) = 1 - Phi(z) (with proof); probabilities between and beyond values; comparing results by z-values; inverse normal and finding mu, sigma, or both from probabilities; normal approximation to B(n, p) when np > 5 and nq > 5, continuity correction (with proof); combining a normal probability with a binomial one.
Assumed: how to read the normal table for z with three decimals (and where it is); where inverse-normal values such as 0.8416, 0.6745, 1.0364 come from; the notation Phi inverse; the algebra of subtracting two equations that have sigma in the denominator.

## math.s2.poisson
Taught: Poisson Po(lambda), its conditions (singly, independently, constant rate) and how they fail; P(X = r) = e^-lambda lambda^r / r!, mean = variance = lambda; step-by-step recurrence; "at most", "at least", "more than"; scaling the mean to another interval; Poisson approximation to B(n, p) when n > 50 and np < 5 (with proof); normal approximation N(lambda, lambda) when lambda > 15 with continuity correction; comparing a data set's mean and variance.
Assumed: the series 1 + x + x^2/2! + ... = e^x; the limit (1 + x/n)^n -> e^x (called "standard"); how cumulative Poisson totals such as P(X <= 7) are found; sigma with limits (Stretch hint).

## math.s2.linear
Taught: E(aX + b) and Var(aX + b) (with proof); Var(X) = E[(X - mu)^2] = E(X^2) - mu^2 (proof); E and Var of aX + bY for independent X and Y, variances add for a difference (with proof); X1 + X2 versus 2X; sum of n independent observations; independent normals combine to a normal; steps for "total" and "difference" questions; sum of independent Poissons is Poisson, a difference or multiple is not; the mean of a sample (stretch).
Assumed: E of an expression such as E[(X - mu)^2], and that E of a sum is the sum of the E's; the cumulative Poisson value P(X + Y <= 11); that the "lift" in Exam practice question 8 is the passengers' ferry lift.
