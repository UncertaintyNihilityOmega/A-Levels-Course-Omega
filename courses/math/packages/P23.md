## math.fm.hyperbolic · Hyperbolic functions (5–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 2.1):
- 2.1a understand the definitions of sinh x, cosh x, tanh x, sech x, cosech x, coth x in terms of the exponential
  function;
- 2.1b sketch the graphs of hyperbolic functions;
- 2.1c prove and use identities involving hyperbolic functions (e.g. cosh²x − sinh²x ≡ 1, sinh 2x ≡ 2 sinh x cosh x,
  and similar results corresponding to the standard trigonometric identities);
- 2.1d understand and use the definitions of the inverse hyperbolic functions and derive and use the logarithmic
  forms.
Before you start: [Logarithms and exponentials](#math.p3.logs), trig identities from [Further trigonometry](#math.p3.trig),
quadratics in eˣ.
Cover: definitions; values at 0; odd/even; graphs (sinh, cosh, tanh with asymptotes y = ±1, sech, cosech, coth) as
`:::plot`s; the hanging chain (catenary) as the real context; identities proved from definitions (proving is examined:
`:::eg (Exam style)` with marks, June 2025 FP2 Q6(a) "starting from the definitions … prove that 1 − tanh²u =
sech²u" [3]); Osborn's rule as a checking tool only (it is not a proof; say so); solving equations (a cosh x + b sinh x
= c via eˣ quadratic; via identities); inverse functions with domains and ranges (cosh⁻¹ principal value ≥ 0);
deriving sinh⁻¹x = ln(x + √(x² + 1)) and the others (examined derivations as `:::eg` with marks); MF19 gives the
identities listed in FACTS.md and the log forms: say which must be derived anyway when asked.
Pitfalls: cosh⁻¹ has two candidate log values; sign errors in sinh 2x-type identities; rejecting eˣ < 0 without a
reason.
Figures: two `:::plot`s (sinh/cosh with ½eˣ; tanh with asymptotes).

## math.fm.diff · Further differentiation and Maclaurin series (6–8 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 2.3):
- 2.3a differentiate hyperbolic functions and differentiate sin⁻¹x, cos⁻¹x, sinh⁻¹x, cosh⁻¹x and tanh⁻¹x;
- 2.3b obtain an expression for d²y/dx² in cases where the relation between x and y is defined implicitly or
  parametrically;
- 2.3c derive and use the first few terms of a Maclaurin's series for a function (derivation of a general term is not
  included, but successive "implicit" differentiation steps may be required, e.g. for y = tan x following an initial
  differentiation rearranged as y′ = 1 + y²).
Before you start: [Further differentiation](#math.p3.diff) (chain, product, quotient, implicit, parametric first
derivatives), [Hyperbolic functions](#math.fm.hyperbolic), binomial series from [Modulus and polynomials](#math.p3.algebra).
Cover: derivatives of sinh, cosh, tanh (from definitions) and sech, cosech, coth; inverse trig derivatives by implicit
differentiation (sin y = x ⇒ …) and the inverse hyperbolic ones (from log forms and implicitly); chain-rule
combinations; second derivatives: implicit (differentiate again, substitute y′), parametric (d²y/dx² = d/dt(dy/dx) ÷
dx/dt — the classic slip is dividing by d²x/dt²; June 2025 FP2 Q6(c), (d)); Maclaurin: the idea of matching
derivatives at 0, the general MF19 formula (given), standard series on MF19 (eˣ, ln(1 + x), sin, cos, tan⁻¹, sinh,
cosh, tanh⁻¹ with validity) and composing them (e^(2x) sin x by multiplying, ln(1 + sin x) by successive
differentiation), the y′ = 1 + y² technique for tan x, validity ranges, using a series to approximate a value or a
limit.
Pitfalls: d²y/dx² for parametric curves; factorials in the denominators; truncating products too early or too late.
Widget: embed **fm-maclaurin** in the Maclaurin section.

### Widget spec: fm-maclaurin (widgets/fm-maclaurin.js)
- `data-f="exp|sin|cos|ln1p|atan|sinh|cosh|tan"` (default sin), `data-n="3"` (degree), `data-x="-4,4"`.
- Canvas (W.axes): the function (solid, labelled) and its Maclaurin polynomial of degree n (dashed, labelled "degree
  n"), with the interval of validity shaded or marked for ln(1 + x) (−1 < x ≤ 1) and tan⁻¹x (|x| ≤ 1); y-range follows
  the function on the window, the polynomial is clipped.
- Controls: function buttons (`.w-tabs`), degree slider 0–15 (only the degrees that add a term change the curve; say
  so), an x₀ slider with a readout of f(x₀), the polynomial value and the error.
- Readout: the polynomial written out (first 4 non-zero terms then "+ …"). Coefficients computed exactly from known
  series formulas (no numeric differentiation). Node-test the coefficients against the MF19 series.

## math.fm.integ · Further integration: hyperbolic and inverse forms (6–8 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 2.4a): integrate hyperbolic functions and recognise integrals of functions of the form
1/√(a² − x²), 1/√(x² + a²) and 1/√(x² − a²), and integrate associated functions using trigonometric or hyperbolic
substitutions as appropriate (including use of completing the square where necessary, e.g. to integrate
1/√(x² + 2x)). Note: any integration question may require 9709 techniques applied to harder cases (by parts for
∫eˣ sin x dx, the substitution t = tan ½x).
Before you start: [Integration techniques: parts, substitution and partial fractions](#math.p3.inttech), [Further integration](#math.p3.integ),
[Hyperbolic functions](#math.fm.hyperbolic), [Further differentiation and Maclaurin series](#math.fm.diff).
Cover: ∫sinh, cosh, sech², and products/powers via identities (cosh²x = ½(cosh 2x + 1)); the three standard inverse
forms (MF19 gives them: say so) and why (differentiate the answer); the substitutions x = a sin θ, x = a sinh u,
x = a cosh u; completing the square then a standard form; √(a² + x²) and √(x² − a²) themselves by substitution;
1/(a² − x²) and 1/(x² − a²) in log form (9709 MF19 entries); t = tan ½x for ∫1/(a + b cos x) (MF19 gives sin and cos
in t); by parts twice for eˣ sin x; definite integrals in exact log form (sinh⁻¹ written as ln).
Pitfalls: forgetting dx = a cosh u du; limits not changed; mixing sinh⁻¹ and sin⁻¹ forms.
