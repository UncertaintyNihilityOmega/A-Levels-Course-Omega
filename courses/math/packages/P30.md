# P30 · Further Mechanics: variable forces and impacts

Chapters: `math.fm.variable`, `math.fm.impulse`, `math.fm.oblique`. Paper: 9231 Paper 3 (FM). New widget:
**fm-impact**. Read COURSE.md, FURTHER.md, kit/STYLE.md and the gold chapter math.fm.roots first. g = 10. Build on
[Momentum and collisions](#math.m.momentum), [Kinematics: describing motion](#math.m.kinematics), [Differential equations](#math.p3.diffeq).

## math.fm.variable · Linear motion under a variable force (5–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 3.5a): solve problems which can be modelled as the linear motion of a particle under the action of
a variable force, by setting up and solving an appropriate differential equation; including use of v dv/dx for
acceleration where appropriate; calculus restricted to 9709 Pure Mathematics 3; only separable differential equations.
Cover: F = ma with a = dv/dt or v dv/dx (derive v dv/dx by the chain rule as an `:::eg`); choosing the form (force in
terms of t → dv/dt; in terms of x → v dv/dx; in terms of v → either, depending on what is asked); resistance
proportional to v or v² (terminal speed, time to halve speed, distance to stop); forces like k/x² (escape-type
problems), forces in terms of x (June 2025 FM Q1 shape: v in terms of x, then x in terms of t); setting directions
and signs; initial conditions; interpreting limits (v → terminal value as t → ∞).
Pitfalls: sign of a resistance; integrating v dv/dx as if it were dv/dx; losing the mass when dividing.

## math.fm.impulse · Direct impact and restitution (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.6a, 3.6b direct cases):
- 3.6a recall Newton's experimental law and the definition of the coefficient of restitution, the property
  0 ≤ e ≤ 1, and the meaning of the terms "perfectly elastic" (e = 1) and "inelastic" (e = 0);
- 3.6b use conservation of linear momentum and/or Newton's experimental law to solve problems that may be modelled as
  the direct impact of two smooth spheres, or the direct impact of a smooth sphere with a fixed surface.
Cover: momentum recap (link); impulse I = mv − mu (as in 9709 if there; else teach briefly); separation speed =
e × approach speed (not on MF19: learn); direction conventions (take right as positive; unknown velocities as v₁, v₂
in the positive direction); two equations, two unknowns; loss of kinetic energy; sphere hitting a wall, bouncing
ball heights (eⁿ geometric series, link [Series: binomial expansion and progressions](#math.p1.series)); successive collisions (three spheres in a line;
conditions for a further collision, e.g. "show that there is no further collision if e < …"); June 2025 FM Q6(a)
shape (a KE ratio condition).
Pitfalls: sign conventions; e applied to speeds in the wrong order; assuming KE is conserved.
Figures: before/after diagrams (hand SVG, two circles with velocity arrows labelled, "before" and "after" rows).

## math.fm.oblique · Oblique impact (5–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 3.6b oblique cases): use conservation of linear momentum and/or Newton's experimental law to solve
problems that may be modelled as the oblique impact of two smooth spheres, or of a smooth sphere with a fixed surface.
Cover: smooth surfaces: the impulse is along the line of centres (or the normal to the wall), so the velocity
component perpendicular to it is unchanged; sphere on a wall: parallel component kept, normal component × e;
angle and speed after impact; KE loss; two spheres: resolve along and perpendicular to the line of centres (a figure
with the line of centres at an angle to the motion), momentum and restitution along the line, perpendicular
components unchanged; deflection through 90° results (equal masses, e = 1); successive impacts with two walls
(June 2025 FM Q6(b) shape, sin(α + β)).
Pitfalls: applying e to the whole velocity; resolving along the wrong line; angle with the wall vs with the normal.
Widget: embed **fm-impact** (wall mode first, then two spheres).

### Widget spec: fm-impact (widgets/fm-impact.js)
- `data-mode="wall|spheres"`, wall: `data-u="5" data-angle="40" data-e="0.6"` (angle between the velocity and the
  wall); spheres: `data-m1 data-m2 data-u1 data-u2 data-angle1 data-angle2 data-e` (angles to the line of centres).
- Canvas: before and after velocity arrows (labelled with speeds to 3 s.f. and angles to 1 d.p.), the line of
  centres or the wall, components along and perpendicular drawn dashed on a "show components" toggle.
- Sliders for e (0–1), speeds and angles; readouts: speeds and directions after impact, impulse, KE before, after,
  and lost; "perpendicular components unchanged" note. Node-test with e = 1 equal masses (90° separation) and e = 0.
