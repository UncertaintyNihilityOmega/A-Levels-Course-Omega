[Discrete random variables: binomial and geometric](#math.s1.drv), [The Poisson distribution](#math.s2.poisson),
[Hypothesis testing](#math.s2.hypothesis), [Comparing two means](#math.fm.twosample).

## math.fm.nonpar · Non-parametric tests: the sign test (4–5 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 4.4a, 4.4b for the sign test, 4.4c and 4.4d sign-test parts):
- 4.4a understand the idea of a non-parametric test and appreciate situations in which such a test might be useful
  (e.g. when sampling from a population which cannot be assumed to be normally distributed);
- 4.4b understand the basis of the sign test;
- 4.4c use a single-sample sign test to test a hypothesis concerning a population median;
- 4.4d use a paired-sample sign test to test for identity of populations.
Cover: parametric vs non-parametric (assumptions, what is lost); median hypotheses; signs of x − m₀, the number of +
signs ~ B(n, ½) under H₀; one- and two-tailed tests with exact binomial probabilities (compare a tail probability
with α, two-tailed doubles); paired-sample sign test on differences; normal approximation for large n with continuity
correction (link 9709); comparing with the t-test (June 2025 FS Q5(b) shape: the sign test reaching the opposite
conclusion to Wilcoxon [3]); experimental design comments (order effects, Q5(c)).
Pitfalls: testing the mean instead of the median; one-tailed probability for a two-tailed test; including the
wrong tail.

## math.fm.wilcoxon · Wilcoxon tests (6–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 4.4b–d Wilcoxon parts):
- 4.4b understand the basis of the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, including knowledge that
  Wilcoxon tests are valid only for symmetrical distributions;
- 4.4c use a single-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test (with normal approximations where appropriate) for a median;
- 4.4d use a Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test and a Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate, to test for
  identity of populations, including normal approximations.
Cover: signed-rank: differences from m₀, rank absolute values, P and Q, T = min(P, Q), compare with the MF19 critical
value (reject if T ≤ table value: MF19 gives "the largest value of T which will lead to rejection"), why symmetry
matters; matched pairs on differences (June 2025 FS Q5(a) shape [6]); normal approximation N(n(n + 1)/4,
n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/24) with continuity correction (MF19 gives mean and variance: say so); rank-sum: rank the combined
samples, Rₘ for the smaller sample (m ≤ n), W = min(Rₘ, m(n + m + 1) − Rₘ), table; normal approximation for Rₘ;
choosing between sign, signed-rank, rank-sum, paired t and two-sample t (a decision table).
Pitfalls: ranking the signed differences instead of the absolute values; the wrong sample's rank sum; "accept" wording.
Figures: a ranking table (Markdown) per test, no SVG needed.

## math.fm.pgf · Probability generating functions (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 4.5):
- 4.5a understand the concept of a probability generating function (PGF) and construct and use the PGF for given
  distributions (including the discrete uniform, binomial, geometric and Poisson distributions);
- 4.5b use formulae for the mean and variance of a discrete random variable in terms of its PGF, and use these
  formulae to calculate the mean and variance of a given probability distribution;
- 4.5c use the result that the PGF of the sum of independent random variables is the product of the PGFs of those
  random variables.
Cover: G(t) = E(tˣ) = Σ P(X = x)tˣ; G(1) = 1; reading probabilities as coefficients (and by differentiating at 0);
PGFs of the four standard distributions derived (geometric series and the exponential series; MF19 gives
G, E(X) = G′(1), Var(X) = G″(1) + G′(1) − {G′(1)}², not the four PGFs: say so); means and variances from G;
unknown constants from G(1) = 1 (June 2025 FS Q6 shape: a PGF in t² with a constant to find [6]); sums of
independent variables (binomial + binomial with the same p, Poisson + Poisson); recognising a distribution from its
PGF; Y = aX + b gives G_Y(t) = t^b G_X(tᵃ).
Pitfalls: G″(1) is not E(X²); forgetting the term for x = 0; multiplying PGFs of dependent variables.
Widget: embed **fm-pgf**.

### Widget spec: fm-pgf (widgets/fm-pgf.js)
- `data-dist="binomial|geometric|poisson|uniform"`, `data-params="n=6,p=0.3"` (or `p=0.25`, `lambda=2`, `n=6`).
- Two panels stacked: the probability bar chart P(X = x) (bars labelled) and the graph of G(t) for 0 ≤ t ≤ 1.2 with
  the tangent at t = 1 drawn and labelled "gradient G′(1) = E(X) = …".
- Controls: distribution tabs, parameter sliders; readouts: G(t) written out (closed form), G(1) = 1, E(X), Var(X)
  from G′ and G″, and the same from the formulas (they agree). A "sum of two" toggle multiplies by a second
  independent copy and shows the resulting distribution. Node-test means and variances against the MF19 formulas.
