## math.fm.matrices · Matrices: algebra, determinants and inverses (6–8 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.4):
- 1.4a carry out operations of matrix addition, subtraction and multiplication, and recognise the terms zero matrix
  and identity (or unit) matrix (including non-square matrices; at most 3 rows and columns);
- 1.4b recall the meaning of "singular" and "non-singular" for square matrices and, for 2×2 and 3×3 matrices,
  evaluate determinants and find inverses of non-singular matrices (notation det M, I);
- 1.4c understand and use (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹ for non-singular matrices (extension to more than two matrices may be
  required).
Before you start: simultaneous equations, [Algebra from scratch](#math.f.algebra); no 9709 matrix content exists, so
this chapter teaches from zero. Warm-up from f.algebra, p1.quadratics, p3.algebra.
Cover: order (rows × columns), equality, + − and scalar multiples, when a product exists, row-by-column
multiplication (a worked layout), AB ≠ BA (a counterexample), associativity, zero and identity matrices, powers
A², A³; 2×2 determinant ad − bc; singular ⇔ det = 0; 2×2 inverse (swap, negate, divide); 3×3 determinant by
expansion along the first row (minors, cofactor sign pattern), then along a row or column with zeros; 3×3 inverse
by the matrix of cofactors, transpose, divide by det (show one full layout with every cofactor; then a check
AA⁻¹ = I); solving for an unknown that makes a matrix singular (a quadratic or cubic in k); (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹ with a
one-line reason in a `:::proof`; matrix equations AX = B ⇒ X = A⁻¹B (order matters); using the inverse to solve a
2×2 or 3×3 system (full geometric treatment is FP2's [Three equations, three planes](#math.fm.systems)).
Pitfalls: multiplying in the wrong order; forgetting the cofactor signs; transposing too early or not at all;
det(kA) = k^n det A (not k det A).
Exam practice: unknown constants in a matrix, "find the values of k for which M is singular", "find M⁻¹ in terms of
k", (AB)⁻¹ given A⁻¹ and B.
Figures: none needed; a small table showing the cofactor sign pattern.
Links: ExamSolutions inverse 3×3 (not in FURTHER.md: use the index link), Khan Academy matrices, 3Blue1Brown
matrix multiplication, MadAsMaths linear algebra booklets.

## math.fm.transform · Matrices as transformations (5–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.4d, 1.4e):
- 1.4d understand the use of 2×2 matrices to represent geometric transformations in the x-y plane: the relationship
  between the transformations represented by A and A⁻¹; AB represents B followed by A; how the area scale factor is
  related to the determinant; find the matrix of a given transformation or sequence of transformations (rotation,
  reflection, enlargement, stretch, shear; other 2D transformations may appear but no knowledge of them is expected);
- 1.4e understand "invariant" for points and lines under a matrix transformation and solve simple problems:
  invariant points of a given matrix, invariant lines through the origin, showing every line of a given gradient is
  invariant.
Cover: the images of (1, 0) and (0, 1) are the columns (the key idea; a figure of the unit square and its image);
standard matrices: rotation about O through θ anticlockwise, reflection in y = x tan θ (cos 2θ, sin 2θ form), in the
axes and y = ±x, enlargement centre O, stretches parallel to the axes, shears (x-axis fixed: (1 k; 0 1)); naming a
transformation from its matrix (June 2025 Q4(a): "state the type of each transformation and make clear the order");
composition (AB = B first), inverse transformation; |det| = area scale factor and negative det = orientation
reversed; invariant points: Mx = x (a line of invariant points or only the origin); invariant lines through O:
y = mx mapped to itself, giving a quadratic in m; invariant lines not through O (y = mx + c, compare); the
difference between a line of invariant points and an invariant line.
Pitfalls: order of composition; "invariant line" ≠ "line of invariant points"; the angle convention (anticlockwise
positive).
Widget: embed **fm-matrix** in the "columns are images" section and again for invariant lines, with a sentence of
what to try.
Figures: one hand SVG (`fig small`): unit square OABC and its image under a shear, labelled, with the area factor.
Board box: UK Further Maths also covers 3D transformation matrices (sure for AQA and Edexcel Core Pure); 9231 is 2D
only.

### Widget spec: fm-matrix (widgets/fm-matrix.js)
- `data-m="a,b,c,d"` (the matrix (a b; c d), default "1,1,0,1"), `data-shape="square|triangle|L"` (default square),
  `data-show="grid,det,invariant"` (comma list of extras; default all), `data-presets="rotation,reflection,shear,
  stretch,enlargement"` (buttons that load an example).
- Canvas (W.axes, range following the image, at least −4…4): the shape (outline) and its image (filled, labelled
  "image"), the images of i and j as arrows labelled "Mi" and "Mj", optional faint transformed grid.
- Controls: four number boxes a b c d (W.sliderNum or number inputs with labels "a", "b", "c", "d"), preset
  buttons, a "Show A⁻¹" button that animates back (respect reduced motion: jump instead).
- Readouts (W.readout): det M; "area scale factor |det| = …"; "orientation reversed" when det < 0; "singular: the
  plane collapses onto a line" when det = 0; invariant lines through the origin: y = mx for each real
  root of bm² + (a − d)m − c = 0, and x = 0 when b = 0 (the y-axis maps to itself), drawn dashed and listed as text; "line of invariant points" when 1 is an eigenvalue with a full line.
- Bad attributes: show a `.w-err` message and fall back to the identity. Test the invariant-line logic in Node with
  shears, rotations (none), reflections (two lines, one of invariant points), stretches.
- P26 embeds it again for eigenvectors (`data-show="eigen"` adds eigenvalue readouts: real eigenvalues and their
  directions; complex ones say "no real eigenvectors"). Build that option now.

## math.fm.induction · Proof by induction (5–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.7):
- 1.7a use the method of mathematical induction to establish a given result (e.g. Σr³ = ¼n²(n + 1)², the nth term
  of the sequence u₁ = 1, uₙ₊₁ = 3uₙ − 1 is ½(3ⁿ⁻¹ + 1), a formula for the nth power of a 2×2 matrix, an expression
  in powers of n that is divisible by 8);
- 1.7b recognise situations where conjecture based on a limited trial followed by inductive proof is a useful
  strategy, and carry this out in simple cases (e.g. the nth derivative of x eˣ, Σ r·r!).
Before you start: [Summation of series](#math.fm.series), [Matrices: algebra, determinants and inverses](#math.fm.matrices),
[Proof: how mathematicians know](#math.proof), differentiation of products from [Further differentiation](#math.p3.diff).
Cover: the domino idea (`:::analogy`); the four parts and the exact wording the mark scheme wants (FURTHER.md:
B1 base, B1 hypothesis, M1 A1 step, A1 conclusion); every proof in the chapter is an `:::eg (Exam style)` with
marks, never `:::proof`. Types, one section each with 2–3 worked examples: sums (add the (k + 1)th term, factor out
common factors, aim for the target form written down first); divisibility (write f(k + 1) − f(k) or f(k + 1) − m·f(k)
as a multiple of the divisor); recurrence relations (June 2025 Q3: uₙ₊₁ = 6uₙ + 5, prove uₙ = 6ⁿ − 1 [5]);
matrix powers (multiply Mᵏ⁺¹ = MᵏM, both orders work); derivatives (nth derivative of x eˣ or x e^(2x); d/dx of the
hypothesis); inequalities only if simple (2ⁿ > n, optional, as stretch). Conjecture then prove: compute n = 1, 2, 3,
guess, prove. A spot-the-error where the student "assumes what is to be proved" or skips the base case.
Pitfalls: writing "assume true for all n"; not using the hypothesis visibly; conclusion missing "true for n = 1".
Figures: none needed (maybe a `proof` widget scene if the Writer wants one: add `S.induction` in widgets/proof.js
only if it adds something; optional).
Board box: UK Further Maths also proves by induction (sums, divisibility, matrices; sure).
