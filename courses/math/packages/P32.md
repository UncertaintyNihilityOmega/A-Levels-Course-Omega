## math.fm.chisq · Chi-squared goodness of fit (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 4.3a, 4.3b):
- 4.3a fit a theoretical distribution, as prescribed by a given hypothesis, to given data (questions will not involve
  lengthy calculations);
- 4.3b use a χ²-test, with the appropriate number of degrees of freedom, to carry out the corresponding goodness of
  fit analysis (classes should be combined so that each expected frequency is at least 5).
Cover: observed and expected frequencies; X² = Σ(O − E)²/E (not on MF19: learn it) and the alternative ΣO²/E − N;
degrees of freedom = classes after combining − 1 − number of parameters estimated from the data; fitting uniform,
binomial (p given or estimated, June 2025 FS Q3 shape: p estimated from data, an expected value to show, combine
classes, a model criticism [8]), Poisson (λ estimated by x̄), geometric, given ratios, and a continuous distribution
(normal or a given PDF, with class boundaries); the test is one-tailed (upper tail); conclusion in context; reasons a
model may be unsuitable (independence, constant p).
Pitfalls: combining classes after computing (O − E)²/E; ν not reduced for estimated parameters; expected
frequencies rounded too early.
Widget: embed **fm-chisq**.

## math.fm.contingency · Chi-squared tests for independence (4–5 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 4.3c): use a χ²-test, with the appropriate number of degrees of freedom, for independence in a
contingency table (Yates' correction is not required; where appropriate, rows or columns are combined so that the
expected frequency in each cell is at least 5).
Cover: H₀ "no association between … and …"; expected = row total × column total ÷ grand total (why, from
independence); ν = (r − 1)(c − 1) after combining; combining rows or columns sensibly (adjacent, meaningful
categories); the 2×2 case; which cells contribute most (interpret the largest (O − E)²/E terms in context); a full
exam-style example with a 3×4 table.
Pitfalls: ν from the original table size after combining; "dependent" wording; percentages instead of frequencies.
