## math.fm.rational · Rational functions and their graphs (6–8 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 1.2a): sketch graphs of simple rational functions, including the determination of oblique
asymptotes, in cases where the degree of the numerator and the denominator are at most 2; including determination of
the set of values taken by the function, e.g. by the use of a discriminant. Detailed plotting is not required, but
sketches are expected to show significant features: turning points, asymptotes and intersections with the axes.
Before you start: [Quadratics](#math.p1.quadratics) (discriminant), [Functions](#math.p1.functions) (range),
[Differentiation](#math.p1.diff) and [Further differentiation](#math.p3.diff) (quotient rule), polynomial division
from [Modulus and polynomials](#math.p3.algebra).
Cover, case by case: (linear)/(linear): vertical and horizontal asymptotes; (linear)/(quadratic) and
(quadratic)/(quadratic): horizontal asymptote from leading coefficients or y = 0; (quadratic)/(linear): oblique
asymptote by division (y = mx + c, the remainder term → 0); the behaviour either side of each vertical asymptote
(sign test just left and right); intersections with axes; turning points by the quotient rule; the set of values
taken (range) by writing y = f(x), rearranging into a quadratic in x and requiring discriminant ≥ 0, which also gives
the turning-point y-values without calculus (show both methods agree); whether the curve crosses its horizontal
asymptote (solve f(x) = k); sketching checklist (`:::steps`). Exam pattern (June 2025 Q7, 15 marks): asymptotes [2],
stationary points [4], sketch with intersections [3], a related sketch [1], an inequality in exact form [5] (the last
part links to math.fm.graphs: write the inequality step with critical values from the sketch).
Pitfalls: missing the oblique asymptote; wrong sign of the curve near a vertical asymptote; discriminant inequality
direction; claiming the curve never crosses a horizontal asymptote.
Figures: `:::plot` for each case (use the domain trick in kit/STYLE.md to stop the curve running through labels near
asymptotes; draw asymptotes with `--vline`/`--hline` and an extra `--f` for an oblique line, labelled). Optionally
embed the existing `graph` widget with a parameter (`data-params`) to vary one coefficient and watch the asymptote
and turning points move (grep `data-params` in chapters for the attribute format).
Board box: in UK Further Maths, rational-function sketching is in AQA's core content but in an optional unit for
other boards: if unsure, describe 9231 only.

## math.fm.graphs · Graphs related to y = f(x) (4–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 1.2b): understand and use relationships between the graphs of y = f(x), y² = f(x), y = 1/f(x),
y = |f(x)| and y = f(|x|), including use of such sketch graphs in the course of solving equations or inequalities.
Before you start: [Rational functions and their graphs](#math.fm.rational), modulus graphs from
[Modulus and polynomials](#math.p3.algebra), transformations from [Functions](#math.p1.functions).
Cover: y = |f(x)| (reflect the negative parts), y = f(|x|) (reflect the right half), y = 1/f(x) (zeros become
vertical asymptotes, asymptotes become zeros, maxima become minima, sign kept), y² = f(x) (only where f(x) ≥ 0,
symmetric in the x-axis, vertical tangents where f(x) = 0 crosses, √ of values, the shape of the curve near a
simple root); each applied to a rational function and to a polynomial; solving |f(x)| < k, 1/f(x) > k with sketches;
inequalities with rational functions solved exactly (multiply by the square of the denominator or use critical
values from the sketch) — the June 2025 Q7(e) style "find in exact form the set of values of x for which …".
Pitfalls: y² = f(x) drawn below the axis where f < 0; losing solutions by multiplying an inequality by a quantity of
unknown sign.
Figures: `:::plot` pairs (f and the related graph, two series max per plot).

## math.fm.polar · Polar coordinates (6–8 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.5):
- 1.5a understand the relations between Cartesian and polar coordinates, and convert equations of curves from
  Cartesian to polar form and vice versa (the convention r ≥ 0 will be used);
- 1.5b sketch simple polar curves for 0 ≤ θ < 2π or −π < θ ≤ π or a subset of either; sketches show significant
  features: symmetry, coordinates of intersections with the initial line, the form of the curve at the pole and the
  least/greatest values of r;
- 1.5c recall the formula ½∫r² dθ for the area of a sector, and use this formula in simple cases.
Before you start: radians, arcs and sectors from [Circular measure](#math.p1.circular), trig identities from
[Further trigonometry](#math.p3.trig), integration from [Integration techniques: parts, substitution and partial fractions](#math.p3.inttech).
Cover: pole, initial line, (r, θ); x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, r² = x² + y², tan θ = y/x (quadrant care); converting
equations both ways (circles r = 2a cos θ, lines r = a sec θ, cardioids); r ≥ 0 means some θ give no points (e.g.
r = cos 2θ only where cos 2θ ≥ 0); sketching: table of values, symmetry (θ → −θ), max/min r by calculus on r(θ),
tangents at the pole (r = 0 gives the directions), standard shapes: circle, cardioid a(1 + cos θ), limaçon, rose
r = a sin nθ, spiral r = aθ or ae^(kθ) (June 2025 Q5: a spiral, sketch [2], area in exact form [6]); greatest
distance from the initial line (maximise y = r sin θ); area: ½∫r² dθ with the `:::proof` from thin sectors (not
examined, so folded); area of a loop, area between a curve and a line or two curves (find intersections first), use
of symmetry; integrals of sin², cos² via double angles; spiral areas with integration by parts.
Pitfalls: limits in degrees; integrating r instead of r²; counting a region twice when using symmetry; forgetting
the ½.
Widget: embed **fm-polar** in the sketching section and again in the area section.
Figures: polar curves cannot be drawn with `:::plot` (it is y = f(x) only): use the widget, plus at most one hand
SVG (circle grid and initial line) where a static figure is needed; compute points with Python in data/.

### Widget spec: fm-polar (widgets/fm-polar.js)
- `data-r="1+cos(t)"` (r as a function of t = θ, same expression syntax as the `graph` widget: reuse its parser if
  it is exposed on W, otherwise a small safe parser; no eval), `data-range="0,2*pi"`, `data-area="a,b"` (optional
  sector to shade), `data-presets="cardioid,rose,spiral,circle"`.
- Canvas: polar grid (circles and rays every π/6, labelled 0, π/2, π, 3π/2), the initial line labelled, the curve
  traced from θ = a to the slider value of θ, the current point P and the ray OP, and points where r < 0 are skipped
  (say "r < 0: no point (r ≥ 0 convention)" in the readout).
- Controls: θ slider (W.sliderNum, shows θ in terms of π to 2 d.p.), Play/Pause (W.animate, reduced motion respected),
  preset buttons, an "area" toggle that shades the region from a to θ.
- Readouts: θ, r, (x, y), and when shading: "area = ½∫ r² dθ ≈ …" (Simpson with 400 strips).
- Bad attributes: `.w-err` and a cardioid default. Node-test the parser and the area (cardioid a = 1 gives 3π/2).
