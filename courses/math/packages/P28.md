Chapters: `math.fm.projectiles`, `math.fm.com`, `math.fm.rigid`. Paper: 9231 Paper 3 (FM, 1 h 30 min, 50 marks).
New widget: **fm-topple**. Read COURSE.md, FURTHER.md, kit/STYLE.md and the gold chapter math.fm.roots first.
FM assumes 9709 Mechanics: link [Forces and equilibrium](#math.m.forces), [Kinematics: describing motion](#math.m.kinematics),

"Take \(g = 10\)" in every question. Diagrams: hand SVG force diagrams, `fig small`, every force labelled.

## math.fm.projectiles · Projectiles (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.1):
- 3.1a model the motion of a projectile as a particle moving with constant acceleration and understand any
  limitations of the model;
- 3.1b use horizontal and vertical equations of motion to solve problems on the motion of projectiles, including
  finding the magnitude and direction of the velocity at a given time or position, the range on a horizontal plane
  and the greatest height reached;
- 3.1c derive and use the Cartesian equation of the trajectory of a projectile, including problems in which the
  initial speed and/or angle of projection may be unknown. Not: vector methods; the bounding parabola.
Cover: components of the initial velocity; horizontal motion at constant speed, vertical with suvat; time of flight,
range 2V² sin θ cos θ/g = V² sin 2θ/g (derived, not on MF19), maximum range at 45°, greatest height; velocity at a
point (magnitude and direction, above/below the horizontal); projection from a height, from a cliff, landing on a
lower or higher level; the trajectory y = x tan θ − gx²/(2V² cos²θ) (on MF19: say so; derive it as an `:::eg` with
marks) and the 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ form giving a quadratic in tan θ (two angles to pass through a point, June 2025
FM Q2 shape: clearing a wall); modelling assumptions (particle, no air resistance, flat Earth, constant g) and their
effect.
Widget: embed the existing `projectile` widget (grep its attributes in chapters/).
Pitfalls: using sin for the horizontal component; one angle only when two exist; g sign.

## math.fm.com · Centres of mass (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.2a–d):
- 3.2a calculate the moment of a force about a point (coplanar forces only);
- 3.2b use the result that the effect of gravity on a rigid body is equivalent to a single force acting at the centre
  of mass of the body, and identify the position of the centre of mass of a uniform body using considerations of
  symmetry;
- 3.2c use given information about the position of the centre of mass of a triangular lamina and other simple shapes
  (proofs of the MF19 results are not required);
- 3.2d determine the position of the centre of mass of a composite body by considering an equivalent system of
  particles (simple cases only, e.g. a uniform L-shaped lamina, or a uniform cone joined at its base to a uniform
  hemisphere of the same radius).
Cover: moments recap (link 9709 if taught there, else teach: moment = force × perpendicular distance, sense); centre
of mass of particles in a line and in a plane (Σmx/Σm); uniform laminas by symmetry; MF19 table (FACTS.md: triangle,
hemisphere solid and shell, arc, sector, cone) in a `:::key` marked "given on MF19"; composite laminas with a table
(part, mass or area, x̄, ȳ); removed parts (negative mass); solids of revolution made of cones, hemispheres, cylinders
(June 2025 FM Q4 shape: cone with a hemisphere removed); wire frameworks; a lamina suspended from a point (hangs with
the centre of mass vertically below; angle with the vertical by tan).
Pitfalls: using lengths instead of masses for mixed solids; measuring from different origins; the cone's ¾h from the
vertex (¼h from the base).
Figures: hand SVG of an L-shaped lamina with axes and the centre of mass marked; one of a suspended lamina.

## math.fm.rigid · Equilibrium of a rigid body (6–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.2e, 3.2f):
- 3.2e use the principle that if a rigid body is in equilibrium under the action of coplanar forces then the vector
  sum of the forces is zero and the sum of the moments of the forces about any point is zero, and the converse of
  this;
- 3.2f solve problems involving the equilibrium of a single rigid body under the action of coplanar forces,
  including those involving toppling or sliding.
Cover: resolving twice and taking moments once (choosing the point to kill unknowns); rods on rough ground against
smooth or rough walls (ladders), rods hinged at a wall with a string, rods resting on pegs; friction at limiting
equilibrium (F = μR, link 9709); a lamina or solid on an inclined plane: toppling when the line of action of the
weight passes outside the base (tan α = r/ḡ condition), sliding when tan α > μ, which happens first (June 2025
FM Q4(b) shape); a horizontal force pushing a block: topples or slides; three-force equilibrium (lines of action
meet at a point) as a tool.
Widget: embed **fm-topple**.
Pitfalls: moments of a force not perpendicular to the rod (use components or perpendicular distance); friction
direction; claiming toppling and sliding together without checking.

### Widget spec: fm-topple (widgets/fm-topple.js)
- `data-shape="cuboid|cylinder|cone"` with `data-w`, `data-h` (base width or radius and height; cone uses ¼h),
  `data-mu="0.6"`.
- Canvas: the solid standing on a plane whose angle α the reader raises with a slider (0–60°); the centre of mass G
  marked, the vertical line through G dashed, the lower edge of the base marked as "pivot".
- Readouts: α, tan α, "G is above the base" / "G is outside the base: topples", "friction holds" / "slides
  (tan α > μ)", and which happens first as α increases, with the critical angles tan⁻¹(width/height of G) and
  tan⁻¹ μ to 1 d.p.
- Sliders for α, μ, and dimensions; static drawing (no animation needed; if the solid tips, draw it tipped with a
  word label, no colour-only signal). Node-test the critical angles.
