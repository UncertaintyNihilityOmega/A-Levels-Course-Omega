## math.fm.systems · Three equations, three planes (4–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 2.2a, 2.2b):
- 2.2a formulate a problem involving the solution of 3 linear simultaneous equations in 3 unknowns as a problem
  involving the solution of a matrix equation, or vice versa;
- 2.2b understand the cases that may arise concerning the consistency or inconsistency of 3 linear simultaneous
  equations, relate them to the singularity or otherwise of the corresponding matrix, solve consistent systems, and
  interpret geometrically in terms of lines and planes (e.g. three planes meeting in a common point, or in a common
  line, or having no common points).
Cover: Ax = b; det ≠ 0: unique solution x = A⁻¹b (one point); det = 0: eliminate to find either a consistent system
(a sheaf: common line, parametric solution x = …t) or inconsistent (triangular prism, or two/three parallel planes);
how to tell which (elimination giving 0 = 0 or 0 = k; normals parallel or not); a parameter k: values making the
matrix singular, then for each value whether the system is consistent; a figure or the widget for each geometric
case (five pictures in a table with their algebraic signatures).
Pitfalls: concluding "no solution" from det = 0 alone; describing a sheaf as a prism.

## math.fm.matrices2 · Eigenvalues and eigenvectors (6–8 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 2.2c–f):
- 2.2c understand the terms "characteristic equation", "eigenvalue" and "eigenvector" as applied to square matrices;
  including use of the definition Ae = λe to prove simple properties, e.g. that λⁿ is an eigenvalue of Aⁿ;
- 2.2d find eigenvalues and eigenvectors of 2×2 and 3×3 matrices (restricted to real and distinct eigenvalues);
- 2.2e express a square matrix in the form QDQ⁻¹, where D is a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues and Q is a matrix whose
  columns are eigenvectors, and use this expression (e.g. in calculating powers of 2×2 or 3×3 matrices);
- 2.2f use the fact that a square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation (e.g. in finding successive powers
  of a matrix or finding an inverse matrix; 2×2 or 3×3 only).
Cover: the idea (directions a transformation only stretches; link to invariant lines through O in
[Matrices as transformations](#math.fm.transform)); det(A − λI) = 0; 3×3 characteristic cubic (factor theorem to
solve; the coefficient check: sum of eigenvalues = trace, product = det, link to [Roots of polynomial equations](#math.fm.roots));
eigenvectors by solving (A − λI)e = 0 or by the vector product of two rows; any non-zero multiple; proofs from
Ae = λe as `:::eg (Exam style)` (eigenvalues of Aⁿ, A + kI, A⁻¹; June 2025 FP2 Q8(a) shape [2]); diagonalisation
A = QDQ⁻¹ (order of columns matches D), Aⁿ = QDⁿQ⁻¹ with a worked 2×2 and a 3×3; using a variant like
Aⁿ written via P and D (the real paper asked for a P and D with a given product form: read the question's form);
Cayley–Hamilton: A satisfies its characteristic equation; find A³, A⁻¹ from it.
Pitfalls: Q and D in different orders; Q⁻¹ computed wrongly (check QQ⁻¹ = I); sign of the λ³ term in a 3×3
characteristic equation.
Widget: fm-matrix with `data-show="eigen"` for a 2×2 example, with what to try.
