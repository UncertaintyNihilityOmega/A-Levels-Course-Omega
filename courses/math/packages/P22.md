# P22 · Further Pure 1: vectors and planes

Chapters: `math.fm.vectors`, `math.fm.vectors2`. Paper: 9231 Paper 1 (FP1). New widget: **fm-planes**.
Read COURSE.md, FURTHER.md, kit/STYLE.md and the gold chapter math.fm.roots first.
Both chapters build on [Vectors](#math.p3.vectors) (lines r = a + tb, scalar product, angle between lines, foot of
the perpendicular from a point to a line, skew lines): recall, never re-teach. 9709 excludes the vector product and
the shortest distance between skew lines (FACTS.md 3.7 "Not:"); they are new here.

## math.fm.vectors · Vectors: the vector product and planes (6–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.6a, 1.6b):
- 1.6a use the equation of a plane in any of the forms ax + by + cz = d or r·n = p or r = a + λb + μc, and convert
  equations of planes from one form to another as necessary in solving problems;
- 1.6b recall that the vector product a × b of two vectors can be expressed either as |a||b| sin θ n̂, where n̂ is a
  unit vector, or in component form as (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂)i + (a₃b₁ − a₁b₃)j + (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)k.
Cover: the vector product: definition, right-hand rule (a figure), component form with a memory layout (the
"cover-up" or determinant layout), properties (a × b = −b × a, a × a = 0, perpendicular to both), area of a
triangle ½|AB × AC| and parallelogram; a × b = 0 ⇔ parallel. Planes: a normal vector, r·n = p (p = a·n), the
Cartesian form from n = (a, b, c); parametric form r = a + λb + μc and its normal b × c; plane through three points
(June 2025 Q6(a) [5]); converting between all three forms (Cartesian → parametric by choosing two direction vectors in
the plane); checking a point lies in a plane; |p|/|n| as the distance of the plane from O when n is not a unit
vector (and when it is); parallel planes.
Pitfalls: sign of the j component; using a point instead of a direction in r = a + λb + μc; forgetting to check the
normal against both direction vectors.
Figures: one hand SVG of a plane (parallelogram in perspective) with its normal n and a point A; one of a × b with
the right-hand rule. Embed the existing `vectors3d` widget only if it helps (it shows lines, not planes).
Widget: embed **fm-planes** for a plane and its normal.

## math.fm.vectors2 · Vectors: intersections, angles and distances (6–8 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 1.6c): use equations of lines and planes, together with scalar and vector products where
appropriate, to solve problems concerning distances, angles and intersections, including: determining whether a
line lies in a plane, is parallel to a plane or intersects a plane, and finding the point of intersection of a line
and a plane when it exists; finding the foot of the perpendicular from a point to a plane; finding the angle between
a line and a plane, and the angle between two planes; finding an equation for the line of intersection of two
planes; calculating the shortest distance between two skew lines; finding an equation for the common perpendicular
to two skew lines.
Cover, one section each, with a `:::steps` method box and exam-style examples: line and plane (substitute the line
into the plane: one solution, none = parallel, all = in the plane); foot of the perpendicular from P to a plane, the
distance |ax₁ + by₁ + cz₁ − d|/√(a² + b² + c²) (derive it in an `:::eg`, the formula is not on MF19), reflection of a
point in a plane; angle between a line and a plane (sin φ = |b·n|/(|b||n|), the complement of the angle with the
normal; a figure), angle between two planes (normals; acute angle, June 2025 Q6(b) [4]); line of intersection of two
planes (direction n₁ × n₂, a point by setting one coordinate to 0, or solve); shortest distance between skew lines
|(a₂ − a₁)·(b₁ × b₂)|/|b₁ × b₂| and the common perpendicular (general points P(λ), Q(μ), PQ perpendicular to both
directions, two equations); distance from a point to a line (with the vector product, as an alternative to the 9709
method); an unknown parameter that makes the distance a given value (June 2025 Q6(c) [7]).
Pitfalls: using cos instead of sin for line–plane angles; obtuse angles left unconverted; not checking skew lines
are not parallel.
Figures: hand SVG of the angle between a line and a plane (line, its projection, the normal), of two skew lines with
their common perpendicular.

### Widget spec: fm-planes (widgets/fm-planes.js)
- `data-planes="a,b,c,d; a,b,c,d; …"` (one to three planes ax + by + cz = d), `data-line="a1,a2,a3|d1,d2,d3"`
  (optional line), `data-point="x,y,z"` (optional).
- 3D view on W.view3d + W.orbit (drag to rotate, plus buttons/sliders to rotate for keyboard users; start at an
  asymmetric angle with depth cues, as kit/WIDGETS.md requires). Each plane drawn as a translucent patch clipped to a
  cube −5…5, labelled Π₁, Π₂, Π₃, with its normal arrow.
- Readouts: for one plane: normal, distance from O; with a point: foot of the perpendicular and distance; with a
  line: "meets the plane at …", "parallel", or "lies in the plane", and the angle between line and plane; for two
  planes: angle between them and the line of intersection r = a + t(n₁ × n₂) (or "parallel"); for three planes
  (used by P26): "meet at a single point (…)", "meet in a common line", "form a triangular prism (no common point)",
  "two (or three) are parallel", or "coincide", decided by det and ranks.
- Bad attributes: `.w-err`, default one plane x + 2y + 2z = 6. Node-test the classification on all five
  three-plane cases.
