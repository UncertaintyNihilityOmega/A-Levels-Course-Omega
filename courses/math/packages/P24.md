## math.fm.reduction · Reduction formulae (5–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 2.4b): derive and use reduction formulae for the evaluation of definite integrals, e.g.
∫₀^{π/2} sinⁿx dx, ∫₀¹ xⁿ e^(−x) dx; in harder cases hints may be given, e.g. ∫₀^{π/4} secⁿx dx by considering
d/dx(tan x secⁿ⁻²x).
Before you start: integration by parts from [Integration techniques: parts, substitution and partial fractions](#math.p3.inttech), [Proof by induction](#math.fm.induction)
(the idea of a recurrence), [Further integration: hyperbolic and inverse forms](#math.fm.integ).
Cover: what Iₙ means; deriving a formula by parts (choose u and dv so the power drops), boundary terms that vanish;
using a given derivative hint (differentiate, integrate both sides between limits); applying it repeatedly down to I₀
or I₁ (compute them); xⁿeˣ, xⁿ e^(−x), sinⁿ, cosⁿ, tanⁿ (via tan² = sec² − 1, Iₙ + Iₙ₋₂), secⁿ, (ln x)ⁿ, xⁿ√(1 − x),
sinhⁿ and coshⁿ types; exact answers; June 2025 FP2 Q2 shape: show a formula [4], then an exact value [3]. Every
derivation is an `:::eg (Exam style)` with marks (examined).
Pitfalls: sign errors in by parts; evaluating boundary terms at 0 incorrectly; stepping the index by 1 when the
formula steps by 2.

## math.fm.rectangles · Sums, rectangles and bounds (4–5 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 2.4c): understand how the area under a curve may be approximated by areas of rectangles, and use
rectangles to estimate or set bounds for the area under a curve or to derive inequalities or limits concerning sums
(rectangles of unit width or width tending to zero, e.g. ln(n + 1) < Σ_{r=1}^{n} 1/r < 1 + ln n; the limit of a sum
like (1/n)Σ f(r/n) as an integral).
Before you start: [Integration](#math.p1.integ), [Summation of series](#math.fm.series), the trapezium rule idea from
[Further integration](#math.p3.integ).
Cover: upper and lower rectangles for an increasing or decreasing function (a figure for each: a hand SVG with the
curve, rectangles of unit width from x = 1 to n, labelled heights f(1), f(2), …); sum of areas vs the integral gives
inequalities; which way the inequality goes; shifting the index to get both bounds; harmonic numbers and ln n;
bounds for Σ 1/r², Σ √r, Σ e^(−r) type sums; limits of (1/n)Σ f(r/n) as width → 0; estimating a sum to a given
accuracy. June 2025 FP2 Q4 shape: a diagram of the curve with n − 1 unit rectangles, "by considering the sum of the
areas … show that …" [5], then "use a similar method to find a lower bound" [4].
Widget: embed `area` (riemann mode) once, with a sentence: compare left and right rectangles with the exact area.
Pitfalls: rectangles of the wrong side for a decreasing function; the first or last term left out of the sum.

## math.fm.arclength · Arc length and surface area (5–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 2.4d): use integration to find arc lengths for curves with equations in Cartesian coordinates,
including the use of a parameter, or in polar coordinates; surface areas of revolution about one of the axes for
curves with equations in Cartesian coordinates, including the use of a parameter. Not: surface areas of revolution
for polar curves.
Before you start: [Polar coordinates](#math.fm.polar), parametric differentiation from [Further differentiation](#math.p3.diff),
volumes of revolution from [Integration](#math.p1.integ), [Further integration: hyperbolic and inverse forms](#math.fm.integ).
Cover: s = ∫√(1 + (dy/dx)²) dx (a `:::proof` from small chords, folded), the dy-version, parametric
∫√(ẋ² + ẏ²) dt, polar ∫√(r² + (dr/dθ)²) dθ; curves chosen so the root simplifies (cosh curves, x^{3/2}, cycloid,
cardioid, astroid); surface area 2π∫y ds about the x-axis and 2π∫x ds about the y-axis, Cartesian and parametric;
exact answers; numeric answers to 3 s.f.; none of these formulas is on MF19 (learn them).
Pitfalls: forgetting to square both derivatives; using y instead of x for rotation about the y-axis; polar surface
area (not examined).
Figures: one `:::plot` of a catenary with the arc marked; one hand SVG of a small chord δs with δx, δy.
