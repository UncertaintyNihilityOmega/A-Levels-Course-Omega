## math.fm.series · Summation of series (5–7 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 1.3):
- 1.3a use the standard results for Σr, Σr², Σr³ to find related sums;
- 1.3b use the method of differences to obtain the sum of a finite series (partial fractions may be needed to put a
  general term in a suitable form);
- 1.3c recognise, by direct consideration of a sum to n terms, when a series is convergent, and find the sum to
  infinity in such cases.

Before you start: sigma notation and AP/GP sums from [Series: binomial expansion and progressions](#math.p1.series); partial fractions from
[Partial fractions and the binomial series](#math.p3.partial); factorising cubics from [Modulus and polynomials](#math.p3.algebra). Warm-up from those.

What to cover, in order:
1. Sigma notation refresher, linearity (Σ(au_r + bv_r) = aΣu_r + bΣv_r), Σ1 = n (the classic slip: Σ3 = 3, not 3n),
   sums that start at r = m: Σ_{r=m}^{n} = Σ_{1}^{n} − Σ_{1}^{m−1}; sums to 2n; sums of odd/even terms.
2. The three MF19 results in a `:::key` (they are given; say so). Why Σr works (Gauss pairing) as a `:::proof`; Σr²
   and Σr³ are proved by induction in [Proof by induction](#math.fm.induction) (link forward; the proof here is the
   telescoping identity (r + 1)³ − r³ = 3r² + 3r + 1 in a folded `:::proof`).
3. Related sums: Σr(r + 1), Σ(2r − 1)², Σr(r + 1)(r + 2); factorising the result fully ("show that … =
   ¼n(n + 1)(n + 2)(n + 3)"): take out common factors before expanding. Exam pattern (June 2025 Q1(a)): "use standard
   results from MF19 to show that Σ … = n(an² + bn + c)" [3].
4. Method of differences: f(r) − f(r + 1) telescoping, writing out the first three and last two lines, cancellation
   diagram (a table, not a figure), two-term gaps (f(r) − f(r + 2)) leave four terms; general terms 1/(r(r + 1)),
   1/((2r − 1)(2r + 1)), 1/(r(r + 1)(r + 2)) via partial fractions; also non-fraction forms like r·r! = (r + 1)! − r!
   and differences of square roots.
5. Convergence: from S_n, let n → ∞; terms like 1/(n + 1) → 0; S∞ when the limit is finite; a series whose S_n grows
   without bound diverges (Σr). Questions: "deduce the sum to infinity", "explain why the series converges".
6. Exam-style long question in the June 2025 Q1 shape: (a) standard results [3], (b) differences [4], (c) deduce
   S∞ [1].

Pitfalls (`:::warn`): Σ_{r=1}^{n} c = nc; dropping terms at the far end in differences (write the last two lines);
sums from r = n + 1 to 2n; expanding instead of factoring.
Figures: one `:::plot` of S_n against n (as points via a step-like `--f` is not possible: use a `--f` of the closed
form, e.g. S_n = 1 − 1/(n + 1), with an `--hline 1` asymptote and the caption saying the sum approaches 1).
Links (FURTHER.md): Method of differences (ExamSolutions), MadAsMaths further topics booklets, PMT FP1 papers.
Board box: UK Further Maths and Edexcel IAL FP1 also have Σr, Σr², Σr³ and the method of differences (sure).
Proofs: the Σr formula by pairing (folded `:::proof`); a full induction proof belongs to math.fm.induction.
