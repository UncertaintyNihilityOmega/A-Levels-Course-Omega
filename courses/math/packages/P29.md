## math.fm.elastic · Hooke's law and elastic energy (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.4):
- 3.4a use Hooke's law as a model relating the force in an elastic string or spring to the extension or
  compression, and understand the term modulus of elasticity;
- 3.4b use the formula for the elastic potential energy stored in a string or spring (proof not required);
- 3.4c solve problems involving forces due to elastic strings or springs, including those where considerations of
  work and energy are needed (e.g. a particle moving horizontally or vertically or on an inclined plane while
  attached to one or more strings or springs, or a particle attached to an elastic string acting as a "conical
  pendulum").
Cover: natural length l, extension x, modulus λ (in newtons), T = λx/l (MF19, say so); strings slacken (no
compression), springs compress; equilibrium positions (one string, two strings either side, vertical hanging);
EPE = λx²/(2l) (MF19), where it comes from (area under T against x, a `:::proof` folded); energy equations with KE,
GPE, EPE and work against friction; maximum speed where the acceleration is zero (June 2025 FM Q5 shape [6]); greatest
extension where v = 0; particle on an inclined plane; the conical pendulum with an elastic string is in
math.fm.circular (forward link).
Pitfalls: using the stretched length instead of the extension; forgetting that a string goes slack; EPE when x < 0
for a string.
Figures: hand SVG of a particle on a string on a slope with all forces.

## math.fm.circular · Circular motion: horizontal circles (5–6 hours)
Outcomes (FACTS.md 3.3a–c):
- 3.3a understand the concept of angular speed for a particle moving in a circle, and use the relation v = rω;
- 3.3b understand that the acceleration of a particle moving in a circle with constant speed is directed towards the
  centre of the circle, and use the formulae rω² and v²/r (proof not required);
- 3.3c solve problems which can be modelled by the motion of a particle moving in a horizontal circle with constant
  speed.
Cover: radians per second, rpm conversions (June 2025 FM Q3(b) "revolutions per minute"); acceleration towards the
centre (MF19 gives it; a folded `:::proof` from velocity vectors is optional); "resultant force towards the centre =
mrω²"; particle on a rotating disc with friction (limiting when about to slip outwards); conical pendulum (inextensible
and elastic string, tension components); a particle inside a smooth cone or bowl; banked tracks (cars, cyclists, with
and without friction, speed range); two strings to a vertical rod; resolving vertically (no acceleration) and
horizontally (towards the centre).
Pitfalls: radius ≠ string length; including a "centrifugal force"; friction direction on a banked track.
Figures: hand SVG of a conical pendulum and of a car on a banked track with forces.

## math.fm.vertical · Circular motion: vertical circles (5–6 hours)
Outcome (FACTS.md 3.3d): solve problems which can be modelled by the motion of a particle in a vertical circle
without loss of energy, including finding a normal contact force or the tension in a string, locating points at
which these are zero, and conditions for complete circular motion.
Cover: speed varies (energy conservation between two points, height as r − r cos θ or r + r cos θ: one figure
fixing the angle convention); radial equation T − mg cos θ = mv²/r (and the version above the centre); particle on a
string (complete circles need T ≥ 0 at the top: v² ≥ gr there; u² ≥ 5gr at the bottom), particle on a rod (needs
v ≥ 0 at the top), bead on a ring, particle inside a smooth sphere or tube, particle on the outside of a sphere
(leaves when R = 0: cos θ = 2/3 from rest at the top); where the string goes slack and the particle becomes a
projectile (link [Projectiles](#math.fm.projectiles)); June 2025 FM Q7 shape (inside a sphere, reactions compared, a
cut-away part) [10].
Pitfalls: string vs rod conditions; angle measured from the wrong vertical; energy equation missing a height term.
Widget: embed **fm-vcircle**.

### Widget spec: fm-vcircle (widgets/fm-vcircle.js)
- `data-mode="string|rod|outside"`, `data-u="7"` (speed at the bottom in m/s; for outside: speed at the top),
  `data-r="1"`, `data-m="0.5"`, g = 10 (stated on screen).
- Canvas: the circle, the particle at angle θ from the lowest point (from the top for outside), the string or
  reaction arrow, and the path drawn: full circle if it completes, otherwise the circular arc up to the leaving point
  then the projectile parabola (string) or the swing back (rod), with the leaving point labelled.
- Controls: u slider, θ slider (the particle position), Play/Pause animation (W.animate; reduced motion shows the
  still path), mode tabs.
- Readouts: v at θ (energy), T or R at θ (radial equation), "completes the circle" / "string slack at θ = …°" /
  "comes to rest at θ = …°", and the critical speed √(5gr) (string), √(4gr) (rod). Node-test the leaving angles.
