# FACTS: Mathematics

Verified exam facts (Oct 2026, from the Organizer). Chapters state paper details only from this file.
The LEAD session adds the syllabus learning outcomes below.

## Exam structure
- CAIE 9709: P1 1h50/75 (AS60/AL30), P2 1h15/50 (AS only), P3 1h50/75 (AL30), P4 Mech 1h15/50, P5 S1 1h15/50, P6 S2 1h15/50 (each AS40/AL20)
  AS routes P1+P2, P1+P4, P1+P5; AL: 1,3,4,5 or 1,3,5,6 (not 4+6). Formula list MF19.
- 9231 FM: FP1 2h/75, FP2 2h/75, FM 1h30/50, FS 1h30/50; AS = P1+P3 or P1+P4

Compact form: paper duration/marks (AS weighting %/A Level weighting %).

From the 9709 syllabus (LEAD, Oct 2026):
- AS: two components in the same series. P1 + P2 is AS only and cannot be carried forward to the A Level.
  A Level: P1 + P3, then P4 + P5 or P5 + P6. Staged (AS one year, rest later) or all in one series.
- AOs: AO1 Knowledge and understanding 55% AS / 52% AL; AO2 Application and communication 45% AS / 48% AL
  (per paper AO1/AO2: P3 45/55, every other paper 55/45).
- Accuracy: non-exact answers to 3 s.f., angles in degrees to 1 d.p., unless the question says otherwise.
- Equipment: ruler and scientific calculator for all papers; graphical calculators, computers and calculators
  with symbolic algebra/differentiation/integration not permitted; no marks for unsupported calculator answers.
  Protractor and compasses not required. MF19 is shared with 9231, so it holds formulas 9709 does not need.
- Trapezium rule is in P2 (2.5) but not listed in P3 (3.5); tan⁻¹x, partial fractions, (1 + x)ⁿ rational n,
  by parts, substitution, f′/f, vectors, DEs and complex numbers are P3 only.

## Syllabus learning outcomes
Source: 9709 syllabus 2026–2027 (version 4, Dec 2025), pp. 19–39, and MF19 pp. 43–49. "Not:" = excluded.

### Paper 1 Pure 1
- 1.1 Quadratics: complete the square (vertex, sketch); discriminant (number of real roots, "repeated root"); solve quadratic equations and inequalities (factorise, complete the square, formula); simultaneous linear + quadratic by substitution; equations quadratic in a function of x (x⁴ − 5x² + 4 = 0, equations in √x, tan²x = 1 + tan x).
- 1.2 Functions: terms function, domain, range, one-one, inverse, composition; range in simple cases; gf exists only if the range of f is within the domain of g; test one-one, find inverse in simple cases; f and f⁻¹ as reflections in y = x (draw the mirror line); transformations y = f(x) + a, f(x + a), af(x), f(ax) and simple combinations, described as "translation", "reflection", "stretch"; algebraic or trig functions or graphs with given features.
- 1.3 Coordinate geometry: line from two points or point + gradient; forms y = mx + c, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), ax + by + c = 0; distance, gradient, midpoint, intersections, parallel and perpendicular gradients; circle (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r² and x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0; line–circle problems using tangent ⊥ radius, angle in a semicircle, symmetry; intersections ↔ solutions (values of k for which y = x + k meets, touches or misses a curve). Not: implicit differentiation.
- 1.4 Circular measure: radian definition, degrees ↔ radians; s = rθ and A = ½r²θ, with lengths, angles and areas of triangles.
- 1.5 Trigonometry: graphs of sin, cos, tan for any angle in degrees or radians (y = 3 sin x, y = 1 − cos 2x, y = tan(x + π/4)); exact values for 30°, 45°, 60° and related angles; principal values sin⁻¹x, cos⁻¹x, tan⁻¹x (as inverse functions); identities tan θ ≡ sin θ / cos θ and sin²θ + cos²θ ≡ 1 (prove, simplify, solve); all solutions in a given interval. Not: general solutions.
- 1.6 Series: (a + b)ⁿ for positive integer n, notation ⁿCᵣ and n!; AP and GP nth term and sum of n terms; "in AP" ⇔ 2b = a + c, "in GP" ⇔ b² = ac; questions with more than one progression; GP convergence (|r| < 1) and S∞. Not: greatest term, properties of coefficients.
- 1.7 Differentiation: gradient as the limit of chord gradients (informal; e.g. chord from x = 2 to 2 + h on y = x³); notation f′(x), f″(x), dy/dx, d²y/dx²; xⁿ for rational n, multiples, sums, chain rule; tangents, normals, increasing/decreasing, rates of change incl. connected rates; stationary points and their nature (second derivative; alternatives allowed if no method stated); use in sketching. Not: formal first principles, points of inflexion.
- 1.8 Integration: reverse of differentiation; ∫(ax + b)ⁿ dx for rational n ≠ −1, multiples, sums; constant from a given point; definite integrals incl. simple improper ones (∫₀¹ x^(−1/2) dx, ∫₁^∞ x^(−2) dx); area between a curve and lines parallel to the axes, a curve and a line, two curves; volume of revolution about either axis, incl. a region not bounded by the axis (y = 9 − x² and y = 5 about the x-axis).

### Paper 2 Pure 2 (AS only; same as Paper 3 topics 3.1–3.6 without the P3 extras)
- 2.1 Algebra: |x|, graph of y = |ax + b|, |a| = |b| ⇔ a² = b², |x − a| < b ⇔ a − b < x < a + b; divide a polynomial of degree ≤ 4 by a linear or quadratic; factor and remainder theorems incl. (ax + b). Not: y = |f(x)| or y = f(|x|) for non-linear f.
- 2.2 Logs and exponentials: logs ↔ indices, laws of logs (not change of base); eˣ and ln x, inverse functions, graphs incl. y = e^(kx) for k > 0 and k < 0; solve equations and inequalities with the unknown in an index; reduce y = kxⁿ (ln y against ln x) and y = k(aˣ) (ln y against x) to linear form and read constants from gradient and intercept.
- 2.3 Trigonometry: sec, cosec, cot, graphs of all six functions; sec²θ ≡ 1 + tan²θ, cosec²θ ≡ 1 + cot²θ; sin, cos, tan of (A ± B); sin 2A, cos 2A, tan 2A; a sin θ + b cos θ as R sin(θ ± α) or R cos(θ ± α); simplify, evaluate exactly, solve.
- 2.4 Differentiation: eˣ, ln x, sin x, cos x, tan x with multiples, sums, composites; product and quotient rules; first derivative of parametric and implicit functions, tangents and normals.
- 2.5 Integration: e^(ax+b), 1/(ax + b), sin(ax + b), cos(ax + b), sec²(ax + b); trig identities in integration (sin²x, cos²2x); trapezium rule, over- or under-estimate from a sketch. Not: general substitution.
- 2.6 Numerical solution: locate a root by a graph or a sign change; notation for a sequence of approximations converging to a root; how xₙ₊₁ = F(xₙ) relates to the equation; root to a given accuracy; iterations may fail to converge. Not: the convergence condition.

### Paper 3 Pure 3 (A Level)
- 3.1 Algebra: as 2.1, plus partial fractions for denominators (ax + b)(cx + d)(ex + f), (ax + b)(cx + d)², (ax + b)(cx² + d) (numerator degree below denominator's); (1 + x)ⁿ for rational n, |x| < 1, adapted forms such as (2 − x)^(−1/2) with their range of validity. Not: general term, top-heavy fractions.
- 3.2 Logs and exponentials: as 2.2.
- 3.3 Trigonometry: as 2.3.
- 3.4 Differentiation: as 2.4 plus tan⁻¹x. Not: derivatives of sin⁻¹x, cos⁻¹x.
- 3.5 Integration: 2.5 standard forms plus 1/(x² + a²) (e.g. 1/(3 + 2x²)); trig identities; partial fractions; f′(x)/f(x) (x/(x² + 1), tan x); integration by parts (x sin 2x, x²e^(−x), ln x, x tan⁻¹x); a given substitution, definite or indefinite (sin²2x cos x with u = sin x). The trapezium rule is listed for P2 only.
- 3.6 Numerical solution: as 2.6.
- 3.7 Vectors (2D and 3D): column, i j k, AB-arrow and bold notation; add, subtract, scalar multiples, geometric meaning (OABC parallelogram ⇔ OB = OA + OC; midpoint ½(OA + OB)); magnitude, unit, displacement and position vectors; line r = a + tb; parallel, intersecting or skew, point of intersection; scalar product: angle between lines, foot of the perpendicular, 3D solids. Not: general ratio theorem, shortest distance between skew lines, vector product.
- 3.8 Differential equations: form a DE from a rate statement (incl. a constant of proportionality); separate variables (any 3.5 technique); particular solution from an initial condition; interpret in context.
- 3.9 Complex numbers: real part, imaginary part, modulus, argument, conjugate; notation Re z, Im z, |z|, arg z, z*; arg usually −π < θ ≤ π (0 ≤ θ < 2π accepted); equal ⇔ real and imaginary parts equal; + − × ÷ in Cartesian form (full working); non-real roots of real polynomials come in conjugate pairs; Argand diagram; × and ÷ in polar form r(cos θ + i sin θ) = re^(iθ), |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|, arg(z₁z₂) = arg z₁ + arg z₂ and division versions; the two square roots in exact Cartesian form (e.g. of 5 + 12i); geometric effect of conjugating, +, −, ×, ÷; loci |z − a| < k, |z − a| = |z − b|, arg(z − a) = α.

### Paper 4 Mechanics (particles; no vector notation in papers; g = 10 m s⁻²)
- Assumes sin(90° − θ) ≡ cos θ, cos(90° − θ) ≡ sin θ, tan θ ≡ sin θ / cos θ, sin²θ + cos²θ ≡ 1, and P1 algebra.
- 4.1 Forces and equilibrium: force diagrams; components and resultants by calculation (not scale drawing); equilibrium: vector sum zero, or components in any direction sum to zero (resolving expected; triangle of forces or Lami allowed but never required or named); contact force = normal + frictional components; smooth contact and its limitations; limiting friction, limiting equilibrium, coefficient of friction, F = μR or F ≤ μR ("about to slip" = limiting); Newton's third law.
- 4.2 Kinematics (1D): distance and speed scalars; displacement, velocity, acceleration vectors; "deceleration"; s–t and v–t graphs (area under v–t = displacement; gradients = velocity, acceleration); calculus in t (P1 techniques only); constant-acceleration formulae, possibly several equations or two particles.
- 4.3 Momentum (1D): definition and vector nature; conservation in direct impact of two bodies, incl. coalescing. Not: impulse, coefficient of restitution.
- 4.4 Newton's laws: constant mass, constant forces incl. friction, tension (light inextensible string), thrust (rod); other resistances only when stated; W = mg with g = 10; vertical motion and inclined planes (rough plane: different acceleration up and down); connected particles (string over a smooth pulley, car towing a trailer by rope or rigid tow-bar).
- 4.5 Energy, work, power: W = Fd cos θ (no scalar product); KE and GPE; work–energy principle and conservation of energy, incl. non-straight paths (smooth curved slide); power as rate of doing work, average power = work done / time, P = Fv; instantaneous acceleration of a car on a hill against resistance.

### Paper 5 Probability & Statistics 1 (P1 algebra only; notation P(A), P(A ∪ B), P(A ∩ B), P(A|B), A′)
- 5.1 Representation of data: choose a representation and discuss pros/cons; stem-and-leaf (incl. back-to-back), box-and-whisker, histograms, cumulative frequency graphs; mean, median, mode, range, IQR, standard deviation to compare data sets; cumulative frequency for median, quartiles, percentiles, proportions above/below/between; mean and SD from raw data, grouped data, Σx and Σx², coded Σ(x − a) and Σ(x − a)², up to two data sets.
- 5.2 Permutations and combinations: the two terms; selections; arrangements in a line with repetition (NEEDLESS) and restrictions (together / not together); people in two or more rows. Not: arrangements in a circle.
- 5.3 Probability: equiprobable outcomes, counting with permutations/combinations (two dice, balls from a bag); addition and multiplication; mutually exclusive and independent events, independence via P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B); conditional probability (sample space, tree diagram, P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)). Explicit P(A ∪ B) formula not required.
- 5.4 Discrete random variables: probability distribution table, E(X), Var(X); B(n, p) and Geo(p), pᵣ = p(1 − p)^(r−1) for r = 1, 2, 3, …; recognise suitable models; E and Var of binomial, E of geometric (1/p). No proofs.
- 5.5 Normal distribution: model a continuous variable, normal tables, sketches; X ~ N(μ, σ²): find P(X > x₁) and related, or find x₁, μ, σ from a probability (show full standardisation); normal approximation to the binomial with continuity correction when np > 5 and nq > 5.

### Paper 6 Probability & Statistics 2 (assumes P5 and P3 calculus)
- 6.1 Poisson: Po(λ) probabilities; mean = variance = λ; model for random events; approximation to binomial (n > 50 and np < 5, approx.); normal approximation to Poisson with continuity correction (λ > 15, approx.). No proofs.
- 6.2 Linear combinations: E(aX + b) = aE(X) + b, Var(aX + b) = a²Var(X); E(aX + bY) = aE(X) + bE(Y); Var(aX + bY) = a²Var(X) + b²Var(Y) for independent X, Y; aX + b normal if X normal; aX + bY normal for independent normals; X + Y Poisson for independent Poissons. No proofs.
- 6.3 Continuous random variables: pdf properties (one interval, domain may be infinite, e.g. 3/x⁴ for x ≥ 1); probabilities, mean, variance; median and percentiles from areas. Not: the cumulative distribution function.
- 6.4 Sampling and estimation: sample vs population, need for randomness, why a method is unsatisfactory, random numbers; X̄ as a random variable, E(X̄) = μ, Var(X̄) = σ²/n; X̄ normal if X normal; Central Limit Theorem informally (large n); unbiased estimates of mean and variance from raw or summary data ("unbiased" in simple terms); confidence interval for a mean (normal population with known variance, or large sample); approximate confidence interval for a proportion from a large sample. Not: named sampling methods (quota, stratified).
- 6.5 Hypothesis tests: nature of a test; one- and two-tailed; null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, rejection (critical) region, acceptance region, test statistic; conclusions in context; test on a single observation from a binomial or Poisson (direct probabilities or normal approximation); test for a population mean (normal with known variance, or large sample); Type I and Type II errors and their probabilities (normal, binomial, Poisson).

### MF19 (given in every paper)
- Pure: sphere volume and surface area, cone volume and curved surface area, arc rθ, sector ½r²θ; quadratic formula; AP uₙ and Sₙ; GP uₙ, Sₙ, S∞; (a + b)ⁿ with ⁿCᵣ = n!/(r!(n − r)!), (1 + x)ⁿ for rational n, |x| < 1; trig identities (tan quotient, three Pythagorean, compound angle, double angle); principal value ranges; derivatives of xⁿ, ln x, eˣ, sin, cos, tan, sec, cosec, cot, tan⁻¹x, product and quotient rules, parametric rule; integrals of xⁿ, 1/x, eˣ, sin, cos, sec², 1/(x² + a²), 1/(x² − a²), 1/(a² − x²), by parts, f′/f; scalar product a·b = |a||b| cos θ.
- Mechanics: only the four constant-acceleration formulae (v = u + at, s = ½(u + v)t, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as).
- Statistics: mean and SD (ungrouped and grouped); E(X), Var(X); binomial, geometric, Poisson pᵣ with means and variances; continuous E(X), Var(X); unbiased estimators x̄ and s²; CLT X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n); sample proportion ~ N(p, p(1 − p)/n); normal tables.
- Not given (students must learn): the trapezium rule, laws of logarithms, exact trig values, radian conversion, line and circle equations, distance and midpoint, the chain rule, area and volume integrals, F ≤ μR, W = mg, KE and GPE, P = Fv, W = Fd cos θ, momentum, Newton's laws, P(A|B), counting rules, approximation conditions, continuity corrections, Var(aX + bY), confidence-interval formulas.
