@summary Twenty-nine short questions that mix every topic of Mechanics (forces, kinematics, Newton's laws, momentum, work, energy and power), ordered from ★ to ★★★. Each answer is a mark scheme, and each question names the chapter to revise.

:::why
In the exam you are never told which chapter a question comes from. A mechanics question could be solved with a resolved force, a suvat equation, an energy equation or conservation of momentum, and choosing the right one is half of the marks. This page mixes everything on purpose, so that you have to decide every time.
:::

:::tip How to use this page
Do the questions in order, a few at a time, on paper. Take \(g = 10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) throughout. ★ questions are one idea (2 to 4 marks), ★★ questions are exam standard for a typical Paper 4 question, ★★★ questions need two or more ideas together. Always start with a diagram and a sign convention. Mark yourself with the scheme, and under each answer the **Revise** link takes you to the chapter for that topic. Keep a list of the chapters that come up more than once: they are your weak spots.
:::

:::try Warm-up ★
Q: [2 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★ A block of mass 10 kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at \(30^\circ\) to the horizontal. Find the component of its weight down the plane and the normal reaction of the plane on the block.
A: The weight is 100 N. Down the plane: \(100\sin 30^\circ = 50\) N. [B1]
Perpendicular to the plane: \(R = 100\cos 30^\circ = 86.6\) N. [B1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★ Two forces act on a particle: 6 N due east and 8 N due north. Find the magnitude of their resultant and the angle it makes with the 6 N force.
A: The forces are at right angles, so the magnitude is \(\sqrt{6^2 + 8^2}\). [M1]
\(= 10\) N. [A1]
The angle with the 6 N force satisfies \(\tan\theta = \dfrac86\), so \(\theta = 53.1^\circ\). [A1]
Q: [4 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★ A car increases its speed uniformly from 5 m s⁻¹ to 25 m s⁻¹ in 8 s. Find its acceleration and the distance it travels in this time.
A: Use \(v = u + at\): \(25 = 5 + 8a\). [M1]
\(a = 2.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
Use \(s = \tfrac12(u + v)t\): \(s = \tfrac12(5 + 25)(8)\). [M1]
\(s = 120\) m. [A1]
Q: [2 marks] (#math.m.momentum) ★ A particle of mass 2 kg moving at 6 m s⁻¹ collides directly with a stationary particle of mass 4 kg on a smooth horizontal surface, and the two particles coalesce. Find the speed after the collision.
A: Conservation of momentum: \(2 \times 6 = (2 + 4)v\). [M1]
\(v = 2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
Q: [2 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★ A particle of mass 3 kg is pulled vertically upwards by a light string. It accelerates upwards at 2 m s⁻². Find the tension in the string.
A: Newton's second law, upwards: \(T - 3 \times 10 = 3 \times 2\). [M1]
\(T = 36\) N. [A1]
Q: [2 marks] (#math.m.energy) ★ A ball of mass 0.5 kg is released from rest at a height of 20 m above the ground. Ignoring air resistance, find its speed as it reaches the ground.
A: Energy is conserved: \(\tfrac12(0.5)v^2 = 0.5 \times 10 \times 20\). [M1]
\(v^2 = 400\), so \(v = 20\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
Q: [2 marks] (#math.m.energy) ★ A car is driven at a constant speed of 15 m s⁻¹ by a driving force of 400 N. Find the power developed by the engine.
A: Use \(P = Fv\): \(P = 400 \times 15\). [M1]
\(P = 6000\) W (6 kW). [A1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★ A block of mass 10 kg rests on rough horizontal ground, where the coefficient of friction is 0.3. A horizontal force of 25 N is applied to the block. Does the block move? State the friction force.
A: The normal reaction is \(R = 100\) N, so the maximum (limiting) friction is \(0.3 \times 100 = 30\) N. [M1] [A1]
The applied force 25 N is less than 30 N, so the block stays at rest, and the friction force is 25 N (not 30 N). [A1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★ A particle accelerates uniformly from rest to 8 m s⁻¹ in 4 s, then moves at a constant 8 m s⁻¹ for 6 s. Find the total distance travelled.
A: The distance is the area under the \(v\)–\(t\) graph. The first stage is a triangle: \(\tfrac12 \times 4 \times 8 = 16\) m. [M1] [A1]
The second stage is a rectangle: \(8 \times 6 = 48\) m, so the total is 64 m. [A1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★ A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 15 m s⁻¹ from ground level. Find the greatest height it reaches and the time before it returns to the ground.
A: At the top \(v = 0\): \(0 = 15^2 - 2(10)h\). [M1]
\(h = 11.25\) m. [A1]
It takes 1.5 s to reach the top (\(0 = 15 - 10t\)) and 1.5 s to fall back, so the total time is 3 s. [A1]

:::try Exam standard ★★
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★★ A particle moves in a straight line. Its displacement \(s\) metres from a fixed point \(O\) at time \(t\) seconds is \(s = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t\), for \(t \ge 0\).

(a) Find an expression for the velocity of the particle at time \(t\). [2]

(b) Find the velocity when \(t = 3\). [1]

(c) Find the times at which the particle is instantaneously at rest. [2]
A: (a) Velocity is the derivative of displacement: [M1]
\[ v = \frac{ds}{dt} = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 \]
[A1]

(b) At \(t = 3\): \(v = 54 - 54 + 12 = 12\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]

(c) Set \(v = 0\): \(6(t^2 - 3t + 2) = 6(t - 1)(t - 2) = 0\). [M1]
So \(t = 1\) and \(t = 2\). [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★★ A particle of weight 60 N hangs in equilibrium from two light strings attached to a horizontal ceiling. One string makes an angle of \(30^\circ\) with the horizontal and the other makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the horizontal, on the opposite side. Find the tension in each string.
A: Let \(T_1\) be the tension in the string at \(30^\circ\) and \(T_2\) the tension in the string at \(60^\circ\). Resolve horizontally: [M1]
\[ T_1\cos 30^\circ = T_2\cos 60^\circ \]
[A1] Resolve vertically: [M1]
\[ T_1\sin 30^\circ + T_2\sin 60^\circ = 60 \]
[A1] From the first equation \(T_1 = T_2/\sqrt3\). Substituting: \(\dfrac{T_2}{2\sqrt3} + \dfrac{\sqrt3\,T_2}{2} = 60\), so \(\dfrac{2T_2}{\sqrt3} = 60\). Hence \(T_2 = 30\sqrt3 = 52.0\) N and \(T_1 = 30\) N. [A1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★★ A crate of mass 6 kg rests on rough horizontal ground. A boy pushes the crate with a force of 30 N directed at \(25^\circ\) below the horizontal. The crate is on the point of moving. Find the coefficient of friction between the crate and the ground.
A: Resolve vertically: \(R = 60 + 30\sin 25^\circ = 72.68\) N. [M1]
Resolve horizontally, in limiting equilibrium: \(F = 30\cos 25^\circ = 27.19\) N. [M1]
\[ \mu = \frac{F}{R} = \frac{27.19}{72.68} = 0.374 \]
[A1]
Q: [6 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★★ Particles \(P\) and \(Q\), of masses 5 kg and 3 kg, are attached to the ends of a light inextensible string that passes over a smooth fixed pulley. The particles hang vertically and are released from rest with the string taut.

(a) Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. [4]

(b) Find the speed of \(P\) when it has moved 2 m. [2]
A: (a) For \(P\) (moving down): \(50 - T = 5a\). [M1]
For \(Q\) (moving up): \(T - 30 = 3a\). [M1]
Adding: \(20 = 8a\), so \(a = 2.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
Then \(T = 30 + 3(2.5) = 37.5\) N. [A1]

(b) Use \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\): [M1]
\(v^2 = 0 + 2(2.5)(2) = 10\), so \(v = 3.16\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
Q: [4 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★★ A car of mass 1000 kg tows a trailer of mass 400 kg along a straight horizontal road by a light tow-bar. The driving force of the car's engine is 3500 N. The resistances to motion are 500 N on the car and 200 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration of the car and the tension in the tow-bar.
A: For the whole system: \(3500 - 500 - 200 = 1400a\). [M1]
\(a = 2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
For the trailer alone, with tension \(T\): \(T - 200 = 400 \times 2\). [M1]
\(T = 1000\) N. [A1]
Q: [4 marks] (#math.m.energy) ★★ A block of mass 8 kg is pulled from rest up a rough plane by a constant force of 80 N acting parallel to the plane, which is inclined at an angle \(\alpha\) to the horizontal where \(\sin\alpha = 0.6\). The block moves 5 m along the plane, and the friction force is 12 N. Find the speed of the block after it has moved 5 m.
A: The block rises \(5 \times 0.6 = 3\) m, so the gain in potential energy is \(8 \times 10 \times 3 = 240\) J. [B1]
The work done by the 80 N force is \(80 \times 5 = 400\) J, and the work done against friction is \(12 \times 5 = 60\) J. [B1]
Work–energy: \(400 - 60 - 240 = \tfrac12(8)v^2\). [M1]
\(100 = 4v^2\), so \(v = 5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★★ Particle \(P\) starts from rest at a point \(O\) and moves in a straight line with constant acceleration 2 m s⁻². Three seconds later, particle \(Q\) passes through \(O\) moving in the same direction with constant speed 16 m s⁻¹. Find the times, measured from the start of \(P\)'s motion, at which the particles are level, and the distance from \(O\) at each of these times.
A: The displacement of \(P\) is \(s_P = \tfrac12(2)t^2 = t^2\). [B1]
The displacement of \(Q\) is \(s_Q = 16(t - 3)\) for \(t \ge 3\). [B1]
They are level when \(t^2 = 16t - 48\), that is \(t^2 - 16t + 48 = 0\), so \((t - 4)(t - 12) = 0\). [M1]
\(t = 4\) s or \(t = 12\) s. [A1]
The distances from \(O\) are \(4^2 = 16\) m and \(12^2 = 144\) m. [A1]
Q: [3 marks] (#math.m.energy) ★★ A car of mass 800 kg moves at a constant speed of 20 m s⁻¹ up a straight hill inclined at an angle \(\alpha\) to the horizontal, where \(\sin\alpha = \frac1{20}\). The engine works at a constant rate of 24 kW. Find the resistance to the motion of the car.
A: The driving force is \(D = \dfrac{P}{v} = \dfrac{24\,000}{20} = 1200\) N. [M1]
The weight component down the hill is \(800 \times 10 \times \tfrac1{20} = 400\) N. [A1]
At constant speed, \(1200 = 400 + R\), so \(R = 800\) N. [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★★ Two forces act on a particle at \(O\): a force of 10 N in the direction of the positive \(x\)-axis, and a force of 14 N at \(60^\circ\) above the positive \(x\)-axis. Find the magnitude of the resultant force and the angle it makes with the \(x\)-axis.
A: Resolve in the \(x\) and \(y\) directions: [M1]
\[ X = 10 + 14\cos 60^\circ = 17, \qquad Y = 14\sin 60^\circ = 12.12 \]
[A1] The magnitude is \(\sqrt{17^2 + 12.12^2} = \sqrt{436}\). [M1]
\(= 20.9\) N. [A1]
The angle with the \(x\)-axis: \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{12.12}{17}\), so \(\theta = 35.5^\circ\). [A1]
Q: [4 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★★ A skier of mass 70 kg moves down a straight slope inclined at an angle \(\alpha\) to the horizontal, where \(\sin\alpha = 0.2\). The resistance to the skier's motion is a constant force \(R\) N. Starting from rest, the skier travels 20 m in 5 s. Find the acceleration of the skier and the value of \(R\).
A: Use \(s = ut + \tfrac12at^2\): \(20 = \tfrac12 a(25)\). [M1]
\(a = 1.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
Newton's second law down the slope: \(70 \times 10 \times 0.2 - R = 70 \times 1.6\). [M1]
\(140 - R = 112\), so \(R = 28\) N. [A1]
Q: [4 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★★ A particle moves in a straight line. Its speed increases uniformly from 3 m s⁻¹ to \(V\) m s⁻¹ over the first 10 s. It then moves at constant speed \(V\) m s⁻¹ for 15 s. The total distance travelled in the 25 s is 315 m. Find the value of \(V\).
A: The distance in the first stage is the area of a trapezium: \(\tfrac12(3 + V)(10) = 5(3 + V)\). [B1]
The second stage is \(15V\), so the total is \(5(3 + V) + 15V = 315\). [M1]
\(15 + 20V = 315\). [A1]
\(V = 15\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]
:::

Q: [10 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★★★ A particle \(A\) of mass 5 kg lies on a rough horizontal table, where the coefficient of friction is 0.2. A particle \(B\) of mass 3 kg hangs vertically. The particles are connected by a light inextensible string that passes over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table. The string is taut and \(B\) is 1.8 m above the floor. The system is released from rest and the table is long enough for \(A\) never to reach the pulley.

(a) Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. [5]

(b) Find the speed of \(B\) when it hits the floor. [2]

\(B\) does not rebound.

(c) Find the total distance \(A\) travels from the moment of release until it comes to rest. [3]
A: (a) The normal reaction on \(A\) is 50 N, so the friction force is \(0.2 \times 50 = 10\) N. [B1]
For \(A\): \(T - 10 = 5a\). [M1]
For \(B\): \(30 - T = 3a\). [M1]
Adding: \(20 = 8a\), so \(a = 2.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
Then \(T = 10 + 12.5 = 22.5\) N. [A1]

(b) \(v^2 = 0 + 2(2.5)(1.8) = 9\). [M1]
\(v = 3\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]

(c) When \(B\) lands the string goes slack, and \(A\) decelerates under friction alone: \(5a' = 10\), so \(a' = 2\). [M1]
\(0 = 3^2 - 2(2)s\), so \(s = 2.25\) m. [A1]
The total distance is \(1.8 + 2.25 = 4.05\) m. [A1]
Q: [7 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★★★ A particle \(P\) starts from rest at a point \(O\) and moves in a straight line. Its acceleration at time \(t\) seconds is \((8 - 2t)\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

(a) Show that the velocity of \(P\) at time \(t\) is \((8t - t^2)\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [2]

(b) Find the displacement of \(P\) from \(O\) at the instant when it is first at rest after \(t = 0\). [3]

(c) Find the total distance travelled by \(P\) in the first 12 seconds. [2]
A: (a) Integrate the acceleration: \(v = 8t - t^2 + c\). [M1]
When \(t = 0\), \(v = 0\), so \(c = 0\) and \(v = 8t - t^2\). [A1]

(b) \(v = t(8 - t) = 0\) at \(t = 8\) (the next time after \(t = 0\)). [B1]
Integrate again, with \(s = 0\) at \(t = 0\): \(s = 4t^2 - \tfrac13t^3\). [M1]
At \(t = 8\): \(s = 256 - \dfrac{512}{3} = \dfrac{256}{3} = 85.3\) m. [A1]

(c) At \(t = 12\): \(s = 576 - 576 = 0\), so \(P\) is back at \(O\). It travelled \(\dfrac{256}{3}\) m out and the same distance back (\(v < 0\) for \(t > 8\)). [M1]
The total distance is \(\dfrac{512}{3} = 171\) m. [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.momentum) ★★★ Particle \(P\) of mass 0.4 kg is moving at 5 m s⁻¹ on a smooth horizontal surface when it collides directly with particle \(Q\) of mass 0.1 kg, which is at rest. After the collision \(P\) continues in the same direction with speed 3 m s⁻¹. Afterwards \(Q\) slides onto a rough part of the surface where the coefficient of friction is 0.5. Find the distance \(Q\) slides on the rough surface before it comes to rest.
A: Conservation of momentum: \(0.4 \times 5 = 0.4 \times 3 + 0.1v\). [M1]
\(2 = 1.2 + 0.1v\) [A1] so \(v = 8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), the speed of \(Q\). [A1]
On the rough surface the friction is \(0.5 \times 1 = 0.5\) N, so the deceleration is \(\dfrac{0.5}{0.1} = 5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [M1]
\(0 = 8^2 - 2(5)d\), so \(d = 6.4\) m. [A1]
Q: [6 marks] (#math.m.energy) ★★★ A car of mass 1500 kg has a maximum engine power of 36 kW. The car travels up a straight road inclined at an angle \(\alpha\) to the horizontal, where \(\sin\alpha = 0.04\). The resistance to motion is a constant 900 N.

(a) Find the maximum steady speed of the car up the road. [3]

(b) Find the acceleration of the car when it is moving up the road at 15 m s⁻¹ with the engine working at maximum power. [3]
A: (a) At steady speed the driving force balances the resistance and the weight component: \(D = 900 + 1500 \times 10 \times 0.04 = 1500\) N. [M1]
\(P = Dv\), so \(36\,000 = 1500v\). [M1]
\(v = 24\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). [A1]

(b) At 15 m s⁻¹ the driving force is \(D = \dfrac{36\,000}{15} = 2400\) N. [M1]
Newton's second law up the road: \(2400 - 900 - 600 = 1500a\). [M1]
\(a = 0.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.forces) ★★★ A block of mass 5 kg lies on a rough plane inclined at an angle \(\alpha\) to the horizontal, where \(\sin\alpha = 0.6\) and \(\cos\alpha = 0.8\). The coefficient of friction is 0.5. A force of magnitude \(P\) N, acting up the plane parallel to a line of greatest slope, is applied to the block. Find the range of values of \(P\) for which the block remains at rest.
A: The normal reaction is \(R = 50 \times 0.8 = 40\) N, so the maximum friction is \(0.5 \times 40 = 20\) N. The weight component down the plane is \(50 \times 0.6 = 30\) N. [B1]
Least \(P\): the block is about to slide down and friction acts up the plane: \(P + 20 = 30\). [M1]
\(P = 10\). [A1]
Greatest \(P\): the block is about to slide up and friction acts down the plane: \(P = 30 + 20\). [M1]
\(P = 50\), so the block remains at rest for \(10 \le P \le 50\). [A1]
Q: [6 marks] (#math.m.kinematics) ★★★ A stone is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m s⁻¹ from the top of a cliff 25 m above the sea. The stone moves freely under gravity and falls into the sea.

(a) Find the time taken for the stone to reach the sea. [2]

(b) Find the speed of the stone as it reaches the sea. [2]

(c) Find the greatest height of the stone above the sea. [2]
A: (a) Take upwards as positive; the displacement at the sea is \(-25\): \(-25 = 20t - 5t^2\). [M1]
\(t^2 - 4t - 5 = 0\), so \((t - 5)(t + 1) = 0\) and \(t = 5\) s (rejecting \(t = -1\)). [A1]

(b) \(v = 20 - 10(5) = -30\), so the speed is 30 m s⁻¹. [M1] [A1]

(c) At the highest point \(v = 0\): the rise above the cliff is \(\dfrac{20^2}{2 \times 10} = 20\) m. [M1]
The greatest height above the sea is \(25 + 20 = 45\) m. [A1]
Q: [5 marks] (#math.m.momentum) ★★★ Particle \(P\), of mass 3 kg, moves at 6 m s⁻¹ towards particle \(Q\), of mass \(m\) kg, which moves at 4 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction, on a smooth horizontal surface. After the collision \(P\) moves back at 1 m s⁻¹ and \(Q\) moves away at 6 m s⁻¹, each in the direction opposite to its original direction.

(a) Find the value of \(m\). [3]

(b) Find the total loss of kinetic energy in the collision. [2]
A: (a) Take the original direction of \(P\) as positive. Conservation of momentum: [M1]
\[ 3(6) + m(-4) = 3(-1) + m(6) \]
[A1] \(18 - 4m = -3 + 6m\), so \(10m = 21\) and \(m = 2.1\). [A1]

(b) Kinetic energy before and after the collision: [M1]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Before} &= \tfrac12(3)(36) + \tfrac12(2.1)(16) = 70.8\ \text{J} \\ \text{After} &= \tfrac12(3)(1) + \tfrac12(2.1)(36) = 39.3\ \text{J} \end{aligned} \]
The loss is \(70.8 - 39.3 = 31.5\) J. [A1]
Q: [6 marks] (#math.m.newton) ★★★ A lift moves vertically upwards. In the first 4 s it accelerates uniformly from rest at 1 m s⁻². It then moves at constant speed for 8 s, and then decelerates uniformly at 2 m s⁻² until it stops. A passenger of mass 70 kg stands on the floor of the lift throughout.

(a) Find the reaction of the floor on the passenger during each of the three stages of the motion. [4]

(b) Find the total distance travelled by the lift. [2]
A: (a) During acceleration, Newton's second law upwards: [M1]
\(R - 700 = 70(1)\), so \(R = 770\) N. [A1]
At constant speed there is no resultant force: \(R = 700\) N. [B1]
During deceleration the acceleration is \(-2\): \(R - 700 = 70(-2)\), so \(R = 560\) N. [A1]

(b) The speed after the first stage is \(4\) m s⁻¹, and the last stage lasts \(\dfrac{4}{2} = 2\) s. The area under the \(v\)–\(t\) graph is [M1]
\[ \tfrac12(4)(4) + 4 \times 8 + \tfrac12(4)(2) = 8 + 32 + 4 = 44\ \text{m} \]
[A1]
