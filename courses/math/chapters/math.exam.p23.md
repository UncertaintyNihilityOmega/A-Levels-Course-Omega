:::why
Papers 2 and 3 share one set of chapters because Paper 3 contains all the topics of Paper 2, except the trapezium rule, and adds more. You need to know which paper you are sitting, what each paper adds, and how to spend the time. Paper 3 has the highest share of problem-solving marks of any 9709 paper.
:::

## The two papers at a glance

| Fact | Paper 2 | Paper 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Pure Mathematics 2 | Pure Mathematics 3 |
| Time | 1 hour 15 minutes | 1 hour 50 minutes |
| Marks | 50 | 75 |
| Weighting | 40% of AS only | 30% of A Level |
| Used in | AS route P1 + P2 (AS only: it cannot count towards an A Level) | A Level routes 1 and 2 |
| Tools | scientific calculator, ruler, MF19 | scientific calculator, ruler, MF19 |
| Knowledge / application | 55% / 45% | 45% / 55% |

You need the content of Paper 1 for both. Paper 3 is the only paper where application (AO2) carries more marks than knowledge (AO1): you must choose methods, link topics and explain, not just carry out routines.

## Timing plans

| | Marks | Minutes available | Minutes per mark | At 1.3 min per mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 2 | 50 | 75 | 1.5 | 65 minutes, leaving 10 to check |
| Paper 3 | 75 | 110 | 1.47 | 98 minutes, leaving 12 to check |

On Paper 3 the long questions combine topics (an integral that needs partial fractions, a vector problem with a perpendicular foot, a complex-number locus). Spend your first minute on each long question reading all parts, because later parts often hint at the method.

:::warn The trapezium rule belongs to Paper 2
The syllabus lists the trapezium rule under Paper 2 (topic 2.5) and not under Paper 3. If you sit Paper 3, the safe plan is still to know it, since it is a short rule; but do not expect it to be the centre of a Paper 3 question.
:::

## MF19: given and not given

**Given:** the standard derivatives (including \(e^x\), \(\ln x\), the six trig functions, \(\tan^{-1}x\)), the product, quotient and parametric rules, the standard integrals (including \(\dfrac{1}{x^2 + a^2}\)), the by-parts formula and \(\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}dx\), the compound and double-angle formulae, the three Pythagorean identities, the binomial series for \((1 + x)^n\) with rational \(n\) and \(|x| < 1\), and the scalar product \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta\).

**Not given (learn these):** the laws of logarithms; the trapezium rule; the chain rule; the exact trig values; the definition and properties of the modulus; the factor and remainder theorems; the equation of a line in vector form; and the properties of complex numbers (modulus, argument, conjugate, loci). The R-form (\(R\sin(\theta + \alpha)\)) is built from the compound-angle formulae, which are given.

:::exam MF19 is shared with Further Maths
The booklet is the same for 9709 and 9231, so it holds formulas that 9709 never needs. Do not panic if you see an unfamiliar line: read it, ask whether the topic is on your syllabus, and ignore it if not.
:::

## The marks students lose most often

- **Modulus:** \(|x| < 3\) is a single interval; \(|x| > 3\) is two pieces; \(|a + b| \ne |a| + |b|\).
- **Logarithms:** inventing a law (\(\log(x + y) = \log x + \log y\)); forgetting to check that a solution keeps the argument of a log positive.
- **Trigonometry:** \(\sin(A + B) \ne \sin A + \sin B\); the sign in \(\cos(A \pm B)\) is opposite to the bracket; the R-form's \(\alpha\) in the wrong quadrant.
- **Calculus with trig:** differentiating or integrating with the calculator in degree mode. The derivative of \(\sin x\) is \(\cos x\) only for radians.
- **Integration:** forgetting to divide by the coefficient of \(x\) (\(\int e^{3x}dx = \tfrac13 e^{3x}\)); the sign of \(\int\sin x\); using \(\ln\) when the numerator is not the derivative of the denominator (\(\int\dfrac{1}{x^2 + 4}dx\) is an inverse tangent).
- **By parts and substitution:** dropping the minus sign in \(uv - \int v\,du\); omitting the limits from \(uv\); leaving the limits in \(x\) after substituting.
- **Partial fractions and series:** missing the \(\dfrac{B}{x + 2}\) term when the factor is repeated; factoring out the constant before raising to the power (\(\sqrt{4 + x} = 2\sqrt{1 + x/4}\)) and stating the range of validity.
- **Numerical methods:** claiming a root from a sign change when the function is discontinuous (\(1/x\)); not stating "\(f\) is continuous" with the sign change.
- **Differential equations:** adding the constant after taking the exponential.
- **Vectors:** measuring an angle between vectors that do not start at the same point.

## Final week

:::checklist Final week
- [ ] I have done at least two full timed papers (Paper 2 or Paper 3) in the last 14 days and marked them
- [ ] I can state the laws of logarithms and solve equations with the unknown in an index
- [ ] I can solve a trig equation with compound or double angles and write \(a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta\) in the R-form
- [ ] I can differentiate products, quotients, \(e^x\), \(\ln x\) and trig functions, and find a tangent from a parametric or implicit equation
- [ ] I can integrate standard forms and use an identity such as \(\sin^2x = \tfrac12(1 - \cos 2x)\)
- [ ] I can find a root by sign change and by iteration, and I know when each fails
- [ ] (Paper 3) I can split into partial fractions and integrate, and I can integrate by parts and by a given substitution
- [ ] (Paper 3) I can find the angle between two lines, decide if they intersect, and find the foot of a perpendicular
- [ ] (Paper 3) I can solve a separable differential equation with a given condition
- [ ] (Paper 3) I can add, multiply and divide complex numbers, and sketch a locus
:::

:::recap
- Paper 2: 1 h 15, 50 marks, 40% of an AS only. Paper 3: 1 h 50, 75 marks, 30% of an A Level.
- Paper 3 contains Paper 2's topics (except the trapezium rule) and adds partial fractions, the rational binomial series, by parts, substitution, \(f'/f\), \(\tan^{-1}x\), vectors, differential equations and complex numbers.
- Paper 3 gives more weight to application (55%): practise questions that combine topics.
- Plan 1.3 minutes per mark; that leaves about 10 minutes (Paper 2) or 12 (Paper 3) to check.
- Learn what MF19 omits: the laws of logs, the trapezium rule, the chain rule, exact trig values and the complex-number results.
- Calculus with trig needs radian mode; check D or R before every calculation.
:::
