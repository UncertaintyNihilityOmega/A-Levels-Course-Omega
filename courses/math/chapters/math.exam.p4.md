:::why
Mechanics is the paper where the same twelve ideas, forces, Newton's second law, constant acceleration, energy and momentum, are tested in many costumes. Students who draw the diagram first, write \(F = ma\) before any numbers, and keep their signs straight collect marks that others lose. This guide shows how to prepare for it.
:::

## The paper at a glance

| Fact | Paper 4 |
|---|---|
| Name | Mechanics |
| Time | 1 hour 15 minutes |
| Marks | 50 |
| Weighting | 40% of an AS (with P1), 20% of an A Level |
| Used in | AS route P1 + P4; A Level route 1 (P1 + P3 + P4 + P5). It cannot be combined with P6 |
| Tools | scientific calculator, ruler, MF19 |
| Gravity | \(g = 10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) |
| Accuracy | 3 s.f.; angles to 1 d.p. unless the question says otherwise |

Paper 4 treats bodies as particles and does not use vector notation. It assumes Paper 1 algebra, plus \(\sin(90^\circ - \theta) \equiv \cos\theta\), \(\cos(90^\circ - \theta) \equiv \sin\theta\), \(\tan\theta \equiv \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\) and \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1\). Knowledge (AO1) is 55% of the marks and application (AO2) is 45%.

## A timing plan

The paper gives 75 minutes for 50 marks, which is **1.5 minutes per mark**. Aim for **1.3 minutes per mark** on the first pass: that is 65 minutes of work and about 10 minutes to check. A 3-mark part should take about 4 minutes, and a 6-mark part about 8 minutes.

Mechanics rewards the diagram. Spend the first minute of every question drawing it, marking the forces, the positive direction, and every given value. Many students who do this find the equations almost write themselves.

## Formulas: what MF19 gives you and what you must learn

**You are given only the four constant-acceleration formulae:**

\[v = u + at, \qquad s = \tfrac12(u + v)t, \qquad s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2, \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as\]

**You must learn everything else,** including:

| Topic | Formula or fact |
|---|---|
| Weight | \(W = mg\) with \(g = 10\) |
| Newton's second law | \(F = ma\) (resultant force) |
| Friction | \(F \le \mu R\); limiting friction \(F = \mu R\) ("about to slip") |
| Work done | \(W = Fd\cos\theta\) |
| Kinetic energy | \(\tfrac12 mv^2\) |
| Gravitational potential energy | \(mgh\) |
| Power | \(P = Fv\); average power \(= \) work done \(\div\) time |
| Momentum | \(mv\), conserved in a collision: \(m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2\) |
| Equilibrium | the resultant is zero: resolve in two perpendicular directions |

:::exam Know the four formulae, and know when each applies
The four formulae need **constant** acceleration, so they are not used when the acceleration is given as a function of time. Then you differentiate or integrate (the P1 calculus). If the question gives \(v\) as a function of \(t\), the acceleration is \(\dfrac{dv}{dt}\) and the displacement is \(\int v\,dt\).
:::

## The marks students lose most often

- **Invented forces:** adding a "force of motion" or a force with no source. Every force on the diagram needs a cause (weight, normal reaction, friction, tension, thrust, driving force).
- **Friction:** using \(F = \mu R\) when the body is not at the point of slipping. Friction equals whatever stops motion, up to \(\mu R\).
- **Signs:** one positive direction per equation. A ball thrown up has \(u > 0\) but \(a = -10\). A negative displacement is not a negative distance.
- **Connected particles:** writing two equations that disagree about the direction of the acceleration; putting friction on the particle that has none.
- **Weight and mass:** using \(mg\) where \(m\) belongs, or the other way round, in \(F = ma\).
- **Energy:** forgetting to subtract work done against friction; mixing the vertical height (for GPE) with the slope length (for friction).
- **Momentum:** inconsistent signs for velocities in opposite directions.
- **Slopes:** resolving along and perpendicular to the plane, with the wrong trig function for each component.

## Final week

:::checklist Final week
- [ ] I have done at least two full timed Paper 4 papers in the last 14 days and marked them
- [ ] I draw a force diagram for every question before writing any equation
- [ ] I can resolve forces, find a resultant and solve a limiting-friction problem
- [ ] I can use all four constant-acceleration formulae and choose the right one
- [ ] I can read a v–t graph for displacement, acceleration and distance
- [ ] I can write \(F = ma\) for connected particles on a pulley and on a slope
- [ ] I can use conservation of momentum, including coalescing
- [ ] I can use the work–energy equation with friction and a slope
- [ ] I can find the acceleration of a car using \(P = Fv\) on a hill, and its maximum speed
- [ ] I take \(g = 10\), say so in the working, and give 3 s.f.
:::
