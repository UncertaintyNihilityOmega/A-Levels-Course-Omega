:::why
Paper 1 is the biggest single paper in 9709: it is 60% of an AS Level and 30% of a full A Level, and every other paper uses its algebra and calculus. A good plan for the 1 hour 50 minutes turns the mathematics you know into marks you collect.

## The paper at a glance

| Fact | Paper 1 |
|---|---|
| Name | Pure Mathematics 1 |
| Time | 1 hour 50 minutes |
| Marks | 75 |
| Weighting | 60% of AS, 30% of A Level |
| Used in | every AS route (P1 with P2, P4 or P5) and both A Level routes |
| Tools | scientific calculator and ruler; MF19 formula booklet supplied |
| Accuracy | 3 s.f.; angles to 1 d.p. unless the question says otherwise |

You answer every question. Cambridge says 55% of the marks test knowledge and understanding (AO1) and 45% test application and communication (AO2).

## A timing plan

The paper gives you 110 minutes for 75 marks, which is about **1.47 minutes per mark**. Aim for **1.3 minutes per mark** on the first pass, so that you finish with time to check.

| Part of the paper | Marks | Time at 1.3 min per mark |
|---|---|---|
| A 4-mark question | 4 | about 5 minutes |
| A 6-mark question | 6 | about 8 minutes |
| A 10-mark question | 10 | about 13 minutes |
| The whole paper | 75 | about 98 minutes, leaving about 12 to check |

Rules of thumb: do not spend more than twice the "mark time" on one question without moving on; leave a gap and come back. A question stuck at 4 minutes over its time costs the marks of the one you never reach. Do the first lines of a hard question anyway, because method marks are awarded for a correct start.

## Formulas: what MF19 gives you and what you must learn

**You are given** (MF19): the arc length \(r\theta\) and sector area \(\tfrac12 r^2\theta\); the quadratic formula; the \(n\)th term and sum of an AP and GP, and \(S_\infty\); the binomial expansion of \((a + b)^n\) with \(\binom{n}{r}\); the trig identities (tan as a quotient, the Pythagorean identities, compound and double angle); the derivatives and standard integrals listed, including \(x^n\); and the volume and surface area of the sphere and cone.

**You must learn:** the equation of a line and of a circle; distance and midpoint; the gradient condition for perpendicular lines; the exact trig values for 30°, 45° and 60°; degree–radian conversion; the chain rule; how to find area and volume by integration; the condition for the discriminant (distinct, repeated, no real roots); how to find an inverse function; and the transformations of graphs.

:::exam Look in MF19 before you try to remember
MF19 holds some formulas you may have memorised from a textbook (for example, the derivative of \(x^n\)). Use the booklet, and spend your memory on what it leaves out. The key point: **it never tells you when to use a formula.** That is the skill this guide trains.
:::

## The marks students lose most often

These come from the warning boxes in the Paper 1 chapters.

- **Quadratics:** dividing by \(x\) and losing a solution; sign slips in the formula (type \((-4)^2\), never \(-4^2\)); writing a combined inequality that cannot be true.
- **Functions:** reading \(fg(x)\) in the wrong order (it means \(f(g(x))\)); treating \(f^{-1}\) as \(\dfrac{1}{f}\); transforming in the wrong order.
- **Coordinate geometry:** forgetting the minus sign when flipping a gradient for a perpendicular; reading the centre of \((x + 4)^2 + \ldots\) as \(+4\); using \(r\) instead of \(r^2\).
- **Circular measure:** putting degrees into \(r\theta\); forgetting that a sector's perimeter includes the two radii.
- **Trigonometry:** losing solutions when the angle is \(2x\) or \(x - 20^\circ\); a calculator in the wrong mode.
- **Series:** a missing bracket round \(3x\) so that \((3x)^2\) becomes \(3x^2\).
- **Differentiation:** forgetting the inside derivative in the chain rule; differentiating a product bracket by bracket.
- **Integration:** missing \(+c\); dividing by the old power; squaring the difference when finding a volume instead of the difference of squares.

:::warn Method before arithmetic
In all of these, the slip is cheap and the lost mark is large. Slow down on the first line of each question, and check the sign of every answer before you move on.
:::

## Final week

:::checklist Final week
- [ ] I have done at least two full timed Paper 1 papers in the last 14 days, and marked them with the mark scheme
- [ ] I can complete the square and use the discriminant without looking anything up
- [ ] I can solve a trig equation in degrees and radians and list every solution in the interval
- [ ] I can differentiate with the chain rule and find the nature of a stationary point
- [ ] I can integrate to find an area between a curve and a line, and a volume of revolution
- [ ] I can find the equation of a tangent and a normal to a curve and a circle
- [ ] I know the formulas MF19 does not give me (line, circle, exact trig values, radians, chain rule)
- [ ] I check the calculator's D or R before every trigonometric calculation
- [ ] I have reviewed my error log and redone the questions I got wrong
- [ ] I know the plan for the paper: 1.3 minutes per mark, leave gaps, check at the end
:::
