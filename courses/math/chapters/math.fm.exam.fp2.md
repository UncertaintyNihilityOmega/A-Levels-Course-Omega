## What the front page says

The June 2025 Paper 2 (9231/21) front page, and the June 2026 one, are the model:

- You write your answers on the question paper, in the space provided, and use the extra lined page at the end (with the question number) if you need more. The specimen paper for examination from 2027 says answers go in a separate printed answer booklet, so read your own paper's instructions.
- Black or dark blue pen, with an HB pencil for diagrams and graphs; no erasable pen or correction fluid.
- The list of formulae MF19 is provided.
- Use a calculator where appropriate, but show all necessary working: **no marks are given for unsupported answers from a calculator**.
- Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place for angles in degrees, unless the question specifies otherwise.

:::exam Exact means exact
"Find the exact value" wants a fraction, a surd, \(\pi\), \(\ln\) or an inverse hyperbolic form, never a decimal. "Show that" gives you the answer, so every line leading to it must be written. Where a question says "hence", use the result of the earlier part: it is usually the only efficient route.
:::

## A timing plan

The paper gives 120 minutes for 75 marks, which is **1.6 minutes per mark**. Plan for **1.4 minutes per mark on a first pass**: 105 minutes for the whole paper, leaving 15 minutes to return to gaps and to check.

| Question size | Marks | Time at 1.4 min per mark |
|---|---|---|
| a short question | 5 | about 7 minutes |
| a medium question | 7 | about 10 minutes |
| a medium to long question | 10 | about 14 minutes |
| a long question | 14 | about 20 minutes |
| the longest question seen in 2025 | 15 | about 21 minutes |
| the whole paper | 75 | about 105 minutes, leaving 15 to check |

Rules of thumb:

1. **Read first, for 3 minutes.** Mark the questions whose first line you can already write.
2. **Every part is an invitation to start.** Even when you cannot finish a part, write the first method line: it earns the M1.
3. **Do not sink time into one part.** Leave a gap, move on, and come back. A later part that says "hence" can use a printed "show that" result without your proof.
4. **Use the structure.** A reduction formula in part (a) is used for a definite integral in part (b); a de Moivre identity in part (a) gives an equation to solve in part (b). The question tells you which tool to use.
5. **Check with a second route** in the last 15 minutes: differentiate your integral, substitute your solution of a DE back, multiply \(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\).

## Formulas: what MF19 gives you and what you must learn

**You are given** (MF19) for Paper 2:
- the Maclaurin series in general, and for \(\mathrm{e}^x\), \(\ln(1 + x)\) (\(-1 < x \le 1\)), \(\sin x\), \(\cos x\), \(\tan^{-1}x\) (\(-1 \le x \le 1\)), \(\sinh x\), \(\cosh x\) and \(\tanh^{-1}x\) (\(-1 < x < 1\));
- \(\cosh^2x - \sinh^2x \equiv 1\), \(\sinh 2x \equiv 2\sinh x\cosh x\) and \(\cosh 2x \equiv \cosh^2x + \sinh^2x\);
- the logarithmic forms \(\sinh^{-1}x = \ln\left(x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}\right)\), \(\cosh^{-1}x = \ln\left(x + \sqrt{x^2 - 1}\right)\) (\(x \ge 1\)) and \(\tanh^{-1}x = \tfrac12\ln\dfrac{1 + x}{1 - x}\) (\(\lvert x\rvert < 1\));
- the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\), \(\sinh x\), \(\cosh x\), \(\tanh x\), \(\sinh^{-1}x\), \(\cosh^{-1}x\), \(\tanh^{-1}x\);
- the integrals of \(\sec x\), \(\operatorname{cosec}x\), \(\sinh x\), \(\cosh x\), \(\operatorname{sech}^2x\), and of \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}}\), \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}}\), \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}}\);
- \(t = \tan\tfrac12x\) with \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) in terms of \(t\), and all the 9709 entries (binomial, geometric series, trigonometric identities, standard derivatives and integrals, integration by parts).

**You must learn** (not on MF19):
- the definitions of \(\sinh\), \(\cosh\), \(\tanh\), \(\operatorname{sech}\), \(\operatorname{cosech}\), \(\coth\) in terms of \(\mathrm{e}^x\), and the derivatives of the reciprocal ones;
- de Moivre's theorem, the \(n\)th roots of unity and the \(z^n \pm z^{-n}\) relations;
- reduction formulae (you derive them each time, by parts);
- the arc length and surface area of revolution formulae, in Cartesian, parametric and (for length) polar form;
- the integrating factor method, the auxiliary equation cases, the trial forms for particular integrals and the clash rule;
- the characteristic equation, \(\mathbf{Ae} = \lambda\mathbf{e}\), \(\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\) and the Cayley–Hamilton theorem; and the determinant and inverse of a matrix, from Paper 1;
- how three planes can meet (a point, a line, no common point).

:::exam MF19 is long: use it
Check MF19 for any standard series, derivative or integral before you derive it. The marks go to the work that connects the formula to the question. The most common wasted time is deriving a Maclaurin series that MF19 prints, and the most common lost marks are on formulae it does not print: the reciprocal hyperbolic derivatives, de Moivre, the reduction chain and the DE trial forms.
:::

## Proofs, identities and "show that"

Paper 2 asks you to **derive** things, and the layout earns the marks.

**Hyperbolic identities.** Start from the definitions \(\cosh x = \tfrac12(\mathrm{e}^x + \mathrm{e}^{-x})\) and \(\sinh x = \tfrac12(\mathrm{e}^x - \mathrm{e}^{-x})\). Work on the more complicated side, expand, and finish on the other side. Do not work on both sides at once. A "prove" of a double-angle identity can use MF19's \(\cosh^2x - \sinh^2x \equiv 1\), but the definitions are the safe start.

**Multiple angles by de Moivre.** Write \((c + \mathrm{i}s)^n\) with \(c = \cos\theta\) and \(s = \sin\theta\), expand with the binomial theorem, reduce powers of \(\mathrm{i}\) (\(\mathrm{i}^2 = -1\), \(\mathrm{i}^3 = -\mathrm{i}\)), then equate real and imaginary parts. Replace \(s^2\) by \(1 - c^2\) only at the end.

**Reduction formulae.** The next example is the layout. Notice the three habits that score: both ends of each boundary term are evaluated, the parts choice is stated, and the specific value at the end uses the chain of indices.

:::eg (Exam style) Let \(I_n = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} x^n\sin x\,dx\) for \(n \ge 0\). (a) Show that \(I_n = n\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)^{n-1} - n(n - 1)I_{n-2}\) for \(n \ge 2\). (b) Hence find the exact value of \(I_2\).
(a) Take \(u = x^n\) and \(\dfrac{dv}{dx} = \sin x\), so \(\dfrac{du}{dx} = nx^{n-1}\) and \(v = -\cos x\): [M1]
\[I_n = \Big[-x^n\cos x\Big]_0^{\pi/2} + n\int_0^{\pi/2} x^{n-1}\cos x\,dx\] [A1]
The boundary term is 0 at \(x = \tfrac\pi2\) (because \(\cos\tfrac\pi2 = 0\)) and 0 at \(x = 0\) (because \(n \ge 2\) makes \(x^n = 0\)). Integrate by parts again with \(u = x^{n-1}\) and \(\dfrac{dv}{dx} = \cos x\): [M1]
\[\begin{aligned}
I_n &= n\Big[x^{n-1}\sin x\Big]_0^{\pi/2} - n(n - 1)\int_0^{\pi/2} x^{n-2}\sin x\,dx \\
&= n\left(\frac\pi2\right)^{n-1} - n(n - 1)I_{n-2}
\end{aligned}\] [A1] [A1]
(b) With \(n = 2\): \(I_2 = 2\left(\dfrac\pi2\right) - 2I_0\), and \(I_0 = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\,dx = \big[-\cos x\big]_0^{\pi/2} = 1\). [M1]
So \(I_2 = \pi - 2\). [A1]
:::

The structure of marks is the same for any reduction formula: M1 for the first parts, A1 for its result, M1 for the second parts, A1 for the boundary terms stated, A1 for the given result, then a method mark and an accuracy mark for the specific value.

**First and second order DEs.** For a second order equation with conditions, the layout never changes: auxiliary equation, complementary function, particular integral, add them, then use the conditions.

:::eg (Exam style) Find the solution of \(y'' + 4y' + 3y = 6\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\) for which \(y = 0\) and \(y' = 1\) when \(x = 0\).
Auxiliary equation \(m^2 + 4m + 3 = 0\), so \(m = -1\) or \(m = -3\). [M1]
The complementary function is \(y = A\mathrm{e}^{-x} + B\mathrm{e}^{-3x}\). [A1]
Try the particular integral \(y = \lambda\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\): then \(y' = -2\lambda\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\) and \(y'' = 4\lambda\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\), and substituting gives \((4\lambda - 8\lambda + 3\lambda)\mathrm{e}^{-2x} = 6\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\). [M1]
So \(-\lambda = 6\) and \(\lambda = -6\). [A1]
The general solution is CF plus PI:
\[y = A\mathrm{e}^{-x} + B\mathrm{e}^{-3x} - 6\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\] [B1]
Use \(y(0) = 0\): \(A + B - 6 = 0\). [M1]
Differentiate the whole expression, \(y' = -A\mathrm{e}^{-x} - 3B\mathrm{e}^{-3x} + 12\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\), and use \(y'(0) = 1\): \(-A - 3B + 12 = 1\), so \(A + 3B = 11\). [M1]
Subtracting gives \(2B = 5\), so \(B = \tfrac52\) and \(A = \tfrac72\):
\[y = \tfrac72\mathrm{e}^{-x} + \tfrac52\mathrm{e}^{-3x} - 6\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\] [A1]
:::

Two habits matter here: add the PI **before** you differentiate for the second condition, and always substitute back (\(y(0) = \tfrac72 + \tfrac52 - 6 = 0\)).

**Statements that score in the other topics.**
- *Eigenvalues:* write \(\det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I}) = 0\), expand, factorise, then for each \(\lambda\) solve \((\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I})\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{0}\). In \(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\) the order of eigenvalues in \(\mathbf{D}\) must match the order of eigenvectors in \(\mathbf{Q}\).
- *Rectangles:* sketch the curve and a rectangle first, say which rectangles are upper and lower, and count them.
- *Arc length:* write the formula, then the derivative, then the root of the sum.
- *Systems of equations:* a zero determinant means "not exactly one solution": test consistency before you say "no solution".

## The marks students lose most often

These come from the warning boxes in the Paper 2 chapters.

- **Hyperbolic:** taking \(\sinh x = \pm\) a value when \(x > 0\) decides the sign; \(\cosh 2x \equiv \cosh^2x - \sinh^2x\) is wrong (that is always 1); writing \(\pm\) inside \(\cosh^{-1}\) instead of when you solve \(\cosh x = c\).
- **Differentiation and Maclaurin:** the derivative of \(\cosh x\) is \(+\sinh x\); \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) is not \(\dfrac{d^2y/dt^2}{d^2x/dt^2}\); dividing by \(3\) instead of \(3!\); dropping terms too early in a product of series.
- **Integration:** using \(\sin^{-1}\) when the root has a plus sign; keeping the old limits after a substitution; mixing the signs of \(\sinh^2u\) and \(\sin^2\theta\) integrals.
- **Reduction formulae:** forgetting the second end of a boundary term; stepping by 1 when the formula steps by 2 (the chain \(6 \to 4 \to 2 \to 0\)).
- **Rectangles:** "wrong side": for a decreasing curve the left rectangles are the upper ones; the wrong count: unit rectangles on \([1, n]\) number \(n - 1\).
- **Arc length and surface:** an unsquared derivative or a missing 1 under the root; \(\sqrt{9\sin^2t\cos^2t} = 3\lvert\sin t\cos t\rvert\), not the signed product; using \(y\) as the distance when rotating about the \(y\)-axis; leaving out \(2\pi\).
- **De Moivre:** raising the angle to the power \(n\) and leaving the modulus; the wrong quadrant for the argument; \(\mathrm{i}^2 = -1\) in the binomial terms; \((1 - c^2)^2 = 1 - 2c^2 + c^4\), not \(1 - c^4\).
- **Roots of unity:** writing \(n\theta = 0\) instead of \(n\theta = 2\pi k\) and losing \(n - 1\) roots; carrying \(\omega\) from one question to another.
- **Systems of planes:** writing "no solution" because the determinant is zero; calling a common line a triangular prism.
- **Eigenvalues:** an eigenvector is a direction (any non-zero multiple); \(\det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I})\) starts with \(-\lambda^3\) for a 3 × 3 matrix; the eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B}\) are not the sums; \(\mathbf{D}\) and \(\mathbf{Q}\) in different orders.
- **First order DEs:** not dividing by the coefficient of \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) first; \(P = -\dfrac4x\), not \(-4\); a missing \(+c\) before dividing by the integrating factor.
- **Second order DEs:** a trial form with the cosine but no sine; using the conditions on the CF alone; an unnoticed clash with the CF (multiply by \(x\)).

:::warn Check the last line
The mark for the final answer depends on every line before it, so the check is worth the minute: differentiate an integral, substitute a DE solution back, test an eigenvector with \(\mathbf{Ae} = \lambda\mathbf{e}\), use \(\theta = 0\) in an identity.
:::

## Reading the marks

Mark schemes for 9231 use M1, A1, B1, DM1 (a method mark that depends on an earlier M), A1 FT and B1 FT (follow-through from your own earlier wrong answer), and abbreviations such as SOI (seen or implied), AWRT (answer which rounds to) and ISW (ignore subsequent working). Marks are awarded positively: they are not deducted for errors, so write every method line, even when you doubt the answer. A "show that" gives the final answer, so the marks go to the lines of working that lead to it, and a jump straight to the given result loses them.

## How to practise

1. **By topic, once.** Finish the chapter practice for each topic first.
2. **Mixed.** Use [Mixed practice: Further Pure](#math.fm.mixed.pure) to practise choosing the method, with questions from both Pure papers.
3. **One timed paper.** Take [Mock Paper 2: Further Pure Mathematics 2](#math.fm.mock.p2) in one sitting of 2 hours, with your calculator and MF19 only. Mark it line by line.
4. **Real papers.** Use the 9231 Paper 2 past papers (the links box) after the chapters are done. One timed paper at a time, marked with its mark scheme.
5. **Error log.** For each lost mark write the topic, what you wrote and what to write instead. Redo the question a week later.
